This guide explains exactly how the game works, which bets to place, and where UK players can find quality live baccarat tables.

How Baccarat Works: Punto Banco Explained

The version you’ll encounter at virtually every online casino is Punto Banco — “Punto” meaning player and “Banco” meaning banker. Unlike some older baccarat variants, you make no decisions about drawing cards. The game follows fixed rules: you simply bet on which side will win.

Each round deals two initial cards to both the player and banker positions. Card values: Aces = 1, 2–9 = face value, 10s and face cards = 0. Only the last digit of the total counts — so a hand of 7 and 8 (totalling 15) is worth 5. The side closest to 9 wins. A third card may be drawn based on the fixed drawing rules, but that happens automatically — you never choose.

FAQ — Baccarat the UK

What is the house edge in online baccarat?▾
The banker bet carries a house edge of approximately 1.06% (after the standard 5% commission). The player bet is 1.24%. The tie bet is around 14.4% — one of the worst bets in any casino game. For the best long-term results, always bet banker.
What is Punto Banco?▾
Punto Banco is the standard version of baccarat played at virtually every online casino. ‘Punto’ means player and ‘Banco’ means banker. Unlike some older baccarat variants, players do not make any drawing decisions — the rules dictate when each side draws a third card. It is entirely a game of chance.
Should I bet on the banker or the player in baccarat?▾
Standard advice is always to bet banker. The banker bet wins slightly more often due to the drawing rules favouring the banker position. The 5% commission on banker wins accounts for this advantage, but the net house edge is still lower than the player bet.

What Is RTP? The Honest Explanation

RTP stands for Return to Player. It is a percentage that describes what a slot game is theoretically designed to pay back to players over a very large number of spins. A reported RTP of 96% means: across millions of spins, the game is designed to return £96 for every £100 wagered. The other £4 is the house edge — the casino's mathematical advantage.

There are two critical things to understand about RTP:

  1. It is a long-run average, not a session guarantee. In any individual playing session — whether that is 100 spins or 1,000 — your result can be dramatically different from the stated RTP. You could lose far more than 4% per spin, or you could win many times your stake. RTP describes the statistical equilibrium that emerges over enormous sample sizes, not what will happen in your session.
  2. It is a theoretical figure. RTP is calculated during game certification and design. It assumes optimal play conditions and an infinite number of spins. The figure you see published is derived from laboratory testing of the game's algorithm, not from real player results at live casinos.
🧮 The maths of RTP: a plain example

You play a slot with a reported 96% RTP at £1 per spin for 100 spins. Total staked: £100. The expected loss at exactly 96% RTP would be £4. But the actual range of outcomes in 100 spins is enormous — standard deviation on a typical slot means you could lose £30, win £50, or hit a large feature win of £200+. The expected value of £4 loss only converges to that figure reliably over tens of thousands or millions of spins. For a recreational player in a typical session, RTP is a measure of which games are less bad mathematically, not a predictor of session outcomes.

RTP and Volatility: The Two Numbers That Matter

RTP and volatility are separate but related measures that together describe a slot's character.

Volatility (also called variance) describes the distribution of wins: how frequently wins occur and how large they are relative to stake. It is not published as a number by game providers, but is usually described as low, medium, or high.

The key insight: RTP and volatility are independent. A high-RTP game can be high or low volatility. A low-RTP game can be high or low volatility. The best combination for a player who wants to minimise long-run losses is high RTP + low-to-medium volatility. The best combination for a player hunting a large win is high RTP + high volatility (though this requires bankroll to withstand the losing streaks).

Which Types of Slots Tend to Have Higher RTPs?

Rather than citing specific games — because RTPs can vary between casino versions of the same game — here are the game type patterns that tend to carry higher RTPs industry-wide:

Classic 3-reel slots

Traditional 3-reel slots with simple mechanics and few or no bonus features have historically offered some of the highest reported RTPs in the slot category. Without complex bonus rounds (which typically have their own built-in variance), the base game mechanics can sustain a higher return. Many classic slots from established European providers are reported to carry RTPs above 97%. Always check the in-game information screen for the specific version you are playing.

Video slots from regulated-market providers

Game developers building primarily for regulated markets (MGA, UKGC) typically design to higher RTP floors than those targeting less regulated jurisdictions. Developers headquartered in Scandinavia, the UK, and Malta have a tradition of high-RTP game design. Industry discussion frequently highlights certain providers — NetEnt, Play'n GO, Quickspin, Yggdrasil — as associated with higher baseline RTPs, though again this varies by specific title and casino version.

Table game-inspired slots and hybrid games

Some slot titles incorporate mechanics borrowed from blackjack, baccarat, or other table games with low house edges. These hybrids sometimes carry RTPs closer to their table game inspirations (95%+) rather than typical slot averages (94–96%).

Progressive jackpot slots: a note

Progressive jackpot slots present a nuanced RTP picture. The headline RTP figure includes the value of the progressive jackpot contribution. Because jackpots are won rarely, the expected RTP for any individual player who does not hit the jackpot is effectively lower than the stated figure. The stated RTP is accurate only if you assume the jackpot will eventually be won and you have an equal chance of winning it. For most players in most sessions, the effective RTP of a progressive jackpot slot is lower than the stated number. Play progressives for the jackpot dream, not for RTP optimisation.

How to Find a Slot's RTP: The Practical Method

  1. Open the game in demo mode and look for an information button (usually labelled "i", "?", or "Rules").
  2. The in-game RTP is the most reliable figure because it reflects the specific version the casino is running — some providers offer operators different RTP variants of the same game.
  3. Cross-check on the game provider's official website — most major providers publish RTP documentation for their catalogues.
  4. Treat third-party aggregator RTP figures as reported estimates, not verified facts, unless the aggregator cites the specific casino's version.

RTP vs House Edge: The Same Number, Two Perspectives

RTP and house edge are two ways of describing the same mathematics:

  • A slot with 96% RTP has a 4% house edge.
  • A slot with 97.5% RTP has a 2.5% house edge.
  • European roulette has a 2.70% house edge (97.30% RTP equivalent on outside bets).
  • Blackjack with basic strategy has roughly a 0.5% house edge (99.5% RTP equivalent).

This comparison is important: slots, even at high RTPs, typically carry higher house edges than optimal-play table games. A 97% RTP slot has a 3% house edge. Blackjack at 0.5% is six times better mathematically. If your goal is to minimise mathematical losses, table games outperform slots. If your goal is entertainment, the slot's bonus features, visuals, and gameplay may justify the higher house edge as the cost of that entertainment.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does RTP mean in slots?

RTP (Return to Player) is the theoretical percentage of wagered money a slot pays back over millions of spins. 96% RTP means the game is designed to return £96 per £100 staked on average. It is a long-run statistical average, not a session guarantee.

Does a higher RTP mean I will win more?

No. RTP is a long-run theoretical average. In any individual session, results are determined by the random number generator. High RTP reduces the mathematical house edge over time but does not guarantee any specific outcome in any given session. No slot guarantees wins.

What is a good RTP for a slot?

Industry averages run from roughly 94% to 97%. Above 96% is considered good; above 97% is high. Always verify in the game's own information screen, as the same game may have different RTP versions at different casinos.

What is volatility and how does it relate to RTP?

Volatility describes how wins are distributed — low volatility pays small amounts often; high volatility pays large amounts rarely. RTP and volatility are independent measures. A high-RTP game can be high or low volatility. Match volatility to your bankroll and playing style.

Where can I check a slot's RTP?

Open the game and look for an "i" or "?" information button. The in-game RTP is the most reliable figure as it reflects the specific version the casino runs. Cross-check on the game provider's official website. Treat third-party figures as reported estimates.

Can casinos change a slot's RTP?

Some providers offer multiple RTP versions of the same game, allowing operators to select which version to run. This means the same game name can have a different RTP at different casinos. Always check the in-game information screen at the specific casino you are playing.

The Credit Card Gambling Ban in the UK, Explained

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 31 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

The Short Version

Credit cards cannot be used for gambling in the UK under rules introduced to protect players from betting with borrowed money. Since 2020, all major bookmakers and casinos have been required to decline credit card deposits. Debit cards, e-wallets (PayPal, Skrill, Neteller), and bank transfers remain accepted. Revolut and other digital banks are treated as debit cards unless you use a credit feature.

Who the Ban Applies To

The prohibition binds licensed operators. Since the UKGC currently licenses remote betting only, the hard legal ban applies to bookmakers. Offshore-licensed casinos serving UK players are not yet inside the UKGC system — some still list credit cards. When UK casino licensing opens, the ban will extend to every licensed casino automatically.

What It Means at the Bookmaker

For sports betting the ban is already fully in force, because bookmakers are the operators the UKGC actually licenses today. In practice: a credit card is declined at the cashier of any UK-licensed betting site, UK banks (AIB, Bank of the UK, PTSB) block gambling-coded credit transactions, and Revolut blocks credit-funded gambling payments the way it does in the UK. Funding a betting account now means debit card, bank transfer, or an e-wallet funded from your own money. Attempting to route credit through a wallet to dodge the rule can breach the operator's terms and void winnings — and defeats the point: never bet borrowed money. Full method-by-method guide: Revolut guide.

Why the Ban Exists

Gambling with borrowed money is the single strongest predictor of gambling harm. Research behind the UK ban found a large share of credit-card gamblers were classified as problem gamblers. Removing credit from the equation forces gambling back inside money you actually have — which is the first rule of safe play anyway.

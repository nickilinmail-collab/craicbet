How Casino Bonuses Actually Work: The Maths

Before we cover bonus types, let's establish the fundamental arithmetic. A casino bonus is a marketing cost the operator is willing to pay to acquire your account. Understanding what it actually costs them — and what it can realistically return to you — is the foundation of making intelligent bonus decisions.

Every bonus has a wagering requirement (also called playthrough). This tells you how many times you must bet the bonus amount before withdrawing winnings. A £100 bonus at 35x means wagering £3,500. If the game you are playing has a 3% house edge (the other 97% returned to players on average), those £3,500 of play carry an expected loss of £105 — more than the bonus itself. This is why high wagering requirements can make a bonus mathematically negative.

The formula: Expected value of bonus = (Bonus amount × Slot RTP%) − (Bonus amount × Wagering requirement × House edge). The only way a bonus has positive expected value is if RTP is very high relative to wagering requirements — which is rare in standard offers.

🧮 Wagering requirement reality check
  • Under 25x: Player-friendly; worth considering
  • 25x–35x: Industry average; moderate clearing difficulty
  • 35x–50x: High; bonus value is significantly eroded
  • Over 50x: Very high; bonus rarely converts to withdrawable cash
  • Zero wagering: Rare but genuinely valuable; treat winnings as cash immediately

Types of Casino Bonus Available to UK players

Welcome Bonus (Match Deposit)

The most common type. The casino matches your first deposit by a percentage — typically 100% up to a capped amount. Example: 100% match up to £200 means depositing £200 gives you £200 in bonus funds, for £400 to play with. The bonus funds (not your deposit) carry the wagering requirement. Your deposit is typically available to withdraw at any time, but the bonus funds are locked until cleared.

Watch for: maximum bet per spin limits while a bonus is active (often £5 per spin); game contribution restrictions; expiry clocks (often 7–30 days to clear the wagering requirement).

Free Spins

Free spins are awarded on specific slot games, typically with a fixed spin value (often £0.10 per spin). One hundred free spins at £0.10 = £10 total value before wagering. Winnings from free spins are usually converted to bonus funds with their own wagering requirement. The key number is not how many free spins you get — it is the spin value and the wagering requirement on the winnings.

No Deposit Bonus

Free credits or spins given on registration, before any deposit. These are the highest-value offers for risk-free exploration because you have nothing at stake. However, they come with the strictest wagering requirements, tight withdrawal caps (often £20–£50 maximum withdrawal from no-deposit winnings), and game restrictions. Treat them as a free trial of the casino, not a realistic income source.

Reload Bonus

A match deposit offer for existing players on subsequent deposits. Usually lower percentages (25–50%) and lower caps than welcome bonuses. Reload bonuses are where the ongoing value of a casino relationship sits — a casino with generous, frequent reloads is often more valuable long-term than one with a large welcome offer and poor ongoing promotions.

Cashback Bonus

A percentage of net losses returned as bonus funds or, in better offers, as cash. Cashback typically carries lower wagering requirements than match bonuses (often 1x or zero). A 10% weekly cashback on losses up to £500 is a genuinely useful ongoing offer because it softens losing sessions without requiring additional deposits. Check whether cashback is on net losses or gross losses, and whether it is paid as bonus funds or real cash.

Loyalty Schemes and VIP Programmes

Points-based systems where play earns points redeemable for bonus funds or cash. The better programmes have clear conversion rates published upfront. Be cautious of VIP schemes that offer high-value perks in exchange for increased betting volumes — responsible gambling guidance suggests that bonuses contingent on higher spend can be problematic for some players.

Game Contribution to Wagering Requirements

Not all games contribute equally to clearing a wagering requirement. Here are typical contribution rates (they vary between casinos; always check the specific T&Cs):

  • Video slots: 100% — every £1 bet clears £1 of wagering requirement
  • Classic slots: 100% (sometimes 50% — check per casino)
  • Live slots (live game shows): 50–100% — varies
  • Roulette: 10–20%, sometimes 0% — frequently restricted
  • Blackjack: 5–20%, sometimes 0% — low contribution due to low house edge
  • Live casino (live roulette, live blackjack): Often 0% — frequently excluded
  • Video poker: 10–20% — often restricted

The practical implication: unless you like slots, many casino bonuses are effectively only usable on slots. If you prefer table games or live casino, consider playing without a bonus active so your stake is real cash with no restrictions.

How to Claim a Casino Bonus Safely

  1. Read the full T&Cs before depositing — not the marketing summary, the actual terms. Look for wagering requirement, game contributions, max bet per spin, expiry, and withdrawal cap.
  2. Check whether the bonus is opt-in or automatic — some casinos auto-apply bonuses on deposit. If you do not want the bonus, opt out before depositing; some casinos do not allow you to remove a bonus once applied.
  3. Set a deposit limit before claiming — go to the account settings and set a daily or weekly deposit limit equal to what you planned to deposit. This protects you from impulse reloads during a session.
  4. Stick to 100%-contribution games — use slots to clear wagering, not roulette or live casino where contributions are low.
  5. Track your wagering progress — most casinos show a wagering progress bar in your account. Monitor it. Know how much remains before you can withdraw.
  6. Withdraw as soon as the wagering clears — do not continue playing with winnings from a cleared bonus unless you are making a deliberate, funded decision to do so.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a wagering requirement?

A wagering requirement (also called playthrough) tells you how many times you must bet the bonus amount before withdrawing winnings. A £100 bonus at 35x means wagering £3,500 before you can withdraw. Always read this number before claiming any bonus.

Are casino bonuses legal in the UK?

Yes. Casino bonuses are legal for UK players at internationally licensed sites. No UKGC casino licence exists yet (expected 2027–2028), so UK-focused casinos operate under MGA, UKGC, or similar licences. Read our full UKGC casino licence explainer.

What is a no deposit bonus?

A no deposit bonus gives you free credits or spins without depositing first. These carry high wagering requirements and withdrawal caps. Use them to try a casino risk-free; treat them as a trial, not a likely source of withdrawable cash.

Do free spins winnings count as cash?

Usually not immediately. Free spin winnings are typically awarded as bonus funds with their own wagering requirement before they become withdrawable cash. Check the specific wagering requirement on free spin winnings, not just on the deposit match.

What is a good wagering requirement?

Under 30x is player-friendly; 30x–40x is average; above 40x the bonus is difficult to clear. Zero-wagering bonuses exist but are rare. Always compare wagering requirements, not just the headline bonus amounts.

Can I use a casino bonus on any game?

No. Most bonuses restrict game contributions to wagering. Slots typically count 100%; table games and live casino often count 10–20% or are excluded entirely. Read the game contribution table in the T&Cs before claiming.

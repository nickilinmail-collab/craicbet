the UK’s £10 Stake and £3,000 Win Limits: What’s Actually Coming

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Buried in Schedule 3 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 are two numbers that would reshape online casino play in the UK more than any licence or tax: a maximum stake of £10 per game and a maximum win of £3,000. They are written into the Act itself — not a proposal floated in a press release — but they are not yet biting, because the licences they attach to do not exist yet. Here is where things actually stand.

What the Act says

For a “relevant game” — the Act’s term covering games of chance such as slots, roulette and other casino-style games — Schedule 3 sets a maximum relevant payment (the stake) of £10 and maximum winnings of £3,000. Section 83 gives the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) the power to vary either figure, but only with the consent of the Minister. So the numbers are a starting point set by the Oireachtas, with a built-in mechanism to move them later.

Proposal or law? The honest answer: enacted, not yet in force

This is the nuance most coverage gets wrong in both directions. The limits are law on paper — they were passed with the Act in October 2024. But they apply to holders of UKGC gaming licences, and UKGC has issued none: since July 2026 it licenses remote betting only, and online casino (gaming) licences are not expected before 2027–2028. Until a gaming licensing regime commences, no operator in the UK is actually bound by the £10/£3,000 caps. The practical question is not whether the limits exist, but in what form they commence — and Section 83 means they could be adjusted before day one.

What £10 / £3,000 would mean for slots players

For most recreational slots play, a £10 stake cap changes little — typical spins in the UK are well under that. The £3,000 win cap is the bigger deal. Progressive jackpots, high-volatility slots where the appeal is a rare 5,000x hit, and bonus-buy features would all be capped or withdrawn. Honest maths point: a win cap also quietly worsens effective RTP on volatile games, because the rare top-end wins that balance the long losing stretches get truncated while the losing stretches stay the same.

Poker’s particular problem

Industry pushback has been loudest from poker. Commentary in the poker press has argued that limits designed for house-banked casino games could make normal poker products unworkable if applied to tournaments and cash games, where “winnings” routinely exceed £3,000 by design — a £50 tournament with 100 runners already breaches the cap for the winner. How peer-to-peer games will be treated is reported to be one of the open implementation questions UKGC must resolve before gaming licences launch; no final position has been published.

What happens between now and commencement

Expect the limits to be a central argument in the run-up to gaming licensing: operators will lobby for Section 83 adjustments, public health advocates will defend the caps, and UKGC will have to set positions on poker, live casino and jackpot games. Anything you read presenting final figures for 2027 today is speculation — the only fixed points are the Schedule 3 numbers and the power to vary them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the £10 stake and £3,000 win limits in force in the UK?

No. They are written into Schedule 3 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, but they attach to UKGC gaming licences — and none have been issued. UKGC currently licenses remote betting only, with casino licensing expected around 2027–2028.

Can the limits be changed before casino licensing starts?

Yes. Section 83 of the Act allows UKGC to vary the maximum stake and maximum winnings figures with the consent of the Minister, so the Schedule 3 numbers are a starting point rather than a final word.

Do the limits apply to sports betting?

No. The caps apply to “relevant games” — casino-style games of chance. Remote betting, which UKGC has licensed since July 2026, has no equivalent statutory stake or win cap in Schedule 3.

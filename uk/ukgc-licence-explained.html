UKGC Licence Explained: What It Means for UK Punters

Last updated: 6 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

The UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) is the independent regulator for gambling in Great Britain. Any operator offering gambling to UK customers — sports betting, casino, poker, bingo — must hold a current UKGC remote operating licence. This guide explains what that licence means in practice for punters, how to verify one, and what protections it gives you.

📌 Key facts about the UKGC
  • Established under the Gambling Act 2005, updated by the 2024 White Paper
  • Regulates remote (online) and non-remote (physical premises) gambling in Great Britain
  • 2,800+ licensed operators as of 2026
  • Public register available at gamblingcommission.gov.uk
  • Operators pay annual fees based on gross gambling yield; fines for breaches routinely exceed £1 million

What the UKGC Licence Requires from Operators

A UKGC-licensed operator must comply with the Licence Conditions and Codes of Practice (LCCP), which cover:

  • Fair and transparent terms — bonus terms, wagering requirements and withdrawal conditions must be clearly stated
  • Responsible gambling tools — deposit limits, session limits, loss limits, reality checks and self-exclusion must be accessible in-app
  • GamStop participation — mandatory membership of the national self-exclusion scheme
  • No credit card deposits — banned since April 2020
  • Affordability checks — background checks for net losses above £500/month; income verification at higher thresholds (phased in 2024–2026)
  • Age verification — operators must verify age before allowing any gambling activity
  • Complaints handling — an Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) scheme must be available

How to Verify a UKGC Licence in 3 Steps

  1. Find the licence number in the operator's footer or under Settings → Legal / About. It is usually in small text alongside the legal company name and registered address.
  2. Go to gamblingcommission.gov.uk/check-if-an-operator-is-licensed and search by company name or licence number. Status must show Active.
  3. Cross-check with GamStop — all UKGC-licensed operators must participate. If the operator is not on GamStop, its UKGC licence claim should be treated with scepticism.

What to Do If You Have a Problem with a UKGC-Licensed Operator

UKGC-licensed operators are required to have an Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) process. If you have a complaint:

  1. Raise the complaint with the operator first through their official complaints process (usually under Help / Contact).
  2. If unresolved after 8 weeks, escalate to the operator's ADR provider — this is listed in the operator's terms and conditions.
  3. Report serious concerns (unlicensed activity, repeated LCCP breaches) to the UKGC directly at gamblingcommission.gov.uk/contact-us.

UKGC Casino Licence: When Will Online Casinos Be Licensed in the UK?

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 10 min read

💡 Current Status (November 2026)

The UKGC does not yet issue casino or gaming licences. Only remote betting licences are available (since July 2026). Casino licences are on the UKGC's roadmap and are expected to become available in 2027 or 2028. All online casinos currently serving UK players operate under international licences.

The Current State of Casino Licensing in the UK

As of November 2026, the UKGC has successfully launched its first tranche of licences — remote betting licences — covering online sports betting and similar wagering activities. This was a landmark achievement for UK gambling regulation.

However, the licensing framework for online casino games (slots, table games, live dealer games, and poker) has not yet been finalised. This means there is currently no UK domestic licence that covers online casino gaming. UK players who want to play casino games online do so at operators licensed by international regulators such as the MGA (Malta), UKGC (UK), or Gibraltar Gambling Commissioner.

What Will UKGC Casino Licences Cover?

While the final details are still being developed, the UKGC casino licence framework is expected to cover:

  • Online Slots: All real-money slot games offered to UK players
  • Table Games: Blackjack, roulette, baccarat, and other casino table games
  • Live Dealer Games: Games streamed from live studios with real dealers
  • Poker: Online poker cash games and tournaments
  • Virtual Sports: Simulated sports events with real-money betting

What This Means for UK players

When UKGC casino licences become available, it will bring several important changes:

  • Full Domestic Protection: UK players will have the same level of regulatory protection for casino games as they now have for sports betting.
  • Dispute Resolution: If you have a problem with an online casino, you will be able to complain to the UKGC — an UK regulator that understands UK consumer law.
  • National Self-Exclusion: The planned national self-exclusion register will cover casino operators as well as bookmakers.
  • Quality Assurance: UKGC-licensed casinos will need to meet UK standards for fairness, security, and responsible gambling.

What Happens to Offshore Casinos?

Once the UKGC begins licensing casinos, operators serving UK players without a UKGC licence may face enforcement actions. The UKGC has the power to:

  • DNS Blocking: Request ISPs to block access to unlicensed sites from the UK
  • Payment Processing Restrictions: Prevent UK financial institutions from processing transactions with unlicensed operators
  • Fines: Impose financial penalties on operators that continue to serve the UK without a licence

This does not mean all offshore casinos will disappear. Major operators licensed by the MGA, UKGC, and Gibraltar are likely to apply for UKGC casino licences, ensuring continued service to UK players under the new framework. Smaller or less reputable operators may choose not to apply, and UK players may lose access to them.

Which Operators Are Likely to Apply?

While no operator has publicly confirmed plans to apply for a UKGC casino licence (since applications are not yet open), several types of operators are well positioned:

  • Operators already holding UKGC betting licences — they have an established relationship with the regulator
  • Large international operators with MGA or UKGC licences and an existing UK customer base
  • Operators with a physical presence in the UK (offices, employees, or UK directors)

What Should UK players Do Now?

Until UKGC casino licences are available, UK players should:

  1. Choose well-licensed operators: Prioritise casinos with MGA, UKGC, or Gibraltar licences for the strongest player protection
  2. Verify licences: Check the operator's licence on the regulator's website, not just the casino's own claims
  3. Use responsible gambling tools: Set deposit limits, use reality checks, and take advantage of self-exclusion if needed
  4. Stay informed: Follow CraicBet for updates as the UKGC casino licensing framework develops

FAQ: UKGC Casino Licences

When will the UKGC start licensing online casinos?▾
Casino licence applications are expected to open in 2027, with the first licences likely issued in late 2027 or 2028. The UKGC is currently developing the licensing framework through consultation with the industry and other stakeholders.
Can I still play at online casinos in the UK right now?▾
Yes. UK players can legally play at internationally licensed online casinos. The fact that the UKGC does not yet issue casino licences does not make it illegal to play — it simply means casino games are regulated by offshore authorities rather than the UK domestic regulator.
Will my favourite casino be blocked when UKGC licensing starts?▾
That depends on whether your casino applies for and receives a UKGC licence. Major operators with MGA or UKGC licences are likely to apply. Smaller or less reputable operators may not, and could eventually be blocked. The transition period is expected to give operators time to apply.
How much will a UKGC casino licence cost?▾
The fee structure for UKGC casino licences has not yet been announced. For reference, UKGC remote betting licence fees and the costs charged by comparable regulators (MGA, UKGC) suggest that application fees and annual levies will be substantial but manageable for established operators.

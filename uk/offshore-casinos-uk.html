💡 Key Facts

Why All UK Casinos Are “Offshore”

If you play online casino games in the UK, you are playing at an offshore casino. That is not because you are doing anything unusual or risky — it is simply because the UKGC does not yet issue casino licences.

The UKGC began issuing remote betting licences in July 2026, but casino and gaming licences are still being developed and are expected in 2027–2028. Until then, every online casino accepting UK players operates under a licence from another jurisdiction: Malta, the UK, Gibraltar, Curaçao, or elsewhere.

This situation is similar to what existed in many European countries before they established dedicated online gaming regulators. It is legal, it is normal, and millions of UK players use offshore casinos safely every day.

How to Check if an Offshore Casino is Safe

Before you deposit money at any online casino, run through this safety checklist:

  1. Verify the licence: Check the casino's claimed licence on the regulator's website. Do not rely on the casino's own statements — verify independently. Look for the licence number, usually displayed in the casino's footer.
  2. Check the licence type: Make sure the licence covers casino games, not just sports betting. Some operators have a betting licence from one regulator but offer casino games under a different (potentially weaker) licence.
  3. Look for SSL encryption: The site should use HTTPS (look for the padlock icon in your browser). This protects your personal and financial data.
  4. Check payment methods: Reputable casinos support mainstream payment methods (Visa, Mastercard, Revolut, bank transfer). A casino that only accepts cryptocurrency or obscure e-wallets may be a red flag.
  5. Read the terms and conditions: Pay attention to withdrawal limits, wagering requirements, and KYC verification procedures. Unreasonable terms are a warning sign.
  6. Search for player reviews: Look for reviews on independent platforms (not the casino's own site). Pay attention to patterns in complaints, especially about delayed or refused withdrawals.

Red Flags: Signs of an Unsafe Casino

  • No verifiable licence or a licence from an unknown jurisdiction
  • Unrealistically generous bonuses with unclear terms
  • Delayed or refused withdrawals reported by multiple players
  • No live chat or customer support available
  • Poorly designed website with broken features
  • No responsible gambling tools (deposit limits, self-exclusion)
  • Requirement to download software to play (legitimate casinos work in-browser)

FAQ: Offshore Casinos the UK

Are offshore casinos legal in the UK?▾
Yes. UK players can legally play at offshore casinos. Since the UKGC does not yet issue casino licences, all online casinos serving the UK operate under offshore licences. There are no laws criminalising UK players for using these services.
Which offshore licence is safest for UK players?▾
The UKGC (UK Gambling Commission) offers the strongest player protections, followed by the MGA (Malta Gaming Authority) and Gibraltar. We recommend prioritising casinos with one of these three licences.
Is a Curaçao licence trustworthy?▾
A Curaçao licence provides basic regulatory oversight, but it is weaker than the MGA, UKGC, or Gibraltar. Many reputable casinos hold Curaçao licences, but if something goes wrong, you have fewer regulatory protections and limited dispute resolution options. We recommend it only for casinos with an established positive track record.
Will offshore casinos be blocked when UKGC starts licensing?▾
Potentially. The UKGC has the power to request DNS blocking of unlicensed operators. However, major operators with MGA or UKGC licences are expected to apply for UKGC licences, ensuring continued access for UK players. A transition period is likely.
What should I do if I have a problem with an offshore casino?▾
First, contact the casino's customer support. If that does not resolve your issue, complain to the casino's licensing authority (MGA, UKGC, or whoever issued their licence). Each regulator has a player complaints procedure. Keep records of all communications and transactions.

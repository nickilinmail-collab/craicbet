What Happened: Stake Geo-Blocked the UK in 2026

Stake.com was one of the world's most heavily marketed online gambling platforms, known for its crypto-friendly approach and sponsorship deals with high-profile sports figures. However, following the introduction of the UK's new gambling licensing regime from 1 July 2026, the platform found itself on the wrong side of UK law.

The UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) contacted Stake and required the company to geo-block UK players. Stake complied and introduced restrictions preventing users with UK IP addresses from accessing the platform. This was reported by the UK Times and independently noted by gambling industry sources including casinos.com.

UK players who attempt to visit Stake.com are now shown a geographic restriction message. Accounts previously held by UK residents were also affected.

Why UKGC Blocked Stake: Operating Without a Licence

Under the UK's Gambling Regulation Act 2024, any operator offering remote betting services to customers in the UK must hold a UKGC remote betting licence. The UKGC began issuing these licences from 1 July 2026. By November 2026, 33 operators have been licensed.

Stake.com did not hold — and had not applied for — a UKGC remote betting licence at the time the UKGC intervened. This meant Stake was accepting bets from UK customers without the regulatory authorisation required under UK law.

The UKGC's enforcement action against Stake is part of a broader effort to bring unlicensed offshore operators into compliance. Stake is not the only platform affected; several other operators have been required to implement similar geo-blocks pending licence applications.

Will Stake Return to the UK?

Stake has publicly indicated that it may apply for a UKGC remote betting licence in order to legally re-enter the UK market. However, as of November 2026, no such licence has been granted and Stake does not appear on the UKGC's official licensed operators register.

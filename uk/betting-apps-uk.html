Best Betting Apps in the UK 2026: Complete Guide

Last updated: 6 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The UK has one of the world's largest and most competitive betting markets, with over 2,800 UKGC-licensed operators. For most UK punters, the mobile app is now the primary way to bet — this guide explains what separates a good UK betting app from a poor one, how to verify UKGC licensing, and what responsible gambling tools the law requires.

📌 Quick summary — UK betting apps
  • All legal UK betting apps must hold a UKGC remote betting licence — check at gamblingcommission.gov.uk
  • Credit card deposits are banned in the UK since April 2020; debit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay are allowed
  • All UKGC-licensed apps must participate in GamStop, the UK's national self-exclusion scheme
  • Apps must provide deposit limits, loss limits and self-exclusion within the app itself
  • Affordability checks apply from £500/month net losses — operators may request income verification

What to Look For in a UK Betting App

Speed and stability: In-play betting on a football match or horse race requires near-instant odds refresh and bet acceptance. Apps that freeze or crash at peak times — Saturday 3pm, Cheltenham Festival, Grand National — are a practical problem. Check App Store and Google Play reviews specifically for match-day performance before downloading.

In-play interface: The in-play section is used under time pressure. A well-designed app surfaces live markets for active events clearly, makes it easy to switch between sports, and shows a live scoreboard alongside the odds. A poorly designed app buries in-play markets behind menus.

Cash-out: Cash-out allows you to settle a bet before the event ends, locking in a profit or reducing a loss. Partial cash-out lets you close part of the position while leaving the rest running. These features need to work reliably on mobile — a cash-out button that fails to respond during a key moment is a significant functional failure.

Payments: Apple Pay, Google Pay, Revolut and debit cards should be available for instant deposits. Credit cards for gambling deposits have been banned in the UK since April 2020. Withdrawal speeds matter: most licensed apps process withdrawals in 1–3 business days; some offer faster routes.

UKGC Licensing: What to Check

Every legal UK betting app must hold a current UKGC licence. Here is how to verify one:

  1. Find the licence number in the app's footer or under Settings → About / Legal. It is usually in small text alongside the legal company name.
  2. Search gamblingcommission.gov.uk using the operator's legal company name. The register is public and updated regularly. Status should show "Active".
  3. Check GamStop participation — all UKGC-licensed operators must participate. If an app claims to be "UKGC licensed" but is not on GamStop, that is a red flag.
  4. Credit card deposits rejected — a UKGC-licensed app will not accept credit card deposits. If it does, it is not operating lawfully for UK customers.

GamStop: The UK's National Self-Exclusion Scheme

GamStop is unique to the UK — there is no equivalent in Ireland or most other markets. When you register at gamstop.co.uk, you are blocked from all UKGC-licensed gambling sites and apps for a minimum of 6 months (you can choose 1 year or 5 years). The exclusion applies automatically across all participating operators — you do not need to contact each one individually.

All UKGC-licensed betting apps are required to participate in GamStop. This is one of the clearest ways to verify a UK app is legitimate: search for the operator on the GamStop website, or simply try to register through GamStop and check if the operator appears. Responsible Gambling Features Required by the UKGC

Under UKGC licensing, all betting apps must provide responsible gambling tools that are genuinely accessible — not hidden. Required tools include deposit limits, session time limits, loss limits, reality check reminders, and self-exclusion (via GamStop and the operator's own system). These must be accessible within the app itself, typically under Account → Responsible Gambling or similar.

From 2024–2026, the UKGC rolled out enhanced affordability checks: operators must carry out background checks on customers with net losses exceeding £500/month, and may request income verification for higher loss thresholds. This is a legal requirement, not an optional check.

Frequently Asked Questions — Betting Apps UK

Are betting apps legal in the UK?

Yes. Betting apps are legal in the UK when the operator holds a current UKGC licence. The UKGC public register at gamblingcommission.gov.uk lists all licensed operators. Any app promoting gambling to UK customers without a UKGC licence is operating illegally.

Can I use Apple Pay on betting apps in the UK?

Yes. Many UKGC-licensed betting apps accept Apple Pay for deposits. Credit card deposits have been banned in the UK since April 2020, but Apple Pay linked to a debit card is permitted. Availability varies by operator — check payment options before signing up.

What is GamStop and do betting apps use it?

GamStop is the UK's national self-exclusion scheme. All UKGC-licensed operators are required to participate. When you register with GamStop, you are blocked from all participating operators for at least 6 months. See our GamStop guide for full details on how it works and how to register.

How do I check if a betting app is UKGC licensed?

Find the legal company name in the app's footer, then search at gamblingcommission.gov.uk/check-if-an-operator-is-licensed. The licence status should show as Active. All UKGC-licensed operators are also required to participate in GamStop.

What are affordability checks on UK betting apps?

From 2024, the UKGC requires operators to carry out background affordability checks on customers with net losses above £500/month, and to request income verification at higher thresholds. This is a legal requirement. If an app requests income documents, that is a sign it is operating under UKGC rules, not a reason to avoid it.

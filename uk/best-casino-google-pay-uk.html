Google Pay offers Android users a fast, secure way to deposit at online casinos. Like Apple Pay, it uses tokenisation to protect your card details. Here's what UK Android users need to know.

Google Pay at Online Casinos

To use Google Pay at a casino, select it in the deposit section and authenticate using your fingerprint, PIN, or face unlock. Your linked debit or credit card is charged. No card details are stored by the casino — only a unique transaction token is transmitted.

Supported Banks in the UK

Google Pay works with most UK banks (AIB, Bank of the UK, PTSB, Ulster Bank) and digital banks (Revolut, N26, Bunq). Add your card in the Google Pay app and use it at any supported casino. Some credit card issuers block gambling transactions — check with your bank if deposits are declined.

Google Pay Withdrawals

As with Apple Pay, most casinos process Google Pay withdrawals back to your underlying card rather than directly via Google Pay. Processing times are the same as standard debit card withdrawals (1–3 business days).

Google Pay vs Revolut for Casino Deposits

Both are fast and convenient. The key difference: Revolut has a built-in gambling block feature, which can be enabled or accidentally active. Google Pay itself doesn't have a gambling-specific block, though your underlying bank may. For seamless deposits without unexpected blocks, Google Pay linked to a standard AIB or BOI debit card is often more reliable.

Limits and Fees

Google Pay itself charges no transaction fees. Your bank's standard card charges apply (usually none for UK debit cards). Casino minimum deposits via Google Pay are typically £10–£20.

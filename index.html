Online Gambling in Ireland — What You Need to Know

Online gambling is fully legal in Ireland. Irish players have the right to access online casinos licensed in any jurisdiction, and there are no laws prohibiting individuals from playing at offshore gambling sites. Here's a comprehensive overview of everything you need to know:

The Legal Framework

Ireland's gambling legislation is undergoing its most significant transformation in decades. The Gambling Regulation Act 2022 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country's first dedicated gambling regulator. GRAI has begun issuing remote betting licences, with online casino and gaming licences expected to follow in 2027-2028. Until these new licences are available, Irish players can legally use casinos holding international licences from jurisdictions like Malta (MGA), the United Kingdom (UKGC), Gibraltar, and Curacao.

Tax on Winnings

One of the most attractive aspects of gambling in Ireland: casino winnings are completely tax-free for players. Ireland taxes gambling operators (through betting duty and licensing fees), not the players themselves. Whether you win €10 or €1,000,000, you keep every cent. This applies to all forms of gambling including casino, sports betting, poker, and lottery.

Age Requirements

You must be 18 years or older to gamble online in Ireland. All licensed casinos are required to verify your age during the registration and KYC process. Providing false information about your age is a violation of the casino's terms and may result in your account being closed and winnings forfeited.

Responsible Gambling

GRAI is developing comprehensive responsible gambling standards for the Irish market. In the meantime, all reputable casinos offer tools including deposit limits, loss limits, session time reminders, cooling-off periods, and self-exclusion. If gambling stops being fun, support is available through Gambling Awareness Ireland and other organisations.

GRAI Licensed Casinos — What to Expect

When GRAI begins issuing online casino licences, Irish players can expect enhanced protections including mandatory spending limits, stronger identity verification, integration with a national self-exclusion register, and stricter advertising standards.

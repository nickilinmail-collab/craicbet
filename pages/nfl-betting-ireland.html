NFL Betting from Ireland: Sundays That Start at Six

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

American football has found a real Irish audience — helped along by the NFL bringing a regular-season game to Croke Park in 2025 — and its schedule suits this side of the Atlantic better than most US sports. The main Sunday slate kicks off at 6pm Irish time, the late games around half nine, and the Sunday night game for the committed. One game week, a huge menu of markets, and a sport where the betting maths is unusually well defined. This guide covers the structure honestly.

The spread is the sport

NFL mismatches are constant, so the handicap (spread) market is where nearly all the money goes. A -6.5 favourite must win by 7 or more; the +6.5 underdog covers by losing by 6 or fewer, or winning. Both sides are typically priced around 10/11, which sets the famous break-even bar: you must beat the spread about 52.4% of the time before you make a cent. That number is the honest core of all NFL betting — every angle, stat and strong opinion is an attempt to clear it.

Key numbers: why 3 and 7 rule everything

NFL scoring runs on field goals (3) and touchdowns (7), so game margins cluster hard on those numbers — far more games are decided by exactly 3 or exactly 7 points than by 4, 5 or 6. The consequence: the difference between -2.5 and -3.5 is enormous, while the difference between -4.5 and -5.5 barely matters. Crossing a key number is worth paying for; a half-point that does not cross one usually is not. If you learn one piece of NFL betting craft, make it this.

Totals and the shape of a game

Over/under lines price expected combined scoring, and they respond to pace, weather at outdoor stadiums, and quarterback quality more than to reputations. Totals also have their own softer key numbers (41, 44, 47 — combinations of 3s and 7s). The same discipline applies as spreads: have a reason rooted in how the game will actually be played, not a feeling that two famous offences must mean points.

Teasers: the margin trap with the friendly face

A teaser lets you move the spread in your favour — typically 6 points — on two or more games, in exchange for much shorter combined odds. It feels like buying safety; it is usually buying margin. Honest maths: a standard two-team 6-point teaser needs each leg to win roughly 73% of the time to break even, and random 6-point moves do not get an average bet there. The exception old hands talk about — teasing through both key numbers, e.g. a +1.5 underdog up to +7.5 — is real but narrow, and bookmakers have tightened teaser pricing for exactly that reason. Treat teasers as a specialist tool, not a comfort blanket.

Props, same-game multiples and the Sunday menu

Player yardage lines, touchdown scorers and same-game multiples dominate the advertising around NFL Sundays. They carry wider margins than spreads and totals, correlations inside same-game multiples are priced by the bookmaker (not left as a gift), and injury news minutes before kick-off can gut a prop. Small stakes, entertainment budget, no chasing at midnight when the late games turn.

The honest summary

Learn 52.4%, respect 3 and 7, pay for half-points only when they cross a key number, and keep teasers and props in the entertainment column. The NFL week is long and the slate is deep — discipline, not volume, is the edge available to a punter in Ireland.

Frequently Asked Questions

What time do NFL games kick off in Irish time?

The main Sunday slate kicks off at 6pm Irish time, the late window around 9:25pm, and the Sunday night game after 1am. Thursday and Monday night games also land after 1am Irish time.

Why are 3 and 7 called key numbers in NFL betting?

Because field goals score 3 and touchdowns (with the kick) score 7, final margins cluster on those numbers far more than on their neighbours. A spread moving across 3 or 7 changes the real win probability sharply, so those half-points are worth far more than others.

Are teaser bets good value?

Usually not. A two-team 6-point teaser at standard prices needs each leg to win about 73% of the time to break even, and an average 6-point move does not add that much. The narrow exception is teasing through both key numbers, and bookmakers price defensively against it.

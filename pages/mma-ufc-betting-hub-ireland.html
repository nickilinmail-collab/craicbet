How UFC Betting Odds Work

UFC betting covers a wider range of markets than many punters realise. The fight winner market is the simplest entry point, but the depth of available markets is comparable to football for major cards.

Market What You Bet On Typical Odds Range
Fight Winner (Moneyline) Which fighter wins the bout, by any method Even money to 4/1+ for heavy underdogs
Method of Victory Fighter A wins by KO/TKO, submission, or decision 2/1 to 10/1+ depending on fighter style
Round Betting Which fighter wins, and in which round Often 6/1 to 25/1 — high precision required
Over/Under Rounds Whether the fight goes past a specified round total (e.g. 2.5 or 4.5) Near even money for well-matched fights
Goes the Distance Yes/No: does the fight last all scheduled rounds? Typically evens to 2/1 either side
Draw The judges score the fight a draw 25/1 to 50/1+ — rare but it happens
How to read UFC odds: Irish bookmakers typically display odds in fractional (e.g. 2/1) or decimal (3.00) format. A fighter priced at 1/4 is a heavy favourite — you stake €4 to win €1 profit. A fighter at 3/1 is an underdog — a €1 stake returns €3 profit plus your stake back. The bookmaker builds a margin into these prices; the sum of all implied probabilities across both fighters will exceed 100%. This is the “overround” and it represents the bookmaker’s take.

Method of victory odds reflect each fighter’s finishing style. A wrestler with a high submission rate will typically have tighter odds for a submission finish than a striker; the inverse applies to a puncher with a low grappling game. Studying finishing history is a core part of MMA handicapping. No analysis guarantees a correct prediction — aucun pronostic n’est garanti.

UFC Events Calendar

The UFC holds events globally on most weekends of the year. The structure of a typical card is:

  • Main Card — typically five bouts, headlined by a title fight or high-profile matchup. Main cards in the United States start between 10pm and midnight EST, which is 3am–5am Irish time (Sunday morning).
  • Preliminary Card (Prelims) — four to six bouts, often beginning around 8pm EST (1am Irish time).
  • Early Prelims — available on the UFC’s streaming platform, beginning around 6pm EST (11pm Irish time Saturday).

Numbered events (UFC 300, UFC 310, etc.) are the flagship cards and typically feature title bouts. “Fight Night” events are smaller cards, often held in international markets including European venues. Both card types are covered by GRAI-licensed bookmakers operating in Ireland.

For current event dates and fight cards, check the official UFC website or the sports section of the GRAI-licensed bookmaker you use. Specific dates and matchups are not listed here to avoid publishing outdated scheduling information.

MMA vs Boxing Betting

Both sports share the moneyline format and method of victory markets, but there are meaningful differences that affect how punters approach them:

Factor MMA / UFC Boxing
Finish methods KO/TKO, submission, decision, disqualification KO/TKO, decision, disqualification, technical draw
Round length 5 minutes per round; non-title bouts 3 rounds, title bouts 5 rounds 3 minutes per round; typically 10–12 rounds for major bouts
Draw likelihood Rare (split/majority decisions possible) More common at professional level; draws can be intentional strategy
Over/under market Total rounds, usually at 1.5, 2.5 or 4.5 Total rounds, commonly at 7.5, 9.5 or 10.5
Submission market Yes — a core method of victory option No — not applicable to boxing rules
Live market depth Varies; some operators pause markets between rounds Often more continuous in-play markets due to longer rounds

The key practical difference is the finish method. In MMA, a fighter can lose via submission from a dominant position — a scenario that does not exist in boxing. This makes method of victory analysis in MMA more complex, requiring knowledge of a fighter’s wrestling defence and grappling submissions, not only their striking record.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular UFC bet type?

The fight winner (moneyline) market is the most popular UFC bet type. You pick which fighter wins the bout by any method. It is the simplest market and available on every UFC card at GRAI-licensed bookmakers. Method of victory and round betting attract more experienced punters looking for bigger returns in exchange for greater precision.

What does "method of victory" mean in UFC betting?

Method of victory is a market where you predict not only who wins the fight, but how they win it. Common options include KO/TKO (knockout or technical knockout), submission, and decision (unanimous, split or majority). Because you must call both the winner and the finish type, odds are substantially longer than the fight winner market. See our MMA betting guide for a full breakdown of how each settlement type works.

Can I bet live on UFC fights in Ireland?

Live UFC markets typically include fight winner, round betting and over/under rounds. Odds move quickly between rounds, so having your selections ready before the event starts is advisable. Main cards begin in the early hours of Sunday morning in Ireland (US Saturday night), so check kick-off times before planning your session.

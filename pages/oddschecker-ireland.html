Oddschecker Ireland: Odds Comparison for Irish Punters

Odds comparison sites let you see the prices offered by multiple bookmakers on the same event in one place, so you can quickly find the best available price before placing your bet. Getting the best odds consistently is one of the most effective ways to improve your long-term returns — even small differences compound significantly over hundreds of bets.

How to Use Odds Comparison in Ireland

The process is straightforward: search for your event on a comparison site, look across the row of bookmaker prices, and open an account with the one offering the highest odds. For regular punters, maintaining accounts with several Irish-licensed operators means you can almost always get the best price available without switching sites frequently.

StepAction
1Search the event (match, race, or market)
2Compare all bookmaker prices in the row
3Click the highest price to open the bet slip

What Odds Comparison Sites Don't Show Ireland

Comparison sites typically show the headline match odds market but may not cover all niche markets (correct score, first scorer, specials). They also don't account for existing promotional prices, personal enhanced odds offered to your account, or reload bonuses that could affect the effective price. For horse racing, they often include Best Odds Guaranteed indicators — useful for identifying which sites will upgrade your price if the SP is bigger than your early price.

Limitations for Irish Punters

Not all operators available to Irish punters appear on every comparison site. Only operators holding a GRAI remote betting licence (from 1 July 2026) or a valid Revenue remote bookmaker permission are legally able to accept bets from Irish residents. When a comparison site lists an operator, verify its Irish licence status before depositing — the GRAI public register is the definitive source. Unlicensed operators have no consumer protection obligations under Irish law.

What does DNB mean in betting?

Draw No Bet — if you back a team and the match draws, your stake is returned. Only a loss results in losing your bet. The draw is removed as a result that costs you money.

Is DNB the same as Asian Handicap 0?

Yes — mathematically identical. AH 0 and Draw No Bet have the same settlement rules: draw = stake returned, win = win, loss = lose. The notation differs by market style.

Are DNB odds lower than outright win odds?

Yes — because DNB includes draw insurance. The odds are lower to reflect the fact that you can't lose your stake in the event of a draw.

When should I use Draw No Bet?

When you're confident in a team but worried about a draw. DNB reduces profit on a win but provides a safety net. Worth using when draw probability is high and the team's win margin is uncertain.

What happens to a DNB bet in an accumulator if one game draws?

The DNB leg is treated as a void — stake for that leg is returned and it's removed from the acca. The remaining legs continue at adjusted odds.

Odds Converter: Fractional, Decimal & American

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Irish betting sites quote fractional odds by default, exchanges and stats sites live in decimals, and American sportsbooks use plus/minus moneylines. Same bet, three dialects. This page gives the conversion formulas, a reference table, and the one habit worth building: turning any price into an implied probability before you judge it. For quick conversions, our odds converter tool does the arithmetic for you.

The three formats in one line

Fractional 5/2 means “win 5 for every 2 staked”. Decimal 3.50 means “total return of 3.50 per 1 staked, stake included”. American +250 means “win 250 on a 100 stake” (and −150 means “stake 150 to win 100”). All three describe the identical price.

Conversion formulas

Fractional to decimal: divide the fraction and add 1 — 5/2 → 2.5 + 1 = 3.50. Decimal to fractional: subtract 1 and express as a fraction — 1.80 → 0.80 = 4/5. Decimal to American: if the decimal is 2.00 or more, (decimal − 1) × 100; if below 2.00, −100 ÷ (decimal − 1). American to decimal: positive lines are (line ÷ 100) + 1; negative lines are (100 ÷ |line|) + 1.

Implied probability: the only number that matters

From decimal odds, implied probability is simply 100 ÷ decimal. So 2.00 is 50%, 1.50 is 66.7%, 5.00 is 20%. From fractions, probability = denominator ÷ (numerator + denominator): 5/2 is 2 ÷ 7 = 28.6%. Every betting judgement reduces to one question — do you believe the true chance is higher than the implied probability? If you cannot answer that, the format of the odds is the least of your problems.

Why Irish sites show fractions — and why decimals are better for you

Fractions are racing heritage: the on-course boards and generations of Irish and British punters speak 5/2 and 100/30 natively. But fractions hide small price differences — 11/8 vs 7/5 takes a moment’s arithmetic, while 2.38 vs 2.40 is instant. Decimals also make margin visible: add up 100 ÷ odds for every runner and the amount over 100% is the bookmaker’s take. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert fractional odds to decimal?

Divide the fraction and add 1. So 5/2 becomes 2.5 + 1 = 3.50, and 4/5 becomes 0.8 + 1 = 1.80.

What is implied probability?

The chance of winning that a price assumes: 100 divided by the decimal odds. Decimal odds of 4.00 imply 25%. Compare that with your own estimate of the true chance to judge whether a bet is value.

Why do Irish bookmakers use fractional odds?

Tradition from horse racing, where on-course boards have always shown fractions. Almost every licensed site lets you switch to decimal display in your account settings, which makes prices easier to compare.

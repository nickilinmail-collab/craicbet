GRAI Licence vs MGA Licence: Which Is Better for Irish Bettors?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Both GRAI and MGA (Malta Gaming Authority) are legitimate gambling regulators — but for Irish bettors, the differences matter considerably. This page compares the two licences head-to-head from an Irish player's perspective: jurisdiction, consumer protection, enforcement, self-exclusion and complaint rights.

What Is the GRAI?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) is Ireland's national gambling regulator, established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. GRAI began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026. As of November 2026, approximately 43 remote betting licences have been issued. GRAI is a domestic Irish regulator — it has full legal jurisdiction in Ireland, can enforce in Irish courts, and is directly accountable to the Irish Oireachtas (parliament).

What Is the MGA?

The Malta Gaming Authority is a gambling regulator based in Malta. It is one of the longest-established online gambling regulators in the EU and licenses a very large number of operators worldwide, including many widely used by Irish players. The MGA has relatively strong standards by international comparison — significantly better than offshore jurisdictions like Curaçao or Kahnawake — but it is a Maltese regulator, not an Irish one.

Jurisdiction: The Core Difference for Irish Players

This is the most important difference. GRAI has full legal jurisdiction in Ireland. If a GRAI-licensed operator acts unlawfully, GRAI can:

  • Investigate the operator under Irish law.
  • Issue financial penalties enforceable in Irish courts.
  • Revoke the operator's right to serve Irish customers.
  • Seek criminal prosecution under Irish law.

The MGA has jurisdiction in Malta. If an MGA-licensed operator acts unlawfully towards an Irish customer, the MGA can investigate under Maltese regulatory law and can sanction the operator in Malta. But the enforcement path is longer, the law applied is not Irish consumer law, and the MGA has no power to act in Irish courts.

For most practical situations (operator follows the rules, your bets are settled fairly), the difference may not be felt. Where it matters is when something goes wrong — and for Irish players, having a domestic regulator is a materially stronger position.

The National Gambling Exclusion Register

Perhaps the starkest practical difference: the NGER (National Gambling Exclusion Register) only covers GRAI-licensed operators. If you self-exclude via the NGER, you are protected across all GRAI-licensed sites. MGA-licensed sites that are not also GRAI-licensed are not part of the NGER.

The MGA does have self-exclusion requirements for MGA-licensed operators, and Malta has its own national exclusion register (the Responsible Gaming database). However, that system does not cross jurisdictions — an Irish exclusion is not automatically applied to MGA-only operators. If you need Ireland-wide exclusion coverage, GRAI-licensed operators are the only route.

Which Is Better for Sports Betting Specifically?

For online sports betting, a GRAI licence is unambiguously better for Irish players. From 1 July 2026, only GRAI-licensed operators are legally operating in Ireland for sports betting. An MGA-licensed operator without a GRAI licence is operating in breach of Irish law when taking sports bets from Irish customers.

For online casino gaming, the picture is more nuanced because GRAI has not yet issued casino licences. Until casino licences are available (expected 2027–2028), Irish players who play casino games are typically using MGA-licensed sites. The MGA is a reasonable fallback in this period — it is an EU regulator with meaningful standards — but it is not equivalent to having an Irish GRAI licence.

The Verdict: GRAI for Betting, MGA Is Acceptable Fallback for Casino

For Irish sports betting: GRAI is the only legitimate licensed route. Use a GRAI-licensed bookmaker. For Irish casino players until GRAI casino licences are issued: MGA-licensed sites are the strongest available alternative (ahead of Curaçao or unlicensed). Once GRAI casino licences are issued, the same analysis as sports betting will apply — prefer GRAI.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a GRAI licence or MGA licence better for Irish bettors?

For Irish bettors specifically, GRAI is better. GRAI is Ireland's national regulator with Irish jurisdiction, can enforce in Irish courts, and provides NGER coverage. MGA is respected but has no specific Irish enforcement powers.

What is the MGA?

The Malta Gaming Authority is an EU gambling regulator that licenses a large number of online gambling operators worldwide. It has relatively high standards compared to offshore alternatives but is not an Irish regulator.

Can I complain to GRAI about an MGA-licensed site?

No. GRAI only has jurisdiction over GRAI-licensed operators. Problems with MGA-licensed sites must be escalated to the MGA in Malta.

Are MGA-licensed sites legal in Ireland?

For sports betting: operators need a GRAI licence to legally take bets from Irish customers. For casino: GRAI has not issued casino licences yet, so MGA-licensed casino sites are in a legal grey area for players (not criminal, but not GRAI-protected).

Does the National Gambling Exclusion Register cover MGA-licensed sites?

No. The NGER only covers GRAI-licensed operators. MGA-only sites are not part of the Irish NGER system.

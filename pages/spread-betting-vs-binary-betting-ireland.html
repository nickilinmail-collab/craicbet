Spread Betting vs Binary Betting Ireland 2026: Variable Loss vs Fixed Risk

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Spread betting and binary betting are two specialised formats that differ fundamentally from fixed odds. Spread betting involves variable profit and loss — you can lose (or win) more than your initial stake. Binary betting involves fixed loss. This guide explains both for Irish punters, covering mechanics, regulation and when each might suit a sophisticated bettor.

How Spread Betting Works

In sports spread betting, you buy (bet higher) or sell (bet lower) a “spread” on a quoted index. Example: a supplier quotes “total goals in this match: 2.3 — 2.5”. If you buy at 2.5 for €10/goal, every goal above 2.5 earns €10; every goal below 2.5 costs €10. In a 5-0 match, your profit is (5 − 2.5) × €10 = €25. In a 0-0, your loss is (2.5 − 0) × €10 = €25. Losses are unlimited — they scale with how wrong you are.

Binary Betting: Fixed Risk

Binary betting is a fixed-odds format where you bet on whether a quoted index will finish above or below a set level. The key difference from spread betting: your maximum loss is capped at your stake. If you buy “Total goals above 2.5” at 60 (out of 100) for €1 per point, you risk a maximum of €60 and can win a maximum of €40 (100 − 60). This is structurally closer to fixed odds than spread betting.

Regulation in Ireland

Sports spread betting straddles two regulatory regimes. As a financial product with leverage and variable loss, UK spread betting firms are regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Their sports betting division may also require a GRAI remote betting licence for Irish customers — this remains an evolving area. Binary betting on sports, with its fixed risk, falls more clearly under GRAI betting regulation. When in doubt, check the licence type displayed in the site footer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is spread betting on sports?

Sports spread betting involves buying or selling a quoted index (e.g., total goals, win margin). Your profit or loss scales with how correct you are — losses can exceed your initial stake, unlike fixed odds betting.

Is spread betting legal in Ireland?

Sports spread betting by FCA-regulated firms is available to Irish customers. GRAI regulation of spread betting as a distinct product category is still evolving. Check the licence type displayed on any site before depositing.

What is binary betting?

Binary betting applies a fixed-risk format to market indices. You bet on whether a quoted value will finish above or below a level. Unlike spread betting, your maximum loss is your stake — there is no unlimited downside.

Can I lose more than my stake on spread betting?

Yes. In spread betting, losses scale with how wrong you are — there is no fixed maximum loss. This is the key risk difference from fixed odds betting. Never spread bet without understanding the full loss potential.

Which is safer: spread betting or fixed odds?

Fixed odds betting has a known maximum loss (your stake). Spread betting has variable and potentially unlimited loss. For risk management, fixed odds or binary betting is safer for the typical Irish punter.

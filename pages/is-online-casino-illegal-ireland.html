Is Online Casino Illegal in Ireland? What Irish Law Says (2026)

Short answer: Playing online casino games is not a criminal offence for individual Irish players. However, as of November 2026, no online casino holds a GRAI licence, which means all casino sites serving Irish players operate without Irish regulatory oversight. This creates real risks for players — even if they face no legal consequences personally.

⚠️ Key distinction: Irish law targets unlicensed operators, not individual players. You will not be prosecuted for playing at an online casino. But you lose important consumer protections that GRAI licensing would provide.

What Does Irish Law Actually Say?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 created a comprehensive new licensing framework for gambling in Ireland, overseen by the GRAI. Under this Act:

  • Operating a gambling service in Ireland without a GRAI licence is a criminal offence — for the operator
  • Individual players are not targeted by criminal sanctions for using gambling sites
  • The GRAI can require unlicensed sites to geo-block Ireland and can seek court injunctions to enforce compliance
  • Remote casino gaming licences have not yet been created — this is expected from 2027

Why Are There No Licensed Casinos in Ireland in 2026?

GRAI began with remote betting licences — covering sports betting, horse racing, and fixed-odds wagering. Casino-style gaming (slots, roulette, blackjack, live dealer) requires a separate licence category that has not yet been introduced.

This means every online casino site currently serving Irish players — regardless of where it is licensed (Malta MGA, Gibraltar, UK Gambling Commission) — is unlicensed under Irish law. The GRAI does not yet have a casino licence to issue, so this is a legal gap rather than a regulatory failure on any specific operator's part.

What Are the Risks of Playing at an Unlicensed Casino in Ireland?

Even though you won't be prosecuted, there are real practical risks:

  • No GRAI complaints process: If you have a dispute with the casino, the GRAI cannot intervene on your behalf
  • No guaranteed refunds: If an unlicensed site closes or restricts withdrawals, the GRAI has limited ability to recover your funds
  • No Irish responsible gambling rules: Unlicensed sites are not required to meet GRAI's responsible gambling standards
  • Site may be blocked: If the GRAI acts against a site you use, your account could be blocked and access to funds complicated
  • Some sites are outright scams: Without GRAI oversight, there is no Irish authority verifying that games are fair

What Should Irish Casino Players Do Right Now?

  • Prefer casinos licensed by reputable EU authorities: Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) and UK Gambling Commission are the strongest
  • Avoid casinos with no traceable regulatory licence at all
  • Never use sites that have been warned or blacklisted by the GRAI
  • Set your own deposit limits — do not wait for mandatory limits that only apply to licensed operators
  • Keep records of deposits and withdrawals in case of a dispute

What Changes When GRAI Issues Casino Licences?

Once GRAI begins issuing remote gaming licences (expected from 2027), Irish players at licensed casino sites will gain:

  • Access to the GRAI complaints mechanism for unresolved disputes
  • Mandatory deposit limits, loss limits, and cooling-off periods
  • Participation in Ireland's national self-exclusion register
  • Verified RTP (Return to Player) percentages for all games
  • Segregated player funds protection

Is Matched Betting Legal in Ireland

Matched betting itself — placing bets and lay bets to cover all outcomes and extract free bet value — is not illegal in Ireland. However, it breaches most operators' terms and conditions. If detected, accounts are gubbed (stake-limited) or closed. The practice is legal from a criminal law perspective but carries the practical risk of losing access to promotional offers and having accounts restricted.

Is Arbitrage Betting Legal in Ireland

Arbitrage betting (arbing) — exploiting odds differences between operators to guarantee a profit — is legal under Irish law. It is not illegal gambling; it is a form of mathematical trading. Operators, however, consider it a breach of terms and will restrict or close accounts of known arbers. Using multiple GRAI-licensed accounts at different operators to arb is legal but commercially risky (account restriction).

Is Daily Fantasy Sports Legal in Ireland

Daily fantasy sports (DFS) platforms like DraftKings are not explicitly regulated under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 in Ireland, as the Act primarily covers traditional fixed-odds betting and casino games. The legal status of DFS as a game of skill vs. chance remains unresolved in Irish law. GRAI has not issued DFS-specific licences. Players participate at their own risk with no formal Irish consumer protection framework.

Is Spread Betting Legal in Ireland

Financial spread betting is regulated by the Central Bank of Ireland, not GRAI. Sports spread betting occupies a grey area — it is not covered by standard GRAI betting licences. Operators offering sports spread betting to Irish residents need appropriate authorisation. Most Irish-facing operators do not offer sports spread betting; it remains more common in the UK market.

Is Poker Legal in Ireland Online

Online poker is legal in Ireland when played on a licensed platform. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 covers poker as a gaming product, requiring a GRAI gaming licence. Casino gaming licences — which cover poker — are expected to be issued from 2027–2028. Until then, Irish players access online poker via operators holding licences from other EU jurisdictions (Malta, Gibraltar, Isle of Man) or unexpired Revenue permissions.

Is Bingo Legal Online in Ireland

Online bingo is legal in Ireland under the transitional arrangements of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Operators with valid Revenue permissions continue to offer bingo during the transition. GRAI gaming licences (which will cover bingo) are expected from 2027–2028. Always check that the platform displays a valid licence in its footer before depositing.

GRAI Licence Number: How to Check Ireland

Every GRAI-licensed betting site must display their licence number in the site footer. The format is GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-XXXX. You can independently verify any licence on the GRAI public register at grai.ie. If a site claims to be GRAI-licensed but you cannot find them on the register, do not deposit.

Can I Bet on Unlicensed Site from Ireland: Legal Position

Irish law does not criminalise individual punters for using unlicensed betting sites. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 imposes obligations on operators, not customers. The practical risk is consumer protection: unlicensed sites are not required to segregate funds, honour bonus terms, or participate in dispute resolution. The GRAI has powers to request ISPs block access to unlicensed sites.

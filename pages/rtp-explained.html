RTP Explained — What Every Irish Player Should Know

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 10 min read

If you've ever looked at a slot game's info screen, you've seen "RTP" followed by a percentage. But what does it actually mean, and should it change how you play? This guide breaks down everything Irish players need to know about Return to Player.

💡 Quick Definition

RTP (Return to Player) is the percentage of all wagered money a slot is programmed to pay back to players over time. A slot with 96% RTP will, on average, return €96 for every €100 wagered — over millions of spins.

What Does RTP Mean?

RTP stands for Return to Player. It's a theoretical statistical calculation that represents the long-term expected payback of a casino game. Expressed as a percentage, it tells you how much of total wagers a game will return to players over its lifetime.

For example, if a slot has an RTP of 96.50%, it means that for every €100 wagered across all players over millions of spins, the game will pay back €96.50 on average. The remaining €3.50 is the casino's profit margin — the house edge.

Important: RTP is calculated over millions of spins. In any single session, your results can vary wildly. You might win €500 from €10, or you might lose €100 without a single meaningful win. RTP does not predict your individual experience.

How Is RTP Calculated?

RTP is determined by the game's mathematics, programmed into the software by the developer. Independent testing labs (like eCOGRA, iTech Labs, GLI, and BMM Testlabs) audit and certify these figures to ensure accuracy.

The calculation considers every possible outcome of the game — every winning combination, bonus feature, and jackpot — weighted by its probability of occurring. The formula is straightforward:

RTP = (Total Amount Returned to Players ÷ Total Amount Wagered) × 100

RTP vs. House Edge

RTP and house edge are two sides of the same coin:

  • RTP 96% = House Edge 4%
  • RTP 97.3% = House Edge 2.7%
  • RTP 99% = House Edge 1%

The higher the RTP, the lower the house edge, and the better the theoretical return for the player. That's why choosing high-RTP games is one of the smartest decisions you can make.

Over time, a higher RTP means your money lasts longer and you have a better statistical chance of walking away with winnings. Consider this comparison:

Volatility Explained — Low, Medium & High

RTP tells you how much a slot pays back. Volatility (also called variance) tells you how it pays back. Two slots can have the same RTP but feel completely different to play.

How to Find a Slot's RTP

  1. In-game info: Open the slot, click the menu or "i" icon, and look for the game rules or paytable. RTP is usually listed there.
  2. Casino game library: Some casinos display RTP directly on the game tile or in the game description.
  3. Developer websites: Check the game provider's website (e.g., NetEnt, Pragmatic Play, Play'n GO). They publish official RTP figures.
  4. Review sites: Our slot reviews always list the RTP for every game we cover.

⚠️ RTP Can Vary by Casino

Some game providers offer multiple RTP settings for the same slot. A casino might run Gonzo's Quest at 96.00% while another runs it at 95.97% or even lower. Always check the RTP at your specific casino, not just the "headline" figure.

Common RTP Myths — Debunked

❌ Myth vs. Reality

  • "A slot is due for a payout." — False. Every spin is independent. Past results do not influence future outcomes. The random number generator (RNG) ensures each spin is completely separate.
  • "Casinos can change RTP mid-session." — False. RTP is set in the game software and certified by independent labs. Casinos cannot adjust it during gameplay. They can only choose from pre-set RTP configurations.
  • "Higher RTP means I'll always win." — False. RTP is calculated over millions of spins. In a single session, anything can happen. You could win big on a low-RTP slot or lose on a high-RTP one.
  • "Playing at certain times of day improves your odds." — False. RNG-based slots produce the same random results regardless of time, day, or how many players are online.
  • "New slots pay better to attract players." — False. The RTP is fixed at launch and doesn't change over time. New slots are not more generous.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is RTP in slots?▾
RTP (Return to Player) is the percentage of all wagered money that a slot is designed to pay back to players over time. A 96% RTP slot will theoretically return €96 for every €100 wagered, averaged over millions of spins. It's the opposite of the house edge.
What is a good RTP for a slot?▾
The industry average is around 96%. Anything above 97% is considered high RTP and player-friendly. Slots above 98% are rare and excellent value. Below 94% is considered low and best avoided if you're budget-conscious.
Does RTP guarantee I'll win?▾
No. RTP is a long-term statistical average calculated over millions of spins. In any single session, your results will vary significantly from the stated RTP. You could win far more or far less than the RTP suggests. Think of it as a guide to the game's generosity, not a promise.
What's the difference between RTP and volatility?▾
RTP tells you how much a slot pays back over time (the total amount). Volatility tells you how it pays back (the pattern). A low-volatility slot pays small amounts frequently. A high-volatility slot pays larger amounts rarely. Both can have the same RTP but feel very different to play.
Can casinos lower a slot's RTP?▾
Some game providers offer multiple RTP configurations (e.g., 96.5%, 95%, 94%). A casino can choose which setting to use, but it cannot change it mid-session or per-player. Reputable casinos display the active RTP in the game's info screen. Always check the RTP at the casino you're playing at, not just the headline figure from the developer.

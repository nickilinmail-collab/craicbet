The Marquee Races for Irish Punters

The Cheltenham Gold Cup (Day 4)

The Gold Cup is jump racing's championship race — 3 miles 2 furlongs, 22 fences, run in mid-March conditions that can range from Good to Heavy going. Irish-trained horses have dominated the race in the modern era. The Gold Cup field typically includes the season's leading staying chasers from both sides of the Irish Sea, with the market defined by the Irish contenders.

Ante-post markets for the Gold Cup open the previous summer and offer the best prices on horses that subsequently perform well through the winter. A horse priced at 20/1 in September that wins three Grade 1s before Cheltenham might go off at 5/1 on the day — the ante-post value was at the longer price. The key ante-post risk is non-runners: always check whether Non-Runner No Bet terms apply.

The Champion Hurdle (Day 1)

The Champion Hurdle is the supreme test for two-mile hurdlers. Speed, jumping fluency, and the ability to handle Cheltenham's uphill finish define the market. It is typically a tighter field than the Gold Cup (10–15 runners versus 20+), which means shorter prices but also slightly lower margin for the bookmaker. The Champion Hurdle has seen significant Irish domination; the market tends to reflect this clearly.

The Queen Mother Champion Chase (Day 2)

The two-mile chase championship rewards supreme jumping and speed. The race regularly features the best two-mile chasers from both countries and tends to have among the most competitive markets of the week — two or three horses often share market leadership going into the race.

The Stayers' Hurdle (Day 3)

Three-mile hurdle championship. St Patrick's Thursday is the Festival's most Irish-flavoured day: the race honours the patron saint's day, and Irish-trained staying hurdlers have been extremely successful in this race.

The Novice Races

The Festival's four Grade 1 novice races — the Supreme Novices' Hurdle, the Arkle Trophy, the Ballymore Novices' Hurdle, and the RSA Chase — are where Irish point-to-point form provides the strongest edge. Why Irish Horses Dominate Cheltenham

Irish domination at Cheltenham is structural, not cyclical. The reasons it persists year after year:

  1. The point-to-point pipeline: Ireland's amateur point-to-point system is the most productive nursery for future Cheltenham horses in the world. Each spring, elite National Hunt prospects are sourced from Irish point-to-points by the major training operations, then targeted at Cheltenham two or three years later. This creates a consistently deep supply of specifically Cheltenham-prepared horses.
  2. Cheltenham-type tracks in Ireland: Ireland's major National Hunt tracks (Leopardstown, Punchestown, Navan) all feature the uphill, undulating terrain similar to Cheltenham. Irish horses arrive at Cheltenham having raced on similar ground all winter; UK-trained horses from flat tracks have more adaptation to do.
  3. Festival-focused preparation: Ireland's top yards build entire seasons around Cheltenham. The Leopardstown Christmas Festival, the Dublin Racing Festival (February), and the Grade 1 cards at Navan and Naas serve as stepping stones. Each race is chosen with Cheltenham in mind.
  4. Market efficiency: Because Ireland dominates, Cheltenham prices are well-calibrated for Irish runners but can underestimate UK challengers in certain races where the Irish presence is thinner — the Handicap Chase divisions, for example.

Cheltenham Betting Strategy: Where the Value Lives

Ante-Post Betting: The Long Game

The best prices on Grade 1 Cheltenham races are available from the summer before the Festival — often September–October. At that point, the market is thin and speculative: a horse priced at 25/1 for the Gold Cup in September that remains healthy and wins a Leopardstown Grade 1 in January will likely be 5/1–8/1 in February. The ante-post punter captures that price compression as a return.

Key ante-post considerations:

  • Prioritise horses with no reported injury concerns and a clear Festival target
  • Check Non-Runner No Bet availability before committing large stakes
  • Consider each-way ante-post bets on horses at 12/1+ in deep fields like the Gold Cup
  • Monitor markets through the winter — significant drifts in January often signal concerns

Festival Handicaps: The Punter's Favourite

The Festival's competitive handicaps — the Coral Cup, the Pertemps, the Ladbrokes Trophy, the Martin Pipe — offer the best expected value for a punter who does the form work. These races feature 20–30 runners, wide-open markets, and significant Irish trainer representation. The winning price is typically 10/1–33/1. Each-way betting at 1/4 odds, 5 places (where offered) can be excellent value in these fields.

Irish trainer bias in handicaps: Irish trainers often target Festival handicaps with horses that have been lightly raced in Ireland to protect a low official rating, then targeted at a specific Cheltenham handicap. If a leading Irish handler sends a horse to Cheltenham that has run only twice, that is a significant preparation flag.

The Novice Divisions: Where Knowledge Pays

Irish point-to-point form is the most reliable Cheltenham predictor in the novice divisions. A horse that won a high-quality Irish point-to-point against top opposition before joining a leading trainer has a strong track record at Cheltenham. Practical Tips for Festival Week

  • Book early prices on major races: Champion Hurdle and Gold Cup prices tighten dramatically in the final week. If you have a strong opinion on a front-runner, taking an early price in January or February is usually correct.
  • Check NRNB terms: Non-Runner No Bet on ante-post markets is widely available during Festival week itself; less common for bets placed months earlier. Know what happens if your selection is withdrawn before committing.
  • Use Best Odds Guaranteed: BOG means that if the starting price on the day is higher than the morning price you took, you are paid at SP. Essential for Irish punters whose fancied runners are sometimes backed down significantly before the off.
  • Bet Festival day by day: Do not front-load your stake on Day 1. The Festival has four days of exceptional racing; spreading your activity across all four days gives you more opportunities and prevents an early loss from defining the week.
  • Withdraw winnings promptly: At a GRAI-licensed bookmaker, withdrawal of winnings is a licence condition. If you have a good day, withdraw the profit before the next day — it is a simple discipline that keeps festival bets in proportion.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Cheltenham Festival 2027?

The Cheltenham Festival 2027 takes place over four days in March 2027. Exact dates are typically the second week of March, Tuesday to Friday. Day 1 is Champion Day (Champion Hurdle); Day 2 is Ladies Day (Queen Mother Champion Chase); Day 3 is St Patrick's Thursday (Stayers' Hurdle); Day 4 is Gold Cup Day. We will update this page with confirmed dates as soon as they are announced.

Why do Irish trainers dominate Cheltenham?

Irish domination is structural: Ireland's point-to-point system produces the best National Hunt prospects in the world; Irish tracks mimic Cheltenham's undulating terrain; leading Irish yards build entire seasons around the Festival; and the volume and quality of Irish Grade 1 racing all winter prepares horses specifically for Cheltenham conditions. This dominance has been consistent for over a decade and is not expected to change for 2027.

What is the best betting market at Cheltenham for Irish bettors?

For value, the Festival handicaps (Coral Cup, Pertemps Final, etc.) offer the widest open markets. For informed advantage, the novice divisions (Supreme, Arkle, Ballymore, RSA Chase) reward knowledge of Irish point-to-point form. The Gold Cup and Champion Hurdle are efficiently priced but carry the Festival's most prestige and volume.

Should I bet ante-post or on the day at Cheltenham?

Ante-post betting offers significantly better prices on front-runners in Grade 1 races. The risk is non-runners (you lose the stake unless NRNB terms apply). For horses with clear Festival targets and no injury history, ante-post bets in January–February typically capture price compression as a return. On the day, Best Odds Guaranteed covers any morning-to-SP movement in your favour.

Is the Cheltenham Gold Cup the most bet race in Ireland?

Yes — the Gold Cup consistently registers the highest single-race betting volumes in Ireland of any National Hunt race, with some Irish bookmakers processing more bets on Gold Cup day than on any other single day of the year. The strong presence of Irish-trained runners drives exceptional public engagement and punter interest.

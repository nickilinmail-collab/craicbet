Is Online Gambling Legal in Ireland? [2026 Update]

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 14 min read

💡 The Short Answer

Yes, online gambling is legal in Ireland. Irish players aged 18 and over can legally gamble online at licensed operators. The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) now regulates the industry, and there are no laws that criminalise players for using online casinos or bookmakers.

The Legal Status of Online Gambling in Ireland

Online gambling in Ireland occupies a well-defined legal position as of 2026. The Gambling Regulation Bill 2022 modernised Ireland's gambling laws and created the GRAI as the country's first dedicated gambling regulator. Here is what is legal and what is not:

What Is Legal

  • Online Sports Betting: Legal and regulated by the GRAI under remote betting licences (first issued July 2026)
  • Online Casino Games: Legal for Irish players at internationally licensed casinos. GRAI casino licences are not yet available, so operators use offshore licences (MGA, UKGC, Gibraltar, Curaçao)
  • Online Poker: Legal at licensed operators
  • Online Lottery: The National Lottery is licensed and regulated; other lottery-style games are available at international operators
  • Live Dealer Games: Legal at licensed operators

What Is Not Legal

  • Operating without a licence: It is illegal to operate a gambling business serving Ireland without appropriate licensing. However, players are not criminalised for using unlicensed sites.
  • Underage Gambling: You must be 18 or over to gamble in Ireland. Operators must verify your age before allowing play.
  • Money Laundering: Using gambling platforms for money laundering is a criminal offence under Irish and EU law.

The Gambling Regulation Bill 2022

The Gambling Regulation Bill 2022 was the most significant reform of Irish gambling law in nearly a century. It replaced the outdated Betting Acts (1931) and Gaming and Lotteries Acts (1956) with a comprehensive modern framework. Key provisions include:

  • Creation of the GRAI as an independent statutory regulator
  • A comprehensive licensing framework for all forms of gambling
  • Mandatory player protection measures (deposit limits, self-exclusion, reality checks)
  • Strict advertising standards with statutory enforcement
  • A Social Impact Fund financed by operator levies
  • Powers to block unlicensed operators (DNS blocking, payment processing restrictions)

The Role of the GRAI

The GRAI is Ireland's answer to regulators like the UK's Gambling Commission and Malta's Gaming Authority. Its responsibilities include:

  • Licensing: Issuing and monitoring gambling licences (currently remote betting; casino licences expected 2027–2028)
  • Player Protection: Ensuring operators implement responsible gambling tools and comply with advertising codes
  • Enforcement: Investigating complaints, issuing fines, suspending or revoking licences
  • Research: Commissioning research into gambling behaviour and its social impact in Ireland

Gambling Age in Ireland

The minimum legal age for all forms of gambling in Ireland is 18 years old. This applies to:

  • Online casinos and sports betting
  • Retail bookmakers
  • Amusement arcades (gaming machines)
  • National Lottery products
  • Bingo halls

Licensed operators must verify your age during the registration process. This typically involves providing a government-issued ID (passport, driving licence, or Public Services Card) and proof of address.

Taxes on Gambling Winnings in Ireland

Good news for Irish players: gambling winnings are not taxed in Ireland. Whether you win €10 or €10 million, your winnings are yours to keep in full. This applies to all forms of gambling — casino, sports betting, poker, lottery, and everything in between.

The tax burden falls on operators, not players. Bookmakers and gambling operators pay a betting duty (currently 2% of turnover for remote operators), but this is not passed on to players as a deduction from winnings.

Why Irish Players Use Offshore Casinos

Since the GRAI does not yet issue casino licences, all online casinos serving Irish players operate under offshore licences. This is completely legal and is simply a consequence of the regulatory timeline. The most common offshore licences held by casinos popular with Irish players are:

  • MGA (Malta Gaming Authority): The most trusted European casino licence. Malta is an EU member state with strict regulatory standards.
  • UKGC (UK Gambling Commission): The world's strictest regulator. Many UKGC operators accept Irish players.
  • Gibraltar Gambling Commissioner: A well-established regulatory authority with a strong reputation.
  • Curaçao eGaming: The most common licence globally but with less strict oversight than the MGA or UKGC.

Responsible Gambling Resources in Ireland

If you or someone you know has a problem with gambling, these Irish resources can help:

  • Gambling Awareness Trust: Freephone 1800 753 753 (24/7)
  • GamblingCare.ie: Free counselling and support services
  • Gamblers Anonymous Ireland: Peer support meetings across Ireland and online
  • GRAI Complaints: If you have a dispute with a licensed operator, you can complain to the GRAI

FAQ: Online Gambling Law in Ireland

Is it illegal to gamble online in Ireland?▾
No. Online gambling is legal in Ireland. Irish players aged 18+ can legally use licensed online casinos, sports betting sites, and poker rooms. There are no laws criminalising players for online gambling.
Do I have to pay tax on my casino winnings?▾
No. Gambling winnings are tax-free in Ireland. The tax is paid by operators (as betting duty), not by players. Your winnings — whether from slots, sports bets, poker, or the lottery — are yours to keep in full.
What is the legal gambling age in Ireland?▾
The legal gambling age in Ireland is 18. This applies to all forms of gambling, both online and offline. Operators must verify your age before you can play.
Do I need a VPN to gamble online in Ireland?▾
No. Online gambling is legal in Ireland, so there is no need to use a VPN. In fact, using a VPN to access gambling sites may violate the operator's terms of service and could cause issues with withdrawals.
Are online casinos regulated in Ireland?▾
Partially. The GRAI now regulates remote betting in Ireland, but casino licences are not yet available (expected 2027–2028). Online casinos serving Irish players operate under international licences from the MGA, UKGC, Gibraltar, or other jurisdictions. Learn more →
Can I be arrested for online gambling in Ireland?▾
No. Irish law does not criminalise players for gambling online. The legal obligations fall on operators (to be licensed and compliant), not on individual players.

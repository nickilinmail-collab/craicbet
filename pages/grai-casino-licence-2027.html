The Official Timeline to 2027–2028

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced a phased licensing framework for Ireland. GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) is rolling this out in stages, starting with the highest-volume, most commercially established categories and building the more complex frameworks afterwards. Here is where things stand as of November 2026:

Remote betting licences (B2C)1 July 2026✅ Active — 33+ licences issued

It is important to understand why casino licences come after betting. Betting already operated under Revenue excise licences in Ireland, so the migration path was well-established. Online casino gambling has never been domestically licensed in Ireland — GRAI is building that entire regulatory framework from scratch, which is why it takes longer. The Act provides the authority; GRAI must now develop the specific codes of conduct, technical standards, and compliance processes for gaming operators.

What GRAI Casino Licensing Will Require

While GRAI has not published a detailed casino licence specification yet, the Act and the existing betting licence framework give a clear picture of what will be required. Applicants should expect to demonstrate:

  • Fit and proper persons: ownership and management checks covering criminal records, financial history, and prior regulatory sanctions.
  • Anti-money laundering procedures: documented AML/CTF policies and designated compliance officers.
  • Certified fair gaming systems: RNG (random number generator) certification from an accredited testing laboratory for all casino games.
  • Responsible gambling tools: mandatory deposit limits, session time limits, self-exclusion linked to the national self-exclusion register, and player verification processes.
  • GRAI codes of conduct compliance: advertising restrictions, player communication standards, and ongoing reporting obligations.
  • Irish player fund protections: requirements are likely to include segregated player fund accounts, similar to MGA standards.

The application fee and renewal structure will be published when applications open. Based on the betting licence fees set by S.I. 37/2026, gaming licence fees are likely to be tiered by operator scale.

Which Casinos Are Likely to Apply?

Large, well-established operators holding MGA or UKGC licences are the most likely early applicants for a GRAI casino licence. These are operators that already meet rigorous regulatory standards and have the compliance infrastructure to add a new jurisdiction.

Smaller or less-established casinos — particularly those operating under lighter-touch licences such as Curaçao or Anjouan — may not pursue a GRAI licence, either because they cannot meet the requirements or because the costs outweigh the benefits for their business model. If a casino you use does not apply for a GRAI licence when applications open, that itself tells you something about where it sits on the compliance spectrum.

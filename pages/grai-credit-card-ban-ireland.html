GRAI Credit Card Gambling Ban Ireland: What Payment Methods Are Allowed?

Yes — credit card gambling is banned in Ireland under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, which the GRAI enforces. Licensed operators cannot accept credit card payments for bets or casino deposits. Allowed payment methods include debit cards (Visa Debit, Mastercard Debit), bank transfer, Revolut, Apple Pay, Google Pay, PayPal and other e-wallets funded by a debit card or bank account. The ban applies to all GRAI-licensed operators serving Irish customers.

GRAI-licensed betting sites in Ireland cannot accept credit card deposits for gambling. This ban mirrors the approach taken by the UK Gambling Commission in 2020 and is part of the GRAI's responsible gambling framework under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. This page explains what is banned, what is allowed, and why the rule exists.

The Credit Card Ban: What Is Covered

Under GRAI licensing conditions, the following payment methods are not permitted for gambling deposits on licensed Irish sites:

  • Credit cards — any card where funds are drawn on credit (Visa Credit, Mastercard Credit, American Express, etc.).
  • Credit-based e-wallets — e-wallet or payment services that are funded by or linked to a credit facility rather than a debit or pre-paid arrangement.
  • Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) services — services such as instalment credit that defer payment are classified as credit-based and are not permitted for gambling deposits.
  • Overdraft-funded deposits — while harder to monitor technically, using an authorised overdraft to fund a deposit is considered a credit-based payment that falls within the spirit of the ban.

What Payment Methods Are Allowed

The following payment methods are accepted on GRAI-licensed betting sites:

  • Debit cards — Visa Debit and Mastercard Debit (funds drawn directly from your current account) are permitted. These are the most common payment method on licensed Irish betting sites.
  • Bank transfers — direct bank-to-bank transfers from your current account.
  • Approved e-wallets and digital payments — services like PayPal (when funded by a linked debit account rather than credit), Skrill, Neteller and similar — where these are funded from debit sources. Operators must take steps to ensure e-wallet deposits are not credit-funded.
  • Revolut and digital bank debit cards — debit-based digital banking is acceptable (see below for nuance).
  • Pre-paid cards with funds already loaded — pre-paid vouchers or cards where the money has already been deposited from a debit source are permitted.

Revolut, N26 and Digital Banks

Digital banking services like Revolut and N26 issue debit cards linked to accounts you fund from your salary or other debit sources. Using a Revolut debit card to deposit on a licensed betting site is acceptable under GRAI rules, because the funds are genuinely yours — not borrowed.

The complication arises when these platforms offer credit features (Revolut Credit, N26 credit products). Using those credit products to fund gambling deposits would fall within the ban. GRAI rules focus on the nature of the funds rather than the brand of the payment service.

In practice: use the debit functionality and you are within the rules. Use credit features to fund gambling and you are not, regardless of which platform it is.

Why the Credit Card Ban Exists

The core problem with credit-card gambling is straightforward: when you lose money gambling on credit, you owe that money back with interest. Gambling losses on credit become debt. For problem gamblers, this dynamic can be devastating — they may chase losses to try to clear debts they already owe, compounding the problem.

Research cited in the development of the UKGC ban (which GRAI has drawn on) found that a meaningful proportion of problem gamblers used credit cards to fund gambling, specifically because it allowed them to bet beyond their current funds. The ban directly targets this behaviour by restricting gambling deposits to money the customer actually has.

The ban does not prevent someone from using credit unwisely — a person can withdraw cash on a credit card and deposit that. But it removes the seamless path from "click to deposit" using credit that made it so easy to gamble with borrowed money.

Credit Card Ban on Unlicensed Sites

Unlicensed offshore sites are not subject to the credit card ban. Many continue to accept credit cards and even market to customers using credit card reward points or cashback as an incentive. This is one concrete example of a consumer protection that disappears when you move to an unregulated site.

The Broader Payment Landscape: Withdrawals

The ban applies to deposits. Withdrawals are a separate process, governed by the operator's payment procedures. Most GRAI-licensed sites will pay winnings back to the payment method used for the original deposit (where possible) or via bank transfer. Some operators support debit card withdrawals even when the deposit method was a bank transfer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are credit cards banned for gambling in Ireland?

Yes. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI licensing conditions, GRAI-licensed operators cannot accept credit card deposits for gambling.

What payment methods can I use on GRAI-licensed betting sites?

Debit cards (Visa Debit, Mastercard Debit), bank transfers, and approved e-wallets funded from debit sources. Credit cards, BNPL services and credit-based payment methods are not permitted.

Does the credit card ban apply to withdrawals?

The ban applies to deposits only. Withdrawals are processed according to the operator's payment procedures, typically back to the deposit method or via bank transfer.

Can I use Revolut to fund a betting account?

Yes, using Revolut's debit card functionality. Using Revolut's credit products to fund gambling would not be permitted under GRAI rules. The key is whether the funds are debit-based (your own money) or credit-based (borrowed).

Why was the credit card ban introduced?

To prevent people from gambling with borrowed money. Gambling losses on credit become debt repaid with interest. The ban restricts deposits to money the customer actually has, addressing a key driver of gambling-related financial harm.

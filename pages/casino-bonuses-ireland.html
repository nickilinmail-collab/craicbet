How Wagering Requirements Work — A Worked Example

Wagering requirements (also called "playthrough") are the number of times you must bet the bonus amount before you can withdraw winnings. They're the single most important factor in determining a bonus's real value.

🧮 Worked Example: €100 Bonus with 35x Wagering

Bonus received: €100
Wagering requirement: 35x
Total bets needed: €100 × 35 = €3,500
If playing slots (96% RTP): Expected loss: €3,500 × 4% = €140
Expected value of bonus: €100 − €140 = −€40

In this example, the expected value is actually negative — meaning on average, you'd lose money clearing the bonus. This is why lower wagering requirements (20x–30x) are so much more valuable. At 20x wagering, the same €100 bonus would require €2,000 in bets with an expected loss of only €80, giving you a positive expected value of €20.

Key takeaway: Always divide the bonus by the wagering requirement and multiply by the house edge. If the expected loss exceeds the bonus value, it's a bad deal. As a rule of thumb, 35x or lower is fair, 20x–25x is excellent, and anything above 50x is tough to clear.

Understanding Wagering Requirements

Wagering requirements are the single most important factor when evaluating any casino bonus, yet they're also the most misunderstood. Put simply, a wagering requirement tells you how many times you must bet the bonus amount before you can withdraw any winnings.

Here's a practical example: if you receive a €100 bonus with a 30x wagering requirement, you need to place a total of €3,000 in bets before your bonus winnings become withdrawable. This doesn't mean you need to lose €3,000 — it's the total amount wagered, including wins that you reinvest.

How different games contribute: Not all games count equally towards wagering requirements. Slots typically contribute 100% — every €1 you bet counts as €1 towards the requirement. Table games like blackjack and roulette usually contribute only 10-20%, meaning you'd need to bet 5-10 times more to clear the same requirement. Live dealer games often contribute 0-10%. Always check the bonus terms for the specific contribution rates.

What's a good wagering requirement? For Irish players, we consider 20x-30x to be fair, 31x-40x to be average, and anything above 40x to be high. Some casinos offer "no wagering" bonuses where your winnings are immediately withdrawable — these are increasingly popular and we flag them in our reviews.

Time limits matter too: Most bonuses must be wagered within 7-30 days. If you don't meet the requirement in time, the bonus and any associated winnings are forfeited. We recommend choosing bonuses with at least 14 days to give yourself a comfortable window.

Maximum bet rules: While wagering a bonus, there's usually a maximum bet limit (typically €5-10 per spin). Exceeding this can void your bonus entirely. This rule exists to prevent players from placing one large bet to quickly clear the requirement — casinos want you to play through the bonus gradually.

Maximum withdrawal caps: Some bonuses limit how much you can withdraw from bonus winnings. A €100 bonus with a €500 withdrawal cap means that even if you win €2,000, you can only cash out €500. We always highlight these caps in our reviews because they significantly affect the real value of a bonus.

How to Calculate a Casino Bonus's Real Value

Three inputs determine whether a bonus is worth taking:

  1. Bonus amount — what the casino gives you
  2. Wagering requirement — total bets required
  3. Game RTP — average return of the slot or game you will play

Formula: Expected cost = Wagering requirement × (1 − RTP)

Example: €100 bonus, 35× wagering, playing a slot at 96% RTP.
Wagering = €3,500. Cost = €3,500 × 4% = €140 expected loss to clear the bonus.
Since the bonus is only €100, the expected net result is −€40. This bonus has negative expected value at 96% RTP.

A bonus becomes positive EV when: Bonus amount > (Wagering × house edge). That typically requires wagering below 20× on a 96%+ RTP game, or low wagering (10×) on any game. Most standard 30–40× bonuses do not meet this threshold.

This does not mean bonuses are always bad — variance means you can win significantly while clearing. But the expected outcome over many plays is negative, which is why casinos can afford to offer them.

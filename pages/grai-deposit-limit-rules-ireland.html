GRAI Deposit Limit Rules Ireland

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

Since 1 July 2026, every GRAI-licensed bookmaker operating in Ireland must offer mandatory deposit limit tools — and must implement any limit reduction immediately. This is not a voluntary initiative. It is a legal requirement under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, enforced by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland as a condition of every GRAI remote betting licence. This guide explains exactly what you are entitled to, how to use these tools effectively, and what to do if a bookmaker fails to deliver.

What the GRAI Requires: The Exact Rules

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI licensing conditions, every holder of a GRAI remote betting licence (RB) must provide customers with:

  • Daily deposit limits — a cap on how much you can deposit in a 24-hour rolling period
  • Weekly deposit limits — a cap on weekly deposits
  • Monthly deposit limits — a cap on monthly deposits
  • Immediate effect for reductions — any request to lower a limit takes effect immediately with no delay
  • Cooling-off period for increases — requests to raise a limit include a mandatory waiting period (minimum 24 hours)
  • Pre-registration availability — limit tools must be available during account setup, before any deposit is made

These are minimum requirements. Individual GRAI-licensed operators may go further: some offer spend-based limits (not just deposit limits), time-based limits (limiting session duration), and loss limits. These are not yet GRAI-mandated but represent best practice.

The Asymmetry Principle: Why It Matters

The core design principle behind GRAI deposit limits is deliberate asymmetry: reducing a limit is fast (immediate), increasing a limit is slow (mandatory cooling-off). This is not arbitrary. Research on gambling harm consistently shows that in-the-moment impulses to spend more are the highest-risk decision points. By making limit reductions instant and limit increases slow, the GRAI framework provides friction at exactly the right moment.

How to Set Deposit Limits: Step-by-Step

The exact steps vary by bookmaker, but the GRAI requires the process to be clearly accessible — not buried in account settings. Standard locations:

  1. Go to My Account or Account Settings
  2. Find Responsible Gambling, Safer Gambling, or Betting Controls section
  3. Select Deposit Limits
  4. Choose daily, weekly, or monthly
  5. Enter the amount
  6. Confirm — a reduction takes effect immediately; an increase begins the cooling-off period

Some GRAI-licensed bookmakers prompt you to set limits during account registration. If yours does not, set them manually immediately after creating the account — before you make your first deposit. This is the safest sequence.

Beyond Deposit Limits: Other GRAI-Mandated Tools

Self-Exclusion

All GRAI-licensed bookmakers must offer self-exclusion. When you self-exclude, the bookmaker must: immediately close your account to betting; stop all marketing communications; and refuse any new account applications from you during the exclusion period. The GRAI is developing a national self-exclusion register that will eventually cover all licensed operators simultaneously — excluding from one site will flag to all. Until this register is live, exclusion is managed per-operator.

Self-exclusion periods are typically a minimum of 6 months; most operators offer 1, 2, or 5-year periods, or indefinite exclusion. Re-registering during an exclusion period is a breach; reputable operators check for exclusion flags before activating new accounts.

Cool-Off / Time-Out

Different from self-exclusion, a cool-off or time-out temporarily suspends your ability to bet for a set period — typically 24 hours, 7 days, or 30 days. During a cool-off, you can still log in and see your balance and bet history, but you cannot place new bets or make deposits. This is useful for a planned break or when you notice a pattern you want to interrupt. It is not as permanent as self-exclusion; when the period ends, full access typically resumes automatically without having to contact support.

Reality Checks

Some GRAI-licensed operators offer reality check notifications — in-session alerts at set intervals (e.g., every 30 minutes) reminding you how long you have been betting and how much you have deposited or wagered in that session. This is not currently a GRAI mandate (it is more common in casino settings) but is offered by responsible operators. Worth enabling if available.

What to Do If a Bookmaker Ignores Your Limit

If a GRAI-licensed bookmaker fails to implement a deposit limit you have requested, or allows you to deposit above your stated limit, the steps are:

  1. Document everything: Screenshot your limit request confirmation, any deposits above the limit, and any communication with the operator.
  2. Raise with the bookmaker first: GRAI regulations require the operator to have an internal complaints process. Submit a formal complaint referencing the deposit limit failure.
  3. If unresolved, escalate to the GRAI: The GRAI is the regulatory body and can investigate licence breaches. Use the official complaints route at grai.ie. A bookmaker that repeatedly fails to implement mandatory tools risks licence sanctions.

The GRAI's ability to escalate sanctions — from formal warnings to fines to licence suspension — means operators have genuine incentive to implement deposit limits correctly. This is a material change from the pre-July 2026 framework.

Deposit Limits vs Spending Limits vs Loss Limits

These terms are sometimes confused:

  • Deposit limit: Restricts how much you can transfer into your betting account from your bank or card. The GRAI-mandated version. Does not stop you betting funds already in your account.
  • Spending limit (stake limit): Caps how much you can wager per bet or per session. Not currently GRAI-mandated; offered by some operators voluntarily.
  • Loss limit: Caps how much you can lose in a given period. If your losses reach the limit, you cannot place new bets until the period resets. Not currently GRAI-mandated; some operators offer it as a best-practice tool.

All three are valuable tools. If your chosen bookmaker offers spending limits or loss limits in addition to the mandatory deposit limits, use them — they provide tighter control over what you can lose, not just what you can put in.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are deposit limits mandatory under GRAI rules?

Yes. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, all GRAI-licensed bookmakers must offer daily, weekly, and monthly deposit limit tools to every customer. These tools must be available from account registration. Any operator without these tools is in breach of its GRAI licence conditions.

How quickly do deposit limit reductions take effect?

Immediately. Under GRAI rules, a limit reduction request takes effect the moment you submit it — no cooling-off period, no delay. This is a legal requirement for all GRAI-licensed bookmakers. If a bookmaker delays a reduction, that is a potential licence breach.

Is there a waiting period to increase a deposit limit?

Yes. GRAI rules require a mandatory cooling-off period before a limit increase takes effect — typically 24–48 hours but can be longer. This deliberate asymmetry is designed to slow impulse spending while making protection immediate.

Can I set deposit limits before making my first deposit?

Yes, and you should. GRAI-licensed bookmakers must make deposit limit tools available during account registration, before any deposit is made. Set your limits first, then deposit. If a bookmaker does not offer this during registration, consider reporting it to the GRAI.

What happens if a GRAI-licensed bookmaker ignores my deposit limit request?

Document the request (screenshot the confirmation) and any subsequent excess deposits. Raise a formal complaint with the bookmaker first. If unresolved, escalate to the GRAI at grai.ie. Failure to implement mandatory responsible gambling tools is a licence breach and can result in GRAI sanctions.

Do deposit limits apply to free bets and bonuses?

Deposit limits restrict your own cash deposits. They do not prevent bookmaker-credited free bets from appearing in your account. Some bookmakers count the qualifying deposit for a bonus toward your limit total; check the specific T&Cs. A deposit limit does not prevent you using funds already in your account.

Accumulator Calculator

📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 2 min read

An accumulator combines multiple selections into a single bet — your returns from each leg roll into the next, multiplying the odds at every step. The appeal is obvious: even four selections at modest odds can produce a substantial payout. The risk is equally clear: one losing leg busts the entire bet. Use this calculator to see your potential return before you place, check your running subtotals mid-event, or work out the remaining combined odds if some legs are already settled.

Please check your inputs — odds must be above 1.00 and stake must be positive.

How Accumulator Odds Work

Decimal odds multiply together. A treble at 2.0, 3.0 and 4.0 gives combined odds of 24.0 — a €10 stake returns €240. Add a fourth leg at 1.5 and the combined odds jump to 36.0, returning €360. Each leg you add increases the potential return while also adding another way to lose.

Bookmaker margin compounds in accumulators. Every leg carries the bookmaker's edge. On a 5-leg acca where each leg has a 5% margin, the combined margin is roughly 23% — meaning the fair combined odds are about 23% higher than what you are offered. Longer accas multiply both your potential return and the house edge.

Frequently Asked Questions

How to Calculate Accumulator Returns

  • Step 1: Write down the decimal odds for each selection
  • Step 2: Multiply all odds together: e.g. 2.10 x 1.80 x 2.50 = 9.45
  • Step 3: Multiply by your stake: EUR10 x 9.45 = EUR94.50 total return
  • Step 4: Subtract stake for profit: EUR94.50 - EUR10 = EUR84.50 profit

How many selections can I include in an accumulator?

Most bookmakers allow accumulators of 2 to 20+ selections, though very long accas can be subject to maximum payout limits. Our calculator supports 2–8 legs, covering the most common sizes. A 2-selection acca is called a double; 3 is a treble; 4+ are called four-fold, five-fold and so on.

What happens if one leg is a void or non-runner?

If a leg is voided (e.g., a match is abandoned or a horse is a non-runner), that leg is removed from the accumulator and the remaining legs continue as if the voided selection was never included. A 5-fold acca with one void becomes a 4-fold. Our calculator does not currently handle void legs — set those to "pending" and remove them from your calculation.

What is acca insurance?

Acca insurance is a promotion where the bookmaker refunds your stake (usually as a free bet) if exactly one leg of your accumulator loses. It is typically available on football accumulators of 5+ legs. The offer reduces the worst-case loss but does not change the odds or the payout if the acca lands.

Are accumulators better value than singles?

Mathematically, no — the bookmaker's margin applies to every leg, so longer accas carry more built-in edge. The appeal is the multiplied payout for a small outlay, not better expected value. If you back value singles, combining them into an acca does not create more value; it just concentrates the risk and return into a single outcome.

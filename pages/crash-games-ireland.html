How Crash Games Work

At the start of each round, you place a bet. A multiplier then appears on screen at 1x and begins to climb — slowly at first, then faster. At any point, you can click “Cash Out” to collect your stake multiplied by the current figure. If you don’t cash out before the multiplier crashes — which can happen at any value above 1x, including immediately — you lose your bet.

The crash point is determined by a provably fair RNG algorithm before the round begins. This means the casino cannot manipulate the outcome mid-round, and players can verify the result cryptographically after each round. The house edge comes from the occasional instant crash at 1x (or just above), which is built into the probability model.

Most crash games also allow an auto cash-out feature: you set a target multiplier (e.g., 2x) and the game cashes out automatically if that level is reached. This removes the emotional pressure of timing your exit manually, though it does not change the underlying odds.

Aviator: The Most Popular Crash Game

Aviator, developed by Spribe, is the dominant title in this category and is available at most online casinos accessible to Irish players. The game uses a plane graphic that flies across the screen as the multiplier rises — when the plane flies off, the round ends. The theme is straightforward; the core mechanics match the crash format described above.

Aviator allows two simultaneous bets per round with separate cash-out decisions. A live chat and a ticker showing what other players are winning (or losing) add a social layer, though these features should not influence your own decisions. Watching others cash out at high multipliers creates a form of social pressure that is worth being aware of.

Provably Fair Mechanics

Provably fair is a cryptographic verification method used in crash games. Before each round, the server generates a seed value that determines the crash point and publishes a hashed version of it. After the round, the original seed is revealed and players can independently verify that the crash point matches what was predetermined. This prevents the operator from adjusting outcomes once bets are placed. It is a meaningful transparency tool — though it does not change the house edge or guarantee any individual outcome.

Volatility and What to Expect

Crash games are extremely high volatility. Rounds that crash at 1x (or very close to it) happen regularly — often more frequently than casual players expect. Conversely, multipliers occasionally reach very high values (50x, 100x, or higher), which creates the impression that large wins are common. They are not: the probability of reaching any given multiplier decreases sharply as that multiplier rises. Setting a realistic, low auto cash-out target is a more consistent approach than chasing large multipliers, though no approach overcomes the house edge over time.

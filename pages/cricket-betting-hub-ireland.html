How Cricket Betting Works

Cricket offers a broader range of markets than most sports, with settlement rules that vary significantly by format. Here is a plain-language overview of the main bet types available on GRAI-licensed sites.

Market What You’re Betting On Format Availability
Match Winner Which team wins the match. In Test cricket, the draw is a third outcome. In T20 and ODI, the draw is rare and usually only applies in the case of a rain-affected no-result. All formats
Top Batsman Which player will score the most runs for their team in a specified innings. Dead heat rules apply if two players score equally. Settlement rules on non-batting vary by operator. All formats; most popular in T20
Top Bowler Which player will take the most wickets for their team in a specified innings. Settlement rules on non-bowling players vary by operator. All formats
Run Totals (Over/Under) Will the total runs in an innings or the whole match go over or under a specified line? Applies to both team totals and individual over totals. Strongest in T20 and ODI
Man of the Match Which player will be awarded the official man of the match accolade. Settled on the official presentation; not related to any statistical threshold. T20 and ODI; major Tests
Series Winner Which team wins a multi-match series outright. If the series is drawn, it settles based on stated operator rules (usually void or divided). Test series; major bilateral ODI series

In-play markets for cricket include next wicket method, runs off next over, and fall of next wicket. Because play can be stopped by weather or bad light, always check the minimum overs clause on each market before placing an in-play bet.

Cricket Formats Explained

The format of a cricket match shapes which markets are available and how odds are priced. The three main formats each carry different betting characteristics.

Test Match The longest and oldest format: up to five days, with each team batting twice. The draw is a genuine and common outcome, which creates a three-way match winner market. Test cricket produces the most innings-by-innings markets and is the format where series betting carries the highest stakes. A No Result is declared if the match cannot be completed.
ODI (One Day International) Each team faces a maximum of 50 overs, with matches completed in a single day. The result is usually decisive; ties are possible but uncommon and are often resolved by a Super Over in knockout fixtures. ODI markets mirror T20 markets but with higher run totals and different scoring rates to account for the longer innings.
T20 (Twenty20) The shortest format: 20 overs per side, matches typically lasting around three hours. High scoring rates and frequent momentum swings make T20 the most active format for in-play betting. Common markets include most sixes, runs in a specific over, wicket in next over and player runs. T20 leagues such as the IPL and ICC T20 World Cup are covered extensively by GRAI-licensed sites.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular cricket bet type?

Match winner is the most popular cricket bet, particularly for T20 and ODI fixtures where the draw is not a typical outcome. Top batsman betting is a close second, especially in T20 cricket where scoring is more compressed and individual innings carry more weight. See our cricket betting basics guide for a full market breakdown.

How does top batsman betting work?

You pick which player will score the most runs for a specified team in a specified innings. If two players score the same number of runs, dead heat rules apply — your stake is divided proportionally. Settlement rules on players who do not bat vary between operators: some void the bet, others settle it as a loss. Always read the operator’s cricket rules before placing a top batsman bet.

GAA Betting in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

GAA betting occupies a unique place in Irish punting culture. No other country has a sporting calendar built around amateur county teams drawing hundreds of thousands to Croke Park every summer — and no other market requires quite the same local knowledge to navigate well. This guide covers the main Gaelic football and hurling markets, what makes GAA betting different from any other sport, and how to find GRAI-licensed bookmakers covering the games.

The GAA Betting Calendar

The two pillars of GAA betting are the All-Ireland Football Championship and the All-Ireland Hurling Championship, both running from spring through to All-Ireland Finals in July (hurling) and July/August (football). The provincial championships — Leinster, Munster, Connacht and Ulster — run from May and form the first stage of the summer series under the current format. The Allianz Leagues (spring, February to April) offer lower-profile but often better-value markets due to thinner trading.

The inter-county season is concentrated from February to August, making it a perfect companion to the quieter periods in European football. The All-Ireland Club Championships in hurling and football extend the calendar into the winter months, with the club finals typically held in February.

Main GAA Betting Markets Explained

Match winner (1X2): The most common market. In Gaelic football, draws are genuinely frequent — far more so than in soccer — because a draw sends the game to a replay rather than extra time. This makes the three-way market meaningful, particularly in provincial games.

Handicap betting: When a game is heavily one-sided, bookmakers offer a points handicap. A strong county receiving a 10-point start means they must win by more than 10 to cover for backers. Handicap markets tend to be tighter-priced and require knowledge of team strength differentials.

First scorer / anytime scorer: Available on bigger championship games. GAA scoring is split between goals (3 points) and points (1 each), so first-goal scorer markets can carry longer odds than equivalent markets in soccer.

Outright winners: The All-Ireland outright markets open in January and attract significant money. Kerry and Dublin in football, Kilkenny and Limerick in hurling have historically dominated, but the market regularly throws up value in provinces where the gap has narrowed.

What Makes GAA Betting Unique

GAA is an amateur sport at inter-county level (players are not paid to play for their counties). This has several implications for punters: players can be unavailable at short notice due to work or injury without public announcement, and team selection information is often less transparent than in professional leagues. Following county GAA media closely — county board social channels, local radio, provincial newspapers — gives an edge that is harder to find in heavily-covered professional sport.

Weather matters more in GAA than almost any other outdoor sport. Heavy rain benefits teams with a strong physical game and reduces scores significantly. Swirling wind at Croke Park or Thurles is a genuine tactical factor that affects both teams unevenly depending on how they play.

Home advantage in provincial games can be pronounced. Neutral-venue All-Ireland games level the playing field, but a Mayo game in Castlebar or a Tipperary match at Semple Stadium carries a crowd factor that is factored inconsistently into the odds.

Hurling vs Football: Different Betting Dynamics

Hurling markets tend to be thinner and less liquid than Gaelic football, particularly outside the top tier of Tipperary, Kilkenny, Limerick, Clare, Cork and Galway. This can mean lower maximum stakes on specialist markets. The pace of hurling and the high-scoring nature of the game (totals of 30+ points are common in top-level matches) make over/under markets interesting when they are offered.

Gaelic football markets are deeper and attract more in-play money, particularly on All-Ireland semi-finals and finals. The lower-scoring nature of football compared to hurling makes correct-score markets more achievable to predict within a range, though bookmakers price them accordingly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GAA betting legal in Ireland?

Yes. GRAI-licensed bookmakers can offer markets on all GAA competitions. All you need is to be 18+ and bet with a GRAI-licensed site.

What are the main GAA betting markets?

Match winner (including draw), handicap betting, first scorer, correct score, half-time/full-time, and total points over/under. Outright markets on provincial and All-Ireland winners are available throughout the summer season.

Can you bet on draws in GAA football?

Yes, and draws are far more common in GAA than in soccer. A draw sends the game to a replay — this makes the draw market meaningful and sometimes valuable, especially in provincial semi-finals and All-Ireland quarter-finals.

Are GAA betting markets as liquid as Premier League football?

No. GAA markets are thinner than top European football, particularly in hurling. Expect smaller maximum stakes on specialist markets like correct score or first scorer, especially for early-round league fixtures.

Does home advantage matter in GAA betting?

Yes — arguably more than in any other sport. Provincial games played at county grounds can see meaningful home advantage. Weather also plays a bigger role than in indoor sports, with heavy rain favouring more physical sides.

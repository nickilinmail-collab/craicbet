GRAI Advertising Watershed Rules: What Operators Can and Cannot Do

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced one of Europe's strictest broadcast gambling advertising watersheds. For anyone wondering why they suddenly see fewer gambling ads during daytime TV or who wants to understand what GRAI-licensed operators can and cannot do, this page lays out the full rules.

The Broadcast Watershed: 5:30am to 9:00pm

The centrepiece of Ireland's new gambling advertising regime is the broadcast watershed. GRAI-licensed operators are prohibited from broadcasting gambling advertisements on television or radio between 5:30am and 9:00pm. This means gambling ads can only run on broadcast media after the 9pm watershed — effectively excluding them from daytime and early evening programming.

This is significantly stricter than the UKGC equivalent, which currently only prohibits gambling ads during programmes made primarily for children. Ireland's approach reflects concerns about exposure during sports broadcasts, daytime television and radio programmes that large numbers of children watch or listen to.

The watershed applies to all broadcast gambling advertising — television channels, radio stations and on-demand streaming services that are licensed in Ireland. It does not, however, automatically apply to international channels viewed in Ireland through satellite or online means, which remains a regulatory gap the GRAI acknowledges.

Digital and Online Advertising Rules

The watershed applies specifically to broadcast media. Online advertising — including social media, search advertising, display advertising and email — is not subject to the same 5:30am–9pm rule, but it is subject to equally important content restrictions:

  • Age-gating is mandatory: Online ads must use audience targeting to exclude under-18s. This applies on all platforms including social media, YouTube and search advertising.
  • No inducements to new customers: Advertising that promotes bonuses or free bets must not target people who have not previously had an account with that operator — this rule directly limits aggressive sign-up bonus marketing.
  • No celebrity or influencer promotion by under-25s: Gambling operators cannot use people who are under 25 years of age — or who appear to be under 25 — in advertising. This covers influencer marketing on social media.
  • No content appealing primarily to minors: Even if technically targeted at adults, advertising content that uses characters, themes or imagery primarily appealing to children is prohibited.

Sponsorship Restrictions

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced specific rules on sponsorships, which were a source of significant political debate during the bill's passage:

  • GAA jersey sponsorships are banned. No GRAI-licensed gambling operator can sponsor Gaelic Athletic Association club or county jerseys. This was a specific provision aimed at reducing gambling's visibility in community sport.
  • Youth and children's events cannot be sponsored. Any event primarily attended by or for under-18s cannot receive gambling sponsorship.
  • Educational sponsorship is banned. Schools, colleges and educational institutions cannot accept gambling sponsorship.
  • Adult professional sports can still be sponsored, subject to the content rules above. Operators can sponsor professional sports events, leagues and teams, but not in ways that target minors.

Mandatory Content Requirements

Every gambling advertisement in Ireland — broadcast, online or in print — must include:

  • A clear 18+ age restriction marker.
  • A responsible gambling message, including reference to gamblingcare.ie or the Gambling Awareness Trust freephone helpline (1800 753 753).
  • No promises of guaranteed winnings or misleading representations of winning probability.

Responsible gambling messages must be displayed with reasonable prominence — in a font size and position that is actually readable, not in tiny print at the bottom of the ad.

Unlicensed Operators and the Advertising Ban

An important but sometimes overlooked aspect of GRAI advertising rules: it is illegal for unlicensed operators to advertise gambling services targeting Irish customers. This means that if you see an online gambling ad on an Irish website, sports stream or social media, it should only be from a GRAI-licensed operator. GRAI can pursue enforcement against unlicensed operators who advertise in Ireland, including applying to courts for injunctions and imposing penalties.

In practice, enforcement against offshore operators who advertise via digital channels remains challenging. However, the legal framework is clear, and Irish media organisations that carry advertising for unlicensed operators may also face scrutiny.

The Comparison with Previous Rules

Before the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, gambling advertising in Ireland was governed by a voluntary code (the Gambling Industry Code) and general broadcasting standards. The voluntary code was widely criticised as ineffective. The 2024 Act replaced it with statutory requirements enforceable by GRAI with real financial penalties.

The shift from voluntary self-regulation to statutory enforcement is the most significant change. Operators who previously could ignore advertising standards with minimal consequence now face fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover for breaches — see our page on GRAI enforcement penalties for details.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GRAI advertising watershed?

The GRAI advertising watershed prohibits gambling advertising on broadcast media between 5:30am and 9:00pm. This prevents gambling ads from appearing during hours when children are likely to be watching or listening.

Can gambling operators advertise on social media in Ireland?

Yes, but with strict rules: they must exclude under-18s from targeting, cannot use content appealing primarily to minors, and cannot use anyone under 25 (or who appears under 25) in the advertising.

Are gambling sponsorships banned in Ireland?

GAA jersey sponsorships and sponsorships of youth events are banned under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Adult professional sports sponsorships are permitted subject to GRAI advertising standards.

What must every gambling ad in Ireland include?

Every gambling advertisement must include an 18+ marker and a responsible gambling message directing people to gamblingcare.ie or the Gambling Awareness Trust helpline 1800 753 753.

What happens if an operator breaches the advertising watershed?

GRAI can issue fines up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover, require corrective action, and in serious cases suspend or revoke the operator's licence.

Betting Glossary A–Z: 60+ Terms, Honestly Defined

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

Betting has a vocabulary that assumes you already know it. This glossary covers the 60-plus terms an Irish punter actually meets — on betting sites, at the track, and in the group chat — each in one honest sentence, with links to our full guides where a term deserves more than a sentence.

A–C

Acca / accumulator — one bet combining several selections; every leg must win, and the bookmaker’s margin multiplies across legs — see our acca calculator.

Ante-post — betting on a future event (e.g. Cheltenham in March) weeks or months out, usually at bigger prices but normally with no refund if your pick doesn’t run — see ante-post betting.

Arb (arbitrage) — backing all outcomes across different bookmakers at prices that guarantee a small profit; bookmakers restrict accounts that do it systematically.

Asian handicap — a football handicap market with no draw, including half- and quarter-goal lines — explained in our Asian handicap guide.

Banker — a selection considered near-certain, often the anchor of an acca; the odds say it isn’t certain.

Bet builder — combining several markets from one match into a single price — convenient, but the margins compound; see bet builder maths.

BOG (best odds guaranteed) — a racing concession: take an early price and get paid at the SP if that’s bigger; always a stated promotion, never a right.

BTTS — both teams to score — see our BTTS guide.

Canadian (Super Yankee) — a full-cover bet on five selections: 26 bets (10 doubles, 10 trebles, 5 four-folds, 1 five-fold).

Cash out — settling a bet early at the bookmaker’s offered value, which always embeds a fresh margin — see cash out explained.

Closing line — the final odds before an event starts; beating it consistently is the classic test of whether a punter has real judgement.

Correct score — predicting the exact final score — high odds, high margin; see correct score betting.

D–G

Dead heat — an unseparable tie; your stake is divided by the number of tied competitors — see dead heat rules.

Double / treble — an acca of exactly two or three selections.

Double chance — covering two of the three football outcomes in one bet at a correspondingly short price — see double chance.

Draw no bet — stake refunded if the match is drawn — see draw no bet.

Drift — odds lengthening before an event because money is going the other way; a drifter is the horse doing it.

Each-way — two bets in one — win and place — so the total cost doubles; see our each-way calculator.

Edge — the amount by which your estimate of a probability beats the price; without one, the margin decides the long run.

Evens (even money) — odds of 1/1 (decimal 2.00): win exactly what you stake.

Exchange — a platform where punters bet against each other and the operator takes commission — see exchange vs bookmaker.

Favourite / outsider — the shortest- and longest-priced runners in a market; the jolly is track slang for the favourite.

Fold — counts legs in an acca: a five-fold has five selections.

Form — the recent-results record used to judge a competitor; reading it is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.

Full cover — a system bet placing every possible combination from your selections — Trixie, Yankee, Heinz and friends; see full-cover bets.

Goliath — the biggest common full-cover bet: eight selections, 247 bets.

H–L

Handicap — a market where one side gets a virtual head start to even things up.

Hedge — betting the other way to lock in profit or cap loss on an existing position.

Heinz / Super Heinz — full-cover bets on six selections (57 bets) and seven (120 bets).

HT/FT — predicting the half-time and full-time result in one bet — see HT/FT explained.

Implied probability — the chance a price asserts: 1 divided by decimal odds — the key to everything; see our guide.

In-play / live betting — betting after the event has started, at rapidly repriced odds — see in-play betting.

Joint favourites — two or more runners sharing the shortest price.

Juice / vig — American slang for the bookmaker’s margin.

Lay — to bet against an outcome, taking the bookmaker’s side — possible for punters on exchanges.

Liability — the amount a layer stands to lose if the outcome they laid wins.

Lucky 15 / 31 / 63 — full-cover bets including singles on four, five and six selections (15, 31 and 63 bets) — see Lucky 15 calculator.

M–P

Margin / overround — the sum of a market’s implied probabilities above 100% — the real price of betting; see margin explained.

Nap — a tipster’s most confident selection of the day.

Non-runner — a horse or player withdrawn before the off; single bets are normally refunded and remaining prices adjusted under Rule 4.

NRNB (non-runner no bet) — an ante-post concession refunding stakes on non-runners, usually at shorter prices than the ordinary ante-post book.

Odds-against / odds-on — prices longer than evens (e.g. 3/1) versus shorter (e.g. 1/3) — odds-on means risking more than you win.

Outright — betting on a whole competition’s winner rather than one fixture — see outrights & futures.

Palpable error — an obviously wrong price (a “palp”); bookmaker terms let them void or resettle such bets, a regular source of disputes.

Patent — full cover with singles on three selections: 7 bets.

Photo finish — a finish too close to call by eye, decided from the camera; until then the result is unofficial.

Place — finishing in the paid positions (e.g. top 3–5) rather than winning; the second half of an each-way bet.

Punter — the person reading this glossary.

Push — a stake refund when a handicap or total lands exactly on the line.

R–S

Related contingency — two selections whose outcomes affect each other (a player to score and his team to win); bookmakers won’t acca them at full multiplied odds.

Rule 4 — the standard deduction applied to winning racing bets when a non-runner shortens the remaining field’s true odds.

SP (starting price) — the official odds at the off, compiled from on-course bookmakers; the default settlement price if you didn’t take an early price.

Single — one selection, one bet — the lowest-margin way to back an opinion.

Stake — the amount risked; “stakes returned” matters when comparing fractional and decimal odds.

Steamer — a runner whose odds are shortening fast under a weight of money.

System bet — any multi-combination bet (full covers, folds from a bigger list) as opposed to a straight single or acca.

T–Z

Tissue — the odds compiler’s first draft of a market, before money moves it.

Tote / pool betting — betting into a shared pool at racecourses where the dividend depends on how many holders share it, rather than fixed odds.

Trixie — full cover without singles on three selections: 4 bets (3 doubles, 1 treble).

Under/over (totals) — betting on whether a match statistic clears a line, most commonly 2.5 goals — see over/under goals.

Value — a price longer than the true probability justifies; the only thing a long-term winner is ever buying.

Void bet — a cancelled bet with the stake returned, settled at odds of 1.00 inside an acca — see void bets & settlement.

Yankee — full cover without singles on four selections: 11 bets.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a Yankee and a Lucky 15?

Both cover four selections, but a Yankee is 11 bets (doubles, trebles and the four-fold) while a Lucky 15 adds the four singles to make 15 bets — it costs more but returns something on a single winner.

What does odds-on mean?

Odds shorter than even money, such as 1/3: you must risk more than you stand to win. It signals a strong favourite — not a certainty.

What is the overround in simple terms?

Add up the implied probabilities (1 ÷ decimal odds) of every outcome in a market; whatever exceeds 100% is the overround — the bookmaker’s built-in margin and the real cost of every bet.

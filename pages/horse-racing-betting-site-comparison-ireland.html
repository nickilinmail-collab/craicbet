Best Odds Guaranteed: Who Offers It and on What Races

Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG) means you are paid the starting price if it exceeds the price you took. It is now standard on Irish and UK flat and jumps racing at most GRAI-licensed operators. Where they differ: some extend BOG to French racing; a few cover international cards; some restrict BOG to named races only. If you habitually take early prices, BOG is worth €100–€500 per year to a regular punter.

Ante-Post Betting: Cheltenham and Beyond

Irish punters bet ante-post more than their UK counterparts, partly because of the racing calendar (Cheltenham Festival, Punchestown, Galway, Leopardstown Christmas) and partly because BOG protects early-price punters from the downside of price movement. For ante-post betting, depth of market matters: how many runners are priced, whether each-way ante-post terms are available, and the NRNB rule.

Margins: What You Are Actually Paying Per Race

A win-only market on a 10-runner flat race typically carries a bookmaker margin of 6–8% across the field. Each-way betting implicitly doubles your exposure to this margin (the place part is a separate market). Using an operator that runs tighter margins on racing — even 1% tighter — saves money over hundreds of annual bets. Check our margin calculator to test any race’s real overround.

Irish Racing Specific: Leopardstown, Fairyhouse, Tipperary

All major GRAI-licensed operators cover domestic Irish racing comprehensively. The differences appear in streaming (racing specialist typically streams more Irish fixtures), early betting shows (opening shows available a day before the race), and the depth of place markets (forecast bets, combination forecasts). For serious Irish racing punters, the racing specialist still edges ahead on product depth; for occasional festival betting, any GRAI-licensed operator is adequate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which bookmaker has the best BOG for Irish racing?

Most major GRAI-licensed operators offer BOG on all Irish and UK races. Differences emerge on French racing and international cards — check each operator’s specific BOG terms before betting ante-post.

What is each-way betting?

An each-way bet is two bets in one: a win bet and a place bet. The place portion pays at a fraction of the win odds (usually 1/4 or 1/5) if your selection finishes in the top 2, 3 or 4 places (depending on the race).

Is horse racing tax-free in Ireland?

Yes. Betting winnings, including horse racing, are completely tax-free for Irish players. Operators pay the 2% betting duty.

Can I watch Irish racing live on betting apps?

Some GRAI-licensed operators stream Irish racing live. You typically need a funded account or a recent bet. Racing specialists stream more Irish fixtures than general bookmakers.

What is ante-post betting?

Ante-post betting means placing a bet on a race or event well in advance of the day itself — sometimes months ahead. BOG protects you if the SP is higher, but non-runner risk applies unless NRNB terms are offered.

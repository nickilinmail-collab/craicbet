GRAI Fines and Penalties: What Operators Risk Under Irish Law

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Headline Number

The GRAI can impose financial penalties of up to €20,000,000 or 10% of annual turnover, whichever is greater. For a large international operator, 10% of turnover comfortably exceeds €20m — these are UK/EU-scale sanctions, not token fines.

The Full Sanctions Toolkit

Since the enforcement provisions commenced on 5 February 2026, administrative sanctions include: suspension of a licence, revocation of a licence, imposition of conditions on how an operator may trade, and financial penalties as above. Major financial sanctions are subject to court confirmation — a safeguard, not a loophole.

What Triggers Enforcement

The obligations attached to a GRAI licence read like a player-protection checklist: strict age verification, the credit card ban, an explicit obligation to pay out winnings, anti-money-laundering controls, advertising restrictions, and participation in the National Gambling Exclusion Register once operational. Breach any of them and the sanctions above apply.

The Obligation to Pay Out Winnings

Worth pausing on: Irish law now makes paying your winnings a licence condition. The classic rogue-casino move — confiscating a withdrawal over a technicality — becomes a regulatory breach with a €20m ceiling attached. This is the single biggest practical difference between a GRAI operator and an offshore one.

What About Unlicensed Operators?

Operators serving Irish customers without a required licence face criminal enforcement, and the GRAI remit includes acting against illegal operators. Enforcement against offshore sites takes time in every country that has tried it — but the direction is set. Our guide on playing at unlicensed casinos covers what this means for you personally.

Frequently Asked Questions

How big can a GRAI fine be?

Up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover, whichever is greater, subject to court confirmation for major sanctions.

Can the GRAI take away a licence?

Yes — suspension, revocation, and imposing conditions on a licence, in addition to financial penalties.

Does a GRAI-licensed operator have to pay my winnings?

Yes. Paying out winnings is an explicit licence obligation under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

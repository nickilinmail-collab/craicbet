AIB Gambling Block — Full Details

AIB launched its voluntary gambling block in August 2024, making it the first major Irish bank to do so. The block is opt-in — you have to actively enable it. But once enabled, it cannot be undone. AIB describes it as a "permanent self-exclusion commitment" consistent with responsible gambling principles.

What AIB Blocks

  • AIB Visa Debit card — blocked at all merchants with an MCC gambling code
  • AIB credit card — blocked at gambling merchants (this is in addition to the statutory credit card casino ban)
  • Applies to: online casinos, online betting, in-shop bookmakers, lotteries classified as gambling
  • Not affected: national lottery, charity raffles (depending on merchant classification)

How to Enable the AIB Gambling Block

  1. Log in to AIB Mobile Banking app or online banking
  2. Go to Cards → select your card
  3. Find Gambling Block
  4. Toggle ON — read the confirmation screen carefully
  5. Confirm — this action cannot be reversed
⚠️ Permanent decision: AIB's gambling block cannot be turned off after enabling. If you enable it and change your mind, AIB will not remove it. You will need to use an alternative payment method for any gambling spending going forward.

Bank of Ireland Gambling Block — Full Details

Bank of Ireland introduced its gambling block in May 2025. The design mirrors AIB's approach: voluntary, opt-in, but permanent once activated.

What Bank of Ireland Blocks

  • Bank of Ireland Visa Debit — blocked at gambling merchants
  • Credit card (rollout 2026) — BOI announced an extension to credit cards in 2026, though credit card gambling was already largely stopped by the voluntary industry ban

How to Enable the Bank of Ireland Gambling Block

  1. Open the Bank of Ireland 365 app
  2. Go to Cards & Payments
  3. Select Spending Controls
  4. Find Gambling and toggle it off
  5. Confirm — this is permanent

PTSB Gambling Block — Full Details

PTSB (Permanent TSB) introduced its gambling controls in July 2025. Unlike AIB and Bank of Ireland, PTSB designed its block to be reversible — with a mandatory 48-hour cooling-off period before deactivation. This mirrors Revolut's approach and aligns with what GRAI recommended.

PTSB Block: Enable and Disable

  1. Open the PTSB Explore app
  2. Go to Cards → select your card
  3. Find Payment Controls
  4. Find Gambling
  5. Toggle ON to block; toggle OFF to remove — a 48-hour wait applies on disabling
✓ PTSB is different: If you accidentally enabled the PTSB gambling block, or want to remove it, you can — but you must wait 48 hours. This is designed to prevent impulsive decisions during a session, not to permanently restrict access.

Why Irish Banks Are Doing This

The Irish banking sector's move towards gambling blocks pre-dates GRAI regulation but runs parallel to it. Several factors drove the rollout:

  • GRAI regulation arriving: The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 was passed in October 2024. Banks anticipated mandatory responsible gambling requirements and acted pre-emptively.
  • UK precedent: UK banks introduced gambling blocks years earlier. Irish banks with UK parent operations (Bank of Ireland has significant UK operations) modelled their approach on this.
  • Customer demand: Research by the ESRI and others documented problem gambling rates in Ireland at 1.4% of adults — higher than the European average. Banks faced reputational risk from facilitating problem gambling.
  • Soft2Bet and offshore operators: GRAI's 2026 enforcement actions against unlicensed operators (145 Soft2Bet sites, Stake, Polymarket) heightened awareness of offshore gambling volumes flowing through Irish bank accounts.

Credit Card Casino Ban and Irish Banks

Separate from the gambling block story, Irish law and voluntary industry rules banned credit card gambling deposits in 2020. This applies regardless of which bank issues your credit card. It is not a feature banks can toggle — it is a cross-industry policy enforced at the payment processor level.

  • Credit card gambling ban: Applies to all Irish-issued credit cards. All major operators stopped accepting them from 2020.
  • GRAI formalisation: GRAI's licensing conditions include a mandatory credit card ban. As of July 2026, all GRAI-licensed operators must refuse credit card deposits.
  • Revolut note: Revolut blocks credit card-funded gambling payments even if the card is a Revolut card. This is Revolut's own enforcement of the same rule.

GRAI Context: Why This Is All Happening in 2026

GRAI — the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland — issued its first remote betting licences on 1 July 2026, with 43 operators licensed by September 2026. Casino licences are expected in 2027–2028. As operators come under GRAI oversight, the entire payments infrastructure around Irish gambling is being restructured.

Key GRAI requirements affecting payments:

  • All licensed operators must offer customer-set deposit limits
  • All licensed operators must refuse credit card deposits
  • Operators must have Know Your Customer (KYC) processes — this affects payment method acceptance
  • GRAI has enforcement powers including fines up to €20 million or 10% of turnover

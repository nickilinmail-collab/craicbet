Betting on Draws in Football: The Statistics Behind It

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Draw rates across European football

Draws occur in roughly 24-27% of top-division European football matches, with significant variation by league and season. Serie A and Ligue 1 have historically shown higher draw rates than the Premier League. This matters for betting because bookmakers price the draw based on perceived probability, and in leagues where draws are structurally more common, the draw price can sometimes be better value than the market reflects.

When the draw is a defensible bet

The draw is most defensible in evenly-matched fixtures where both teams have strong defensive records, in games where both sides have something to play for or neither is heavily motivated to attack, and in head-to-head records where draws are historically common. The draw is least defensible when both teams have high-scoring recent form or when one team needs a win for standings purposes.

Draw no bet as an alternative

Draw no bet (DNB) is a market where you back a team to win, but your stake is returned if the match draws. The price is shorter than the win price because the draw is excluded, but DNB eliminates the draw risk entirely. It is a useful market when you think a team will win but are genuinely uncertain about the draw outcome. Some punters use DNB regularly on away sides in defensive fixtures.

The statistical case for the draw

Across a large sample of English football, the draw has historically been priced slightly below its true frequency in certain types of fixture — specifically high-profile matches between closely-ranked Premier League sides where public money drives the favourites shorter than their true probability warrants. Frequently Asked Questions

How common are draws in top-division football?

Draws occur in approximately 24-27% of top-division European football matches, varying by league and season. Serie A and Ligue 1 have shown above-average draw rates historically; the Premier League tends to be slightly below the European average.

What is draw no bet?

Draw no bet (DNB) is a market where you back a team to win, with your stake returned if the match ends in a draw. The odds are shorter than a straight win bet because the draw outcome is removed. It is an alternative to the double chance (home/draw or away/draw) market.

Can you build an accumulator of draws?

Yes. Some punters build accumulators of predicted draws across multiple matches. Each draw predicted at odds of 3.0-3.5 contributes to a multiplied return if all legs draw. The combined margin across all legs is high and the number of possible outcomes increases significantly with each leg added.

Draw Betting Strategy: 1X and X Doubles

Three approaches for backing draws:

  • Straight draw (X): simplest — back the draw at 3.00–4.00; strike rate ~26% across leagues
  • Double chance 1X: backs home win OR draw — typically priced 1.25–1.50; safer but lower return
  • Draw no bet (DNB): stake returned if draw; good for value selections where you fear the draw
  • Draw accumulators: combining 3-4 draws can produce high combined odds, but variance is extreme

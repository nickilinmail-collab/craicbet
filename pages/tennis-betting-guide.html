Tennis Betting Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Tennis is the quiet workhorse of the betting calendar: matches almost every day of the year, two possible outcomes, and statistics that actually describe what will happen on court. For an Irish punter used to three-way football markets, tennis is refreshingly clean — but it has one trap all of its own, and it is written in the small print.

No draw means cleaner maths

A tennis match has two outcomes, so the match winner market has two prices. That makes the bookmaker’s margin easy to see: convert both prices to implied probabilities, add them, and whatever sits above 100% is the house cut. On main-tour matches the two-way margin is typically tighter than a football 1X2, which has three prices to hide the cut across. Fewer outcomes, less fog — tennis is one of the easiest sports in which to compare bookmakers like-for-like.

The retirement trap: read the settlement rules

Here is the trap. Players retire mid-match — heat, injury, a title already secured elsewhere — and bookmakers do not settle those matches the same way. The common rule families are: bets void unless the first set is completed; bets void unless a full match is completed; and ball served, where one point is enough for the bet to stand on whoever advances. The same retirement can be a winning bet with one firm, a void with a second and a loser with a third. Before you bet tennis anywhere, find the settlement rule in the help pages — it matters more often than you would think across a long season.

Games and sets handicaps

When one player is a heavy favourite, the match price is unbettable and the handicap takes over, in two flavours. The set handicap (usually -1.5 sets in a best-of-three) asks the favourite to win in straight sets. The games handicap (say -4.5 games) totals games across the match: win 6-4 6-4 and a -3.5 line lands while -4.5 loses. Games lines reward dominance even in a match that loses a set; set lines are all-or-nothing on straight sets. Decide which story you are telling about the match before choosing between them.

Serve stats beat rankings

Rankings reward twelve months of results on every surface; today’s match is one surface, one day. Service hold and break percentages, recent form on the same court speed, and head-to-head styles (a big server against an elite returner) say more about a single match than the ranking gap does. This is the honest-maths point: the market already knows the rankings, so any edge you have must come from information the headline number hides.

In-play rhythm

Tennis scoring is a staircase — break points and tiebreaks swing prices violently, and the market overreacts to the first break far more than hold statistics justify. If you bet in-play, bet the server’s underlying numbers, not the scoreboard’s emotion, and keep stakes fixed before the match starts.

The honest summary

Tennis offers the cleanest prices in mainstream betting, but the retirement rules are where bookmakers genuinely differ. Know your firm’s rule before the first serve, prefer handicaps to unbettable favourites, and trust hold percentages over ranking numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to my tennis bet if a player retires injured?

It depends entirely on the bookmaker’s settlement rules. The common versions: void unless the first set was completed, void unless the full match was completed, or “ball served” — settled on whoever advances after a single point. The same retirement can win, void or lose the same bet at different firms, so read the rules first.

What is the difference between a set handicap and a games handicap?

A set handicap of -1.5 asks the favourite to win in straight sets in a best-of-three. A games handicap totals games across the whole match — a favourite can drop a set and still cover a games line if their margins are big enough. One is all-or-nothing on sets; the other rewards overall dominance.

Why do tennis markets have lower margins than football?

With only two outcomes, both prices are directly comparable and the house cut is easy to see — convert both odds to implied probabilities and add them. That visibility keeps two-way margins competitive, while three-way football markets spread the cut across more prices.

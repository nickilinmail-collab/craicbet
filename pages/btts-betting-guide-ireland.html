Both Teams to Score (BTTS) Betting in Ireland: Strategy and When to Use It

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Both teams to score — universally shortened to BTTS — is one of the most popular football betting markets in Ireland and across the UK. It is structurally simpler than most markets (did both teams score at least one goal?), widely available, and commonly used as an acca building block. This guide explains what it is, which contexts produce the most BTTS results, how to assess value and what to know before including it in an accumulator.

What Is BTTS Betting?

BTTS Yes means you are betting that both teams will score at least one goal in the match. BTTS No means at least one team will fail to score (a clean sheet for one side, or a 0-0 draw). The result of the match — who wins, the total number of goals — is irrelevant to the bet. A 1-1 draw, a 2-1 win, a 3-2 win: all settle BTTS Yes as a winner. A 1-0 win, a 2-0 win, a 0-0 draw: all settle BTTS No as a winner.

BTTS Yes typically prices between 1.60 and 2.00 on a standard fixture, implying a 50–60% probability range. BTTS No is therefore roughly the complement. Bookmaker margins vary but are typically in the 5–8% range on this market.

Which Leagues Produce Most BTTS Results

BTTS rates vary considerably between leagues. Higher-scoring, more open leagues tend to produce BTTS Yes more frequently. Historical data across major European leagues typically shows:

  • Bundesliga — consistently among the highest BTTS rates in major European leagues, reflecting the league’s high-scoring, attacking style of play.
  • Premier League — moderate-to-high BTTS rates, particularly in matches involving mid-table teams with weaker defensive records.
  • Serie A — historically lower BTTS rates due to traditionally more defensive Italian football, though the league has become more open in recent seasons.
  • League of Ireland — variable rates depending on the specific teams; LOI matches between lower-table clubs can produce clean sheet results more often than expected.
  • Champions League group stage — high BTTS rates due to attacking quality on both sides in many fixtures.

These are general patterns, not guarantees for any specific match. Individual fixture context always matters more than league averages.

How to Identify BTTS Value

Assessing BTTS requires a fixture-by-fixture approach rather than applying league averages mechanically. Key factors:

  • Defensive records of both teams. A team conceding in a high proportion of their recent matches is more likely to let one in. If both teams have poor clean sheet records, BTTS Yes has statistical support.
  • Attacking efficiency. A team that creates chances but converts at a low rate may score less often than their open play suggests. Expected goals (xG) data can be more informative than goals scored.
  • Match context and motivation. A team that needs to attack to get a result (behind in a cup tie, needing a win for the title) is more likely to leave defensive space for the opposition. Cup replays and knockout matches produce BTTS Yes more often than low-stakes league fixtures between comfortable mid-table teams.
  • Price comparison. Compare the BTTS Yes price across bookmakers before placing. On popular fixtures, prices are consistent, but smaller meetings may have slight variations.

BTTS in Accumulator Bets

BTTS Yes is a very popular acca ingredient because individual prices in the 1.75–1.90 range combine quickly. A five-fold BTTS acca at an average of 1.80 per leg gives combined odds of 18.9 — a potentially large return on a small stake.

The mathematical reality: five BTTS Yes bets each at a true probability of 55% (comparable to a 1.80 price with a margin) have a combined probability of 55%&sup5; = approximately 5%. The house edge compounds, as explained in our acca calculator. BTTS accas are entertaining and occasionally win big; they are expensive to run as a systematic strategy.

A combined BTTS and Match Result market (“BTTS and Home Win”) is also widely available, combining two conditions in a single bet for higher odds. This should be treated as a high-margin entertainment bet rather than a core strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a 1-0 result count as BTTS?

No. For BTTS to win, both teams must score at least one goal each. A 1-0 result means only one team scored, so BTTS loses. The final score is what counts — goals in extra time and penalties after a draw do not count for BTTS in most bookmaker rules.

Is BTTS a good market to include in accas?

BTTS is widely used in accas because the odds are typically in the 1.70–2.00 range per selection, making combined odds grow quickly. The house edge still compounds with every leg. Keep accas short — 2 to 3 BTTS selections is a more rational approach than a 6-fold.

Can I combine BTTS with match result?

Yes. BTTS and Win (sometimes written as ‘BTTS & Home Win’ or ‘BTTS & Away Win’) is a popular combined market. The odds are higher because two things must happen simultaneously. This market is available on most major football fixtures.

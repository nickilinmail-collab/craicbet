What Does Double Chance Mean in Betting?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Double chance allows you to cover two of the three possible football outcomes (home win, draw, away win) in a single bet. The trade-off for this safety is lower odds. Here is exactly how the three double-chance options work, with the implied probability maths.

The Three Double Chance Options

  • 1X (Home Win or Draw): Your bet wins if the home team wins or the match draws. Loses only if the away team wins.
  • X2 (Draw or Away Win): Your bet wins if the away team wins or the match draws. Loses only if the home team wins.
  • 12 (Home Win or Away Win): Your bet wins if either team wins. Loses only on a draw.

Implied Probability of Double Chance Outcomes

Double chance implied probability = sum of the two covered outcomes’ individual implied probabilities. If home win = 50%, draw = 25%, away win = 25%:

  • 1X implied: 50% + 25% = 75%
  • X2 implied: 25% + 25% = 50%
  • 12 implied: 50% + 25% = 75%

At these probabilities, 1X and X2 at 75% would be priced around 1.33 decimal. 12 at 75% around 1.33. The bookmaker’s margin means actual prices are slightly lower.

When Does Double Chance Make Sense?

Double chance is most useful when you are confident a certain outcome will not happen, but are less sure between the other two. It is a popular market for:

  • Away underdogs where you want draw or away win (X2 on a solid travelling side)
  • Eliminating the draw risk when backing a heavy favourite (1X provides more insurance than DNB at lower odds)
  • Both teams competitive, one clear winner expected but draw remains possible (12)

When to Use Double Chance

  • 1X: When backing a home team but worried about a draw — common when backing a slight favourite
  • X2: When backing an away team but accepting the draw — useful for strong away sides in difficult fixtures
  • 12: When you expect a winner but think a draw is very unlikely — avoids the draw result without backing a specific team
  • Double chance odds are always lower than 1X2 because you're covering two outcomes — the payout is sacrificed for safety
  • Double chance doesn't apply to markets like Asian handicap (which eliminates draws by design)
  • Useful for accumulators where you want to reduce the risk of one leg losing to a draw

What does double chance mean in betting?

Double chance covers two of the three football outcomes (home win/draw/away win) in one bet. 1X = home or draw, X2 = draw or away, 12 = home or away win.

What is the difference between double chance and Draw No Bet?

Draw No Bet (DNB) covers only the win outcome and returns stake on a draw. Double chance 1X covers win AND draw — it doesn't return stake on draw, it wins. 1X double chance odds are slightly lower than DNB odds.

Is double chance good value?

It can be — particularly X2 on a strong away team where you want to cover both win and draw. But double chance by definition has lower odds than a single outcome, so you need to be confident the excluded result won't happen.

Can I use double chance in an accumulator?

Yes — double chance legs can be added to accas. Each leg must win for the acca to pay out. If a double chance leg pushes or ties (not applicable — DC always has a winner or loser), it is treated normally.

What does 1X mean in betting?

Home win or draw — the 1X double chance option wins if the home team wins or the match ends in a draw. It loses only if the away team wins.

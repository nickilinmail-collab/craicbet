Each-Way Betting on Horse Racing: What It Means and How to Use It

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

What Is Each-Way Betting?

An each-way bet is two separate bets placed at the same time for the same stake: a win bet and a place bet. If you place €10 each-way, you are spending €20 in total — €10 on the horse to win, and €10 on the horse to finish in the places. Both bets use the same odds.

If your horse wins, you collect on both parts. If it places but doesn't win, you lose the win stake but collect on the place stake. If it finishes outside the places, you lose both stakes. It is a simple structure but the value depends entirely on the place terms on offer, which vary significantly by race, field size and bookmaker.

Real example: you back a horse at 10/1 each-way for €5 (€10 total). The place terms are 1/4 odds, 3 places. If the horse finishes second: you lose the €5 win stake, but the place part pays €5 × (10 ÷ 4) = €5 × 2.5 = €12.50 profit, plus your €5 stake returned. Total return: €17.50 from a €10 outlay.

How Place Terms Work in Irish Racing

Place terms define how many places are paid and at what fraction of the win odds. The standard structure in Irish and UK racing is:

  • 5–7 runners: Win only, or 2 places at 1/4 odds
  • 8–11 runners: 3 places at 1/4 odds (most common for non-handicap races)
  • 12–15 runners: 3 places at 1/4 odds, or 4 places in handicaps
  • 16+ runners: 4 places at 1/4 odds (some handicaps go to 5 places)

Festival promotions are where place terms matter most. During Cheltenham or Punchestown, some bookmakers extend to 5 or even 6 places on major handicaps. These extra-place offers can transform an each-way bet: a horse finishing 5th at 20/1 returns a profit on the place part at 1/4 odds when standard terms would have been a loser. Always compare terms on big-field handicaps before placing.

Irish National Hunt racing adds an extra wrinkle: smaller fields are common in jumps races, which can reduce the number of places. A novice hurdle with six runners may pay only two places — meaning your each-way bet needs a podium finish to pay out at all.

When Each-Way Makes Sense

Each-way betting works best when the win odds are long enough that the place part provides meaningful value. The rule of thumb: a horse priced at 7/1 or above is generally worth considering each-way. At 10/1 with 1/4 place terms, the place part pays 2.5/1 — a reasonable return for a horse expected to run well but not necessarily win. At 2/1, the place part pays just 0.5/1 (even money), which is poor value for a horse most expect to place anyway.

Big-field handicaps at festivals are the classic each-way opportunity. A 20+ runner Cheltenham handicap has natural variance, strong place chances for mid-priced selections, and bookmakers often competing on place terms. These are the races where each-way value lives.

When to avoid each-way: small fields (fewer than 8 runners), short-priced favourites (little place value in the fraction), and when you have a genuinely strong win opinion — in that case, a straight win bet keeps the full return if you are right.

Common Each-Way Mistakes

The most common mistake is placing each-way bets without checking the place terms. Assuming 1/4 odds and 3 places applies to every race will cost you — a 7-runner race paying only 2 places at 1/4 odds changes the maths completely.

Second mistake: each-way on short-priced horses. A 5/2 favourite each-way at 1/4 odds returns 10 cents per euro staked on the place part if it places without winning. That is not a bet; that is capital destruction in slow motion.

Third: not using extra-place promotions. When a bookmaker is running 5-place each-way on a 20-runner Cheltenham handicap and standard terms are 4 places, that is genuine edge. Tracking promotions during festival week is part of the homework.

Fourth: betting each-way on a selection you believe will win. If your analysis gives a horse a genuinely strong win chance, backing it each-way dilutes your return when right. The each-way structure is an insurance policy — and insurance costs money every time it is unnecessary.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many places does an each-way bet cover?

It depends on race size and type. Standard terms: 3 places in fields of 8–15 runners, 4 places in 16+ runners. Big festival handicaps sometimes see 5-place promotions. Always check the terms before placing.

What fraction of the win odds does the place part pay?

Usually 1/4 of the win odds for standard races. So at 10/1, the place part pays 2.5/1. Some races (larger handicaps) pay 1/5 odds. Compare terms for high-value selections since it varies by bookmaker.

Is each-way betting worth it on short-priced horses?

Generally no. On a horse at 2/1 or less, the place part pays around even money or less, which is poor value. Each-way betting gives best value on selections priced 7/1 and above, where the place part can still return a decent profit.

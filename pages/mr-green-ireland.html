What Is Mr Green?

Mr Green was founded in Sweden in 2007 and quickly established itself as a premium online casino brand, known for its stylish design, wide games library, and responsible gambling features. The brand built a reputation particularly in Scandinavia and the UK, with a focus on casino products including slots, live casino, and table games.

In 2019, Mr Green was acquired by William Hill, the British bookmaker. Following William Hill’s subsequent takeover by 888 Holdings (now rebranded as Evoke plc), Mr Green became part of a broader portfolio of gambling brands that includes William Hill, 888, and others. Mr Green continues to operate as a distinct consumer-facing brand, holding licences from the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) and the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC).

That said, holding an MGA or UKGC licence does not automatically authorise an operator to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence — and Mr Green as a distinct brand does not appear to hold one.

GRAI Licence Status

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting or gaming products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, Mr Green does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators as a separately authorised brand. While the William Hill brand within the same Evoke plc group holds its own GRAI licence, that authorisation applies to William Hill — not to Mr Green as a separate operator brand.

Casino Licences in Ireland: GRAI Issues Betting Licences Only

An important point for anyone researching Mr Green in an Irish context: the GRAI currently issues remote betting licences only. As of November 2026, zero remote gaming (casino) licences have been issued by the GRAI. Casino gaming licences are expected to become available in 2027–2028, subject to the GRAI completing the relevant regulatory framework.

This matters because Mr Green is primarily a casino brand. Even if Mr Green were to obtain GRAI authorisation in the future, that would require the GRAI first beginning to issue casino gaming licences. Any operator — Mr Green or otherwise — that claims to hold a GRAI casino licence as of 2026 would be making a false claim. The GRAI has not issued any.

For betting products specifically, some operators have begun obtaining GRAI remote betting licences. Mr Green does not appear among them.

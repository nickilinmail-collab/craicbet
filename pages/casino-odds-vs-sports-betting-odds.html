Casino games and sports betting both involve a house advantage, but they're structured differently. Understanding both helps you make more informed decisions about where your gambling budget offers the most value.

Casino House Edge

Casino games have a fixed mathematical house edge determined by the game's rules. European roulette: 2.7%. Blackjack (basic strategy): ~0.5%. Slots: 3%–8% typically. The house edge is consistent — it doesn't change based on what you bet or what you know. It's a structural feature of the game's design.

Sports Betting Margin (Overround)

Sports betting bookmakers build a margin (or overround) into their odds. A fair-odds bet on a 50/50 coin flip would pay 2.00 (even money) each way. A bookmaker might offer 1.90/1.90 — paying only 90% of fair value on each side. The overround is typically 5%–10% on mainstream sports.

Skill in Sports Betting

Sports betting has a skill element — a knowledgeable bettor can find markets where the bookmaker's odds are mispriced, achieving positive expected value over time. Casino games (except blackjack/video poker strategy) have no equivalent skill advantage. This makes sports betting theoretically beatable for a skilled bettor in a way that casino games are not.

GAA Football 2027 Favourites — Championship Contenders Betting Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 Updated November 2026⏱ 4 min read

Looking ahead to the 2027 All-Ireland Football Championship, a small group of counties are expected to open as market favourites while a broader group of challengers carry realistic aspirations. This guide examines who the contenders are, what makes them favourites, and how Irish punters can find value beyond the obvious selections.

The Traditional Powers

Dublin, Kerry and Galway have been the most consistent All-Ireland football contenders in recent seasons. Dublin's sustained dominance of the 2010s has given way to a more competitive landscape, with multiple counties now capable of winning the Sam Maguire. Kerry's tradition and consistent squad production means they always feature near the top of the market regardless of the previous season's result.

Emerging Contenders

Derry, Armagh, Roscommon and Tyrone have all shown the capacity to reach All-Ireland semi-finals and finals in recent years, disrupting the traditional pecking order. Any of these counties represents a potential value bet if the market has not fully adjusted to their recent improvements.

How to Find Value Beyond the Favourites

  • Spring league form: The National Football League in spring is a genuine guide to championship readiness. Teams that win the league or push through Division 1 carry forward momentum.
  • Provincial form: Winning a provincial title confirms a county has beaten strong opposition under championship pressure.
  • Squad depth: Counties that can sustain form across a 10-week knockout championship without key injury disruption are most likely to go deep.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which county is most likely to win the All-Ireland Football 2027?

Market prices for 2027 will reflect spring league form and squad assessments and will not be published until early 2027. Dublin, Kerry and Galway have been the most consistent All-Ireland football contenders in recent seasons.

Is Ulster the strongest province in Gaelic football?

Ulster football has been highly competitive in recent seasons, with multiple Ulster counties reaching All-Ireland semi-finals and finals. Ulster club championships are contested intensely, and Ulster provincial winners are always genuine All-Ireland contenders.

What is the Tailteann Cup?

The Tailteann Cup is a secondary All-Ireland Gaelic football competition for counties that do not advance to the All-Ireland quarter-finals. It runs alongside the main championship in June and July and was introduced to give more counties meaningful summer championship football.

How does the qualifier system work in GAA football?

Teams that lose in the provincial championship can re-enter the All-Ireland series through the qualifier rounds. The qualifier system gives counties multiple paths to the All-Ireland quarter-finals and creates more matches — including potential upset opportunities for underdog counties.

Cheltenham Festival 2027 Betting: Dates, Key Races and How to Prepare

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Cheltenham Festival is the most significant week in the National Hunt racing calendar and, for many Irish bettors, the defining betting week of the year. With 28 races across four days, enormous prize money and the full weight of Irish racing talent crossing the Irish Sea, Cheltenham attracts more serious betting attention from Ireland than any other event outside of the Galway Festival at home. This guide covers what to know for 2027 as the season builds.

Cheltenham 2027 Dates

The Cheltenham Festival traditionally occupies the second or third week of March. For 2027, the festival is expected to run across four days in mid-March — provisionally 16–19 March 2027, subject to official confirmation by The Jockey Club. Check cheltenham.co.uk for confirmed dates as the season progresses. The four days follow a consistent structure: Champion Day (Tuesday), Ladies Day (Wednesday), St Patrick’s Thursday and Gold Cup Day (Friday).

Ante-post markets for Cheltenham 2027 open immediately after the 2026 festival ends in many cases, with significant liquidity available from the autumn of 2026 onwards as horses begin their campaign preparation.

The Four Key Races to Watch

Cheltenham features 28 races but four Grade 1 contests define the festival hierarchy:

  • Champion Hurdle (Tuesday, 2m). The championship for hurdlers. It rewards speed and jumping accuracy over two miles. Usually a small field of elite contenders.
  • Queen Mother Champion Chase (Wednesday, 2m). The championship for two-mile chasers. Ireland has supplied many winners and the race has historically attracted huge betting volumes from Irish punters.
  • Stayers’ Hurdle (Thursday, 3m). The championship for staying hurdlers. A division that has produced dominant performers capable of shortening significantly through the season.
  • Cheltenham Gold Cup (Friday, 3m 2f). The festival showpiece. The championship for staying chasers, run over the full festival track distance including the uphill finish. The Gold Cup market tends to open very long and tighten significantly as the season progresses and trials are completed.

How Ante-Post Cheltenham Markets Work

Ante-post betting means placing a bet before the final declarations, often months in advance. The main risk is that your selection does not run — through injury, being saved for another race, or connections deciding against the festival — and your stake is lost. To mitigate this, look for non-runner no-bet (NRNB) markets, where your stake is returned if the horse does not start. NRNB is available on many Cheltenham markets from most bookmakers, particularly closer to the festival.

Ante-post prices offer the opportunity to take value on a horse before the market shortens through the season. A horse available at 10.00 in November may be 5.00 by February if winter trials go well. The risk-reward calculation requires balancing price versus the horse’s likelihood of actually running.

Irish vs British Horses at Cheltenham

Irish-trained horses have consistently dominated the Cheltenham Festival in recent years, winning the majority of Grade 1 races in many recent festivals. The major National Hunt stables in Ireland have invested heavily at the top of the market and regularly send their best horses to Cheltenham as a primary target.

British-trained runners have continued to supply competitive horses in several divisions — the two-mile chasing division in particular has produced British winners in recent seasons. For the Gold Cup, the balance of power has shifted considerably toward Irish stables over the past decade, though British trainers have produced competitive contenders and the race remains genuinely open.

For Irish bettors, the practical implication is that the most popular ante-post selections are often Irish-trained, which means the prices on those horses tend to be efficiently priced given the volume of Irish money. British-trained horses in the same races may sometimes be priced at longer odds than their chance warrants, though this varies by season and by horse.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Cheltenham Festival 2027 take place?

The Cheltenham Festival traditionally takes place in mid-March over four days. The 2027 edition is expected in the week of 16–19 March 2027, subject to official Jockey Club confirmation. Check cheltenham.co.uk for the confirmed dates.

What is non-runner no-bet in Cheltenham ante-post markets?

Non-runner no-bet (NRNB) means that if your selection does not run, your stake is returned rather than lost. This removes the main risk of ante-post betting. It is available on many Cheltenham markets but not all — always check the market terms.

How have Irish-trained horses performed at Cheltenham recently?

Irish-trained horses have dominated Cheltenham for many years, winning the majority of Grade 1 contests across the four days in recent festivals. The Champion Hurdle, Stayers’ Hurdle, Champion Chase and Gold Cup have all regularly gone to Irish-trained runners.

Cheltenham 2027: The Irish Punter's Early Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Four Days That Stop a Nation

The Cheltenham Festival runs in mid-March (the 2027 renewal is expected 16–19 March; we will confirm the moment dates are official). For Irish racing people it is the year's true championship — and for Irish betting it is the single biggest week bar none. This page is our living hub for 2027: bookmark it, because everything below updates as the season takes shape.

Why Ireland Dominates the Festival

Irish-trained horses have owned recent Festivals — the Closutton and Cullentra production lines turned the Prestbury Cup (Ireland vs Britain in winners) into a rout for most of the 2020s. For punters, the practical lesson: the strongest ante-post clues come out of the Dublin Racing Festival at Leopardstown in early February, the dress rehearsal where Festival favourites are made and broken.

Ante-Post: The Trade-Off in Plain Terms

Betting months ahead buys bigger odds and sells insurance: in standard ante-post terms, if your horse does not run — injury, re-routing to a different race, the trainer simply changing plans — your stake is gone. Non-Runner No Bet (NRNB) offers change that equation completely, refunding non-runners. The season's rhythm: ante-post value peaks early (autumn), NRNB offers land progressively from January — and the moment a book you use goes NRNB on a race, your downside on that race mostly evaporates.

The Offers Calendar (How Festival Week Really Works)

Festival week brings the year's richest promotions: enhanced each-way places (4–6 places in the big handicaps against a standard 3), free bet packages, and money-back specials on feature races. Frequently Asked Questions

When is Cheltenham 2027?

Mid-March — expected 16–19 March 2027; we update this page the moment dates are official.

What does Non-Runner No Bet mean?

Your ante-post stake is refunded if the horse does not run; without NRNB, a non-runner loses the stake.

Why are Irish horses favourites at Cheltenham?

Irish yards have dominated recent Festivals; the Dublin Racing Festival in February is the key trial.

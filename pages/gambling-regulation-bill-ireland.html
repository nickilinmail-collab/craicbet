Gambling Regulation Bill Ireland — What Changed in 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 10 min read

The Gambling Regulation Bill 2022 is the most significant reform of Irish gambling law in nearly a century. It replaced legislation dating back to 1931 and created the framework for modern, digital-age gambling regulation in Ireland. This guide explains what the Bill contains, what it changed, and what it means for Irish players and operators in 2026.

Background: Why Ireland Needed New Gambling Laws

Before the Gambling Regulation Bill, Ireland's gambling laws were based on:

  • The Betting Act 1931 — written for horse racing bookmakers, 60 years before the internet
  • The Gaming and Lotteries Act 1956 — designed for amusement halls and charity raffles

These laws were completely inadequate for regulating online gambling, smartphone betting apps, live dealer casinos, or social media advertising. Ireland had no dedicated gambling regulator, no mandatory player protection requirements, and essentially no regulatory framework for the hundreds of online gambling sites available to Irish consumers.

Multiple reports and commissions over two decades recommended reform, but legislative progress was slow. The Gambling Regulation Bill finally passed the Oireachtas in 2022 after years of development and debate.

Key Provisions of the Bill

1. Creation of the GRAI

The Bill's centrepiece is the establishment of the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as an independent statutory body. The GRAI has powers to:

  • Issue, monitor, suspend, and revoke gambling licences
  • Set and enforce standards for licensed operators
  • Investigate complaints from players
  • Impose fines and other sanctions for breaches
  • Commission research into gambling behaviour and harm
  • Maintain a public register of licensed operators

2. Comprehensive Licensing Framework

The Bill establishes a licensing framework for all forms of gambling, including:

  • Remote (online) betting — live since July 2026
  • Remote (online) gaming (casino, poker) — expected 2027–2028
  • Retail betting shops
  • Gaming machines and amusement centres
  • Lotteries (excluding the National Lottery, which has separate legislation)

3. Player Protection Requirements

Licensed operators must implement a suite of responsible gambling tools:

  • Deposit limits (daily, weekly, monthly)
  • Session time limits and reality checks
  • Self-exclusion (individual operator and national register)
  • Activity statements and transaction history
  • Cool-down/time-out periods
  • Staff training on identifying problem gambling

4. Advertising Restrictions

The Bill introduces statutory advertising codes covering:

  • No targeting of minors (under 18)
  • Watershed rules for broadcast advertising
  • Social media advertising restrictions
  • Mandatory responsible gambling messaging
  • Prohibition on misleading bonus promotions
  • Restrictions on sponsorship of youth events and teams

5. Social Impact Fund

The Bill creates a Social Impact Fund financed by levies on licensed operators. The Fund supports problem gambling treatment, research, education, and support services. This ensures that the industry contributes to mitigating gambling-related harm.

6. Enforcement Powers

The GRAI has significant enforcement powers under the Bill:

  • Financial penalties for licence breaches
  • Licence suspension and revocation
  • DNS blocking of unlicensed operators
  • Payment processing restrictions
  • Criminal offences for certain breaches (e.g., underage gambling, operating without a licence)

What Changed for Players

The Gambling Regulation Bill brought several concrete changes for Irish players:

  • A regulator that cares: For the first time, Irish players have a dedicated regulator they can turn to with complaints and concerns.
  • Mandatory protections: Deposit limits, self-exclusion, and reality checks are now legal requirements, not optional features.
  • Cleaner advertising: Fewer gambling ads during children's programming, stricter rules on social media promotions.
  • Better transparency: Operators must be transparent about their licence status, terms and conditions, and complaint procedures.

What Changed for Operators

  • Licensing requirement: Operators must hold a GRAI licence to legally serve the Irish market (being phased in by product type).
  • Compliance costs: Significant investment required in responsible gambling tools, staff training, and reporting systems.
  • Social Impact Fund levies: Financial contributions to the fund are mandatory for all licensed operators.
  • Enforcement risk: Non-compliance can result in substantial fines, licence suspension, or revocation.

FAQ: Gambling Regulation Bill

When was the Gambling Regulation Bill passed?▾
The Gambling Regulation Bill was passed by the Oireachtas (Irish Parliament) in 2022. It replaced the outdated Betting Act 1931 and Gaming and Lotteries Act 1956.
Does the Bill make online gambling illegal?▾
No. The Bill regulates online gambling rather than prohibiting it. It creates a licensing framework so that operators can be legally authorised to serve the Irish market with appropriate player protections.
What is the Social Impact Fund?▾
The Social Impact Fund is financed by levies on licensed gambling operators. It supports problem gambling treatment, research, education, and support services in Ireland. The principle is that the gambling industry should contribute to mitigating the harm it can cause.
Can the GRAI block gambling websites?▾
Yes. Under the Bill, the GRAI has the power to request DNS blocking of unlicensed gambling operators and to restrict payment processing to those sites. These powers are designed to protect Irish consumers by making it harder to access unregulated gambling services.

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the most significant overhaul of Irish gambling law in decades. It created GRAI as Ireland's dedicated gambling regulator and introduced new protections for players, including advertising restrictions and mandatory responsible gambling tools. Remote betting licences became required from 1 July 2026; retail from 1 December 2026.

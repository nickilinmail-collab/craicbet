GRAI vs MGA Licence: What Each Means for Irish Punters 2026

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) and the MGA (Malta Gaming Authority) are the two regulatory frameworks most relevant to Irish punters. This guide explains the practical differences between using an operator under each licence — what happens if something goes wrong, who you can complain to, and what your legal rights are.

The Core GRAI Advantage: Irish Jurisdiction

The critical difference: a GRAI-licensed operator that refuses to pay out is breaking Irish criminal law. The GRAI can investigate, sanction and revoke the licence of operators that breach their conditions. You escalate complaints to the GRAI — an Irish body, operating under Irish law, in Ireland. With an MGA-licensed operator, your complaint is handled in Malta under Maltese law. The MGA does have an ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution) process and player fund, but it operates at a distance and under different jurisdiction.

MGA Is Not Weak — But GRAI Is Stronger for Irish Players

The MGA is one of the world’s more respected gambling regulators — significantly more protective than Curaçao, Isle of Man (for some disputes) or Gibraltar. MGA-licensed operators are generally trustworthy. The comparison is not MGA = bad; it is GRAI = better protection for Irish residents specifically, because it uses Irish law and an Irish regulator who can be held accountable by the Irish government.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a GRAI licence?

The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) issues remote betting licences to operators serving Irish customers. Since July 2026, all bookmakers accepting Irish bets must hold a GRAI licence. It provides Irish statutory protections.

What is an MGA licence?

The MGA (Malta Gaming Authority) is a Maltese gambling regulator. MGA licences are held by many reputable online operators. They provide reasonable protections but under Maltese jurisdiction, not Irish law.

Is MGA licensing safe enough for Irish players?

MGA is a reputable regulator with a player protection fund and ADR process. It is significantly more protective than Curaçao. For Irish betting, GRAI provides stronger Irish-specific protections; for casino (where no GRAI licence exists yet), MGA is the best available.

How do I complain about an MGA-licensed bookmaker?

First use the operator’s internal complaints process. If unresolved, escalate to the MGA’s ADR process. MGA complaints are handled in Malta under Maltese law.

Are there GRAI casino licences?

No. The GRAI has not issued any casino (remote gaming) licences as of November 2026. Casino licences are expected 2027–2028. All online casinos currently operate under international licences.

Free Bets and Inducements Under Irish Law: The Nuance Everyone Gets Wrong

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

“Ireland has banned free bets” is one of the most repeated — and most wrong — claims about the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The Act does something more surgical: it keeps general promotions legal under conditions and bans the targeting of inducements at specific people or groups. That distinction decides what your bookmaker can still offer you, so it is worth getting right.

What the Act actually says

Under the Act, inducements such as free bets and special offers may still be offered to the public at large. What is prohibited is directing them at specific individuals or specific groups — the classic “we noticed you stopped betting, here’s €20” retention message, or offers aimed at identified heavy losers. Breaches carry serious teeth: the Act provides for fines of up to €20 million or 10% of turnover, whichever is greater.

The nuance everyone gets wrong

A sign-up free bet advertised to everyone is a general promotion. A bonus fired at you personally because your account went quiet, or because your deposit pattern marks you as valuable, is a targeted inducement — and that is what the law is aimed at. The old VIP playbook, where the biggest losers got the most generous “gestures”, is precisely the practice the Oireachtas set out to end. The grey zone the GRAI will have to police is segmentation: an offer sent to “customers who bet on the Premier League last month” is a group, and groups are covered.

Why the law targets targeting: the ESRI evidence

In June 2025 the ESRI published research commissioned by GRAI and the Department of Justice. In a controlled experiment with 622 men under 40 betting on Euro 2024, participants shown free bets and money-back offers spent over 10% more, the share choosing not to bet at all fell by nearly half, and those shown inducements were three times more likely to take “bad” bets priced below market rates — with people at risk of problem gambling most affected. Most regular bettors surveyed also did not know basic free-bet mechanics, such as that a winning free bet does not return the stake.

What changes for free bets in practice

Expect public, same-for-everyone offers to continue — sign-up free bets, price boosts advertised on site — while personalised retention bonuses, loyalty tiers rewarding losses and “win-back” messages disappear from Irish-licensed operators. Advertising rules reinforce this: gambling ads online will require an opt-in, and a broadcast watershed keeps TV and radio ads off air between 5:30am and 9pm. GRAI codes of practice will fill in the detail, and the inducement provisions commence on the Act’s phased timetable rather than all at once.

The honest maths of a free bet

Even a legal, general free bet is not free money. Stakes are not returned on free-bet wins, so a €10 free bet at evens pays €10, not €20 — its real value is roughly half face value before you account for any wagering conditions. Frequently Asked Questions

Are free bets banned in Ireland?

No. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 allows inducements such as free bets and special offers to be made to the public generally. What it prohibits is targeting them at specific individuals or specific groups.

What counts as a targeted inducement?

An offer directed at a particular person or defined group rather than the public at large — for example a personalised bonus sent because your account went quiet, or VIP-style rewards aimed at identified high-spending customers.

What are the penalties for breaking the inducement rules?

The Act provides for fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover, whichever is greater, alongside GRAI’s licensing powers such as suspension or revocation.

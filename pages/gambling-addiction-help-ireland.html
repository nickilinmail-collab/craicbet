Recognising a Gambling Problem

Gambling becomes a problem when it starts to interfere with your life — your finances, relationships, work, or mental health. The transition from recreational to harmful gambling can happen gradually and is often not obvious to the person themselves until significant damage has been done.

Common warning signs include:

  • Spending more money or time gambling than intended.
  • Chasing losses (gambling to try to win back money already lost).
  • Lying to family or friends about gambling activity or losses.
  • Gambling to cope with stress, anxiety, depression or loneliness.
  • Difficulty stopping or cutting back despite wanting to.
  • Borrowing money or selling belongings to fund gambling.
  • Neglecting responsibilities, work, or relationships because of gambling.

If you recognise several of these patterns, you are not alone — and help is available, free and confidential.

What GRAI Requires from Operators

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI require all licensed operators to provide:

  • Mandatory responsible gambling tools: deposit limits, time limits, reality checks and cooling-off periods must be offered to all customers.
  • Self-exclusion: operators must facilitate self-exclusion requests promptly. The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) enables multi-site exclusion in a single step.
  • Affordability checks: GRAI operators must monitor for signs of problem gambling behaviour and may request financial information from high-spending customers.
  • Advertising restrictions: stricter rules on marketing, including restrictions on targeting vulnerable populations.

These are minimum requirements. Many operators go further with proactive outreach when spending patterns suggest a problem. If you are struggling, contact the site's responsible gambling support team directly — they are required to respond helpfully and confidentially.

Self-Help Steps

If you want to take immediate action before accessing formal support:

  1. Set strict limits: Log into your gambling accounts and set deposit and time limits to the lowest comfortable level. Do this now — it is effective immediately.
  2. Self-exclude: Request self-exclusion from each site you use. Register with NGER to cover all GRAI-licensed operators at once.
  3. Block gambling sites: Install GamStop (gamstop.co.uk) or Gamban (gamban.com) to block access across devices. These cannot be easily reversed.
  4. Tell someone you trust: Telling a family member or friend creates accountability and allows them to support you.
  5. Remove access to funds: Ask a trusted person to manage your finances temporarily if debt is a concern. Contact MABS (mabs.ie) for free money advice.

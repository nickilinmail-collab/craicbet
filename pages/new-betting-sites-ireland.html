New Betting Sites in Ireland 2026

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

“New betting site” used to mean an offshore brand with a flashy bonus and a Curaçao badge. Since the Gambling Regulation Act took effect and GRAI began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026, the phrase has a legal definition in Ireland: a new site is one that has just cleared the regulator. Here is how to judge a new entrant in the licensed era.

Licence check first, everything else second

Before a welcome offer, before the app design, check the licence. Every legal online bookmaker in Ireland must hold a GRAI remote betting licence and display its licence number — the format looks like GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 (that real example is a betting licence valid 16 September 2026 to 15 September 2029). Cross-check the number against the GRAI register itself, not just the operator's footer. A “new site” that cannot show a GRAI number is not new — it is unlicensed, with none of the Act's protections: no Irish complaints route, no national self-exclusion register, no segregated-funds requirements.

What a 2026 welcome offer really looks like

The Act's inducement rules reshaped sign-up offers: targeting restrictions, no VIP-style perks tied to losses, and advertising limits mean the era of the screaming €200 banner is over. Realistic structures now are modest matched free bets with clear terms. Remember the honest maths of a free bet: stakes are not returned on free-bet winnings, so a €25 free bet at even money is worth about €12.50 in expectation, not €25 — and less after any wagering conditions.

Why “new” isn't automatically better

A new entrant has to buy market share, which can briefly mean sharper odds or a generous offer — worth taking. But new also means unproven: slower withdrawals while payment processes bed in, a thinner market range, an untested customer service desk, and in the worst case a brand that exits the market a year later. An established licensee with boring, reliable payouts beats an exciting newcomer that pays slowly every single time it matters.

A five-point checklist for any new site

One: GRAI licence number verified on the register. Two: withdrawal methods and stated timescales in writing before you deposit. Three: welcome offer terms read in full — minimum odds, expiry, stake-not-returned. Four: markets you actually bet on priced competitively (compare its margin on a Premier League match against an established book). Five: responsible gambling tools — deposit limits, time-outs, self-exclusion — easy to find. If any of the five fails, there are licensed alternatives.

The casino question

One thing no new site can legally offer Irish players yet: a GRAI-licensed casino. GRAI issues remote betting licences only; gaming licences are expected in 2027–2028 and none has been issued. A new “betting and casino” brand claiming Irish casino licensing is misrepresenting itself — treat that as disqualifying.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I check if a new betting site is licensed in Ireland?

Find the GRAI remote betting licence number on the site (the format looks like GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001) and verify it on the GRAI register itself. No GRAI number means not legally licensed for Irish customers.

Are new betting sites safe to use?

A GRAI licence covers the legal side. Operationally, new sites are unproven — withdrawal speed and support quality only show up with use — so start small until they are demonstrated.

Can a new site offer casino games in Ireland?

Not legally under an Irish licence. GRAI issues remote betting licences only; casino (gaming) licences are expected in 2027–2028 and zero have been issued. Any claim otherwise is false.

Betano Ireland 2026: GRAI Licence Status & What Irish Punters Should Know

Betano is a fast-growing international bookmaker backed by Kaizen Gaming, with a strong presence across Europe and South America. But does it hold a GRAI licence to serve Irish customers? Here is what Irish punters need to know about Betano's regulatory status, what protections are missing, and where to find fully licensed alternatives.

What Is Betano?

Betano is a sports betting brand operated by Kaizen Gaming International, a Greek company founded in 2012. The brand expanded significantly across European and South American markets throughout the 2020s, establishing itself as one of the larger independent bookmakers in markets such as Portugal, Greece, Germany, Romania and Brazil.

In 2024, Betano launched in the United Kingdom under a UKGC licence, marking a significant push into one of the most regulated betting markets in the world. The brand is also licensed by the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), which provides a baseline of player protection in EU markets.

Betano is notably the official betting partner of UEFA, with sponsorship rights covering both the UEFA Europa League and the UEFA Conference League. This has given the brand strong visibility across football fans in Europe, including Ireland, where both competitions attract significant interest.

As a betting platform, Betano covers over 20 sports with an emphasis on football. Its product includes live betting, in-play markets, a bet builder tool, live streaming of select events, and a broad range of football competitions from Premier League and Champions League down to lower divisions. It is positioned as a premium football-first betting brand.

Betano's GRAI Licence Status in Ireland

Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced a new regulatory framework overseen by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI). From 1 July 2026, any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with remote betting services must hold a GRAI remote betting licence.

As of November 2026, Betano does not appear on the GRAI public register of licensed remote betting operators. The GRAI maintains a publicly searchable register at grai.ie where you can verify the current status of any operator.

By November 2026, 39 operators have been granted GRAI remote betting licences. Betano is not among them based on publicly available information at the time of writing. This situation may change — Betano may apply for a GRAI licence in future — but until it appears on the GRAI register, it is not authorised to operate as a regulated betting site for Irish customers.

⚠️ Note on This Information

Licence statuses can change. This page reflects publicly available information as of November 2026. Before registering with any bookmaker, we strongly recommend checking the current GRAI register at grai.ie to confirm whether that operator holds an active Irish licence.

Can Irish Players Use Betano?

Betano may be technically accessible from Ireland. Its MGA licence covers EU/EEA member states, and Irish players may not be geo-blocked in the same way that GRAI enforcement has forced some operators to restrict access. However, accessible and legal under Irish law are two different things.

An operator without a GRAI licence is not authorised to actively target, advertise to, or accept bets from Irish customers under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Irish players who choose to use an unlicensed operator do so outside the protections the Irish framework provides.

Specifically, without a GRAI licence, Betano is not required to provide Irish customers with:

  • Mandatory deposit limits: GRAI-licensed operators must offer and enforce deposit limits as a condition of their licence. This is not a voluntary opt-in — it is a regulatory requirement.
  • NGEI integration: The National Gambling Exclusion Indicator (NGEI) is Ireland's national self-exclusion register. GRAI-licensed operators must check and honour NGEI entries. An operator without a GRAI licence is not part of this system, so a self-exclusion registered in Ireland will not automatically apply at Betano.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator, you can escalate to the GRAI as a formal complaints body. Betano, without a GRAI licence, is not subject to that process. Your complaints route would be the MGA or UKGC instead, which is slower and more remote for Irish customers.
  • Credit card ban compliance under Irish law: GRAI-licensed operators must comply with Ireland's rules on payment methods for gambling. Unlicensed operators are not bound by Irish-specific requirements in this area.
  • Irish regulatory oversight: The GRAI holds licensed operators to account with enforcement powers including fines of up to €20 million. Betano, operating outside the GRAI framework, is not subject to those Irish enforcement powers.

Betano Features (For Reference)

For informational purposes, here is what Betano offers as a platform. These features exist but Irish players cannot access them with the GRAI protections they would receive from a licensed operator.

  • Sports coverage: Over 20 sports including football, tennis, basketball, horse racing and American sports. Football is the core product.
  • Football markets: Strong coverage of Champions League, Europa League and Conference League (as UEFA's official betting partner), Premier League, Bundesliga, La Liga, Serie A and Ligue 1. Includes outright markets, Asian handicaps and player props.
  • Bet builder: Same-game multiples combining match result, goalscorer, cards and other markets into one bet.
  • Live betting and in-play: Real-time markets during matches with updated odds as play progresses.
  • Live streaming: Select events streamed directly in the platform for account holders with a qualifying balance or recent bet.
  • Mobile apps: iOS and Android apps available in supported markets.
  • Licences held: MGA (Malta) and UKGC (United Kingdom) — both respected regulators, though neither substitutes for a GRAI licence for Irish customers.

The UEFA Europa League and Conference League partnership means Betano branding appears widely across both competitions — on pitchside advertising, broadcast graphics and digital coverage. This gives the brand strong recognition among European football fans regardless of their country of residence.

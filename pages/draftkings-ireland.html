DraftKings Ireland 2026: GRAI Licence Status & What Irish Players Need to Know

What Is DraftKings?

DraftKings Inc. is a US-based sports betting and daily fantasy sports (DFS) company founded in 2012 and headquartered in Boston, Massachusetts. It is publicly traded on the NASDAQ under the ticker symbol DKNG and is one of the two dominant sports betting operators in the United States alongside FanDuel.

DraftKings built its early reputation on daily fantasy sports contests before expanding into regulated sports betting following the US Supreme Court’s 2018 decision to strike down the federal ban on sports wagering. It now operates sportsbook products in dozens of US states, as well as in Canada and the UK. Its UK operation is licensed by the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC), one of the world’s most respected gambling regulators.

That said, holding a UKGC licence does not automatically authorise DraftKings — or any operator — to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence, issued by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

Does DraftKings Have a GRAI Licence?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, DraftKings does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means DraftKings has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation. DraftKings has not publicly announced a GRAI licence application.

DraftKings in Ireland: The History

DraftKings has a limited prior history in the Irish market. Around 2017–2018, the company offered its Daily Fantasy Sports (DFS) product to players in Ireland. DFS contests — where participants draft teams of real players and compete based on statistical performance — were treated as a distinct product from traditional sports betting and were not subject to the same Irish betting licensing requirements at the time.

DraftKings subsequently withdrew its DFS product from several European markets, including Ireland, as it refocused its international strategy. It has not since launched a full regulated sports betting product in the Irish market, nor has it held an Irish betting licence under any predecessor framework.

DraftKings’ international expansion beyond the United States and Canada has been relatively selective. Its primary non-US market is the UK, where it operates under a UKGC licence. Ireland, despite its proximity and shared language, has not been a publicly announced target market for DraftKings under the new GRAI framework as of November 2026.

What Does This Mean for Irish Players?

DraftKings is not currently available as a fully licensed Irish betting site. It does not hold a GRAI remote betting licence, which has been required for any operator legally targeting Irish customers since 1 July 2026 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

This status may change. Ireland’s GRAI licensing framework provides a clear regulatory pathway for DraftKings — or any operator — to enter the Irish market legally. CraicBet will update this page if DraftKings announces a GRAI application or launch.

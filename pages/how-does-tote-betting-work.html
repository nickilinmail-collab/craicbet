How Does Tote Betting Work? Pool Betting vs Fixed Odds Ireland

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

The Tote has been part of Irish horse racing since 1929. Its distinctive green booths were once the dominant betting option at Irish racecourses, alongside the traditional bookmaking ring. Today, Tote betting is available online as well as on-course — and while fixed-odds betting has taken over much of the market, Tote pool betting retains a loyal following and sometimes produces returns that fixed-odds bookmakers simply cannot match. This guide explains how pool betting works from first principles.

Pool Betting vs Fixed-Odds Betting: The Core Difference

In fixed-odds betting, you agree a price with a bookmaker before the race. The price is locked in and known. Win or lose, you know exactly what you stand to receive.

In pool betting (Tote), you are not betting against a bookmaker. You are betting into a shared pool with everyone else who backs that outcome. The winning dividend is calculated after the race by dividing the pool (minus the operator's deduction) among all winning tickets in proportion to their stake. You do not know the exact return until after the race.

This uncertainty is both the drawback and the opportunity of Tote betting. On heavily backed favourites, the dividend can be less than the SP. On lightly backed winners — especially longer-priced outsiders — the dividend can significantly exceed the SP.

The Main Tote Pool Bets

Win Pool: Back a horse to win the race. The simplest Tote bet. The win dividend is declared after the race.

Place Pool: Back a horse to finish in the places (usually 2nd or 3rd, more in bigger fields). The place dividend is declared separately from the win dividend — it is not simply a fraction of the win, unlike each-way place terms in fixed-odds betting.

Exacta: Predict the first two finishers in the correct order.

Trifecta: Predict the first three finishers in the correct order. Very difficult, but big pool returns possible.

Swinger: Predict two horses that will fill any two of the first three places (order does not matter).

Accumulator Pool Bets: Placepot and Jackpot

The Placepot requires you to select a placed horse in each of the first six races at a meeting. If all six selections place, you share the Placepot pool. The Placepot carries over (rolls over) if no one has all six placed horses — building up over successive meetings until it is won. At major festivals like Cheltenham or the Galway Races, Placepot pools of hundreds of thousands of euros are common.

The Jackpot works similarly but requires the winner of each of the first six races. Far harder to land, it typically carries over frequently and builds to very large pools.

These products represent genuine lottery-style value propositions. The minimum stake is usually very small (10p or 20c per line), and multiple selections per race are allowed (perms), increasing the cost but also the coverage.

When Tote Beats Fixed Odds

The Tote Win pool produces a better return than SP when: (1) the winner was lightly backed in the Tote pool — perhaps because professional money went through the fixed-odds market instead; (2) the Tote pool for the meeting is relatively small and one outsider's win creates a large dividend because few people backed it; (3) the horse is popular with the fixed-odds betting public but not with Tote customers, shifting the balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Tote betting?

Tote betting is pool betting: all stakes are pooled and the winning dividend is determined by dividing the pool (minus deductions) among winning tickets. You do not know your exact return in advance.

Can the Tote return more than fixed odds?

Yes. If a winner is lightly backed in the Tote pool, the dividend can exceed the SP. This is the main attraction of Tote betting.

What is the Placepot?

The Placepot requires you to select a placed horse in each of the first six races at a meeting. If all six selections place, you share the Placepot pool, which can carry over from meeting to meeting.

What is the Tote deduction?

The Tote deducts around 13–26% from the total pool before distributing winnings. This is the equivalent of the bookmaker margin in fixed-odds betting.

Is Tote betting available online in Ireland?

Yes. Tote betting is available online at several Irish betting sites and through the official Tote operator. Win, Place and accumulator pools like the Jackpot and Placepot are available.

Main Tote Bet Types in Ireland

  • Win: picks the winner; dividend = pool minus deductions ÷ winning tickets
  • Place: first 3 (or 2 in small fields); lower dividend than win
  • Exacta: 1st and 2nd in correct order; can produce large dividends
  • Trifecta: 1st, 2nd and 3rd in correct order; highest variance, highest potential return
  • Jackpot: select the winner of 6 nominated races; rollover builds the pool

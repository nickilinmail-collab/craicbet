Does Casumo Have a GRAI Licence?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, Casumo does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means Casumo has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

Casino vs Betting Licences in Ireland

This distinction matters particularly for Casumo, which is primarily a casino-first operator. Ireland’s new regulatory framework under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 has created two separate licensing tracks: remote betting licences and remote gaming (casino) licences.

As of November 2026, the GRAI has only issued remote betting licences. Casino gaming licences are a separate regulatory category, and zero casino gaming licences have been issued. The industry expectation is that GRAI casino licences will begin to be issued from 2027–2028, subject to the GRAI completing its gaming licensing framework.

What this means in practice:

  • Betting products (sports, horse racing, football, etc.): operators can apply for and hold a GRAI remote betting licence now.
  • Casino products (slots, live casino, table games): no GRAI gaming licence exists yet. Any operator currently offering casino products to Irish players is doing so outside the Irish casino licensing framework, regardless of what other licences they hold.
  • Casumo specifically: as a casino-first operator, Casumo would need a future GRAI gaming licence to bring its core casino offering under Irish regulatory oversight. It currently holds neither a GRAI betting licence nor a GRAI gaming licence.

This context is important: even if Casumo were to obtain a GRAI betting licence, its casino operations would remain outside Irish regulatory oversight until GRAI gaming licences are available and obtained.

What Does This Mean for Irish Players?

Casumo may remain technically accessible to users with Irish IP addresses via its MGA licence, depending on whether Casumo has implemented geographic restrictions for the Irish market. However, accessible does not mean licensed, and there is a meaningful practical difference for players.

When you use an operator that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

None of this is intended to suggest Casumo is an unsafe operator in general terms — its MGA and UKGC licences provide substantial protections. But Irish players considering Casumo should be clear that they would be operating outside Irish regulatory oversight.

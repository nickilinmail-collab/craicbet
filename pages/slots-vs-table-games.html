Slots vs Table Games — Which Is Better for Irish Players?

📝 CraicBet Team 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 9 min read

It's the oldest debate in online gambling: should you spin the reels or take a seat at the table? Slots and table games each have distinct advantages, and the right choice depends entirely on what kind of player you are. Let's break it down.

💡 Quick Answer

Table games (especially blackjack) offer better odds and reward skill. Slots are more entertaining, require no strategy, and contribute 100% to bonus wagering. Neither is objectively "better" — it depends on your priorities.

The biggest mathematical difference between slots and table games is the Return to Player (RTP). Table games with optimal strategy consistently outperform slots:

Skill vs Luck

Slots are 100% luck. No decision you make affects the outcome. The random number generator determines every spin before the reels even start moving. Choosing when to stop is your only real decision.

Table games involve varying degrees of skill. Blackjack basic strategy can push RTP to 99.5%. Poker requires deep strategic knowledge. Even roulette, while luck-based, rewards understanding of bet types and house edge. If you enjoy learning and improving, table games are far more rewarding.

Bonus Wagering — Where Slots Win Big

Here's where slots have a massive advantage. At virtually every online casino, slots contribute 100% towards wagering requirements. Table games typically contribute only 10–20%, and some are excluded entirely.

If you have a €100 bonus with 35x wagering (€3,500 to clear), playing slots means you need to wager exactly €3,500. Playing blackjack at 10% contribution means you'd need to wager €35,000 — ten times as much.

For bonus players, slots are the only practical option for clearing wagering requirements.

Speed of Play & Bankroll Impact

Slots are fast — a spin takes 3 to 5 seconds, meaning you can easily complete 600–1,000 spins per hour. Table games are slower, with 40–80 hands per hour typical for blackjack.

Faster play means faster losses (and faster wins). Even though a slot might have a lower house edge than you'd expect, the speed of play increases your theoretical hourly loss. A €1 slot at 96% RTP with 600 spins/hour costs you €24/hour on average. Blackjack at €5/hand with 60 hands/hour and 0.5% edge costs you €1.50/hour.

Volatility & Win Potential

Slots offer far greater volatility range. You can choose low-volatility slots for steady, small wins, or high-volatility slots that can pay 10,000x to 50,000x+ your bet in a single spin. Table games are inherently lower volatility — a blackjack hand pays 1:1 (or 3:2 for a natural), and roulette's biggest payout is 35:1.

If you're chasing a life-changing win from a small bet, only slots can deliver that. Progressive jackpot slots have paid out millions from single spins. Table games simply don't offer that kind of upside — but they also don't produce the brutal losing streaks that high-volatility slots can.

Entertainment Value

Slots offer incredible variety — thousands of themes from ancient mythology to TV shows, with elaborate bonus rounds, free spins, cascading reels, and progressive jackpots. They're designed to be visually and aurally stimulating.

Table games offer a different kind of entertainment — strategic depth, decision-making under pressure, and (in live dealer format) social interaction. If you enjoy outsmarting the game, table games provide a richer mental experience.

Bankroll Management — A Different Approach for Each

How you manage your money should change based on what you're playing:

  • Slots: Set a session budget and divide it by your number of desired spins. If you have €50 and want 250 spins, bet €0.20 per spin. Choose low-volatility slots if you want your money to last. Stop when the budget is gone.
  • Table games: Apply the "40 bets" rule — bring enough for at least 40 minimum bets per session. For a €5 blackjack table, that's €200. This gives you enough hands to ride through normal variance.

The key difference: with slots, you can control your exact spend per spin down to the penny. With table games, the pacing is slower but individual bets tend to be higher, so your per-hand bankroll needs to be larger.

The Verdict — Which Is Better for You?

🎰 Choose Slots If You...

  • Want pure entertainment with no strategy required
  • Are clearing a casino bonus or wagering requirements
  • Enjoy chasing big jackpots and high-volatility wins
  • Prefer short sessions with low minimum bets
  • Like variety — thousands of themes and mechanics

🃏 Choose Table Games If You...

  • Want the best possible odds and lowest house edge
  • Enjoy learning strategy and improving over time
  • Prefer a slower, more deliberate pace of play
  • Want your bankroll to last as long as possible
  • Appreciate the social element of live dealer games

The Hybrid Approach — Why Not Both?

Many experienced players use both game types strategically:

  • Clear bonuses with slots — 100% wagering contribution and thousands of eligible games
  • Play table games with deposited funds — better odds and skill-based advantage
  • Use slots for entertainment — when you want to switch off and enjoy the visuals
  • Use table games for serious play — when you want the best return per euro

There's no rule saying you must choose one or the other. The smartest players adapt their game choice to their current goal — bonus clearing, bankroll preservation, jackpot chasing, or pure entertainment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do slots or table games have better odds?▾
Table games generally have better odds. Blackjack with basic strategy has an RTP of 99.5% (0.5% house edge), while the average slot is around 96% (4% house edge). However, some high-RTP slots like Book of 99 (99% RTP) match or beat table game odds. The key difference is that table game odds improve with skill, while slots are entirely luck-based.
Why do slots contribute more to bonus wagering than table games?▾
Casinos restrict table game contributions because skilled players can use low house edge games (especially blackjack) to clear bonuses with minimal risk, essentially guaranteeing profit. Slots have a higher average house edge, making bonus clearance riskier and more profitable for the casino. That's why slots typically count 100% while table games count 10–20% or are excluded.
Can I play both slots and table games to clear a bonus?▾
Yes, you can mix game types, but be aware of the contribution weights. If you wager €100 on slots (100% contribution) and €100 on blackjack (10% contribution), you've effectively cleared €110 of your wagering requirement, not €200. It's usually most efficient to stick to slots until the bonus is cleared, then switch to table games with your own funds.
Are slots rigged compared to table games?▾
No. Both slots and table games at licensed casinos use certified random number generators (RNGs) and are regularly audited by independent testing labs like eCOGRA and iTech Labs. The stated RTP is verified and enforced. Slots aren't rigged — they simply have a higher built-in house edge than most table games.
Which game type is better for a small bankroll?▾
Slots are more accessible with smaller bankrolls thanks to minimum bets as low as €0.10 per spin. Table games typically start at €0.50–€5.00. However, the faster pace of slots means you can burn through a small bankroll quickly. Low-volatility slots at minimum bets, or table games at minimum stakes, are both reasonable options for smaller budgets.

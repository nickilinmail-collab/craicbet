Correct Score Betting in Football: What You Need to Know

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

What is correct score betting?

Correct score betting asks you to predict the exact final scoreline of a football match. A correct score of 1-0, 2-1, 1-1 or any other result is listed at odds reflecting the bookmaker’s assessment of the probability. Because there are many possible outcomes (most markets list 20 to 30 scorelines, plus an ‘any other score’ option), the odds are long and the bookmaker margin across the whole market is significantly higher than the match-result market.

Why correct score carries the highest margin

In a standard match-result market, three outcomes share the bookmaker’s margin. In a correct score market, 20-30 outcomes share it, and the margin applied to each is typically 20-30% above fair probability. This means that on correct score bets, you are paying the highest implicit price of any standard football market. The ‘value’ feel of a 15/1 correct score bet is an illusion: the fair price is often 20/1 or longer.

The most common scorelines and their probability

Poisson distribution models based on average goals scored and conceded suggest that 1-0, 1-1 and 2-1 are consistently the three most common Premier League scorelines, together accounting for roughly 35-40% of results. Even these frequently-occurring scores are priced at 5/1 to 10/1 — below their true probability once bookmaker margin is removed, making them good-looking but systematically poor value.

When to include correct score bets

Correct score betting makes most sense as a small-stake, entertainment-style punt when you have a strong view about a low-scoring match — a 0-0 or 1-0 in a defensive fixture, for example. At 8/1 or longer, a small stake offers a meaningful return if correct. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the bookmaker margin on correct score betting?

Correct score markets typically carry a margin of 20-30% above fair probability, significantly higher than the 5-8% typical on match-result markets. This means the implied probability of all listed scores combined exceeds 100% by a wide margin.

Which correct scores are most common in the Premier League?

1-0, 1-1 and 2-1 are historically the most common Premier League scorelines, together accounting for roughly 35-40% of results. Even so, they are typically priced below their true probability once bookmaker margin is accounted for.

Can correct score bets be included in accumulators?

Yes, some operators allow correct score selections within accumulator bets. The combined margin of an acca built from correct score legs is extremely high — each leg already carries 20-30% excess margin, which then compounds across multiple selections.

How to Reduce Correct Score Risk with Score Casts

A scorecast combines the first goalscorer with the correct score — it pays out at a combined price but only requires both to land. An alternative is:

  • Reverse forecast: backs two scores (e.g. 1-0 and 0-1 both win) — reduces odds but doubles coverage
  • Scorecast: first scorer + correct score combined; implied margin can exceed 40%
  • Exact goals market: betting on the total goals scored (e.g. exactly 2) has a lower margin than specific score

Correct score markets typically carry a 25–40% bookmaker margin — the highest of common football markets. Use sparingly.

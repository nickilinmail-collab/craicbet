Bankroll Management for Punters

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Most punters who go broke do not go broke because their picks were bad. They go broke because their stakes were wrong: too big for the variance their hit rate produces, resized upwards after wins, doubled after losses. Bankroll management is the unglamorous arithmetic that keeps a decent judge of odds in the game long enough for that judgement to matter.

Start with a real bankroll

A bankroll is money set aside purely for betting — an amount you could lose entirely without touching rent, bills or savings. In Ireland that discipline has regulatory backing: GRAI-licensed bookmakers must offer deposit limits, and setting one at the size of your monthly bankroll turns a private rule into an enforced one. If no amount fits that definition, the honest answer is not to bet.

Units: the stake that survives variance

Divide the bankroll into units and stake in units, not euros-that-feel-right. The standard range is 1–2% of bankroll per bet: a €500 bankroll means €5–€10 stakes. That sounds timid until you see the losing runs that normal results produce. A punter who genuinely wins 55% of even-money-ish bets still loses 45% of them — and 0.45 to the power of eight is about 0.17%, which means a run of eight straight losses is expected somewhere in roughly every thousand bets. At 10% stakes that run is ruinous; at 2% it is a bad fortnight.

Flat vs proportional staking

Flat staking bets the same fixed amount every time, recalculated rarely (say monthly). Proportional staking bets a fixed percentage of the current bankroll, so stakes shrink automatically in a downswing and grow in an upswing — mathematically it can never hit zero, which is its great virtue. The practical compromise most disciplined punters use: proportional in spirit, recalculated weekly, never mid-session. What matters far more than the choice between them is that the stake is decided before the bet, by rule, not by confidence.

The maths of a 55% hit rate

Say you hit 55% at average odds of 1.91. Expected value per bet is 0.55 × 1.91 − 1 = +5% — a genuinely strong edge, better than most tipsters ever achieve. Even so, over 100 bets at 1 unit each, swings of 10–15 units either side of expectation are completely normal, and losing months happen to winning punters. If a 5% edge produces that much turbulence, a punter with no edge — which, after the bookmaker’s margin, describes almost everyone — is guaranteed turbulence around a downward slope. Staking rules do not create an edge; they only decide how long you last.

A stop-loss that actually works

Loss-chasing is the single most reliable way to turn a bad day into a destroyed bankroll, so the stop-loss must be set when you are calm and enforced when you are not. The version that works is boring: a fixed session limit (for example 5 units or one evening, whichever comes first), written down, with the next session no sooner than the next day. “Stop when I win it back” is not a stop-loss — it is the chase with paperwork. GRAI-licensed sites must also offer time-outs and self-exclusion through the National Gambling Exclusion Register if limits stop holding.

Honest conclusions

Stake 1–2% of a ring-fenced bankroll, decide stakes by rule before the bet, recalculate on a schedule, and treat a pre-set stop-loss as non-negotiable. Frequently Asked Questions

How much of my bankroll should I stake per bet?

The standard disciplined range is 1–2% per bet, decided by rule before the bet. Larger stakes make ordinary losing runs — which hit even 55% punters — capable of wiping the bankroll out.

Is flat or proportional staking better?

Proportional staking shrinks stakes automatically in downswings and can mathematically never reach zero; flat staking is simpler to run. The choice matters far less than deciding every stake by rule, not by confidence.

What is a realistic stop-loss for a betting session?

A fixed limit set in advance — for example 5 units or one evening, whichever comes first — with the next session no earlier than the next day. GRAI-licensed bookmakers must also offer deposit limits and time-outs that enforce it for you.

Each-Way Terms Explained: What 1/4 Odds and Place Terms Mean

📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

Each-way betting is the most common bet type in Irish horse racing, yet the terminology — "1/4 odds, 3 places" or "1/5 odds, 4 places" — confuses many punters. This guide explains exactly what these terms mean, how the maths works in practice, and when an each-way bet represents good value compared to a straight win bet.

What Are Each-Way Terms?

An each-way bet is two separate bets in one — a win bet and a place bet, each for the same stake. If you place a €10 each-way bet, you are actually staking €20 in total (€10 on the win, €10 on the place).

The place terms define two things: the fraction of the win odds used to settle the place bet, and the number of places that qualify for a payout. These terms are set by the bookmaker for each race and are shown on the race card or bet slip.

For example: "1/4 odds, 3 places" means your place bet pays at one quarter of the win odds, and your horse must finish in the top 3 to collect on the place part.

Worked example — 1/4 odds, 3 places:
Horse priced at 12/1 (13.0 decimal). €5 each-way stake = €10 total.

Win return (if wins): €5 × 13.0 = €65.00
Place return (1/4 of 12/1 = 3/1 = 4.0 decimal): €5 × 4.0 = €20.00
Total if wins: €65 + €20 = €85.00 (profit: €75.00)
Total if places only (2nd or 3rd): €20.00 (profit: €10.00)

1/4 Odds vs 1/5 Odds: The Real Difference

The place fraction is the most important number for each-way value. A higher fraction means a better return on the place part.

1/4 odds (quarter odds): The most common place term in Irish racing. For a horse at 10/1, the place odds are 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5/1 (3.5 decimal).

1/5 odds (fifth odds): Applied in larger fields and big handicap races. For a horse at 10/1, the place odds are 10 ÷ 5 = 2/1 (3.0 decimal). You receive less for the same placing compared to 1/4 terms.

The practical impact is significant. Consider a €5 each-way bet on a 20/1 shot that places (but doesn't win):

  • At 1/4 odds: place odds = 5/1 (6.0 decimal) → return = €5 × 6.0 = €30.00
  • At 1/5 odds: place odds = 4/1 (5.0 decimal) → return = €5 × 5.0 = €25.00

That's a €5 difference on a single place part — multiply this across a race card and the fraction matters. Use the Each-Way Calculator to compare returns under different terms before placing.

How Many Places Are Paid?

The number of places paid depends on the race type, field size and sometimes bookmaker promotion. Standard Irish racing terms are:

Field Size Places Paid Typical Fraction
2–4 runners Win only (no EW) —
5–7 runners 2 places 1/4
8–11 runners 3 places 1/4
12–15 runners 4 places 1/4
16+ runners 5 places 1/4 or 1/5
Handicap 16+ runners 4 places 1/4

These are standard terms. During major festivals such as Cheltenham, Galway and the Curragh Irish Champions Weekend, some bookmakers offer enhanced place terms (e.g. 5 places on a 10-runner race) as a promotional incentive. Always check the actual terms on the bet slip before confirming.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 1/4 odds each way mean?
1/4 odds each way means the place part of your bet is settled at one quarter of the win odds. If your horse is priced at 8/1 (9.0 decimal), the place odds are 2/1 (3.0 decimal). This fraction applies to however many places the race is paying.
How many places are paid in Irish horse racing?
Standard Irish racing terms: 5–7 runners pays 2 places; 8–11 runners pays 3 places; 12–15 runners pays 4 places; 16+ runners pays 5 places. Handicaps with 16+ runners typically pay 4 places at 1/4 odds. These are standard terms — some bookmakers offer enhanced place promotions.
Is each-way betting worth it on a short-priced favourite?
Rarely. If a horse is odds-on (below evens), the place odds at 1/4 or 1/5 will be very small — sometimes as low as 1/10. You're paying twice the stake for a place return that barely covers your outlay. Each-way bets offer best value on horses priced 10/1 or higher, where the place return at 1/4 odds is still a meaningful number.

Self-Exclusion in Ireland: The National Gambling Exclusion Register

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

18+. If gambling is causing you harm right now, skip this page and call the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753 (free, confidential) or visit gamblingcare.ie. This guide covers self-exclusion in Ireland: the new National Gambling Exclusion Register, the tools that work today while it rolls out, and an honest note about why UK GamStop is not the answer here.

What the National Gambling Exclusion Register Is

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) is one of the headline protections of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. One request bars you from every GRAI-licensed operator at once, instead of self-excluding site by site. Licensed operators must connect to the register, refuse service to anyone on it, and stop marketing to them. It is run by the GRAI, with details via grai.ie.

Where the Rollout Stands

The register is provided for in the Act but was not yet operating as of late 2026 — the GRAI's licensing phases come first, and our daily tracker logs each step. A second limit follows from licensing itself: the GRAI licenses remote betting only so far, so even a live register covers licensed bookmakers — not the offshore-licensed casino sites that remain outside the Irish system until casino licensing opens in 2027–2028.

Does GamStop Work in Ireland?

No — and this trips up a lot of Irish searchers. GamStop covers operators licensed in Great Britain, for customers there. An Irish account with an Irish-licensed bookmaker is outside it, and GamStop registration is tied to GB residence. The NGER is Ireland's equivalent; until it operates, use the tools below rather than assuming a UK scheme protects you.

How to Self-Exclude Today, Step by Step

1. Per-site exclusion: every licensed bookmaker and reputable casino offers account-level self-exclusion in its responsible gambling settings — and under the Act a licensed operator must close your account on request. Do this first on every site where you hold an account.
2. Block the money: several Irish banks now offer gambling blocks on cards. Bank of Ireland has a gambling block feature, and Revolut's app includes one with a cooling-off delay before it can be switched off; ask your own bank what it offers. Blocking the payment rail covers sites you have forgotten about.
3. Block the devices: software like Gamban and BetBlocker works at device level and — unlike any register — also blocks unlicensed sites.
4. Join the NGER once it opens: one request, every licensed operator, with marketing cut off too.

NGER Timeline: When Will It Launch?

The National Gambling Exclusion Register is a live-or-die feature of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, but its launch depends on the GRAI licensing rollout. Here is what we know as of November 2026:

  • July 2026: GRAI begins issuing remote betting licences — the register's first scope will be these bookmakers
  • 2027–2028 (expected): Casino (gaming) licences open, expanding the register's reach to online casinos
  • Register launch: Likely tied to the first batch of casino licences — the GRAI has not confirmed a date as of November 2026

Until the register covers online casinos, the most effective protection for Irish players who want to block casino access is a bank gambling block combined with device-level software like Gamban or BetBlocker. These two tools together block the payment rail and the device access — they are the practical alternative to a register that does not yet exist.

How to Set Up a Bank Gambling Block in Ireland

Bank of Ireland: Log into the mobile app, go to Manage → Gambling Block, and toggle it on. You can remove it in the app after a 24-hour cooling-off period. The block applies to debit cards; credit card gambling is already banned under Irish banking rules.

AIB: Contact AIB customer service (branch or phone) to request a gambling merchant block on your account. AIB is rolling out in-app controls — check the app settings under Card Controls.

Revolut: Settings → Security → Merchant controls → Gambling. Toggle off. Revolut adds a 48-hour cooling-off delay before you can remove the block, which is exactly the kind of friction that effective self-exclusion tools should include.

N26: Card settings → Block gambling merchants. Available in the mobile app.

Why Layers Beat Any Single Tool

Each layer has a gap — a register misses offshore sites, blocking software misses a friend's phone, a bank block misses an e-wallet. Together they put real friction between impulse and bet, which is what self-exclusion is for. Our responsible gambling guide covers the warning signs and every tool available to Irish players.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I self-exclude from all betting sites in Ireland?

Once the National Gambling Exclusion Register operates, one request covers every GRAI-licensed operator. Until then, layer per-site exclusion, a bank gambling block and device software like Gamban or BetBlocker. Use all three — each covers what the others miss.

Does GamStop cover Ireland?

No — GamStop covers operators licensed in Great Britain. It has no effect on Irish-licensed sites or on the offshore casinos that most Irish players currently use. Ireland's equivalent is the National Gambling Exclusion Register under the 2024 Act, which is not yet live.

Is the National Gambling Exclusion Register live yet?

Not as of November 2026 — it is provided for in the Act and arrives with the licensing rollout. Casino licences (which will expand the register's reach) are expected from 2027–2028. Check grai.ie, or our daily tracker, for status.

Is BetBlocker really free?

Yes. BetBlocker (betblocker.org) is a free, charity-funded app that blocks 19,000+ gambling sites at device level. It supports a time-lock feature so you cannot remove it immediately on impulse. Gamban costs around €4.49/month but covers more sites (50,000+) and is tighter against workarounds.

Can I remove a bank gambling block whenever I want?

Most banks build in a cooling-off delay: Revolut requires 48 hours; Bank of Ireland has a 24-hour window. This delay is the most important feature of any bank block — it removes the ability to unblock impulsively. Check your bank's exact terms before activating.

How to Self-Exclude from All Betting Sites Ireland

Ireland's national self-exclusion register is managed by the GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland). When GRAI's interoperable exclusion register is fully operational, a single self-exclusion will cover all GRAI-licensed operators. Until then, you must self-exclude individually with each operator using their responsible gambling settings — typically found in Account > Responsible Gambling > Self-Exclusion.

Self-exclusion periods in Ireland: minimum 6 months, options typically 1 year, 3 years, 5 years, or permanent. Permanent self-exclusion cannot be reversed. During the exclusion period, the operator must refuse you service and return your balance.

GRAI Self-Exclusion Register Ireland How It Works

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, GRAI is building a national self-exclusion register that will allow Irish residents to exclude from all GRAI-licensed operators with a single registration. As of late 2026, the register is in development — a timeline for full launch has not been officially confirmed. Meanwhile, GRAI has an interim complaints and exclusion process — contact grai.ie for the current procedure.

How to Set Deposit Limits on Betting Site Ireland

All GRAI-licensed betting sites must offer deposit limits. To set one: go to Account > Responsible Gambling > Deposit Limits, enter your daily/weekly/monthly limit, and confirm. Reductions take effect immediately. Increases require a cooling-off period (typically 24–72 hours) to prevent impulsive limit raises. GRAI-licensed operators cannot process deposits above your confirmed limit.

How to Request Gambling Treatment Ireland Free

Free gambling treatment is available through the HSE's National Gambling Service — a dedicated, free-of-charge counselling service for people affected by gambling problems. Referrals can be self-made (no GP required): contact the Gambling Awareness Trust at 1800 753 753 for a referral. The National Gambling Service offers one-to-one counselling and group support across Ireland. Waiting times vary by location; online and phone counselling options are available with shorter waits.

Self-Exclusion in Ireland: The National Gambling Exclusion Register

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

18+. If gambling is causing you harm right now, skip this page and call the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753 (free, confidential) or visit gamblingcare.ie. This guide covers self-exclusion in Ireland: the new National Gambling Exclusion Register, the tools that work today while it rolls out, and an honest note about why UK GamStop is not the answer here.

What the National Gambling Exclusion Register Is

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) is one of the headline protections of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. One request bars you from every GRAI-licensed operator at once, instead of self-excluding site by site. Licensed operators must connect to the register, refuse service to anyone on it, and stop marketing to them. It is run by the GRAI, with details via grai.ie.

Where the Rollout Stands

The register is provided for in the Act but was not yet operating as of late 2026 — the GRAI's licensing phases come first, and our daily tracker logs each step. A second limit follows from licensing itself: the GRAI licenses remote betting only so far, so even a live register covers licensed bookmakers — not the offshore-licensed casino sites that remain outside the Irish system until casino licensing opens in 2027–2028.

Does GamStop Work in Ireland?

No — and this trips up a lot of Irish searchers. GamStop covers operators licensed in Great Britain, for customers there. An Irish account with an Irish-licensed bookmaker is outside it, and GamStop registration is tied to GB residence. The NGER is Ireland's equivalent; until it operates, use the tools below rather than assuming a UK scheme protects you.

How to Self-Exclude Today, Step by Step

1. Per-site exclusion: every licensed bookmaker and reputable casino offers account-level self-exclusion in its responsible gambling settings — and under the Act a licensed operator must close your account on request. Do this first on every site where you hold an account.
2. Block the money: several Irish banks now offer gambling blocks on cards. Bank of Ireland has a gambling block feature, and Revolut's app includes one with a cooling-off delay before it can be switched off; ask your own bank what it offers. Blocking the payment rail covers sites you have forgotten about.
3. Block the devices: software like Gamban and BetBlocker works at device level and — unlike any register — also blocks unlicensed sites.
4. Join the NGER once it opens: one request, every licensed operator, with marketing cut off too.

Why Layers Beat Any Single Tool

Each layer has a gap — a register misses offshore sites, blocking software misses a friend's phone, a bank block misses an e-wallet. Together they put real friction between impulse and bet, which is what self-exclusion is for. Our responsible gambling guide covers the warning signs and every tool available to Irish players.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I self-exclude from all betting sites in Ireland?

Once the National Gambling Exclusion Register operates, one request covers every GRAI-licensed operator. Until then, layer per-site exclusion, a bank gambling block and device software like Gamban or BetBlocker.

Does GamStop cover Ireland?

No — GamStop covers operators licensed in Great Britain. Ireland's equivalent is the National Gambling Exclusion Register under the 2024 Act.

Is the National Gambling Exclusion Register live yet?

Not as of late 2026 — it is provided for in the Act and arrives with the licensing rollout. Check grai.ie, or our daily tracker, for status.

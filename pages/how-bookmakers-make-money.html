How Bookmakers Make Money: Margins, Overrounds and the Vig Explained

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 4 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

The Overround: Bookmaker’s Built-In Edge

The overround (also called the vig, juice or margin) is the mechanism by which bookmakers guarantee a theoretical profit regardless of which outcome occurs. In a true 50/50 market, both sides should be priced at 2.0 (evens). A bookmaker might price both at 1.91 instead. The implied probability of 1.91 is 52.4% (1 ÷ 1.91). Two sides at 52.4% sum to 104.8%, not 100%. That 4.8% excess is the overround — the bookmaker’s built-in edge before the first bet is placed.

On a three-way football market (1X2), overrounds of 108–112% are common. This means for every €100 theoretically bet across all outcomes, the bookmaker retains approximately €8–€12 in expectation, distributing the rest in winnings.

How Margins Differ by Sport and Market

Margins are not uniform across all sports and markets. Liquid markets with many bettors and clear pricing references — Premier League 1X2, major tennis match winner — tend to carry lower margins of 3–6%. Less liquid or more complex markets carry higher margins: correct score betting can exceed 15%, accumulator markets on minor leagues can carry overrounds above 120%.

Horse racing is structured differently: starting price (SP) markets work on a tote-style pool in which the total payout is determined by what bettors collectively stake, with the bookmaker taking a levy. Fixed-odds horse racing margins also vary, often running at 115–130% across a full field.

Balancing the Book: What Bookmakers Actually Do

The overround provides theoretical profit, but only if bookmakers receive proportional action on all outcomes. In practice, money comes in unevenly — more bets on the favourite, less on the draw. Bookmakers adjust prices in response: if too much money is on Team A, they shorten Team A’s price (making it less attractive) and lengthen Team B’s or the draw (making those more attractive to bring money back).

This is called book balancing or liabilities management. A well-balanced book profits regardless of outcome because the overround is distributed; a poorly balanced book can lose money if a heavily-backed outcome wins. Large bookmakers use sophisticated algorithmic tools and human traders to manage liabilities across thousands of markets simultaneously.

What This Means for You as a Bettor

The overround is the first thing to understand about betting: it is a tax on each bet you place. A 5% margin on a €10 bet costs approximately €0.50 in expected value before you even consider whether your selection is correct. Over thousands of bets, the margin compounds. This is why beating the market over the long term requires both identifying genuine edges in selection AND comparing odds across bookmakers to minimise the margin paid.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an overround in betting?

The overround is the sum of all implied probabilities in a betting market, expressed as a percentage. A market with 108% implied probability has an 8% overround, which is the bookmaker's theoretical profit margin built into the odds.

Do all bookmakers have the same margin?

No. Margins vary between bookmakers and between markets. Comparing odds across multiple GRAI-licensed bookmakers using an odds comparison tool is the simplest way to minimise the margin you pay on each bet.

Can I beat the bookmaker's edge?

It is very difficult. The overround means you must win significantly more than your expected rate just to break even. Long-term profitable bettors use a combination of genuine analytical edge, best-price seeking and disciplined bankroll management.

What Bookmakers Do With Your Stake

  • Every market's implied probabilities sum to more than 100% — that overround is the margin
  • A 5% margin on a 1X2 market means for every €100 bet, the bookmaker keeps ~€5 on average
  • High-margin markets: correct score, scorecast, first goalscorer, novelty bets
  • Low-margin markets: match winner, Asian handicap, exchange prices (Betfair: 2–5% commission)
  • Accumulators multiply the per-leg margin: a 5-leg acca at 5% per leg has a combined margin of ~23%

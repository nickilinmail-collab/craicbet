Autumn Internationals Betting Ireland 2026: Ireland's Test Schedule

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 Updated 10 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

The Autumn Internationals — also known as the November Tests or Autumn Nations Series — are Ireland's most important rugby fixtures outside of the Six Nations and Rugby World Cup. Held over three or four weekends in November, they bring the southern hemisphere's top nations to the Aviva Stadium in Dublin. This guide explains how to bet on the Autumn Internationals, what the key markets are, and what to watch for in 2026.

Ireland's Autumn Internationals in Context

The November Test window is typically the last major rugby action before the Six Nations begins in February. For Ireland, it offers a final opportunity to bed in combinations, test their depth against world-class opposition, and build momentum heading into the annual Six Nations campaign. Ireland's home record at the Aviva during November Tests in recent years has been among the best of any northern hemisphere nation.

The Autumn Nations Series (the branded format involving England, France, Ireland, Scotland, Wales, and Italy) gives the event additional structure. Some fixtures are more commercially significant, and market depth and coverage from bookmakers reflects this — Ireland versus New Zealand or South Africa at the Aviva will be one of the most heavily bet rugby fixtures of the year globally.

Unlike the Six Nations, the Autumn Internationals do not carry a formal tournament structure for overall winner. Each match is a standalone Test, which means outright betting focuses on individual match winner, handicap, and totals markets rather than a tournament winner market.

Autumn Internationals Betting Markets

Match winner is the most popular market. For Ireland versus top southern hemisphere opponents at the Aviva, the match winner is typically priced close to evens or with Ireland as slight favourites. Against tier 2 nations, Ireland are heavy match winner favourites.

Handicap (points spread) is excellent for Ireland matches against tier 2 opposition. When Ireland are priced at 1/8 or shorter on the match winner, the handicap market becomes the more competitive and interesting bet. For top-tier opponents, the handicap line is often set within a few points either way, making it a genuinely two-way market.

Total points over/under: Autumn Internationals between top nations tend to be well-contested and can produce a wide range of scores. Ireland home games against New Zealand or South Africa have historically produced competitive, lower-scoring matches rather than high-scoring blowouts. The total points line for a top-tier Autumn International at the Aviva is typically set around 44–54 points.

Try scorer markets: Available and popular for Autumn International fixtures. As with all rugby, margins run 15–25% across the try scorer field. Use as entertainment rather than a systematic value bet.

Betting on Ireland Against Southern Hemisphere Giants

Ireland versus New Zealand and Ireland versus South Africa at the Aviva are among the most bet rugby matches of any November. Key considerations: Ireland's home record against these sides has significantly improved in recent years; both southern hemisphere sides arrive mid-European-season and their preparation time is limited; Ireland will typically be in better match practice having been in the URC.

These factors have historically contributed to Ireland's strong home record against top southern hemisphere opposition and explain why bookmakers have moved their pricing closer to evens for these fixtures. Always check whether the southern hemisphere team is playing with a full-strength squad — mid-tour rotation or player resting significantly affects the implied probability.

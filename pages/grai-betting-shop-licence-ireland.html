GRAI In-Person Betting Licence: What Irish Betting Shops Need to Know

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

If you use your local betting shop, something important is changing on 1 December 2026. That is the date by which every in-person betting operator in Ireland must hold a valid GRAI licence. The old Revenue Commissioners licences — in place under the Betting Act 1931 for nearly a century — expire on that date. Operating without a GRAI licence after 1 December is a criminal offence.

What Is Changing for Betting Shops

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 replaces the entire framework under which Irish betting shops have operated since 1931. For the first time, a single dedicated regulator — the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI), established formally on 5 March 2025 — oversees all gambling in Ireland, retail and online.

Online (remote) operators already made this transition: GRAI issued the first remote betting licences on 1 July 2026 after opening applications in February. In-person operators are next. Their transition deadline is 1 December 2026.

Applications are managed through the GRAI's new operator portal. Shops that have applied and are awaiting licence approval should seek confirmation from the GRAI about their status before the deadline.

Deadline: 1 December 2026. Every in-person betting operator in Ireland must hold a valid GRAI licence from this date. Revenue Commissioners licences issued under the Betting Act 1931 expire on 1 December 2026. No licence = criminal offence.

From the Revenue Commissioners to the GRAI

For 95 years, Irish betting shops were licensed by the Revenue Commissioners under the Betting Act 1931 — a law designed for an era of on-course bookmaking. The new framework under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 replaces this entirely with a purpose-built regulatory regime that covers player protections, responsible gambling tools, advertising standards and enforcement.

Note: Section 85(1)(c) of the Act — which would allow combined in-person and remote betting licences — has not yet been commenced. Operators who want both in-person and remote licences must currently apply for each separately.

Criminal Penalties for Non-Compliance

Operating without a GRAI licence from 1 December 2026 is not a regulatory technicality — it is a criminal offence under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The penalties are severe:

Unlicensed operation after 1 December 2026:
  • Fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover (whichever is greater)
  • Criminal prosecution with prison sentences of up to 8 years
  • Reputational consequences and potential personal liability for directors

The enforcement framework was brought into force by S.I. No. 31/2026 on 5 February 2026. The GRAI has the power to investigate, issue notices of non-compliance and pursue criminal prosecution.

For Punters: What This Means for You

If you use a physical betting shop, the transition should be largely seamless from your perspective — but there are a few things worth knowing:

  • GRAI licence display: From 1 December, legitimately licensed shops should be able to show their GRAI licence. If a shop cannot, treat that as a warning sign.
  • Consumer protections apply: The same GRAI licence conditions that apply to online operators — mandatory age verification, credit card ban, account closure on request, effective deposit limits — apply to in-person operators too.
  • Right to complain: If a licensed betting shop fails to meet its obligations to you, you can complain to the GRAI under Part 7 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. See our guide to making a GRAI complaint.
  • Self-exclusion: The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) covers in-person as well as remote operators. If you self-exclude via the GRAI, all licensed betting shops must refuse your custom.

Frequently Asked Questions

When must Irish betting shops get a GRAI licence?
In-person betting operators in Ireland must hold a valid GRAI licence from 1 December 2026. Their existing Revenue Commissioners licences expire on that date. Operating without a GRAI licence after that date is a criminal offence.
How many betting shops are left in Ireland?
According to GRAI data, there were 684 retail betting shops in Ireland at the end of 2025, down from 1,385 in 2008 — a decline of more than 50% over 17 years.
What happens if a betting shop operates without a GRAI licence after 1 December 2026?
Unlicensed operation under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is a criminal offence. Penalties can include fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover, and prison sentences of up to 8 years.

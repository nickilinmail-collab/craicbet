Is Online Gambling Legal in Ireland? — Your Complete 2026 Guide

📝 CraicBet Team 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 12 min read

Yes, online gambling is legal in Ireland. Irish players can legally bet online at licensed operators, and as of 2026 the industry is regulated by a dedicated authority. But the legal landscape has a long, complex history. This guide covers everything you need to know — from century-old legislation to the brand-new GRAI.

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Online gambling is legal and regulated in Ireland
  • The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) launched on 1 July 2026
  • Gambling winnings are completely tax-free for Irish players
  • You must be 18 or older to gamble
  • Sports betting has long been licensed; casino-specific licensing is expected Q4 2026

A Brief History of Irish Gambling Law

Ireland's gambling laws have evolved slowly over the decades. For most of the 20th century, the country relied on legislation that was never designed for the internet age.

The Betting Act 1931

The Betting Act 1931 was Ireland's first major piece of gambling legislation. It established a licensing system for bookmakers operating in physical shops across the country. Under this Act, bookmakers needed a licence from the Revenue Commissioners to operate legally. While the Act was groundbreaking for its time, it was written decades before the internet existed and made no provision for online gambling.

The Gaming and Lotteries Act 1956

The Gaming and Lotteries Act 1956 addressed casino-style gaming and lotteries. It permitted certain types of gaming at licensed premises and regulated lotteries and amusement halls. However, casino gaming in the traditional sense (large-scale, purpose-built casinos) was never fully legalised under this Act. Private members' clubs filled the gap for decades, operating in a legal grey area.

The Betting (Amendment) Act 2015

Recognising the explosive growth of online gambling, the Betting (Amendment) Act 2015 was a significant step forward. It extended the licensing regime to remote bookmakers and betting intermediaries operating online. For the first time, online operators offering services to Irish customers needed a licence from the Revenue Commissioners. This brought tax revenue into the State and gave players a basic level of consumer protection.

The Gambling Regulation Bill

After years of discussion and multiple false starts, the Gambling Regulation Bill was finally passed to modernise Ireland's outdated gambling framework. This landmark legislation aimed to consolidate and replace the patchwork of ageing laws with a single, comprehensive regulatory regime covering all forms of gambling — online and offline, betting and gaming.

What Is the GRAI?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) was officially established on 1 July 2026. It is Ireland's dedicated, independent gambling regulator — the first of its kind in the country.

🏛️ What the GRAI Does

  • Issues and enforces gambling licences for operators serving Irish customers
  • Sets standards for player protection, fair gaming, and responsible gambling
  • Monitors compliance and investigates complaints
  • Regulates advertising of gambling products in Ireland
  • Maintains a national self-exclusion register
  • Works with international regulators to tackle unlicensed operators

The GRAI represents a sea change for Irish gambling. Before its establishment, Ireland was one of the few EU countries without a dedicated gambling regulator. Players relied on the protections offered by overseas licences (MGA, UKGC, Gibraltar) rather than an Irish-specific framework.

Tax-Free Winnings for Irish Players

One of the biggest advantages for Irish gamblers: all gambling winnings are completely tax-free. Whether you win €10 or €10 million, you keep every cent.

This applies to all forms of gambling — sports betting, casino games, poker, lottery, and more. Ireland taxes the operators (a 2% turnover tax on betting), not the players. There is no capital gains tax, income tax, or any other levy on your winnings.

💰 Quick Tax Summary

  • Income tax on winnings: None
  • Capital gains tax on winnings: None
  • Betting duty for players: None
  • Reporting requirements: None (for recreational players)

Note: If gambling is your primary source of income, Revenue may classify it differently. Consult a tax adviser if you are a professional gambler.

Age Requirement — 18+ Only

The legal gambling age in Ireland is 18 years old. This applies to all forms of gambling, online and offline. Licensed operators must verify your age before allowing you to deposit or play. Providing false information about your age is a criminal offence.

The GRAI has strengthened age verification requirements. Operators must now use electronic age verification systems and may request photo ID (passport, driving licence) before processing your first withdrawal.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is online gambling legal in Ireland?▾
Yes, online gambling is legal in Ireland. Irish players can legally bet at licensed operators. Sports betting has been formally licensed for years, and the GRAI is now introducing a comprehensive licensing framework for all forms of online gambling, including casinos and poker.
Do I pay tax on gambling winnings in Ireland?▾
No. Gambling winnings are completely tax-free in Ireland. There is no income tax, capital gains tax, or betting duty applied to player winnings. Ireland taxes the operators instead via a 2% turnover tax.
What is the GRAI?▾
The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) is Ireland's dedicated gambling regulator, established on 1 July 2026. It issues licences, sets standards for player protection, regulates advertising, maintains a self-exclusion register, and enforces compliance among operators serving Irish customers.
Is it safe to gamble online in Ireland?▾
It can be, provided you play at properly licensed operators. Look for casinos licensed by reputable authorities such as the GRAI, MGA (Malta Gaming Authority), UKGC (UK Gambling Commission), or Gibraltar. These licences ensure fair gaming, segregated player funds, and access to complaints procedures. Always check the operator's licence before depositing.
What age do I need to be to gamble in Ireland?▾
You must be at least 18 years old to gamble in Ireland. This applies to all forms of gambling — online and offline, including sports betting, casino games, lottery, and poker. Operators are required to verify your age electronically.
Where can I get help with problem gambling?▾
Several free, confidential services are available. Call the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753, visit gamblingcare.ie for free counselling, or contact Gamblers Anonymous Ireland. You can also read our Responsible Gambling guide for more resources and self-assessment tools.

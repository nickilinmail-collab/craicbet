Fairyhouse Racecourse Betting: Irish Grand National and Key Meetings

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

About Fairyhouse

Fairyhouse Racecourse is situated in Co. Meath, approximately 25 kilometres north-west of Dublin city centre. It is one of Ireland's most historically significant racecourses, with a racing heritage stretching back to the nineteenth century. The track is best known as the home of the Irish Grand National, run on Easter Monday each year, and is the centrepiece of Fairyhouse's spring Easter Festival.

Unlike the flat-only Curragh or the National Hunt-only Punchestown, Fairyhouse is a mixed track that stages both flat racing and National Hunt racing. Its programme is dominated by jump racing and its Easter Festival, but flat meetings take place during the summer months. For punters, this mix gives Fairyhouse a broader seasonal appeal than many specialist venues.

The racecourse is known for its atmosphere on big occasions, particularly Easter Monday when the Irish Grand National draws large crowds and significant betting volumes. The meeting has a family-friendly, festive atmosphere that makes it one of the most popular social events on the Irish racing calendar as well as a serious betting occasion.

The Irish Grand National

The Irish Grand National is run at Fairyhouse on Easter Monday and is Ireland's most prestigious and best-known handicap chase. It is run over approximately 3 miles and 5 furlongs and typically attracts fields of 20 or more runners, with horses carrying a wide range of weights under the handicap system. The race has been run at Fairyhouse since 1870, giving it a heritage that places it among the oldest major races in Irish racing.

As a betting event, the Irish Grand National is a handicap chase with a large field, which creates a genuinely competitive market with no standout favourite in most years. The combination of a staying distance, a large field, variable Easter ground conditions and the handicapping system means upsets are common, and each-way value can be found at much longer prices than in Pattern or Graded races.

Key factors for punters to assess before betting the Irish Grand National:

  • Weight: The handicapper's assessment is central in a handicap race. Horses carrying big weights (above 11 stone 7 lbs) face a significant task over the staying trip at Fairyhouse, historically.
  • Going: Easter ground in Co. Meath can range from soft to good depending on the spring weather. The undulating Fairyhouse circuit is more demanding on soft ground, and stamina becomes an even greater premium. Check going reports in the days before the race.
  • Festival fitness: Many Irish Grand National runners have already had a run at the Cheltenham Festival or at Leopardstown's Dublin Racing Festival. The form and fitness level of each runner's most recent race is an important form indicator.
  • Field size and each-way terms: With fields regularly exceeding 20 runners, most GRAI-licensed bookmakers pay each-way on the top 4 or 5 places. Confirm the place terms before betting — they vary by bookmaker.

Easter Festival at Fairyhouse

The Easter Festival at Fairyhouse runs over the Easter weekend, typically three days including Easter Saturday, Easter Sunday and Easter Monday. The Irish Grand National on the Monday is the showpiece, but the full Festival programme includes Graded races across both days that make the entire weekend a significant betting occasion.

The Easter Saturday card at Fairyhouse regularly features Grade 1 novice hurdles and chases, providing a later-season opportunity for novices that may have been aimed at this meeting rather than Cheltenham or Punchestown. For punters, the novice races at the Easter Festival can offer generous prices on horses that trainers have deliberately kept back from the spring festivals. These horses often arrive fresh and lightly-raced, which is an advantage on a testing track like Fairyhouse.

The Festival weekend coincides with the broader Easter bank holiday, which means significant betting volumes across all Irish and British racing. Track Characteristics

Fairyhouse is a right-handed, undulating track. The course is not flat — it rises and falls across the circuit in a way that tests the stamina and jumping ability of National Hunt horses. The undulating nature of the track is a key factor differentiating it from a flatter galloping track like Leopardstown.

For jump racing, the Fairyhouse track suits horses that jump accurately and efficiently. The fences require respect: on undulating ground, horses jumping downhill or uphill fences face different physical demands than on a flat circuit. Horses that have raced at Fairyhouse before and demonstrated they handle the undulations well are worth noting.

The flat course at Fairyhouse is similarly undulating, which means that horses with proven form on undulating tracks — rather than flat galloping tracks like The Curragh — have an advantage. For flat punters, Fairyhouse form does not always translate directly from or to Curragh form, so track suitability is worth factoring into assessments.

Ground at Fairyhouse in autumn and winter tends to be yielding to soft or soft. The Easter Festival can produce a range of conditions from good to soft depending on the year. Summer flat meetings typically see better ground. As always with Irish racing, checking the official going descriptions in the days before any meeting is essential before committing to a bet.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Irish Grand National at Fairyhouse?

The Irish Grand National is run at Fairyhouse on Easter Monday each year. It is Ireland's most famous handicap chase and attracts a large field of runners over approximately 3 miles and 5 furlongs.

Is Fairyhouse right-handed or left-handed?

Fairyhouse is a right-handed track. It is an undulating, mixed flat and jump course in Co. Meath and is considered a testing circuit for National Hunt horses.

Does Fairyhouse host flat racing as well as jump racing?

Yes. Fairyhouse is a mixed track that stages both flat and National Hunt racing. Its profile is dominated by jump racing and the Easter Festival, but flat meetings are also held there during the summer months.

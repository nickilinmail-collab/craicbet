Kalshi Ireland 2026: GRAI Status & What Irish Users Need to Know

Kalshi is a US-based prediction market regulated by the CFTC (US Commodity Futures Trading Commission). It attracted attention as a legally regulated alternative to crypto-based prediction markets like Polymarket. However, in 2026, Kalshi is reported to have restricted Irish users following GRAI pressure — alongside similar action against Polymarket. Here is what that means for Irish users and what GRAI-licensed alternatives exist.

What Is Kalshi?

Kalshi is a US-based prediction market operated by KalshiEX LLC. Unlike Polymarket, which operates as a decentralised, crypto-based peer-to-peer platform, Kalshi is a regulated exchange. It holds a licence from the CFTC (US Commodity Futures Trading Commission) as a Designated Contract Market (DCM) — the same regulatory category as the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) and other major US financial derivatives exchanges.

This regulatory status was significant in the US context. Kalshi spent years in legal proceedings to establish its right to offer event contracts — contracts whose outcome is tied to real-world events such as election results — under CFTC oversight. It won that right, distinguishing itself from prediction markets that operate in informal or unregulated spaces.

Kalshi’s markets cover a range of event types, including political and electoral outcomes, economic indicators, weather events, and other real-world questions. Users trade yes/no contracts on whether a specified event will occur, with positions denominated in US dollars. Kalshi is not a bookmaker and does not operate as a traditional fixed-odds betting platform.

Why Did Kalshi Restrict Irish Users?

According to reporting by GamblingInsider, Kalshi restricted Irish users in 2026 following GRAI pressure — reported as part of the same enforcement wave that saw Polymarket geo-block Irish users. The GRAI, established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, began enforcing remote operator compliance from 1 July 2026.

Under that Act, any operator offering gambling products to Irish customers — broadly defined — is required to hold a GRAI remote betting licence. Kalshi does not hold such a licence. Despite its CFTC-regulated status in the United States, that regulation has no bearing on Kalshi’s obligations under Irish law. The CFTC authorises Kalshi to operate as a US financial derivatives exchange; it does not authorise Kalshi to target Irish customers under Ireland’s gambling regulatory framework.

Rather than seeking a GRAI licence — a process that may face conceptual difficulties given that Kalshi’s CFTC model is not easily mapped to traditional bookmaker licensing — Kalshi is reported to have restricted access for Irish users.

Does Kalshi Have a GRAI Licence?

No. As of November 2026, Kalshi does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. It has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence. You can verify the current position at grai.ie.

Kalshi’s CFTC Designated Contract Market licence is a US federal authorisation that covers KalshiEX LLC’s operation as a financial exchange in the United States. It is a form of financial market regulation, not gambling regulation, and it does not extend to covering Irish users under any Irish law or regulatory framework. The two regulatory regimes are entirely separate.

Whether Kalshi could apply for a GRAI licence is an open question. The GRAI licensing framework is designed for traditional bookmakers; a CFTC-regulated DCM operating event contracts is structurally different, and it is not clear how the GRAI would categorise Kalshi’s offering. As of November 2026, no public application had been reported.

As with all licence questions: always verify at grai.ie before depositing with any operator. This status may change.

GRAI-Licensed Alternatives for Irish Users

The event markets that attracted Irish users to Kalshi — elections, political outcomes, economic questions, and major real-world events — have equivalents in the specials markets offered by GRAI-licensed bookmakers. While these markets differ in structure (fixed-odds rather than exchange-style contracts), they provide regulated access to similar outcomes for Irish users.

GRAI-licensed bookmakers operating in Ireland typically offer specials markets covering:

  • Elections and political events — general election outright markets, by-elections, leadership contests
  • Major sports specials — next manager markets, transfer markets, awards (Ballon d’Or, Sports Personality of the Year)
  • Entertainment specials — awards shows, reality TV outcomes, television events
  • Topical specials — some operators offer seasonal or news-driven specials during major events

These markets do not replicate Kalshi’s full depth or its exchange-style structure, but they represent the most straightforward regulated alternative for Irish users seeking to speculate on event outcomes.

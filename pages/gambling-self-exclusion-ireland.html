Gambling Self-Exclusion Ireland: How the National Register Works

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read
Need help now? If you are struggling with gambling, call the Gambling Awareness Trust free helpline: 1800 753 753 (Monday to Sunday) or visit gamblingcare.ie. This page is an information resource; it is not a substitute for support.

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced Ireland's first centralised self-exclusion system: the National Gambling Exclusion Register, managed by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI). When fully live, a single registration will block you from all GRAI-licensed gambling operators simultaneously — a fundamental shift from the previous system where you had to contact each operator individually. This guide explains what the register is, what rights it gives you, its current status, and what tools are available right now while the register rolls out.

What Is Self-Exclusion and Why It Matters

Self-exclusion is a voluntary, formal decision to stop yourself from gambling by asking operators to block your access. It is not a ban imposed on you — it is a tool you use on yourself, and it is one of the most effective harm-reduction measures available to anyone who feels their gambling has become problematic.

Before the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, self-exclusion in Ireland worked on a brand-by-brand basis: you had to contact each bookmaker or casino individually, and there was no mechanism to prevent you from simply opening an account elsewhere. The result was that someone who genuinely wanted to stop gambling faced a significant practical barrier: tracking down every operator and completing a separate exclusion process for each one, while remaining vulnerable to opening new accounts with operators they had not yet contacted.

The National Gambling Exclusion Register changes this architecture entirely. One registration, one database, one legal obligation on every licensed operator to check it. It is modelled in part on the UK's GamStop scheme but with an important difference: in Ireland, checking the national register is a legal requirement under licence conditions, not a voluntary commitment.

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGEI)

The National Gambling Exclusion Register — sometimes referred to by the GRAI as the NGEI — is established under Part 5 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The GRAI is both the administrator and the enforcement body for the register.

When fully operational, the register will work as follows:

  • You register once on the national register, providing identity information to allow operators to match your record.
  • Your exclusion is immediately visible to all GRAI-licensed operators in Ireland.
  • Every licensed operator is legally required to check the register before opening any new account. If your name appears as excluded, they must refuse to open an account for you.
  • Operators must also check the register regularly against existing accounts and suspend any account that belongs to a registered excluded person.
  • Excluded persons must not be sent marketing material — including bonuses, free bet offers, or promotional emails — by any licensed operator.
  • Minimum exclusion periods and the process for applying to be removed from the register will be set out in GRAI regulations.

Important: Current Status as of November 2026

⚠ Honest status update: The National Gambling Exclusion Register is enacted in law but has not yet been fully commenced as of November 2026. The GRAI is building the technical infrastructure and regulatory framework. The register does not yet accept public registrations. We will update this page when it goes live. In the meantime, see the section below on what tools are available right now.

This is an important distinction. "Enacted" means the legal basis exists and the GRAI has the mandate. "Commenced" means the system is live and accepting users. The register is in the first category, not yet the second. The GRAI has not publicly committed to a specific go-live date as of this writing.

How Operators Must Comply

Under GRAI licence conditions, every GRAI-licensed bookmaker and gambling operator has specific obligations regarding self-exclusion:

Individual Operator Self-Exclusion (Live Now)

All GRAI-licensed operators must currently offer their own self-exclusion mechanism as a licence condition. This is not optional. When you self-exclude from a GRAI-licensed bookmaker:

  • The exclusion must take effect immediately.
  • The operator must stop sending you all marketing communications immediately.
  • The operator must not allow you to reopen an account for the duration of your exclusion period.
  • The operator must return any remaining balance to you after verification.
  • If you approach the operator to end an exclusion early, the operator is permitted (and encouraged by GRAI guidance) to impose a cooling-off period before reinstating access.

To self-exclude from a GRAI-licensed operator right now, go to the responsible gambling section of the site (usually in the account settings or footer links) and follow the self-exclusion process. You can also call the operator's customer support line and request exclusion verbally — but always follow up in writing (email) so you have a record.

National Register Check (When Live)

Once the NGEI is fully operational, operators face an additional and separate obligation: they must check the national register as part of their onboarding process for every new account. A failure to check — or a failure to act on a positive match — is a breach of the operator's GRAI licence and can result in sanctions including fines and licence conditions.

Deposit Limits and Time-Outs: Available Now

While the national self-exclusion register rolls out, GRAI-licensed operators must already offer two other important responsible gambling tools by law:

Deposit Limits

Every GRAI-licensed operator must offer you daily, weekly, and monthly deposit limits. The legal requirement has two important asymmetries built in by design:

  • A request to reduce a deposit limit takes effect immediately.
  • A request to increase a deposit limit is subject to a mandatory cooling-off period (typically 24–48 hours, determined by GRAI regulations). You cannot increase a limit the same day you request it.

This asymmetry is deliberate. It makes it easy to protect yourself and introduces friction before you can spend more. You can set deposit limits before you make your first deposit — GRAI rules require operators to give you this option during account setup.

Time-Outs and Cool-Off Periods

GRAI-licensed operators must also offer time-out features that allow you to pause your account for a defined period — typically from 24 hours to 30 days — without permanently closing it. During a time-out, you cannot place bets or make deposits. Operators must offer this feature and make it easy to find and use. It differs from self-exclusion in that it is temporary and self-reversible (after the period expires).

For a more serious and longer-term break, formal self-exclusion is the appropriate tool — it is not easily reversed and carries stronger obligations on the operator.

What to Use Right Now: Existing Options While the NGEI Rolls Out

If you want to take action on your gambling today and cannot wait for the national register, here are the options available as of November 2026:

Individual Operator Self-Exclusion

Contact each GRAI-licensed operator you use and request self-exclusion directly through their responsible gambling settings. This is a legal requirement for licensed operators and must take effect immediately. Keep a record of each exclusion request.

GamStop

GamStop is a UK-based self-exclusion scheme at gamstop.co.uk. Some operators that hold both UK and Irish licences participate in GamStop, meaning a GamStop registration may block you from some (not all) sites that accept Irish customers. GamStop is not an Irish scheme, is not managed by the GRAI, and does not cover all GRAI-licensed operators. Use it as an additional tool, not a replacement for Irish operator-level exclusion.

Gambling Awareness Trust

The Gambling Awareness Trust provides free, confidential support and can help you work through the exclusion process across multiple operators. Call 1800 753 753 (Monday to Sunday) or visit gamblingcare.ie. The Trust's counsellors can advise on exclusion options, help draft exclusion requests, and refer you to specialist gambling counselling services.

Blocking Software

Tools such as Gamban, BetBlocker (free), and Betfilter block access to gambling websites at the device or network level. They are not a substitute for operator-level exclusion — a determined person can remove them — but they provide an additional practical barrier. Gamban is available via the Gambling Awareness Trust at a subsidised cost for Irish residents.

Your Rights as an Excluded Person

Whether you self-exclude through an individual operator or (when live) through the national register, you have specific legal rights under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI licence conditions:

  • No marketing. Any licensed operator you have self-excluded from must immediately remove you from all marketing lists. Receipt of a marketing email or SMS after a valid exclusion request is a reportable breach.
  • No new account. No licensed operator may open a new account for you during your exclusion period, even if you approach them directly and claim to have changed your mind.
  • Return of balance. Any funds held in your account at the time of exclusion must be returned to you after the standard identity verification process.
  • Complaints escalation. If a GRAI-licensed operator fails to honour your self-exclusion — for example, by allowing you to bet, sending you marketing, or opening a new account — you can complain to the GRAI directly. This is a potential licence breach and the GRAI takes it seriously.

Self-Exclusion and Unlicensed Sites

It is important to understand that self-exclusion — whether through individual operators or the national register — applies only to GRAI-licensed operators. Unlicensed offshore sites are not connected to the national register and are under no obligation to participate. If you use an unlicensed site after self-excluding from licensed operators, your exclusion has no effect there.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the National Gambling Exclusion Register in Ireland?

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGEI) is Ireland's centralised self-exclusion system, established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and managed by the GRAI. When fully live, a single registration will block you from opening or using accounts with all GRAI-licensed gambling operators simultaneously. Operators are legally required to check the register before opening any new account.

Is the National Gambling Exclusion Register live yet?

The National Gambling Exclusion Register is enacted in law under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 but has not yet been fully commenced as of November 2026. The GRAI is building the technical infrastructure. While it rolls out, Irish bettors can use individual operator self-exclusion tools, contact the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753, or use GamStop (which covers some operators serving Ireland).

How does self-exclusion work in Ireland right now?

Currently, self-exclusion operates on a site-by-site basis. Each GRAI-licensed bookmaker must offer self-exclusion as a legal requirement. When you self-exclude, the operator must immediately stop sending you marketing, block your account, and refuse to allow you to open a new account. When the national register is fully live, one registration will cover all GRAI-licensed operators simultaneously.

Does GamStop work in Ireland?

GamStop is a UK-based self-exclusion scheme. Some operators that serve both the UK and Ireland participate in GamStop, but it is not an Irish scheme and does not cover all GRAI-licensed operators. It is a useful additional tool but should not be relied on as a substitute for Irish operator-level self-exclusion or the forthcoming National Gambling Exclusion Register.

Where can I get help with a gambling problem in Ireland?

The Gambling Awareness Trust operates a free, confidential helpline at 1800 753 753 (Monday to Sunday). You can also visit gamblingcare.ie for online resources, self-assessment tools, and referrals to counselling. Gamblers Anonymous Ireland is at gamblersanonymous.ie. If you are in crisis, the Samaritans can be reached at 116 123, free, 24 hours a day.

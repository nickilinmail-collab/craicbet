How Progressive Jackpots Work

A fixed percentage of every bet (typically 1–3%) feeds into the jackpot prize pool. There are two main types:

  • Standalone jackpots: Feed from one game at one casino — grow slowly, win relatively more often.
  • Network progressives: Feed from the same game across thousands of players at hundreds of casinos simultaneously — jackpots grow to millions of euros but are won very rarely.

The RTP Trade-Off

Jackpot contributions come at a cost. If a game has a stated RTP of 92% including the jackpot contribution, and you never win the jackpot, your effective base game RTP is lower. The jackpot contribution is not money the casino keeps — it accumulates in the prize pool — but unless you win it, it represents a portion of your stake that generates no return for you.

This is not a criticism of jackpot slots; it is a transparent explanation of the maths. If you enjoy the excitement of jackpot games and understand this trade-off, that is a fully informed choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do progressive jackpot slots work?

A percentage of every bet feeds the jackpot pool. Network progressives pool contributions from thousands of players across multiple casinos, allowing jackpots to grow to millions of euros before one player wins it all.

Does playing a jackpot slot reduce my RTP?

Yes. Part of your stake funds the jackpot contribution. Unless you win the jackpot, your effective base game RTP is lower than the stated headline figure. This is a transparent trade-off — jackpot stakes fund the prize pool.

Do I need to bet maximum to win a progressive jackpot?

Many jackpot slots require maximum bet for jackpot eligibility. Always check the jackpot rules in the game's paytable before playing.

Are progressive jackpot winnings taxable in Ireland?

Gambling winnings are generally not subject to income tax for Irish residents. For large wins, seek advice from a tax professional regarding any secondary income generated from winnings.

What Happened: Stake Geo-Blocked Ireland in 2026

Stake.com was one of the world's most heavily marketed online gambling platforms, known for its crypto-friendly approach and sponsorship deals with high-profile sports figures. However, following the introduction of Ireland's new gambling licensing regime from 1 July 2026, the platform found itself on the wrong side of Irish law.

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) contacted Stake and required the company to geo-block Irish players. Stake complied and introduced restrictions preventing users with Irish IP addresses from accessing the platform. This was reported by the Irish Times and independently noted by gambling industry sources including casinos.com.

Irish players who attempt to visit Stake.com are now shown a geographic restriction message. Accounts previously held by Irish residents were also affected.

Why GRAI Blocked Stake: Operating Without a Licence

Under Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024, any operator offering remote betting services to customers in Ireland must hold a GRAI remote betting licence. The GRAI began issuing these licences from 1 July 2026. By November 2026, 33 operators have been licensed.

Stake.com did not hold — and had not applied for — a GRAI remote betting licence at the time the GRAI intervened. This meant Stake was accepting bets from Irish customers without the regulatory authorisation required under Irish law.

The GRAI's enforcement action against Stake is part of a broader effort to bring unlicensed offshore operators into compliance. Stake is not the only platform affected; several other operators have been required to implement similar geo-blocks pending licence applications.

Will Stake Return to Ireland?

Stake has publicly indicated that it may apply for a GRAI remote betting licence in order to legally re-enter the Irish market. However, as of November 2026, no such licence has been granted and Stake does not appear on the GRAI's official licensed operators register.

There are two layers of complexity to any potential return:

  • Remote betting licence: This is what GRAI currently issues. Stake would need to apply and be approved for GRAI-0xxx-RB-26-xxxx to serve Irish sports bettors. This is theoretically achievable if Stake meets the GRAI's requirements.
  • Casino gaming licence: Stake is primarily known as a casino platform. GRAI has not yet issued any casino or gaming licences — these are expected to become available from 2027 or 2028. Even if Stake obtains a betting licence, the casino products would remain governed by an offshore licence until GRAI's casino framework is live.

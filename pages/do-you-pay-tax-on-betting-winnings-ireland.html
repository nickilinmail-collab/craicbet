Do You Pay Tax on Betting Winnings in Ireland?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The short answer is no — betting winnings are entirely tax-free for individual punters in Ireland. There is no equivalent of the old UK punters’ betting duty, and Irish Revenue does not treat casual gambling winnings as income. Here is the full picture for 2026.

No Tax on Winnings for Irish Punters

Under Irish tax law, betting and gambling winnings received by individuals are not subject to Income Tax, USC, or PRSI. This applies whether you bet on horse racing, football, casino games, or any other activity. You keep 100% of your winnings. The 2% betting duty is paid by licensed operators on gross stakes received from Irish customers — not by you.

This has been the position in Ireland for decades and was not changed by the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 or the creation of the GRAI. The 2024 Act addressed regulation and consumer protection, not punter taxation.

What About Professional Gamblers?

Irish Revenue distinguishes between casual and professional gambling. If you generate income from gambling in an organised, systematic, and commercial way — as a primary source of livelihood — Revenue may consider it a trade or profession subject to income tax. In practice, this is a high bar: Revenue looks at regularity, the use of a system or strategy, and whether winnings are your main source of income.

For the vast majority of punters, even regular bettors, winnings remain tax-free. If you are unsure about your specific situation, consult a tax adviser.

The 2% Betting Duty: Who Actually Pays It

Operators holding a GRAI remote betting licence pay a 2% betting duty (sometimes called gross betting tax) on turnover — the gross stakes they receive from Irish customers. This is an operator cost. Some operators absorb it in their pricing (slightly lower odds); others operate with the same odds as other markets. Either way, you do not pay it directly.

Gift Tax and Large Winnings

There is no gift tax implication from gambling winnings, even very large ones. Capital Acquisitions Tax (CAT) only applies to gifts or inheritances, not winnings. If you share winnings with others, those transfers are generally not subject to CAT either, though very large transfers may be worth discussing with an adviser.

Betting Tax in Ireland: The Rules

Ireland does not impose income tax on casual gambling winnings for private individuals. The Revenue Commissioners treat betting winnings as windfall gains, not earned income, so recreational punters owe no tax regardless of the amount won. This applies to horse racing, football, casino games, and all other forms of gambling.

Who Pays Betting Tax in Ireland?

  • The operator pays: Licensed Irish bookmakers pay a 3% betting duty on their gross revenue. This cost is typically built into the odds — it's why margins at Irish-facing bookmakers can be slightly higher than at some offshore sites.
  • You don't pay: As a punter, you receive your winnings gross, with no withholding tax applied by the bookmaker.
  • Crypto note: If you gamble with cryptocurrency and later convert your winnings to euros, any gain on the crypto itself may be subject to Capital Gains Tax at 33%. This is a CGT event on the crypto asset, not a tax on gambling winnings per se.

Professional Gambling and Irish Tax

If gambling is your primary source of income, Revenue may assess it as self-employment income. There is no bright-line rule, but factors include the regularity and organisation of your activity, whether you apply a systematic approach, and whether you depend on it for your living. If you believe you may be a professional gambler for tax purposes, consult an Irish tax advisor. For the vast majority of recreational punters, there is no tax obligation on winnings.

Do you pay tax on betting winnings in Ireland?

No — betting winnings are completely tax-free for individual punters in Ireland. There is no income tax, USC, or PRSI on gambling winnings. The 2% betting duty is paid by licensed operators, not players.

What is the 2% betting duty in Ireland?

A tax paid by GRAI-licensed operators on gross stakes received from Irish customers. It is an operator cost, not a player tax. You keep 100% of your winnings.

Can Irish Revenue tax professional gamblers?

In theory yes — if gambling is your primary organised commercial livelihood. In practice this is a very high bar. The vast majority of regular bettors are not affected. Consult a tax adviser if gambling is your main income source.

Do I need to declare betting winnings on my Irish tax return?

No — casual betting winnings are not assessable income and do not need to be declared. If you are a professional gambler whose winnings are treated as trading income, you would declare them, but this is rare.

Are casino winnings also tax-free in Ireland?

Yes — the same rule applies to casino winnings, poker, and other forms of gambling. Individual gambling winnings of any kind are tax-free in Ireland.

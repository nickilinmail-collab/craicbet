Outright Betting: How Tournament Winner Markets Work

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 4 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

What Is Outright Betting?

Outright betting means wagering on who will win an entire competition rather than a single match. Picking the Premier League winner, the All-Ireland SFC champion, Wimbledon, the Ryder Cup or the Grand National outright are all examples of outright bets. Because you are predicting the winner of a multi-round competition, outright bets can remain active for days, weeks or months.

Outright markets are also available for top scorer, relegation, and player of the tournament awards in major competitions.

How Outright Odds Are Priced

Outright odds reflect each competitor’s estimated probability of winning, with the bookmaker’s overround applied across all runners. Because outright fields can include dozens of competitors, the cumulative margin is higher than a two-runner match market — often 110–130% implied probability across all runners. This means the average outright bet has a higher built-in house edge than a standard 1X2 market.

Odds are set initially based on pre-tournament form and historical data, then updated as the competition progresses. Early leaders shorten; early casualties lengthen or become non-runners.

Ante-Post vs In-Running Outrights

Ante-post outright bets are placed before the competition starts (sometimes months in advance). In-running outrights can be placed once the competition is underway, with odds reflecting current standings. Ante-post bets typically carry better odds for strong fancied runners but carry the risk of non-runner void — if your selection withdraws before the competition, most bookmakers void the bet and return the stake (check each operator’s non-runner rule).

In-running outright odds on a leading team or player may be much shorter than the original ante-post price but represent lower risk.

Managing Outright Bet Risk

Outright bets tie up stakes for extended periods. Spreading outright bets across multiple selections (a saver on the second favourite alongside a main selection) is a common risk management approach. Using each-way terms when available — paying for top 2 or 3 — reduces risk on markets where the winner is genuinely uncertain. Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to an outright bet if my selection withdraws?

Most bookmakers void ante-post outright bets and return the stake when a selection withdraws before the event starts. During the competition, withdrawals (retirements) are usually treated as a loser. Always check the specific rules.

Can I cash out an outright bet?

Cash out is available on many outright markets at GRAI-licensed bookmakers. The value will increase if your selection is in strong form and decrease if they are struggling.

What is each-way on an outright?

Each-way on an outright bet pays win odds if your selection wins and a fraction (usually 1/4 or 1/5) of win odds if they finish in a specified top number of places. Terms vary by operator and event.

Finding Value in Outright Markets

  • Each-way value: In a 20-team league, top-4 at 1/4 odds means you effectively get 1/4 of the outright price if your team finishes in the top 4. This can offer better expected value than the win market alone for mid-tier title contenders.
  • NRNB (non-runner no bet): If a selection is withdrawn before the event starts, your stake is returned. Always look for NRNB in outright markets — it eliminates the risk of a key withdrawal costing you.
  • Price movement: Outright odds shorten significantly after a team's first few games. Getting on early in pre-season can offer value if you have a strong view on a team's prospects.

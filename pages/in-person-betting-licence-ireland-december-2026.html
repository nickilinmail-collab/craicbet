In-Person Betting Licence Ireland December 2026 — What Changes for Betting Shops

Ireland's high street betting shops face a major regulatory transition on 1 December 2026. From that date, all in-person betting operators — every betting shop on every high street in Ireland — must hold a GRAI in-person betting licence instead of their existing Revenue Commissioners licence.

This is the second phase of Ireland's gambling licensing overhaul. Phase one (1 July 2026) covered online/remote betting. Phase two covers land-based, in-person betting.

Key Dates — Ireland In-Person Betting Transition

  • 9 February 2026: GRAI began accepting licence applications for both remote and in-person betting operators
  • 1 July 2026: Remote betting licences took effect; online operators needed GRAI licence from this date
  • 30 November 2026: Existing Revenue Commissioners in-person licences expire
  • 1 December 2026: GRAI in-person betting licences take effect; shops without one cannot legally operate

What Changes for Punters Visiting Betting Shops?

For regular punters, the December 2026 transition should be largely seamless if the bookmaker applied for and received a GRAI licence on time. However, there are new protections and requirements you will start to notice:

  • Age verification: GRAI-licensed shops must implement stricter age verification processes
  • Responsible gambling displays: New requirements for in-shop responsible gambling information
  • Advertising restrictions: GRAI has introduced limits on gambling advertising visible from outside betting shops
  • Gaming machine stakes: Limits on betting machine stakes in licensed premises
  • Social Impact Fund levy: Licensed shops contribute to Ireland's fund for gambling harm services

What Happens to Shops That Don't Have a GRAI Licence by December 2026?

Operating a betting shop in Ireland after 1 December 2026 without a GRAI in-person licence is illegal under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The GRAI has criminal enforcement powers and can seek court orders requiring unlicensed operators to cease trading. Fines and sanctions can reach up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover.

Major established chains — which have hundreds of shops across Ireland — have been engaged with the GRAI application process since February 2026 and are expected to transition smoothly.

How to Check if Your Local Betting Shop Is GRAI Licensed

From December 2026, GRAI-licensed premises must display their licence number. You can also verify any in-person operator's licence by checking the official GRAI register at grai.ie/register.

How Do Free Bets Work? SNR vs SR Explained for Irish Punters

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

Free bets are the most common welcome offer at Irish betting sites, and they look simpler than they are. A bookmaker offers you €30 in free bets, you sign up and place a qualifying bet, and the tokens appear in your account. So far, straightforward. But the detail of whether a free bet is SNR (Stake Not Returned) or SR (Stake Returned) makes a significant difference to its actual value — and most punters do not stop to calculate what their "free" bet is genuinely worth before they use it.

SNR Free Bets: The Standard Type

SNR — Stake Not Returned — is by far the most common free bet format in Ireland. It means that when you win with a free bet, you receive the profit only from the bet; the face value of the free bet token is not included in your return.

Example: €30 SNR free bet placed on a selection at 2/1 (3.0 decimal). It wins. Your return is: €30 (stake) × 2.0 (profit at 2/1) = €60 profit — but wait, with SNR you do not get the €30 stake back. You receive €60 profit only. At 3.0 odds: (3.0 − 1) × €30 = €60.

For comparison: a real €30 cash bet at 2/1 that wins returns €60 profit + €30 stake = €90. The SNR free bet returns only €60. That is the SNR cost — you give up the stake return.

SR Free Bets: The More Valuable Type

SR — Stake Returned — free bets include the face value of the token in your winnings. They work exactly like a real cash bet: you receive profit plus stake. These are noticeably more valuable and significantly rarer. Some reload offers and VIP promotions use SR free bets; standard welcome offers almost universally use SNR.

Example: €30 SR free bet at 2/1 (3.0). Wins: (3.0 × €30) = €90 total return including the €30 "stake". Identical to a real cash bet win.

Calculating the Real Value of a Free Bet

The face value of a free bet is not its cash value. Its real value depends on the odds you use it on and your assessment of the probability. As a general rule:

  • SNR free bet used at odds of 2.0 (evens): worth about 50% of face value in expected value terms (you win the profit 50% of the time — in theory)
  • SNR free bet used at odds of 4.0 (3/1): worth about 75% of face value in EV (profit 3× stake, 25% chance of winning)
  • SNR free bet used at odds of 6.0 (5/1): worth about 83% of face value

Longer odds give higher EV as a percentage of face value for SNR free bets. The practical ceiling is around 85–90% — there is always some bookmaker margin embedded in the price. Using SNR free bets at very short odds (1.3, 1.4) is poor value: you win often, but the profit per win is tiny and the SNR structure bites hard.

Qualifying Bets and Qualifying Loss

To unlock a free bet offer, you must first place a qualifying bet — a real-money bet that meets specified conditions (minimum odds, minimum stake, specific markets). This qualifying bet can win or lose at normal risk. If it loses, your net position is: lost qualifying stake + free bet received. If it wins, you are up on the qualifying bet and still receive the free bet.

The qualifying loss is the expected loss from the qualifying bet, calculated from the bookmaker margin at the minimum qualifying odds. At 1/2 (1.5 decimal) with a typical 5% margin, the qualifying loss on a €30 qualifying bet is roughly €1.50. That is the hidden cost of "earning" the free bet.

Key Conditions to Check

Before claiming any free bet offer: (1) minimum odds on qualifying bet; (2) minimum odds on the free bet itself; (3) expiry date — most free bets expire within 7 days; (4) number of free bet tokens vs single lump sum; (5) cash out availability on free bets (many restrict it). Frequently Asked Questions

What does SNR mean on a free bet?

SNR stands for Stake Not Returned. When your free bet wins, you receive only the profit — the original token is not included in your winnings. This is the most common free bet type in Ireland.

What does SR mean on a free bet?

SR stands for Stake Returned. When you win with an SR free bet, you receive the profit plus the face value of the token. SR free bets are more valuable and less common.

What is the actual cash value of a €30 SNR free bet?

It depends on the odds. The practical expected value ranges from about 65–85% of face value depending on what odds you use it at. Longer odds give higher EV percentage on SNR tokens.

What is a qualifying bet for a free bet offer?

A qualifying bet is the real-money bet you must place first to trigger the free bet. It has minimum odds and stake requirements. It is at normal risk and can win or lose.

Can free bets be withdrawn as cash?

No. Free bets cannot be withdrawn — only used on bets. If an SNR free bet wins, the profit (not the token itself) is added to your withdrawable balance.

SNR Free Bet: Worked Example

Most Irish free bets are SNR. If you place a €10 SNR free bet at 4.00 (3/1):

  • If wins: returns €40 total — but stake (€10) is not returned, so net winnings = €30
  • If loses: free bet is gone; no cash loss beyond the qualifying bet already placed
  • To maximise SNR value: use at odds of 3.00+ where the profit portion is large relative to stake

Gambling Sponsorship in Irish Sport: What the Rules Actually Say

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Legal Baseline

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 prohibits gambling sponsorship of events, teams and organisations aimed at children, alongside its ban on advertising that appeals to children. It is narrower than a blanket sport-wide ban — adult professional sport can still carry gambling sponsorship — but it draws a hard line around youth sport for the first time in Irish law.

The GAA Got There First

The GAA banned gambling sponsorship across all its competitions, teams and facilities back in 2018 — years before the Act. That is why you see betting brands on League of Ireland shirts and around rugby, but never on a county jersey. For the biggest sporting body in the country, the debate is already settled.

Where the Pressure Is Heading

The UK Premier League agreed to remove gambling sponsors from the front of shirts from 2026-27, and Irish policy debate tracks the UK closely. With the GRAI empowered to regulate advertising broadly, sponsorship of adult sport is the most likely next frontier — watch the regulatory updates on our tracker.

Why Sponsorship Rules Exist at All

Sponsorship normalises. A child who watches sport with a betting logo on every shirt learns that gambling and sport are the same product — and the research on early exposure is grim reading. The Act treats childhood as a protected space, the same logic behind the watershed.

What the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 Means in Practice

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced phased restrictions on gambling advertising in Ireland, overseen by GRAI. The first restrictions — the broadcast watershed and inducements advertising ban — took effect in September 2026. These rules apply to all operators with a GRAI licence. Further restrictions on social media targeting, jersey sponsorship and stadium naming are expected as GRAI regulations develop in 2027.

  • For bettors: bonus offers still exist but can no longer be advertised on TV or radio before 9pm — check operator websites directly for current promotions
  • For broadcasters: sports programmes broadcast before 9pm cannot carry gambling advertising, including during ad breaks
  • GRAI complaints: breaches of advertising rules can be reported to GRAI at grai.ie
Is gambling sponsorship banned in Irish sport?

The Act bans it for events, teams and organisations aimed at children. Adult professional sport can still carry gambling sponsorship — though the GAA banned it voluntarily in 2018.

Why do GAA teams have no betting sponsors?

The GAA banned gambling sponsorship across all its competitions and facilities in 2018 — ahead of any legal requirement.

Will Ireland ban all gambling sponsorship in sport?

Not yet — but the regulatory direction and UK precedent (Premier League front-of-shirt removal) point that way.

What Is LeoVegas?

LeoVegas is a Swedish online gambling company founded in 2011, with a particular focus on mobile casino and sports betting. The brand built a reputation across Europe for its mobile-first platform and wide range of casino products. In 2022, MGM Resorts International — one of the world’s largest hotel and entertainment companies — completed its acquisition of LeoVegas, making it a subsidiary of the American giant.

LeoVegas holds licences from the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) and the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC), both of which are reputable regulators with established consumer protection requirements. The brand operates across multiple European markets and is legitimately licensed in those jurisdictions where it holds a licence.

That said, holding an MGA or UKGC licence does not automatically authorise an operator to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence.

Does LeoVegas Have a GRAI Licence?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, LeoVegas does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means LeoVegas has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

LeoVegas and Irish Players

LeoVegas previously had a presence in the Irish market in the pre-GRAI era, when operators could serve Irish customers under their MGA licence without a specific Irish authorisation. That landscape changed on 1 July 2026, when the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 came into effect and the GRAI became the competent authority for gambling in Ireland.

LeoVegas may remain technically accessible to users with Irish IP addresses via its MGA licence, depending on whether the operator has implemented geographic restrictions for Ireland. However, accessible does not mean licensed, and the distinction matters for players. When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

None of this is intended to suggest LeoVegas is an unsafe operator in general terms — its MGA and UKGC licences provide substantial protections. But Irish players considering LeoVegas should be clear that they would be operating outside Irish regulatory oversight.

Casino vs Betting: A Note on GRAI Licences

The GRAI framework adds an important dimension for LeoVegas specifically, because LeoVegas is primarily a casino-first operator. Ireland’s GRAI has, as of November 2026, issued remote betting licences only. Zero GRAI gaming (casino) licences have been issued. These are expected from approximately 2027 to 2028, once the GRAI has established the relevant licensing conditions for remote gaming.

What this means in practice:

  • Even operators that do hold a GRAI remote betting licence are authorised only for their betting products under Irish law — their casino sections are not covered by a GRAI gaming licence, because none yet exist.
  • LeoVegas, as a brand built primarily around casino products, would need a future GRAI gaming licence to operate its core casino offering for Irish customers under full Irish regulatory authorisation.
  • Until GRAI gaming licences are issued (expected 2027–2028), no online casino operator — including any GRAI-licensed bookmaker operating a casino section — holds a GRAI gaming licence. This is an industry-wide position, not unique to LeoVegas.

Irish players who are specifically interested in casino gaming should be aware of this two-stage licensing timeline, and should not assume that any operator currently holds a GRAI casino licence, because none exist. For a full explanation of the GRAI framework, see our GRAI licence guide.

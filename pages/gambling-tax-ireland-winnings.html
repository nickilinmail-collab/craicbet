Gambling Tax Ireland 2026 — Do You Pay Tax on Betting Winnings?

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

One of the most searched questions among Irish punters: do you pay tax on betting winnings in Ireland? The short answer is no — but the full picture is slightly more nuanced. This guide explains Irish gambling tax rules for punters, the betting duty that bookmakers pay, and what changed under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

Do You Pay Tax on Gambling Winnings in Ireland?

No. Irish punters do not pay income tax, CGT or any other personal tax on gambling winnings, regardless of the amount. This applies to betting winnings, casino winnings, poker winnings, and lottery prizes. There is no threshold above which winnings become taxable.

The Revenue Commissioners' position is clear: gambling winnings are not treated as income from a trade or profession for an ordinary recreational gambler, and therefore do not fall within the scope of income tax.

Key fact: Gambling winnings in Ireland are tax-free for recreational punters.
This applies to all forms: sports betting, casino, poker, bingo, lotteries. You do not need to declare winnings to Revenue for personal tax purposes.

What About Professional Gamblers?

The "professional gambler" question is more complex. Revenue can, in principle, treat gambling as a trade if it is conducted in an organised, systematic way as a primary income source. In practice, this is rare and has not been the subject of significant Irish case law. If gambling is genuinely your primary livelihood — full-time professional poker, systematic matched betting at scale — it is worth taking professional tax advice. For the vast majority of punters, winnings are tax-free.

Betting Duty — What the Bookmaker Pays

While punters pay nothing, bookmakers in Ireland pay betting duty to Revenue. This is a tax on the bookmaker's turnover (the value of bets placed with them), not a tax on winnings. As of 2026:

The 2% remote betting duty was introduced in 2015 and extended as the online market grew. It is built into the bookmaker's margin — the reason why Irish-facing odds are sometimes slightly shorter than UK odds from the same operator.

Winnings vs Deposit Bonus — Any Difference?

No. Whether you win from a real-money bet or from a free bet, the winnings are treated the same way under Irish tax law — they are not taxable for a recreational gambler. The fact that a free bet was used as the stake does not change the tax treatment of the profit.

What GRAI Changed (and Didn't Change) on Tax

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and the GRAI's establishment did not change the tax treatment of gambling winnings for individuals. GRAI is a regulatory body, not a revenue authority. The Revenue Commissioners remain responsible for tax, and their position on gambling winnings has not changed.

What GRAI did introduce: the Social Impact Fund, to which GRAI-licensed operators contribute. This funds problem gambling treatment and research. It is a levy on operators, not on punters.

Reporting Requirements

Irish punters have no obligation to report gambling winnings to Revenue. You do not need to include them in a Form 11 or Form 12 return. If Revenue ever queries a large cash movement into your account that originated from a bookmaker withdrawal, you should be able to demonstrate it was a gambling win (your account history from the bookmaker is sufficient). There is no official threshold that triggers a reporting obligation for punters.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I pay tax on gambling winnings in Ireland?

No. Recreational gambling winnings are not taxable in Ireland. The Revenue Commissioners do not treat gambling winnings as income for ordinary recreational gamblers. There is no threshold above which winnings become taxable, and no requirement to declare them.

Do I need to declare gambling winnings to Revenue in Ireland?

No. Recreational gambling winnings do not need to be declared to Revenue. You do not include them in a Form 11 or Form 12 return. If Revenue queries a large deposit that came from gambling, your bookmaker account history is sufficient documentation.

What betting tax do bookmakers pay in Ireland?

Bookmakers in Ireland pay 2% betting duty on their turnover (the total value of bets placed with them). Betting exchanges pay 15% duty on their commission. Punters pay no duty on their bets or winnings. The 2% duty is built into bookmakers' odds margins.

Could I be taxed on gambling winnings if I gamble professionally?

In principle, Revenue can treat gambling as a taxable trade if it is conducted systematically as a primary income source. In practice, this is extremely rare in Ireland and has not been widely litigated. For the vast majority of punters — including those who bet regularly or use matched betting — winnings are tax-free. If you are in any doubt, consult a qualified tax adviser.

Do I pay tax on free bet winnings in Ireland?

No. The tax treatment of winnings does not depend on whether you used a real-money stake or a free bet. Profit from a free bet win is not taxable for a recreational gambler, just as any other gambling win is not taxable.

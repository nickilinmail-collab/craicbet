What the GRAI Means for Irish Bettors

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) replaced the previous ad-hoc system under the Betting Acts. All remote (online) betting sites legally serving Irish customers must now hold a GRAI remote betting licence. As of July 2026, only remote betting licences have been issued — there are zero GRAI casino licences in existence. Casino (gaming) licences are expected to be introduced in 2027–2028.

  • How to verify a licence: check grai.ie for the public register of GRAI-licensed operators before depositing
  • Unlicensed sites: operating without a GRAI licence is illegal; unlicensed sites have no consumer protections and Irish players have no recourse for disputes
  • Responsible gambling tools: all GRAI-licensed operators must offer deposit limits, self-exclusion, and access to the National Gambling Exclusion Register

What GRAI Regulation Means for Punters

  • All GRAI-licensed bookmakers must offer mandatory deposit limits and self-exclusion tools
  • National Self-Exclusion Register (Gambling Exclusion Register) being developed under the Act
  • Advertising restrictions: watershed rules for TV/radio introduced in 2024
  • Consumers can verify any bookmaker's licence at grai.ie – always check before depositing

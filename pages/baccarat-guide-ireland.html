How to Play Baccarat

Baccarat is a card comparing game between two hands: the "Player" and the "Banker." These are simply labels — you are not the Player, and the dealer is not the Banker. You bet on which hand you think will be closer to 9, or whether the hands will tie.

Card values in baccarat differ from standard card games:

  • Aces = 1 point
  • Cards 2–9 = face value
  • 10, Jack, Queen, King = 0 points

Only the last digit of a total counts. A hand of 7 + 8 = 15 has a value of 5. A hand of 6 + 4 = 10 has a value of 0 (called "baccarat" — the worst hand). The highest possible score is 9; a "natural" is 8 or 9 on the first two cards.

Third Card Drawing Rules

Third card rules are automatic and fixed — you never make this decision. The basic framework:

  • Player draws a third card if the Player total is 0–5.
  • Player stands on 6 or 7.
  • Banker drawing rules depend on the Banker total and the value of the Player's third card — a matrix of 12 scenarios that the dealer applies automatically.
  • Both hands stand on 8 or 9 (a natural); no third card is drawn.

You do not need to memorise these rules; they are applied automatically in both online and live dealer versions of the game.

Baccarat Strategy

Baccarat is a pure game of chance once the cards are dealt. The drawing rules are fixed, leaving no skill-based decisions after you place your bet. The closest thing to an optimal strategy is:

  1. Always bet Banker for the lowest available house edge (1.06%).
  2. Never bet Tie.
  3. Ignore scorecards (the roadmaps showing previous hand outcomes). Past results have no bearing on future hands in a freshly shuffled shoe.
  4. Set a session limit before you sit down and stick to it. Baccarat's low house edge is only an advantage if you play within your means.

Betting systems (Martingale, Fibonacci, etc.) do not change the house edge. They merely redistribute when you win and lose within a session. Over a large enough sample, the house edge applies in full regardless of the system used.

Baccarat Variants

Mini Baccarat is played on a smaller table with lower stakes and a faster pace. It follows exactly the same rules as full Baccarat and is the most common version online.

Chemin de Fer is the original version played in French casinos; players take turns being the Banker and can exercise some decision-making. Rare online.

Squeeze Baccarat (a live dealer feature) lets one player or the dealer slowly reveal cards by bending them — purely theatrical, with no effect on outcomes.

Speed Baccarat (Evolution Gaming) completes each hand in 27 seconds compared to the standard ~48 seconds. Identical rules, faster session.

Winamax Ireland 2026: GRAI Licence Status & What Irish Players Need to Know

Winamax is a well-regarded French operator known for poker tournaments and competitive sports betting odds — but it operates only in France and Spain. It does not hold a GRAI remote betting licence for Ireland, and does not appear to target the Irish market. Here is what that means for Irish players searching for Winamax, and what GRAI-licensed alternatives are available.

What Is Winamax?

Winamax is a French online poker and sports betting operator, run by Winamax SAS and headquartered in Paris. Founded in 2010, it has grown to become one of the most popular betting platforms in France, particularly known for its poker offering — including large-field online tournaments and an annual live festival — and for its sports betting product, which French players regard as offering competitive odds and a strong football markets focus.

Outside France, Winamax has expanded only to Spain, where it holds a licence from the DGOJ (Dirección General de Ordenación del Juego). Both its French and Spanish operations are under tight domestic regulatory oversight: its French licence is issued by the Autorité Nationale des Jeux (ANJ), the French gambling regulator established in 2020.

Winamax has not sought to enter the Irish, UK, or other English-speaking markets. Its platform is primarily available in French, and its product range — including tax-on-winnings structures in France — is tailored to French regulatory requirements. This is relevant context for Irish users who may encounter Winamax in search results or via French-speaking contacts.

Does Winamax Have a GRAI Licence?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of November 2026, Winamax does not hold a GRAI remote betting licence. It does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed operators. This is consistent with Winamax’s stated market focus: France and Spain only. Winamax has not publicly indicated any plans to expand into Ireland or apply for GRAI authorisation.

It is worth emphasising that the absence of a GRAI licence does not reflect on Winamax’s standing as an operator in its own licensed markets. Within France and Spain it is a legitimately regulated bookmaker. The issue for Irish players is simply that it has not obtained authorisation to operate under Ireland’s specific regulatory framework.

Is Winamax Accessible in Ireland?

Because Winamax targets only France and Spain, it is likely to restrict access from Irish IP addresses or decline registrations from Irish residents. However, even setting aside technical access, the more important point is that accessible does not mean licensed, and there is a meaningful practical difference for Irish players.

When you use a GRAI-licensed bookmaker, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Without a GRAI licence, an operator is not bound by:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

This is not a criticism of Winamax as an operator. Within France and Spain it complies with demanding regulatory requirements. But Irish players would be operating entirely outside Irish regulatory oversight, with no recourse to the GRAI.

Cards and Bookings Betting: Markets and What to Look For

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

What are cards and bookings betting markets?

Cards and bookings betting covers markets on the total number of yellow or red cards in a match, specific players to receive a card, and sometimes the first player to be booked. Total bookings markets typically use a points system (10 points per yellow, 25 points per red) with an over/under line. These markets are independent of the result, making them popular with punters who have a view on a fixture’s aggression levels without certainty on the score.

What drives card rates in a match

The main factors influencing booking rates are: referee appointment (some referees have significantly higher average booking rates per game, available from football statistics databases), fixture type (derbies, relegation battles and high-stakes games produce more cards than routine mid-table matches), and the styles of the teams involved (aggressive pressing teams concede more fouls and can receive more cards). The referee appointment, announced midweek, is the most actionable information for pre-match bookings betting.

Player to be carded markets

Markets on individual players to receive a card are popular specials on major matches. Defensive midfielders, aggressive full-backs and players with existing yellow cards risk a second are priced. These markets carry wide bookmaker margins given the specific nature of the outcome. Card accumulation status (players on five yellows facing suspension) is public information that may not always be fully reflected in market pricing on lower-profile teams.

Total bookings in cup and European football

Cup quarter-finals and European knockout matches often produce fewer cards than league games of similar importance, because teams are more cautious about suspensions across a two-legged tie. Frequently Asked Questions

How is the total bookings market calculated?

Most total bookings markets use a points system: 10 points for a yellow card, 25 for a red (whether a straight red or a second yellow). An over/under line is set for the total points in the match. For example, over 35.5 bookings points means three yellow cards and one red, or four yellows and no red, etc.

How do I find referee booking statistics?

Football reference databases and dedicated referee statistics sites publish average bookings per game for every listed referee. The referee appointment for the upcoming weekend is typically announced on Thursday or Friday and can be found on the relevant league’s official website.

Which fixtures produce the most cards?

Derby matches, relegation battles and high-stakes league games typically produce more cards than routine fixtures. Specific referee appointments are often the most reliable predictor — a referee with a 5.0 average bookings points per game rate will produce more cards than average regardless of fixture type.

Which Leagues and Fixtures Produce Most Cards?

  • High card rate: La Liga (Spanish football), Championship (EFL), lower-league derbies
  • Low card rate: Bundesliga (fewer cards on average), Champions League group stage
  • Derbies (rivalry matches) consistently produce more bookings than neutral fixtures
  • Referees have individual card-giving rates — checking the appointed referee before betting is valuable

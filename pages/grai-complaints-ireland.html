Making a Gambling Complaint to the GRAI

If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed gambling operator in Ireland, the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) provides a formal complaints process. This guide explains when you can complain, how to do it, what GRAI can and cannot do, and what your options are if the operator is not GRAI-licensed.

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

💡 Key Points

  • GRAI complaints only apply to GRAI-licensed operators — check the register at grai.ie first
  • You must try the operator's internal complaints process before going to the GRAI
  • The GRAI is a conduct regulator — it investigates rule-breaking, not every individual financial dispute
  • ADR schemes (eCOGRA, IBAS) can resolve individual disputes faster than a regulatory investigation
  • Keep records: dates, amounts, screenshots, and all communications with the operator

When Can You Complain to the GRAI?

The GRAI accepts complaints from players who have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator and have already attempted to resolve it through the operator's own complaints procedure without a satisfactory outcome. Common valid grounds include:

  • Refused or delayed withdrawal — funds withheld without clear explanation or beyond stated timeframes
  • Misleading bonus terms — terms that differ materially from what was advertised
  • Failure to honour self-exclusion — an operator accepting play or marketing from a self-excluded player
  • Deposit limit breach — an operator allowing deposits beyond a customer's set limit
  • Underage gambling concerns — if you believe an operator is failing adequate age verification
  • Advertising complaints — gambling ads that breach the GRAI's advertising codes

Step 1: Use the Operator's Internal Complaints Process First

Before approaching the GRAI, you must exhaust the operator's own complaints process. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, all GRAI-licensed operators are required to have a formal internal dispute resolution procedure. Typically:

  1. Submit your complaint in writing (email or the operator's complaints form), stating the issue clearly and including all relevant dates, amounts, and reference numbers
  2. The operator must acknowledge your complaint within a reasonable timeframe (commonly 5 business days)
  3. The operator must provide a final response within 8 weeks
  4. Keep all correspondence — you will need it for the GRAI or ADR

If the final response is unsatisfactory, or if 8 weeks pass without resolution, you can escalate.

Step 2: Consider an ADR Scheme

An Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) scheme can adjudicate player disputes faster than a regulatory investigation. GRAI-licensed operators must designate an approved ADR provider. ADR decisions focus on the specific financial outcome of your complaint — whether you should receive a payment or account reinstatement — rather than on the operator's broader conduct.

The GRAI maintains a list of approved ADR providers on its website at grai.ie. Using ADR and submitting a GRAI complaint are not mutually exclusive — you can do both.

Step 3: How to Submit a GRAI Complaint

To make a formal complaint to the GRAI:

  1. Visit grai.ie and navigate to the Complaints section
  2. Provide: your name and contact details, the operator's name and licence number (from grai.ie), a clear description of the issue, dates and amounts involved, copies of your correspondence with the operator, and the operator's final response
  3. Submit the complaint via the GRAI's online form or by post

📝 What to Include

  • Your account username or customer reference number
  • Dates and exact amounts in dispute
  • Screenshots of relevant pages, chats, or emails
  • The operator's final response letter or email
  • A clear summary: what happened, what you expected, and what the operator said

What the GRAI Can and Cannot Do

It is important to have realistic expectations. The GRAI is a conduct regulator, not an ombudsman or financial adjudicator:

  • Can do: investigate the operator's conduct, require the operator to provide information, issue warnings, impose fines, suspend or revoke licences for serious breaches
  • Cannot do: order an operator to pay a specific sum to a specific player (that is an ADR or court function), intervene in disputes about commercial decisions (such as a promotion that was not targeted at you), or investigate operators outside its jurisdiction

Your GRAI complaint is most powerful as a conduct signal — it feeds into the GRAI's regulatory intelligence and can trigger wider investigations where a pattern of operator misconduct is identified.

Complaints About Non-GRAI Licensed Operators

The GRAI has no jurisdiction over operators that do not hold a GRAI licence. If your dispute is with an operator licensed by:

  • Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) — use the MGA Player Support unit
  • UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) — use an approved ADR scheme such as IBAS or eCOGRA
  • Gibraltar Regulatory Authority — contact the GRA directly
  • Curaçao — player protections are weaker; consider the operator's ADR provider if one exists

If you are unsure which regulator covers your operator, check the licence details in the site's footer and cross-reference with the relevant regulator's public register.

Frequently Asked Questions

When can I complain to the GRAI?▾
You can complain to the GRAI when you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that you have been unable to resolve through the operator's internal complaints process. Common grounds include: a refused or delayed withdrawal without explanation, misleading bonus terms, failure to honour self-exclusion or deposit limits, and suspected underage gambling concerns.
Do I have to contact the operator before complaining to GRAI?▾
Yes. The GRAI expects players to have exhausted the operator's internal dispute resolution process before submitting a complaint to the regulator. Most operators must acknowledge complaints within 5 business days and provide a final response within 8 weeks.
What can the GRAI do about my complaint?▾
The GRAI can investigate the operator's conduct, require the operator to provide information, and take enforcement action (warnings, fines, licence suspension or revocation) if it finds a breach. The GRAI is a conduct regulator — it focuses on whether the operator followed the rules, not on adjudicating individual financial disputes.
What if the casino I am complaining about is not GRAI-licensed?▾
The GRAI only has jurisdiction over GRAI-licensed operators. If the operator holds a different licence (MGA, UKGC, Gibraltar, Curaçao), you must use that regulator's complaints process or the operator's designated ADR scheme. For MGA-licensed sites this is the MGA's Player Support unit; for UKGC-licensed sites it is an approved ADR provider such as IBAS or eCOGRA.
How long does a GRAI investigation take?▾
The GRAI does not publish fixed investigation timelines. Complex compliance investigations can take several months. For individual player disputes, using the operator's ADR scheme is typically faster than a regulatory investigation, though the GRAI complaint remains an option in parallel.

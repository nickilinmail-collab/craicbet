Card Deposits vs Withdrawals — What to Expect

Depositing with Visa or Mastercard

Card deposits at online casinos are instant. Once you enter your card details and complete 3D Secure verification, the funds appear in your casino account within seconds. The minimum deposit is typically €10, and there are no fees charged by the casino (though your bank may classify it as a gambling transaction on your statement).

Most casinos will ask you to enter your 16-digit card number, expiry date, CVV, and cardholder name. You'll then be redirected to your bank's 3D Secure page (Verified by Visa or Mastercard SecureCode) to authenticate the transaction. This may involve entering a one-time passcode sent to your phone or approving via your banking app.

Withdrawing to Visa or Mastercard

Card withdrawals are slower than deposits — this is normal across the industry. Once the casino processes your withdrawal request (which can take 0–24 hours), the actual bank transfer typically takes an additional 1–5 business days to appear in your account. The exact timing depends on both the casino and your bank.

Key points about card withdrawals:

  • Funds are returned to the same card used for the deposit (anti-money laundering requirement)
  • Visa Debit withdrawals are generally faster than Mastercard due to Visa's faster processing network
  • Weekend and bank holiday withdrawals may take longer
  • First withdrawals require KYC verification (ID, proof of address, card photo)
  • If your card has expired, the casino will need to process the withdrawal to an alternative method

3D Secure — How It Protects Irish Casino Players

3D Secure (also known as Verified by Visa, Mastercard SecureCode, or Mastercard Identity Check) is a mandatory security layer for online card payments in the EU, including Ireland. Here's how it works at online casinos:

  • Two-factor authentication: After entering your card details, you must verify your identity through a second method — usually a one-time passcode sent via SMS or approval through your banking app
  • Fraud prevention: Even if someone steals your card details, they can't make casino deposits without access to your phone or banking app
  • Chargeback protection: 3D Secure provides stronger evidence for resolving disputes, protecting both you and the casino
  • Mandatory in the EU: Under the PSD2 regulation, Strong Customer Authentication (SCA) is required for all online card payments over €30 — this includes casino deposits

Ireland's Credit Card Gambling Ban Explained

One of the most significant changes under Ireland's new gambling regulation is the ban on credit card gambling. Here's what Irish players need to know:

What's Banned?

Using a credit card (Visa Credit, Mastercard Credit, American Express, or any other credit facility) to make deposits at online casinos, betting sites, bingo sites, or any other gambling operator. This includes credit cards added to digital wallets like Apple Pay or Google Pay.

What's Still Allowed?

Debit cards (Visa Debit, Mastercard Debit) remain fully allowed. Prepaid cards, neobank cards (Revolut, N26), and prepaid vouchers like Paysafecard are also permitted. E-wallets such as PayPal and Skrill/Neteller remain available.

Why Was It Introduced?

The credit card ban is a responsible gambling measure designed to prevent people from gambling with borrowed money. Research has shown that credit card gamblers are more likely to develop problem gambling habits, as they can accumulate debt quickly without the immediate financial impact of spending their own funds. The UK introduced a similar ban in 2020, and Ireland has followed suit as part of its broader gambling regulation overhaul.

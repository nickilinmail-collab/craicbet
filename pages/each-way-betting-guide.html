Each-Way Betting Explained: What It Means and When to Use It

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

What is an each-way bet?

An each-way bet is two bets in one: a win bet and a place bet, each for the same stake and at the same odds. If your selection wins, both parts pay. If it places without winning, only the place part pays — at a fraction (typically 1/4 or 1/5) of the win odds. A €5 each-way bet costs €10 total. It is not insurance against a loss — it is a second bet that returns a reduced payout on a narrower set of outcomes.

Place terms and how they work

The fraction (1/4 or 1/5 of the win odds) and number of places depend on the market and field size. In a horse race with 5–7 runners, three places at 1/4 odds is typical. In a 16+ runner handicap such as a festival handicap, four or five places at 1/4 may apply. In golf outrights, 1/5 odds for a top-5 or top-8 finish is common. Always check the terms for each specific market before placing.

When each-way betting makes sense

Each-way is most sensible when backing longer-priced selections where the place part represents genuine value. At 10/1 or longer, the place return at 1/4 (2.5/1) covers a meaningful portion of the win stake. At shorter prices — below 5/1 — the place return barely covers costs and you’d often be better off backing the selection to win outright or not at all. Each-way at 7/4 is rarely a good use of a double stake.

Worked example from a festival handicap

You back a horse at 14/1 each-way, €5 each-way (€10 total), with 1/4 odds for top 4 places. If it wins: €5 × 15 = €75 (win), plus €5 × 4.5 = €22.50 (place) = €97.50 return. If it places third: only the place part returns €22.50. If it finishes fifth: both bets lose, €10 gone. The maths shows why longer-priced each-way bets return meaningful place income, and shorter-priced ones often do not.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is an each-way bet paid out?

An each-way bet consists of two equal bets: a win bet and a place bet. If your selection wins, both bets pay — the win bet at full odds plus one (decimal) and the place bet at the fraction of those odds. If it only places, the win bet loses and the place bet pays at the fraction of the win odds.

What does 1/4 odds a place mean?

The place part of your each-way bet is paid at one quarter (1/4) of the win odds. So if your selection is priced at 12/1, the place part pays at 3/1 (12 divided by 4). The number of places covered depends on field size and the specific race or market terms.

Is each-way betting worth it in golf?

Golf outrights are one of the most popular each-way betting markets because fields are large (60–156 players) and place terms can extend to top 5 or top 8. At 25/1 or longer, the place return at 1/5 odds for a top-5 finish represents meaningful value compared to a straight win bet.

Each-Way Betting: Complete Irish Guide

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

An each-way (EW) bet is two equal bets in one: a win bet and a place bet. If your selection wins, both parts pay. If it finishes in a placing position but not first, only the place part pays — at a fraction of the win odds. Each-way betting is central to horse racing in Ireland, but it also applies to golf, football outrights and other multi-runner markets.

How Each-Way Betting Works

When you place a €10 each-way bet, you are placing €10 on the win and €10 on the place — a total outlay of €20. The win part pays the full price if your selection wins. The place part pays a fraction of the win odds (typically 1/4 or 1/5) if your selection finishes within a specified number of positions.

The key term to understand is place terms: how many positions are paid and at what fraction of the win odds. These are set by the bookmaker and displayed alongside the market. Standard place terms for horse racing are:

Race type & runnersPaid placesPlace fraction
Non-handicap, 5–7 runnersTop 21/4 odds
Non-handicap, 8+ runnersTop 31/4 odds
Handicap, 12–15 runnersTop 31/4 odds
Handicap, 16+ runnersTop 41/4 odds
Some big festivalsTop 4–51/5 odds

Always check the specific place terms before betting — they are shown on the bet slip and can differ between bookmakers, especially for major festival races.

Worked Example: Horse Racing Each-Way

You back a horse at 10/1 (decimal 11.0) each-way at €5 each-way (€10 total stake), and the race is a 16-runner handicap with standard 1/4 odds, top 4 paid.

Scenario 1 — Horse wins:

  • Win part: €5 × 11.0 = €55.00 return (profit €50)
  • Place part: 10/1 × 1/4 = 2.5/1 (decimal 3.5); €5 × 3.5 = €17.50 return (profit €12.50)
  • Total return: €72.50 on a €10 stake — profit €62.50

Scenario 2 — Horse finishes 3rd (places but does not win):

  • Win part: €5 lost
  • Place part: €5 × 3.5 = €17.50 return (profit €12.50 on the place portion)
  • Total return: €17.50 on a €10 stake — net profit €7.50

Scenario 3 — Horse finishes 5th (outside the places):

  • Both bets lost. Total loss: €10.

When Each-Way Value Exists

Each-way betting is not always good value. The golden rule: the longer the price, the more attractive each-way becomes. Backing a 2/1 favourite each-way at 1/4 odds means your place portion pays just 4/1 on a 2-bet outlay — you need the horse to win anyway to generate a meaningful return. By contrast, backing a 16/1 shot each-way means the place portion at 1/4 odds pays 4/1 — a worthwhile standalone return even if the horse only places.

Big-field handicaps (16+ runners) are where each-way betting is most popular and most logical in Ireland: four paid places provide a wider safety net, and the competitive nature of large-field handicaps means prices tend to be longer across the board. Cheltenham, Punchestown and Galway festival handicaps are classic each-way markets.

Golf is the other major each-way sport. Majors and WGC events typically offer top-5 or top-8 place terms at 1/4 or 1/5 odds. Backing an outsider at 50/1 each-way (top 8, 1/5 odds) means the place part pays 10/1 — meaningful money on a small stake if your pick makes a Sunday run.

Football outrights (league winners, top-4 finishes) are sometimes offered each-way. Terms and value vary significantly by bookmaker.

Each-Way Accumulators and Free Bets

An each-way accumulator doubles the number of bets and applies each-way terms to every leg. A 4-fold each-way accumulator is actually 2 accumulators: one requiring all 4 to win, one requiring all 4 to place. The place acca pays reduced each-way returns on all legs if all 4 place. If even one leg is unplaced, both bets in that leg lose — meaning the accumulator is bust.

Each-way and free bets: Most free bets are stakes-not-returned (SNR), meaning the stake is not returned on a winning bet. On an each-way free bet, the SNR structure usually applies to the win part only. Some bookmakers split the free bet across both legs; others apply it only to the win leg and require a cash stake on the place leg. Check the specific terms before using a free bet each-way — the value calculation changes significantly depending on how the operator handles it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does each-way mean in betting?

Each-way (EW) is two separate bets of equal size: one on the selection to win, and one on it to place (finish in a specified position). If your selection wins, both bets pay. If it places but does not win, only the place part pays at reduced odds.

What are typical each-way place terms for horse racing?

For non-handicap races with 5–7 runners, place terms are usually 1/4 odds for top 2. For non-handicaps with 8+ runners, 1/4 odds top 3. For handicaps with 16+ runners, 1/4 odds top 4. Some big handicaps may use 1/5 odds. Always check before placing.

Does each-way betting cost double the stated stake?

Yes. An each-way bet is two equal bets. If you place a €5 each-way bet, you are staking €5 on the win and €5 on the place, so the total outlay is €10.

When does each-way value exist?

Each-way value is most likely with big-field handicap races (16+ runners) where the place terms cover 4 positions, and with longer-priced selections (10/1 and above) where the reduced place odds still represent a worthwhile payout. Backing 2/1 favourites each-way is rarely good value.

How does each-way affect a free bet?

Most free bets are stakes-not-returned (SNR). On an each-way free bet, the SNR structure usually applies to the win part only. Check whether the free bet covers both legs before using it each-way, as the terms differ between operators.

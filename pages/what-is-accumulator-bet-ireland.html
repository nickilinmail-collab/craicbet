What Is an Accumulator Bet? Acca Explained for Beginners Ireland

An accumulator (acca) is a single bet that combines four or more selections, where every selection must win for the bet to pay out. The odds multiply together, so a €5 acca on four 2/1 picks returns €405 — but if even one selection loses, the whole bet loses. Doubles and trebles work the same way with two or three selections. Accas offer big returns from small stakes, at a much lower probability of winning.

The accumulator — or "acca" — is Ireland's most popular bet type. Walk into any pub during a Saturday football afternoon and you will hear people checking their phones: "Three legs up, just the Manchester City game to go." The appeal is obvious: combine four modest-odds selections and suddenly you are potentially looking at a triple-figure return from a small stake. This guide explains exactly how accas work, how to calculate the return, and the honest maths behind why they are so heavily promoted by bookmakers.

What Is an Accumulator Bet?

An accumulator combines two or more individual selections into a single bet. The critical feature: all selections must win. If even one leg loses, the entire bet is lost. In exchange for this all-or-nothing structure, the potential return multiplies dramatically because the winnings from each leg are rolled onto the next.

Terminology by number of selections:

  • 2 selections = Double
  • 3 selections = Treble
  • 4+ selections = Accumulator (Acca)

Most bookmakers use "acca" colloquially for anything from a double upward, though technically a double and treble have specific names.

How to Calculate an Acca Payout

The calculation uses decimal odds. Multiply all the decimal odds together, then multiply by your stake.

Example: Four selections at 1.80, 2.10, 1.60, and 2.50. Combined: 1.80 × 2.10 × 1.60 × 2.50 = 15.12. Stake €10 × 15.12 = €151.20 total return (€141.20 profit).

Converting fractional to decimal first: 4/5 = 1.80, Evens = 2.0, 7/4 = 2.75. Then multiply.

That €10 producing €141 profit is exactly why accas are so popular. It is also why bookmakers love selling them: each leg's margin multiplies into the combined price, and the probability of all four legs winning is much lower than it feels.

The Honest Maths Behind Accas

Bookmakers build a margin into every individual market — typically 4–8% on major football matches. That margin compounds in an acca: a 5% margin on each of four legs gives an approximate combined margin of 1 − (0.95)⁴ ≈ 19%. In practical terms, your 4-fold acca is a roughly 19% disadvantage before any results are known.

Compare this to placing four separate singles: you face the 5% margin four times, but independently — each single stands on its own, and four wins means four independent profits. The expected return on four singles is significantly better than on a 4-fold acca from the same selections. The acca concentrates risk; the singles spread it.

This is why serious value bettors rarely use accas as their primary strategy. The entertainment value is real — a small stake, a big upside — but as a systematic approach to growing a bankroll, the compounded margin works against you. Use accas for what they are: a fun lottery-style punt, with a stake you are entirely comfortable losing.

Types of Accumulator and Related Bets

Beyond the standard acca, there are full-cover bets that place every possible combination of selections:

  • Trixie: 3 selections, all doubles and a treble (4 bets total)
  • Patent: 3 selections, all singles, doubles and treble (7 bets)
  • Yankee: 4 selections, all doubles, trebles and 4-fold (11 bets)
  • Lucky 15: 4 selections, all singles, doubles, trebles and 4-fold (15 bets)

These cover bets give a return even if only some selections win, but they cost more in total stakes. See our full cover bets guide for details.

Acca Insurance: What It Actually Means

Acca insurance — refunding your stake if exactly one leg fails — sounds appealing. The reality: the refund is typically a free bet (winnings only, stake not returned) rather than cash, it is usually capped, and it requires minimum odds per leg. It softens one specific losing scenario while leaving the compounded bookmaker margin untouched. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an accumulator bet?

An accumulator combines two or more selections into a single bet. All selections must win for the bet to pay out. Winnings from each leg roll onto the next, multiplying the potential return.

What is the minimum number of selections in an acca?

Technically two (a double). Three is a treble. Four or more is typically called an accumulator. Most bookmakers use 'acca' colloquially for any multi-selection bet.

What happens if one leg of my acca loses?

The entire accumulator loses. Every selection must win for an acca to pay out. One losing leg — however unlikely — wipes the whole bet.

Can I place an acca on the same match?

No. Standard accumulator rules require each leg to be from a different event. Multiple selections from the same match is called a Bet Builder and operates under different rules.

Why do bookmakers offer acca insurance?

Acca insurance is a marketing tool. It typically refunds your stake as a free bet if exactly one leg fails, usually capped and conditional on minimum odds. The compounded bookmaker margin on the acca itself is unchanged.

Accumulator Example: 5-team Acca

Backing 5 football matches, each at 2.00 (evens), €10 stake:

  • All 5 win: €10 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 × 2 = €320
  • 4 of 5 win: total loss (€10 gone) — the fourth leg kills the acca
  • The combined probability (at fair odds): 50% × 50% × 50% × 50% × 50% = 3.1%
  • With bookmaker margin (~5% per leg): combined chance of winning drops to ~2.5%
  • The expected value is negative — but the high potential return is the appeal

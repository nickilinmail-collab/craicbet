GRAI Deposit Limit Rules: What Betting Sites Must Offer Irish Customers

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, GRAI-licensed betting sites are legally required to offer deposit limits to Irish customers. This is not an optional feature — it is a mandatory licensing condition. This page explains what operators must provide, what they are forbidden from doing, and how to use deposit limits effectively.

What Operators Must Offer: The Basics

Every GRAI-licensed betting site must offer all customers the ability to set:

  • Daily deposit limits — a maximum amount you can deposit in any 24-hour period.
  • Weekly deposit limits — a maximum over a rolling 7-day period.
  • Monthly deposit limits — a maximum over a rolling 30-day period.

These must be accessible from your account settings and clearly signposted. GRAI requires that limit-setting is not buried in obscure menus or made unduly difficult to find — this is part of the prohibition on "dark patterns" in gambling platform design.

Immediate Reductions, Delayed Increases

One of the most important design requirements for GRAI-compliant deposit limits is the asymmetry between reductions and increases:

  • Reductions take effect immediately. If you set a lower deposit limit, it applies instantly — not from your next session or tomorrow. You cannot bypass it by depositing quickly before the change is processed.
  • Increases require a mandatory cooling-off period. If you request a higher deposit limit, the increase does not activate until a waiting period has elapsed. This waiting period is set by GRAI licensing conditions and is designed to prevent an impulsive decision during a losing streak from undoing a limit you set when thinking clearly.

This asymmetry is a evidence-based design principle. Research on problem gambling intervention consistently shows that cooling-off periods for limit increases are one of the most effective technical safeguards.

What Operators Cannot Do

GRAI licensing conditions are not just about what operators must offer — they also prohibit specific practices related to deposit limits:

  • Operators cannot market to limit-setters. If you have set a deposit limit, the operator cannot send you marketing communications encouraging you to increase it, deposit more, or take advantage of "exclusive" higher-tier offers.
  • Operators cannot use dark patterns. Techniques designed to make limit-setting difficult, confusing or discouraging are prohibited. This includes things like making the "set a limit" button harder to find than "increase your deposit," multi-step processes to set a limit when removing one requires a single click, or using language that frames limits negatively.
  • Operators cannot allow limit bypass via payment method switching. Deposit limits apply account-wide, regardless of how you are depositing. You cannot work around a limit by using a different card or payment service.
  • Operators cannot ignore affordability signals. Where spending patterns suggest financial stress, operators are expected to reach out rather than simply processing deposits up to the technical limit.

Deposit Limits and Responsible Gambling Tools Together

Deposit limits work alongside other GRAI-mandated tools:

  • Session time limits can cap how long you spend betting in one sitting, independent of how much you have deposited.
  • Reality checks provide mid-session notifications showing your net win/loss and time spent.
  • Self-exclusion via the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) can completely block your access to all licensed sites if you need a more significant break.

These tools are most effective when used together. A deposit limit prevents overspending but does not stop you from spending your entire limit in one session; session time limits address that separately. See our page on GRAI responsible gambling requirements for the full picture.

Deposit Limits on Unlicensed Sites

Unlicensed offshore sites are not required to offer deposit limits at all. Some do, as a voluntary feature — but there is no enforcement mechanism if they fail to implement them properly or quietly remove them. If a limit-setting feature on an unlicensed site is broken or if the site processes deposits over your limit, you have no regulatory recourse.

This is one concrete practical difference between GRAI-licensed and unlicensed betting sites that directly affects your ability to control your spending. See our guide to unlicensed betting site risks for more.

How to Set Deposit Limits on Your Account

The process is specific to each operator's platform, but under GRAI rules it should be accessible from your account settings or responsible gambling section. If you cannot find it:

  • Search your account settings for "deposit limit," "responsible gambling" or "player controls."
  • Contact the operator's support team and ask them to walk you through it — they are required to assist.
  • If the site makes this unreasonably difficult, that may be a reportable issue to GRAI via their complaints process at grai.ie.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are deposit limits mandatory on GRAI-licensed betting sites?

Yes. All GRAI-licensed betting operators must offer customers the ability to set daily, weekly and monthly deposit limits. This is a mandatory licensing condition.

Can a betting site refuse to let me set a deposit limit?

No. A GRAI-licensed operator cannot refuse to let you set a deposit limit. If a site is making it difficult, that is a potential regulatory breach reportable to GRAI.

How quickly does a deposit limit take effect?

Reductions take effect immediately. Increases require a cooling-off period before activating, to prevent impulsive decisions from undermining limits you set when thinking clearly.

Can operators market higher limits to customers who have set a limit?

No. GRAI licensing conditions prohibit operators from marketing increased deposit limits or higher-spending options to customers who have already set a deposit limit.

Do deposit limits apply to all payment methods?

Yes. Limits set on a GRAI-licensed account apply account-wide, regardless of payment method. Operators cannot allow customers to bypass limits by switching payment methods.

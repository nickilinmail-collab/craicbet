The Curragh Racecourse Betting: Irish Classics and Flat Racing

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

About The Curragh

The Curragh, situated in Co. Kildare on the great limestone plain at the heart of Ireland's bloodstock country, is the home of Irish flat racing. It is the country's premier flat racecourse and has hosted the Irish Classics — the five most important flat races in Ireland — for well over a century. For punters, it represents the highest level of flat race betting that Irish racing has to offer.

The racecourse underwent a major redevelopment and reopened in 2019 with a new grandstand. It stages flat racing only; there is no National Hunt racing at The Curragh. The surrounding plains of Kildare are home to some of Ireland's most famous training yards and stud farms, which means local knowledge runs deep and market moves at Curragh meetings can be meaningful indicators of trainer confidence.

The Curragh's flat-only calendar runs from spring through to October, with the five Irish Classic races spread across the summer months. It also stages several other Group 1 races including the Irish Champion Stakes (now run at Leopardstown in September) and international pattern races that attract high-class runners from Britain and France.

The Irish Classics at The Curragh

The five Irish Classic races form the backbone of The Curragh's prestige and are the centrepiece of the flat racing betting calendar in Ireland:

  • Irish 2,000 Guineas (1 mile, colts and fillies, late May): The opening Irish Classic of the season, often featuring runners that have already contested the English 2,000 Guineas at Newmarket.
  • Irish 1,000 Guineas (1 mile, fillies only, late May): Run on the same weekend as the Irish 2,000 Guineas, this is the premier mile Classic for fillies in Ireland.
  • Irish Derby (1 mile 4 furlongs, colts and fillies, late June/early July): The most valuable Classic in Ireland and one of the most prestigious races in European flat racing. Ante-post markets open months in advance.
  • Irish Oaks (1 mile 4 furlongs, fillies, July): Run the day after or the weekend of the Irish Derby, it is the premier middle-distance Classic for fillies.
  • Irish St Leger (1 mile 6 furlongs, September): The staying Classic, often attracting older horses as well as Classic generation runners, and a key market for staying handicap form.

For punters, the Classics represent some of the most liquid ante-post markets of the entire flat season. Prices are available from early spring and move significantly as trial form is established. The Irish Derby in particular draws strong international interest and is closely watched by ante-post bookmakers.

Track Characteristics

The Curragh is a right-handed, wide galloping track. The course is essentially flat with a gradual undulation but no significant rises or falls that would disadvantage horses in the way a hilly track would. The long straight finish — a feature of the course — means that front-runners can be caught in the closing stages by powerful, late-finishing horses, and the best at the trip tend to come through on merit.

The ground at The Curragh can vary considerably across the season. Early spring meetings can see soft or heavy ground on the Kildare plain, while summer months produce the firm or good-to-firm conditions that many of the best flat horses prefer. The Irish Derby in late June is typically run on good or good-to-firm ground in a normal year, though Irish weather is never guaranteed. Always check the going report before betting.

The wide track means that jockey placement and draw can matter. In sprint races particularly, early positions out of the stalls can be significant. The draw statistics at The Curragh are worth reviewing — in soft ground conditions, a low draw has historically been an advantage in sprint distances, while on fast ground the bias is less pronounced.

Betting on Curragh Flat Racing

Flat racing at The Curragh rewards punters who understand trainer and jockey patterns. The major Kildare yards — among the most powerful in Irish racing — typically give their Classic generation horses a trial run before the big days, and form from early-season meetings at The Curragh carries real weight into the Classics. Horses that have shown ability on the track tend to retain that edge.

For ante-post Classic betting, the approach that has historically offered value is identifying horses that showed significant ability in juvenile (two-year-old) races the previous season and then following their spring trial performances. Winning a Classic trial well, with market support, is a stronger ante-post signal than a narrow win with stable connections quiet in the market.

For in-running and day-of markets, the size of the Curragh fields matters. The Irish Derby can attract fields of 10 or more, with runners from several countries. Each-way terms are important — always check the number of places being paid and at what fraction of the win odds. For the Classics, most GRAI-licensed bookmakers will offer 1/4 odds for top 3 or top 4 places, but confirm before betting.

Frequently Asked Questions

What races make up the Irish Classics at The Curragh?

The five Irish Classics are the Irish 2,000 Guineas, Irish 1,000 Guineas, Irish Derby, Irish Oaks and Irish St Leger. The Guineas races are run in late May, the Derby and Oaks in June/July and the St Leger in September.

Is The Curragh right-handed or left-handed?

The Curragh is a right-handed track. It is a wide, galloping course in Co. Kildare with a long straight finish that suits powerful, strong-staying flat horses.

Does The Curragh host jump racing?

No. The Curragh is a flat-racing only venue. It does not host National Hunt racing. For jump racing in Kildare, Punchestown is the county's major National Hunt track.

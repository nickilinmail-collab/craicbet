Best Betting Apps in Ireland 2026

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The majority of online betting in Ireland now happens on mobile. For most punters, the app is the product — a slow, cluttered or crash-prone app will cost you in-play opportunities and push you to the desktop for anything that requires precision. This guide explains what separates a good betting app from a mediocre one, what to check for Irish GRAI licensing, and what responsible gambling tools the law now requires.

What to Look For in a Betting App

Speed and stability: In-play betting on a football match or a horse race requires near-instant odds refresh and bet acceptance. Apps that freeze, crash or fail to load markets at peak times (Saturday 3pm, festival days) are a practical problem, not just an annoyance. Look for apps with a consistent track record on the App Store or Google Play reviews, specifically on match-day performance.

In-play betting interface: The in-play section of a betting app is used under time pressure. A well-designed app surfaces the live markets for active games clearly, makes it easy to switch between sports, and shows a live scoreboard alongside the odds. A poorly designed app buries in-play markets behind menus and makes placing a bet in-play take too many taps.

Cash-out: Cash-out allows you to settle a bet before the event ends, locking in a profit or reducing a loss. Partial cash-out lets you close part of the position while leaving the rest running. These features need to work reliably on mobile — a cash-out button that fails to respond during a key moment in a game is a significant functional failure. See our cash-out guide for the maths.

Payments: Apple Pay, Google Pay, Revolut and debit cards should be available for instant deposits. Withdrawal speeds matter most: most licensed apps process withdrawals in 1–3 business days; some offer faster routes. Under GRAI rules, credit cards are banned for gambling deposits.

iOS vs Android: What Changes

iOS (iPhone/iPad): Most GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer iOS apps through the App Store. The App Store review process means apps must meet Apple's guidelines, which include restrictions on gambling apps (available only in permitted countries). Irish users can download gambling apps from the Irish App Store, but some operators list their apps in limited App Store regions. If an app does not appear in search, check the operator's website for a direct link to the App Store listing.

Android: Google Play has historically been more restrictive about betting apps in some regions, so some operators offer Android APK downloads directly from their websites rather than through the Play Store. Installing an APK from a third-party source is a security consideration — only download APKs from GRAI-licensed operators' official websites, not from third-party app stores.

Responsible Gambling Features in Apps

Under GRAI licensing, bookmakers must provide responsible gambling tools that are genuinely accessible — not hidden behind six menus. The required tools include deposit limits, session time limits, loss limits, reality check reminders (pop-ups showing how long you have been active), and self-exclusion options. These must be accessible within the app itself.

An app that buries deposit limits behind multiple menu levels, or makes self-exclusion a lengthy process, is not meeting the spirit of the GRAI requirements. If you cannot find the responsible gambling section of an app within two taps from the account menu, that is worth noting before depositing.

Data Usage and Betting on the Go

In-play betting with live scoreboards and streaming can use significant mobile data, particularly during long events like horse racing festivals or cricket. If you use a capped data plan, check your usage on festival days. Most betting apps allow you to disable live video streaming while keeping the in-play market feed active, which significantly reduces data consumption.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I download betting apps on iPhone in Ireland?

Yes. GRAI-licensed bookmakers can offer iOS apps through the App Store. Some operators also offer a web app through the mobile browser. Check whether the specific app is available in the Irish App Store.

Can I use Apple Pay on betting apps in Ireland?

Some GRAI-licensed betting apps accept Apple Pay for deposits. Availability varies by operator. Under GRAI rules, credit cards are banned for gambling deposits, but Apple Pay linked to a debit card is generally permitted — check each operator's terms.

How do I check if a betting app is GRAI-licensed?

Find the legal company name in the app's footer or settings, then search the GRAI public register at grai.ie. Look for a licence containing RB (Remote Betting) with Active status. CraicBet tracks the GRAI register daily on our GRAI tracker.

What responsible gambling features should a betting app have?

GRAI-licensed apps must offer deposit limits, session time limits, session loss limits, reality check reminders and self-exclusion. These tools must be accessible within the app itself.

Is in-play betting available on Irish betting apps?

Yes. Most GRAI-licensed apps offer in-play betting on major sports including football, tennis and horse racing. In-play markets on mobile are updated in near real-time. See our in-play guide for how to use them effectively.

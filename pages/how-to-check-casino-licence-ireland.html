How to Check a Casino Licence in Ireland — The 2-Minute Guide

⚠️ No GRAI casino licence exists in Ireland yet. GRAI has issued remote betting licences only since July 2026. Online casino licences are expected 2027–2028. What this means for you →

💡 Key Takeaways

  • Checking a casino licence takes about two minutes once you know where to look
  • The GRAI public register at grai.ie is the only official source for Irish licences
  • As of October 2026, the GRAI issues remote betting licences only — no online casino holds a GRAI licence yet
  • Online casinos serving Irish players operate on offshore licences — always verify them at the issuing regulator
  • A licence number in a footer means nothing until you confirm it in the regulator's own register

Why Bother Checking a Licence?

A licence is the single strongest signal that a casino answers to somebody other than itself. A licensed operator must verify ages, keep player funds separate from company money, publish fair terms and submit to dispute resolution. An unlicensed one promises all of that — and answers to no one if it breaks the promise.

Checking takes two minutes. Losing a withdrawal to an unaccountable operator takes months of frustration, usually with no result. The maths is simple.

Step 1: Find the Licence Claim (30 seconds)

Scroll to the very bottom of the casino's homepage. Licensed operators state the regulator, the holding company and a licence number in the footer. You are looking for three things:

  • The regulator's name — e.g. Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), Curaçao Gaming Control Board, Gibraltar Gambling Division
  • The legal entity — the company name, which often differs from the brand name
  • The licence number — a reference you can look up

No regulator named at all? Stop here. That is your answer.

Step 2: Verify at the Source (60 seconds)

A footer claim is just text on a page anyone can type. The verification happens at the regulator's own register:

Step 3: Check What the Licence Actually Covers (30 seconds)

This is the step most players skip, and in Ireland right now it matters more than anywhere else. A licence covers specific activities. The GRAI began issuing licences on 1 July 2026 — but only for remote betting (sports bookmaking). Gaming licences, which would cover online casino play, are expected in a later phase.

⚠️ The key fact for October 2026

No online casino holds a GRAI licence, because the GRAI does not issue casino licences yet. Any casino claiming a current “GRAI casino licence” is misrepresenting itself — treat that as a red flag, not a reassurance. Casinos serving Irish players legitimately do so on offshore licences, and the honest ones say so plainly.

Red Flags That End the Check Early

  • No regulator named anywhere on the site
  • A licence seal image that is not clickable and appears nowhere in a register
  • A licence number that returns a different company in the register
  • Claims of a licence type that does not exist (like a GRAI casino licence in 2026)
  • Ireland listed in the restricted countries section of the terms — your winnings can be voided even if you can register

What Happens If You Play at an Unlicensed Casino?

Irish law places regulatory obligations on operators, not on players. You will not be prosecuted for using an unlicensed casino. The practical risks fall on you in other ways:

  • No dispute resolution path: GRAI complaint procedures and ADR schemes only apply to licensed operators. If an unlicensed site voids your winnings or holds your withdrawal, you have no formal recourse in Ireland.
  • No player fund protection: Licensed operators must hold player funds in segregated accounts. Unlicensed sites have no such requirement — if the site disappears, so does your balance.
  • No KYC or age-gate guarantee: Unlicensed sites may have no meaningful identity checks, making them attractive to younger players who should not be gambling at all.
  • Terms can be changed without notice: With no regulator to enforce fair terms, wagering requirements, withdrawal limits and bonus conditions can be amended retroactively.

FAQ

Is it legal for Irish players to use offshore-licensed casinos?

Irish players face no penalties for playing at offshore-licensed casinos. The regulatory obligations fall on operators, not players. That said, playing at a verified, licensed operator — whatever the jurisdiction — is always safer than an unlicensed one.

When will the GRAI license online casinos?

Gaming licence applications are expected in a later phase of the GRAI rollout. Published timelines suggest 2027–2028. No casino licences have been issued as of November 2026 — check grai.ie for current status.

The casino shows an 18+ logo and responsible gambling links. Is that enough?

No. Those are good signs, but they are decorations without a verifiable licence behind them. Always complete the register check at the regulator's own website.

Can a casino have a GRAI casino licence right now (November 2026)?

No. The GRAI has only issued remote betting licences since 1 July 2026. Casino (gaming) licences are a later phase of the rollout, expected from 2027–2028. Any casino claiming a current GRAI casino licence is making a false claim.

How do I find the GRAI public licence register?

Go to grai.ie and navigate to the public licence register. Search by operator or company name. The register shows only active licences and the type of activity covered (betting, not casino, as of late 2026).

What if my casino is not in any register?

That means the licence claim in the footer is unverified. This is a serious red flag. Consider withdrawing your balance and moving to a verifiably licensed operator. No licence = no recourse if something goes wrong.

Why do Irish bettors need an odds converter?

Irish betting is a mix of formats. Horse racing odds — from the racecourse to the betting slip — are still quoted in fractional format: 5/2, 11/4, 7/1. Football and most online sportsbooks switched to decimal odds years ago, while US sports markets like the NBA and NFL use American odds (also called moneyline). Switching between formats is easy with the converter above, but it's also worth understanding the maths behind each — particularly implied probability, which tells you whether a price represents value.

Fractional odds explained

Fractional odds show profit relative to stake. At 5/2, a €2 stake wins €5 profit — total return €7. The left number (numerator) is what you win; the right (denominator) is what you risk. Odds of 1/2 are "odds-on" — you stake €2 to win €1. Evens (1/1) means stake equals profit. This format has been used in Irish and British horse racing for generations and is still the default on course and in some high-street shops. Converting to decimal (divide numerator by denominator, add 1) makes multi-bet calculations much simpler.

Decimal odds explained

Decimal odds show your total return per €1 staked, including the stake itself. At 3.50, a €10 bet returns €35 (€25 profit + €10 stake). The profit alone is decimal minus 1: 3.50 − 1 = 2.50, so €25 on a €10 bet. Decimal odds are now standard on nearly all Irish online sportsbooks for football, tennis, basketball and international markets. They make accumulator maths straightforward — just multiply the decimals together. Most Irish bettors find decimals quicker to work with than fractionals for calculating returns.

American odds explained

American odds use a +/− system based on a €100 reference point. Positive odds (e.g. +250) show profit on a €100 stake: +250 means €250 profit, return €350. Negative odds (e.g. −150) show how much you must stake to win €100: −150 means stake €150, win €100, return €250. American odds are the standard for US sports — NBA, NFL, MLB — and are increasingly common on Irish sportsbooks that cover American markets. Converting to decimal: for +X, divide by 100 and add 1; for −X, divide 100 by the absolute value and add 1.

Implied probability: the most important number

Implied probability converts odds into the percentage chance the bookmaker has priced in. The formula is 100 ÷ decimal odds. At 2.00, implied probability is 50%. At 4.00, it's 25%. Because bookmakers build in a margin, the implied probabilities across all outcomes in a market add up to more than 100% — typically 103–110%. That excess is the bookmaker's edge. By comparing implied probability to your own assessment of a team's chances, you can identify when a price may represent genuine value. Frequently Asked Questions

What is 5/2 in decimal odds?

3.50 — divide 5 by 2 (= 2.50), then add 1. A €10 bet at 5/2 returns €35 (€25 profit + €10 stake back).

What does +200 mean in betting?

A profit of €200 on a €100 stake, total return €300. In decimal odds this is 3.00; in fractional odds it is 2/1.

What are evens in decimal odds?

2.00 — a €10 bet returns €20 (€10 profit plus €10 stake). Evens is 1/1 in fractional format and +100 in American odds.

How do I convert fractional to decimal odds?

Divide the numerator by the denominator, then add 1. For 5/2: 5 ÷ 2 = 2.50, then 2.50 + 1 = 3.50 decimal.

What is implied probability in betting?

100 divided by decimal odds. At 2.00 decimal the implied probability is 50%. It tells you what chance the bookmaker has priced into the odds — compare it to your own assessment to judge value.

Outright Betting Ireland: League Winner & Tournament Tips

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Outright betting — also called futures or ante-post betting — means placing a bet on the winner of a competition or tournament before it concludes. The classic examples are backing a team to win the Premier League or Champions League before the season starts. Odds are typically longer than in-match markets because uncertainty is higher, but the potential returns over a full season can be substantial.

How outright markets work

Outright markets are available from before the season or tournament starts and are updated throughout the competition as results accumulate. The key difference from match betting is timing: an outright placed before the season starts is settled at the end, regardless of how results unfold along the way. If you back a team at 10.0 before the season and they go on to win the league, you collect at 10.0 regardless of how short they may have drifted mid-season. Some bookmakers offer each-way terms on outright markets, paying for the top four, top two, or top finisher depending on the event.

Ante-post risk: each-way terms and withdrawals

A critical consideration in outright betting is what happens when a team or player withdraws before the event concludes. For horse racing ante-post bets, non-runners are typically not refunded (this is a standard ante-post condition). For football outright bets, most bookmakers will refund stakes if the competition is abandoned. Each-way outright bets can be valuable when each-way terms are generous relative to the implied probability of the top-N finish. Checking current each-way terms before placing — and comparing between bookmakers — is worthwhile.

League winner vs top-four markets

League winner markets concentrate risk on a single outcome over nine months. Top-four or top-six finish markets offer more achievable targets and are priced accordingly. For teams with realistic but not certain title ambitions, top-four outright markets often represent better value than win-only markets because the multiple outcomes covered spread the probability. This is particularly relevant for mid-table challengers in the Premier League or Champions League qualification races.

Tournament betting: cups and international competitions

Outright markets on knockout tournaments such as the FA Cup, Champions League, and international tournaments behave differently from league markets. Early upsets eliminate fancied teams, so backing long-priced sides in cup outrights at 20.0–50.0 is a common speculative strategy. Frequently Asked Questions

What is outright betting in football?

Outright betting means backing a team, player or outcome to win a competition or tournament (such as a league title or cup) before it concludes. Bets are settled at the end of the competition at the price available when the bet was placed.

Can you cash out an outright bet?

Many bookmakers offer cash out on outright markets, allowing you to settle the bet before the competition ends. Cash out values fluctuate based on the team's current odds. This can be useful to lock in a profit or limit a loss if the team's prospects change significantly mid-season.

Are each-way terms available on outright football bets?

Yes, many bookmakers offer each-way terms on football outright markets such as league winner or top-four finishes. Terms vary by bookmaker and competition. For large competitions, 1/4 odds for the top 4 is common. Always check the specific terms before placing.

When is the best time to place an outright bet?

Pre-season prices tend to be widest (longest odds), reflecting maximum uncertainty. As the season progresses, prices for leaders shorten and those trailing lengthen. Placing outrights early on strong contenders who you believe will improve offers the best value, but carries the longest time horizon and highest uncertainty.

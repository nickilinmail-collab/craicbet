AML Verification at Irish Betting Sites: What to Expect

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

AML (Anti-Money Laundering) verification is a legal requirement for all GRAI-licensed betting and gambling operators in Ireland. It is separate from standard KYC (Know Your Customer) identity checks and may be triggered at higher spend levels or when operators detect unusual transaction patterns.

AML Documents Commonly Requested

  • Source of funds: recent payslips (last 3 months), employment contract, or business accounts if self-employed
  • Bank statement: showing sufficient funds and income consistent with your gambling activity
  • Source of wealth: for high-value accounts — may include investment statements, inheritance documentation, property sale records
  • All documents must be clear, unedited, and in name matching your registered account details

What Happens If You Decline AML Verification

Under GRAI rules and the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, operators are legally required to perform AML checks. If you decline to provide documentation, the operator must suspend your ability to deposit and may freeze remaining account funds pending verification. Withdrawal of existing winnings is typically allowed while the check is pending, but new deposits are blocked. If the operator cannot complete verification, they may close your account and return funds via bank transfer.

Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG) in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG) is one of the most genuinely useful offers in horse racing betting — not a headline promotional number, but an automatic protection on every qualifying bet. This guide explains exactly how it works, when it applies, what it excludes, and how to compare it with enhanced odds offers at GRAI-licensed Irish bookmakers.

What Best Odds Guaranteed Means

When you take an early price on a horse — the morning line or a price displayed before the race — you are locking in odds at that moment. The Starting Price (SP) is a separate figure: the officially returned price of the horse based on the on-course market at race time. SP can be bigger (better) or smaller (shorter) than the price you took.

Without BOG, you receive your original price regardless of what SP turns out to be. With BOG, if the SP is bigger than your early price, the bookmaker pays you at the SP. If your early price was bigger than the SP, you keep your original price. Either way, you receive whichever is the better of the two.

A simple example: you back a horse at 5/1 (6.00 decimal) the morning before a race. At race time the horse drifts to 8/1 (9.00) SP. With BOG, your winning bet is paid at 8/1, not 5/1. Without BOG, you receive 5/1 regardless of the SP movement.

Why BOG Matters for Horse Racing Bettors

Horse racing prices are genuinely volatile in the hours before a race. Big-race favourites can drift significantly if late market money comes in for another runner. A horse at 5/2 on the morning of Cheltenham's Gold Cup might be 7/2 SP. For a punter who took an early price without BOG, that drift is an invisible cost. For a punter on a BOG-enabled account, it is a free upgrade.

Over the course of a season's worth of racing bets, BOG represents a meaningful improvement in return. The benefit is asymmetric — you gain when your horse drifts (gets longer), and you lose nothing when it shortens (you keep your original shorter price). The expected value contribution of BOG to a regular horse racing bettor is positive.

This is why BOG has become a standard competitive feature for bookmakers in the UK and Ireland targeting racing customers. It is not a promotional gimmick — it is a permanent feature of the account.

When BOG Applies: The Key Restrictions

BOG has consistent exclusions across most bookmakers. Understanding them prevents disappointment:

  • Ante-post markets are almost always excluded. An ante-post bet is placed before race declarations, often weeks or months in advance. SP is not applicable in the same way, so BOG does not apply. Check carefully whether a market is classified as ante-post or non-runner no bet.
  • Enhanced or boosted prices are usually excluded. If you take an individually enhanced price (above the standard market), BOG may not apply — you have already received a promotional price. The specific terms vary by operator.
  • Accumulator legs may have different terms. Some bookmakers apply BOG to each leg of an accumulator; others only offer it on singles. Check the T&Cs.
  • Coverage varies by race and jurisdiction. Most BOG offers cover UK and Irish racing. International meetings (France, US, Australia) may be excluded or have separate terms.

BOG is a commercial decision by each bookmaker, not a GRAI licensing requirement. Terms differ across operators — always read the full conditions on each site and check whether they currently offer BOG before placing.

BOG vs Enhanced Odds Offers

Enhanced odds are a different kind of offer: a bookmaker boosts a specific selection above its standard market price for promotional purposes. For example, a horse priced at 4/1 might be "enhanced" to 6/1. This enhancement often comes with a maximum stake (€5 or €10 is common) and sometimes requires the winnings to be credited as free bets rather than cash.

BOG is simpler: no maximum stake (or a much higher one), no wagering requirement, and it applies automatically to every qualifying bet. For regular horse racing bettors placing normal-sized stakes, BOG is typically the more useful feature. Enhanced odds are better for one-off large bets on headline events, as long as you read the stake cap and cash-out conditions first.

Getting the Most From BOG

The practical implication is straightforward: when you have identified a horse to back, it is generally better to take an early price on a BOG-enabled account than to wait for SP. You retain the upside if the horse drifts and are protected if it shortens. The main exception is if you have specific intelligence that the market will shorten significantly — in which case waiting for SP (or a later in-running price) may be better.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG) mean?

BOG means that if you take an early price on a horse race and the Starting Price (SP) is bigger than the price you took, the bookmaker pays you at the SP. If your early price was bigger than SP, you keep your original price — you always get the best of the two.

Does BOG apply to all horse races?

BOG typically applies to non-ante-post markets on UK and Irish horse racing. Ante-post bets are usually excluded, as are enhanced prices. Terms vary between bookmakers — always check the specific BOG conditions before placing.

What is Starting Price (SP) in horse racing?

The Starting Price (SP) is the officially returned price of a horse at race time, based on the on-course market in the final minutes before the off. It differs from morning prices and from exchange prices.

Is BOG better than enhanced odds offers?

They serve different purposes. BOG automatically protects every qualifying bet with no stake cap or wagering requirement. Enhanced odds boost a specific selection, usually with a stake cap. BOG is typically more reliable as a long-term value-add for regular horse racing bettors.

Do Irish GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer BOG?

BOG availability varies by operator — it is a commercial offering, not a GRAI licence requirement. Some GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer BOG on UK and Irish racing as a standard feature; others do not. Check the terms and conditions of each site to confirm current BOG availability.

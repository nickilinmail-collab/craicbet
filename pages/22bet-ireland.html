What Is 22Bet?

22Bet is an online bookmaker and casino platform operated by TechSolutions Group N.V., a company registered in Curaçao. The site launched in 2017 and has grown to cover a very wide range of sports, including mainstream markets such as football, horse racing, and tennis, as well as a large selection of niche sports and esports. It is particularly noted for the volume of betting markets it offers and for odds that are often competitive across a broad range of events.

22Bet accepts players from many countries and operates across multiple jurisdictions. Its primary licence is issued by the Curaçao Gaming Control Board. It does not, as of this writing, hold licences from the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC), or other European tier-one regulators. The Curaçao licence permits international operations but carries a lower standard of consumer protection compared to MGA, UKGC, or GRAI authorisation.

That said, holding no MGA or UKGC licence — let alone a GRAI licence — has significant implications for Irish players under Ireland’s new regulatory framework, which came into effect on 1 July 2026.

22Bet’s GRAI Licence Status in Ireland

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, 22Bet does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means 22Bet has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

Risks for Irish Players Using 22Bet

22Bet may be technically accessible to users with Irish IP addresses. However, accessible does not mean licensed, and there is a meaningful practical difference for players.

When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — and particularly one operating only under a Curaçao licence — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator — and with a Curaçao-only operator, the available dispute channels are more limited than with an MGA or UKGC-licensed site.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator, which in 22Bet’s case is Curaçao.

None of this is intended to suggest 22Bet has acted in bad faith — it operates in many markets without issue. But Irish players considering 22Bet should be clear that they would be operating entirely outside Irish regulatory oversight, with fewer recourse options than on a GRAI-licensed site.

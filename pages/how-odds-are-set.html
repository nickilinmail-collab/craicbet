How Bookmakers Set Odds: The Maths Behind Every Price

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

From probability to price

Every bookmaker price starts with an assessment of probability. If a bookmaker believes Team A has a 50% chance of winning, the fair (break-even) decimal odds are 2.00 (1 divided by 0.50). But the bookmaker does not offer fair odds: they apply a margin, also called the overround or vig, which ensures that the implied probabilities across all outcomes add up to more than 100%. The excess above 100% is the bookmaker’s built-in edge.

Calculating the overround

To measure the overround on a three-outcome football market, convert each price to an implied probability (1 divided by the decimal odds). If Home is 2.50 (40%), Draw is 3.20 (31.25%) and Away is 2.80 (35.7%), the total is 106.95%. The overround is 6.95%. This means for every €100 staked equally across all three outcomes, the bookmaker expects to retain €6.95 on average. This is the structural cost of betting with any bookmaker.

How odds are adjusted in real time

Bookmakers use a combination of statistical models and trading desk adjustments to manage their position. When large sums are placed on one outcome, the price on that outcome shortens and other prices may lengthen to rebalance. This is why early prices and late prices can differ significantly on high-volume events. The bookmaker’s goal is to balance liability across outcomes, not to predict the result — though sophisticated operators also model true probability to identify mispriced bets from sharp punters.

What this means for punters

Understanding the overround clarifies why consistent long-run profitability is difficult. Even if you bet correctly more often than not, the margin builds in a persistent expected loss per unit staked. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a bookmaker's margin or overround?

The margin (also called overround or vig) is the excess above 100% when all implied probabilities on a market are summed. For example, if the implied probabilities on a three-outcome football market sum to 107%, the overround is 7%. This represents the bookmaker's average expected return per €100 staked.

How do you calculate the implied probability of odds?

Divide 1 by the decimal odds. Odds of 2.50 imply a 40% probability (1 ÷ 2.50 = 0.40). Odds of 1.50 imply 66.7% (1 ÷ 1.50 = 0.667). The implied probability includes the bookmaker's margin, so the true probability the bookmaker estimates is slightly lower than the implied probability.

Do bookmakers adjust odds during a match?

Yes. In-play prices are adjusted continuously based on game state, goals, red cards and other events. Pre-match prices are also adjusted in the days before an event as news emerges (team selection, injuries) and as money comes in on specific outcomes, changing the bookmaker's liability position.

What Moves Odds: The Main Factors

  • Injury news: a star player ruled out can shorten a team's odds from 2.20 to 1.70 in minutes
  • Bet volumes: heavy money on one side shortens that side; bookmakers lay off or adjust prices
  • Sharp money: bets from known professional punters move odds faster than recreational money
  • Market alignment: bookmakers watch exchange prices (Betfair) and each other constantly
  • Taking the early price before team news is a key advantage for experienced punters

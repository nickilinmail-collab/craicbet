How to Place a Bet Online in Ireland — Complete Beginner's Guide

Placing your first bet online can feel daunting, but the process is straightforward once you know the steps. This guide walks you through everything — from picking a GRAI-licensed bookmaker to tracking your bet after it's placed. All betting covered here is legal, regulated activity under Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

Step 1: Choose a GRAI-Licensed Bookmaker

Since 1 July 2026, all online bookmakers accepting bets from Irish residents must hold a remote betting licence issued by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI). Using a licensed operator means your funds are protected and disputes can be escalated to the regulator.

Check our full list of GRAI-licensed bookmakers in Ireland 2026 before you sign up anywhere. The licence number for each operator should be visible in the site footer.

Step 2: Create an Account

Registration takes around five minutes. You'll need to provide:

  • Your full name, date of birth and address
  • A valid email address and phone number
  • Proof of identity (passport, driving licence or national ID)
  • Proof of address in some cases (utility bill, bank statement)

Age verification is a legal requirement under GRAI rules — you must be 18 or over. Some bookmakers verify instantly using a database check; others may ask you to upload documents. You cannot place bets or withdraw funds until verification is complete.

Irish context: Irish residents betting on GAA, horse racing and football will find markets on all major platforms. If you follow the All-Ireland Championship or Cheltenham, look for dedicated Irish racing sections when choosing your site.

Step 3: Make a Deposit

You need funds in your account before betting. Payment methods accepted by GRAI-licensed bookmakers include:

  • Debit card (Visa/Mastercard debit)
  • Revolut (widely used in Ireland)
  • Apple Pay / Google Pay
  • Bank transfer

Important: Credit card deposits at betting sites are banned since 1 July 2026 under GRAI rules. If you try to use a credit card, it will be declined.

Most deposits are instant. Set a deposit limit at this stage if you want to control your spending — GRAI-licensed bookmakers are required to offer deposit limits under responsible gambling obligations.

Step 4: Find a Market

Once your account is funded, navigate to the sport or event you want to bet on. Common sections include:

  • Football — League of Ireland, Premier League, Champions League, internationals
  • GAA — All-Ireland Football and Hurling Championships, provincial championships
  • Horse Racing — Leopardstown, Fairyhouse, Punchestown, Cheltenham, Royal Ascot
  • Greyhound Racing — Shelbourne Park, Shelbourne and other Irish tracks
  • Other sports: rugby, golf, tennis, darts

Within each event you'll find multiple markets — match winner, handicap, over/under goals, first scorer, correct score, and many more.

Step 5: Understand the Bet Slip

When you click on a selection (say, the home team to win a football match), it appears in your bet slip — usually a panel on the right of the screen or a pop-up at the bottom on mobile. The bet slip shows:

  • Your selection and the odds at the time you added it
  • A box to enter your stake (the amount you want to bet)
  • The potential return (stake × odds)

Odds can move between adding a selection and confirming your bet. Most bookmakers ask if you accept changed odds — read the prompt before confirming.

Step 6: Single vs Multiple Bets

A single is one selection — the simplest bet. If you add multiple selections to the bet slip, you can place them as separate singles or combine them into a multiple (double, treble, accumulator). In a multiple, all selections must win for you to get a return, but the odds multiply together — meaning both the potential return and the risk increase. See our accumulator betting guide for more detail.

Step 7: Confirm and Track Your Bet

Once you're happy with your selection and stake, click "Place Bet" (or equivalent). You'll receive a confirmation — save the bet reference number. Your bet history is accessible in the "My Bets" or "My Account" section. Live bets can often be cashed out early if the bookmaker offers that feature.

Is online betting legal in Ireland?

Yes, online betting is fully regulated in Ireland since 1 July 2026 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, enforced by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI). Only use bookmakers holding a valid GRAI remote betting licence.

What age do I need to be to bet in Ireland?

You must be 18 or over. GRAI-licensed bookmakers are legally required to verify your age before you can place a bet or make a withdrawal.

Can I use a credit card to bet in Ireland?

No. Credit card deposits at betting sites have been banned since 1 July 2026 under GRAI rules. Use a debit card, Revolut, Apple Pay, or Google Pay instead.

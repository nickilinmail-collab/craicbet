Cards Betting in Football: Yellow Cards, Bookings Points and Red Cards

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 4 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

What Cards Betting Markets Exist?

Total bookings: over/under on the number of cards (yellow or red) in a match. Bookings points: a scoring system where yellow cards count as 10 points and red cards as 25 points; total points markets set a line. First card: which team receives the first card. Next card: in-play market on which player or team receives the next card. Total yellow cards: separately to red cards. Player to be carded: specific player to receive a yellow or red card during the match.

How Bookings Points Markets Work

The bookings points market converts cards into a numerical total. 10 points per yellow card, 25 per red. A match with 4 yellows and 1 red scores: (4 × 10) + (1 × 25) = 65 points. The bookmaker sets an over/under line, commonly 30–50 points, and you back over or under. Some bookmakers cap total points (e.g. a double yellow resulting in a red counts as 35 points, not 45). Read the specific rules before betting.

Which Leagues Have Most Cards

Spanish La Liga and Italian Serie A have historically produced high card counts. South American leagues and certain international tournaments are notoriously volatile. The Premier League tends toward moderate card totals relative to its physical playing style. Local derbies, relegation battles and high-stakes cup ties across all leagues produce more cards than standard mid-table fixtures.

In-Play Cards Betting

In-play cards betting is popular for high-intensity fixtures. As a game becomes fractious — a rough challenge early on, a contentious refereeing decision, a late equaliser in a tense game — card totals can accelerate rapidly. Identifying games where referee history and team rivalry suggest a volatile second half can be a productive in-play angle. Which Leagues and Referees Produce More Cards?

Card rates vary significantly by league and referee. General patterns for Irish punters to know:

  • La Liga and Serie A produce the most cards per game on average among the top 5 European leagues.
  • Premier League tends to show fewer cards per game — faster, physical football but referees tolerate more contact.
  • Derbies and rivalry matches historically produce more cards than neutral fixtures at the same level.
  • Relegation and promotion battles (tense, high-stakes matches) tend toward more bookings.
  • League of Ireland referee styles differ from European leagues — lower average cards per game in the top flight than La Liga.
How are bookings points calculated?

Bookings points assign 10 points per yellow card and 25 per red card. A second yellow resulting in a red may count as 35 points (yellow + red) or 25 points (red only) depending on the operator's rules.

Can I bet on a specific player to be carded?

Yes. Player to be carded markets are available on most major football matches at GRAI-licensed bookmakers. These markets require accurate knowledge of the player's disciplinary record and the opposition's style of play.

Which referee produces the most cards?

Referee card statistics are publicly available for major leagues. Some referees are known high-bookers; others rarely show cards. Matching a strict referee with two physically confrontational teams is a common angle for cards over bets.

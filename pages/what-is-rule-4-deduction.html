What Is a Rule 4 Deduction? Horse Racing Explained

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

You backed a horse at 5/1, it won, and then you noticed your winnings were slightly less than you calculated. The settlement note says "Rule 4 deduction: 10p in the pound". A horse was withdrawn from the race, your winnings were adjusted, and the reason is a rule that has governed horse racing betting since the 1950s. This guide explains exactly what Rule 4 is, how the deduction scale works, and when you need to know about it.

Why Rule 4 Exists

When you place a bet on a horse race, the price you get reflects the whole field of competitors. If a heavily fancied rival is subsequently withdrawn from the race, your horse's chances improve — the removed competitor can no longer beat you. If your horse now wins, it won a less competitive race than the one you bet on. Rule 4 compensates the bookmaker for this by reducing your winnings in proportion to the benefit you received from the withdrawal.

The rule is named after Rule 4(c) of the Tattersalls Committee Rules, which have governed betting disputes in British and Irish racing since 1886. It is applied by all bookmakers operating in Ireland and Britain and has been standard practice since 1954.

The Rule 4 Deduction Scale

The deduction percentage is determined by the Starting Price (SP) or the odds of the withdrawn horse at the time of withdrawal. A shorter-priced horse (more likely to win) causes a larger deduction; a longer-priced outsider causes a smaller or zero deduction.

The deduction is applied to your profit only, not your stake. Your original stake is always returned in full.

Worked Example

You back a horse at 5/1 for €20. Total potential return: €120 (€100 profit + €20 stake). A 3/1 favourite is withdrawn, triggering a 15p in the pound Rule 4 deduction.

Adjusted profit: €100 × (1 − 0.15) = €100 × 0.85 = €85 profit. Your total return: €85 + €20 stake = €105 instead of €120.

When Rule 4 Does NOT Apply

Rule 4 does not apply to ante-post bets: if a horse is withdrawn after you have placed an ante-post wager, the standard rule is that the bet loses outright (not a Rule 4 deduction). This is a fundamental difference between ante-post and day-of-race betting.

Rule 4 also does not apply to Starting Price (SP) bets placed after final declarations, because the SP is calculated after all withdrawals are known. The SP automatically adjusts to reflect the remaining field without needing a Rule 4 correction.

Multiple Deductions and Caps

If two or more horses are withdrawn from the same race, deductions from each can stack. However, the total combined deduction is capped at 75p in the pound. Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Rule 4 deduction?

A Rule 4 deduction is a reduction applied to your winnings when a horse is withdrawn after you bet. It compensates for the improved chance your horse had due to the removal of a competitor.

Does Rule 4 affect each-way bets?

Yes. Rule 4 deductions apply to both the win and place parts of an each-way bet.

What is the Rule 4 deduction scale?

Based on the SP of the withdrawn horse: odds-on (75p), evens to 6/5 (25p), 2/1 to 5/2 (20p), 3/1 to 4/1 (15p), 9/2 to 11/2 (10p), 6/1 to 9/1 (5p), 10/1 and above (no deduction).

When does Rule 4 NOT apply?

Rule 4 does not apply to ante-post bets (non-runners mean the stake is lost outright) or to SP bets placed after declarations (the SP adjusts automatically).

Can multiple Rule 4 deductions apply to one race?

Yes. If multiple horses are withdrawn, deductions from each can be applied, with a maximum combined deduction of 75p in the pound.

When Does Rule 4 Apply?

  • When a horse or runner is withdrawn from a race after you placed your bet, Rule 4 may be applied
  • The deduction is based on the withdrawn animal's odds at the time of withdrawal
  • Applies to horse racing, greyhound racing and other multi-runner events
  • Rule 4 does NOT apply to ante-post bets (most ante-post bets simply lose if the horse is a non-runner, unless NRNR terms apply)
  • Your bookmaker will notify you of any Rule 4 deductions before settling your bet

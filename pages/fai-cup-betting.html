FAI Cup Betting Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

The FAI Cup is Ireland's knockout competition — the one weekend a season when the Aviva fills for domestic football, and the one competition where a First Division side can genuinely put out a title contender. That unpredictability is exactly what makes cup betting interesting, and exactly why the odds need reading with care.

Why cup football breaks the formbook

League form is built on large samples; a cup tie is one match, often on a tight provincial pitch, in front of a crowd that lifts the underdog. Giant-killings are a running theme of the competition — lower-division sides reach the latter rounds most seasons, and finals between sides from different tiers are not rare. The practical lesson: favourites in one-off knockout ties win less often than their league position suggests, and the market does not always shorten the underdog enough to reflect it.

How your bet settles: the 90-minute rule

The single most expensive misunderstanding in cup betting is settlement. Match result (1X2) markets settle on 90 minutes plus stoppage time. If the tie goes to extra time and penalties, a draw after 90 minutes means the draw wins the 1X2 market — even if "your" team lifts the trophy an hour later. If you want to back a team to progress or to win the cup outright regardless of how, use the "to qualify"/"to lift the trophy" market instead. Always check whether the round you are betting on goes straight to extra time and penalties or to a replay — the match rules, not your assumption, decide how a drawn tie is resolved.

Outright betting: where the value hides

Cup outrights are priced early and reshaped by every round of the draw. The value moments are structural: just after a round in which the big favourites are drawn against each other (someone strong must go out), and just before a draw when a well-fancied side's price hasn't yet adjusted to a kind-looking path. Backing the market leader after the semi-finals rarely pays; by then the price contains everything you know.

The 2026 final: Derry City v Waterford

This season's decider is Derry City against Waterford at the Aviva Stadium on Sunday 8 November 2026 — a pairing that again shows how open the competition is. One-off finals compress the quality gap further still: a cagey opening, a set-piece goal and the formbook is irrelevant. The draw at 90 minutes is persistently under-backed in finals, which is worth knowing whichever side you fancy.

Honest maths for knockout favourites

A 4/7 favourite needs to win 64% of one-off matches to break even. In a league fixture against the same opponent that might be fair; in a final, with extra time looming and legs tightening, it often is not. Small edges in cup betting come from settlement discipline — knowing exactly what your market pays when the tie is level — not from guessing winners.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does my FAI Cup bet include extra time?

No. A standard match result (1X2) bet settles on 90 minutes plus stoppage time. If the tie is level and goes to extra time, the draw is the winning 1X2 result. Use the “to qualify” or outright market if you want extra time and penalties to count.

Who is in the 2026 FAI Cup final?

Derry City play Waterford at the Aviva Stadium, Dublin, on Sunday 8 November 2026.

Are upsets really more common in the FAI Cup?

Yes, relative to league football. A cup tie is a single match, often on the underdog's ground, so favourites win less often than their league form — and sometimes their odds — would imply.

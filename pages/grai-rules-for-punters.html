GRAI Rules That Protect Irish Punters

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Most coverage of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 talks about what it does to operators. This page flips the lens: what actually changes for the person placing a €10 acca on a Saturday. The rules arrive in phases, but the direction is consistent — and several protections already bind every bookmaker on the GRAI register.

No Credit, No Credit Cards

The clearest rule in the Act: a licensed operator cannot accept a credit card, cannot take a payment funded by one through an e-wallet, and cannot lend you money or arrange credit so you can keep betting. Breaking this is a criminal offence, not a slap on the wrist. The honest-maths reading: betting borrowed money is the single fastest way to turn a hobby into a debt, and the law now closes that door at every licensed counter.

Inducements: The End of the "Personal Offer"

The Act prohibits offering an inducement to a specific person or group — the "we miss you, here is a free bet" message that lands just when you have stopped betting. General offers to the public remain legal, subject to regulations the GRAI can make, and those must consider whether an offer encourages excessive gambling. Whatever offers survive, the free bet maths stays the same: stakes are not returned, and terms decide the real value.

Advertising You Will Stop Seeing

The Act provides for a broadcast watershed banning gambling ads on TV and radio between 5:30am and 9:00pm, bans ads designed to appeal to children, and limits social media gambling ads to users who opted in. The watershed guide covers what is in force in the current phase.

Your Money and Your Account

Licensed bookmakers must verify age, pay out winnings, keep customer funds apart from company money, and close your account whenever you ask — without a retention call-centre runaround. The National Gambling Exclusion Register, once operating, will let one request block you from every licensed betting operator at once.

When a Bookmaker Breaks the Rules

You now have somewhere to take it. After the operator's own complaints process, the GRAI can investigate licensees and impose sanctions up to €20 million or 10% of turnover — our complaint guide walks through the route. None of this applies to unlicensed sites, which is the practical argument for the 2-minute licence check before you deposit.

What the Rules Do Not Do

No regulator shortens a bookmaker's odds. Margins, each-way terms and wagering conditions remain yours to judge — the rules just guarantee a floor of conduct underneath them. Protection from sharp practice is not protection from the mathematics of betting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can Irish bookmakers still send me personal free bet offers?

Targeted inducements — offers aimed at a specific person or group — are prohibited for licensed operators. General public offers remain legal, subject to GRAI regulations.

Can I use a credit card with a GRAI-licensed bookmaker?

No. Licensed operators cannot accept credit cards, payments funded by them, or extend credit for betting — and breaching the ban is a criminal offence.

What can I do if a licensed bookmaker treats me unfairly?

Complain to the operator first, then escalate to the GRAI — see our step-by-step complaint guide. Sanctions run to €20m or 10% of turnover.

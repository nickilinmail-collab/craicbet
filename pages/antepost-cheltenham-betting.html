What ante-post means at Cheltenham

Ante-post Cheltenham bets are placed before the final declarations for each race, which close a week before the festival. Prices open months earlier — sometimes in the spring before — and are often significantly longer than festival-week prices. The tradeoff: if your selection does not run, your stake is lost (unless non-runner no-bet terms apply, which you must confirm with your operator).

Best markets for ante-post Cheltenham betting

The Gold Cup, Champion Hurdle, Queen Mother Champion Chase and Stayers Hurdle are the most traded ante-post markets. Novice chases and hurdles also attract ante-post volume because talented unbeaten horses often carry long prices early in the season that shorten rapidly with each subsequent win. The best ante-post value tends to emerge in October and November before the market fully prices in winter form.

Non-runner no-bet availability

Many operators offer NRNB (non-runner no-bet) on certain Cheltenham races closer to the festival, but this is not universal and not always available months in advance. Always check whether NRNB applies to the specific market you are betting. Without NRNB, a training setback, injury or change of plan by the trainer means your stake is forfeit regardless of how close the horse was to running.

When early prices close: the January market shift

January is historically when ante-post prices shorten most sharply for Cheltenham, as horses that have won autumn Grade 1 races become firmly targeted at specific festival races. If your view is that a horse is underpriced, January is often the last window to take a genuinely early price before the market catches up. After that, ante-post prices typically converge with forecast starting prices.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do ante-post Cheltenham markets open?

Some operators open outright markets for the following year's Cheltenham Festival in the spring after the previous festival. Main race ante-post markets typically open in autumn and attract significant volume from October onward as the National Hunt season gets underway.

What is non-runner no-bet and does it apply at Cheltenham?

Non-runner no-bet (NRNB) means your stake is returned if your selection does not take part. It is available on some Cheltenham markets from some operators, but not all. Always confirm NRNB status before placing an ante-post bet, as standard ante-post rules result in losing your stake if the horse does not run.

Which Cheltenham races are best for ante-post betting?

The Gold Cup, Champion Hurdle, Queen Mother Champion Chase and Stayers Hurdle attract the deepest ante-post books and the most competitive prices. Novice races can offer earlier ante-post value on unbeaten horses before the market catches up with their potential.

Key Cheltenham Ante-Post Betting Rules

  • Non-Runner No Bet (NRNR): always check if this applies — it refunds your stake if the horse doesn't run
  • Standard ante-post: without NRNR, stake is lost if horse is a non-runner

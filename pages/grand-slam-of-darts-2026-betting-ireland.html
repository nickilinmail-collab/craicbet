The Grand Slam of Darts, Saturday 14 – Sunday 22 November 2026, is the last big ranking major before the Worlds — and this year it is a different tournament. The field expands from 32 to 48 players for the first time, the groups shrink to three players each, and only the group winner survives. That one change rewrites how the event should be bet, so before you touch the outright market, read the format section below.

Why the New Format Changes the Betting

Under the old format, a seed could lose a group match and still qualify. Not any more. Three-player groups with two matches each and one qualifier mean a single off-night can eliminate anyone — including the favourites. Tie-breaks run through leg difference, legs won and averages before a nine-dart shootout, so even winning one match may not be enough.

The honest maths: short round-robin stages carry far more variance than long knockout matches. Favourites backed at short odds in the outright market now have to survive a coin-flip-adjacent group stage before the bracket even starts. If you like a favourite, their price to win their group will usually be a fairer reflection of the real risk than it was under the old format — and if you fancy an upset anywhere, this is the format for it.

Markets Worth a Look

Match betting remains the cleanest market in darts: no draw in knockout play, low margins, clear settlement. Our darts betting guide covers the market mechanics. Group winner markets suit the new format — you are pricing exactly the hurdle that matters. Outrights: Littler goes for a third straight title as top seed, with Humphries seeded second; but remember the variance argument above before taking short prices nine days from the trophy. 180s and checkout specials carry bigger bookmaker margins — fine for small-stakes fun, poor for value.

The Irish Angle

No Irish player appears in the published 48-player field this year, so there is no home interest to cheer — but the Grand Slam remains one of the most-bet darts weeks of the Irish winter, and every session is live on Sky Sports at Irish-friendly evening times. The next major after Wolverhampton is the PDC World Championship at Alexandra Palace from December, where ante-post markets are already open.

What's Next on the Irish Calendar

The Grand Slam's opening weekend clashes with Cheltenham's November Meeting (13–15 Nov) and Ireland v Fiji at the Aviva, and its final weekend with the Punchestown Premiere Weekend and Ireland v South Africa. Budget for the fortnight as a whole, not event by event.

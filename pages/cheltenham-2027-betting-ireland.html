Cheltenham 2027 Betting Guide for Irish Punters — Festival Preview & Tips

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 CraicBet Editorial 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

The Cheltenham Festival is the biggest betting week of the year for Irish punters — four days in March that dominate the horse racing calendar and generate more betting volume in Ireland than any other event outside the Grand National. Cheltenham 2027 runs 16–19 March. This guide covers the key races, how to bet from Ireland legally, each-way betting explained, and responsible gambling for a week when it matters most.

Why Cheltenham Matters So Much to Irish Punters

No other event in the racing calendar generates the same level of Irish interest as the Cheltenham Festival. Ireland sends the majority of the festival's top horses, with Irish-trained runners winning a substantial portion of Grade 1 races in a typical year. The cultural and financial connection runs deep: Irish racing yards, Irish owners, Irish jockeys and Irish punters are all central to the festival.

For betting volume, Cheltenham week is unmatched. Online bookmakers report their highest single-week handle of the year during the festival. For punters, it brings a unique combination of detailed form study, strong ante-post markets months in advance, and race-week volatility as final declarations and going reports shift prices.

The Gold Cup — Ireland's Holy Grail at Cheltenham

The Cheltenham Gold Cup (3 miles 2½ furlongs, run on 19 March 2027) is the pinnacle of jump racing. Run over fences at the highest level, it demands a combination of jumping ability, stamina and class that places it apart from any other race in the calendar.

Irish-trained horses have historically dominated the Gold Cup, with Ireland's top yards regularly providing the favourites and the winners. The Gold Cup is the race that attracts the largest single-race betting volume at the festival and the one where ante-post markets are most liquid from autumn onwards.

The Champion Hurdle — Speed and Class

Champion Day opens the festival with the Champion Hurdle (2 miles), the championship race for hurdlers. It is a faster, sharper test than the Gold Cup — a race for speed over stamina. Ireland typically fields strong Champion Hurdle contenders and the race regularly attracts significant cross-channel competition.

The Arkle — Next Generation Chasers

The Arkle Challenge Trophy (2 miles) on Champion Day is the novice chase championship — the race where the next generation of staying chasers, potentially future Gold Cup horses, make their mark. Named after the legendary Arkle, the greatest chaser of all time (trained in Ireland by Tom Dreaper, ridden by Pat Taaffe), the Arkle is a race with deep resonance for Irish racing fans.

Ante-Post vs Race-Day Betting — What's the Difference?

Cheltenham betting begins months before the festival, with ante-post markets on major races opening as early as the summer for the following March. Understanding the difference between ante-post and race-day betting is essential for Cheltenham punters.

FactorAnte-PostRace-Day

The key ante-post rule: if your horse does not run, you lose your stake. This is different from race-day betting where non-runners are refunded. Only back a horse ante-post if you are comfortable losing the stake should the horse be scratched before the race.

Each-Way Betting at Cheltenham — Explained

Each-way betting is enormously popular at Cheltenham, particularly in handicap races where the fields are large and the market is competitive. Here is how it works:

An each-way (E/W) bet is two bets of equal size: a win bet and a place bet. If you place a €10 E/W bet, you stake €20 total (€10 on the win, €10 on the place). The place part pays at a fraction of the win odds — typically ¼ odds for 8+ runner races at Cheltenham.

Each-way example (Cheltenham Gold Cup, 20-runner field):

Horse at 10/1 (11.00 decimal) — €10 E/W (€20 total stake)
Place terms: 1/4 odds, first 4 places

If wins: Win part = €10 × 11.00 = €110. Place part = €10 × (10/4 + 1) = €10 × 3.50 = €35. Total return = €145 (profit: €125)
If places (2nd–4th): Win part = lost (€10). Place part = €10 × 3.50 = €35. Total return = €35 (profit: €15)
If unplaced: Both parts lost. Total lost = €20

Each-way betting makes most sense on longer-priced horses in large-field handicaps, where the place return alone can be meaningful even if the horse doesn't win. In small fields (fewer than 5 runners), each-way terms are often unavailable or restricted to two places only — always check the terms before placing.

Irish-Trained Horses at Cheltenham — Historical Dominance

Ireland's domination of Cheltenham is one of jump racing's defining narratives. Irish stables regularly supply the majority of Grade 1 winners, and the Gold Cup has frequently been claimed by Ireland's leading yards. The connection between Irish racing and Cheltenham runs from the legendary Arkle in the 1960s through to modern dynasties at the top of the sport.

When assessing ante-post markets, the form of Irish-trained horses in the autumn Grade 1 novice and championship races (the Fighting Fifth, the Morgiana, the John Durkan, the King George) is essential reading for Cheltenham punters. The Irish Champions Weekend in September and the Christmas and New Year Grade 1s provide the clearest form pointers.

Responsible Gambling During Cheltenham Week

Cheltenham is the biggest betting week of the year for Ireland — and that makes responsible gambling more important, not less. The excitement of the festival, combined with four consecutive days of Grade 1 racing and significant social pressure, creates conditions where losses can escalate quickly.

⚠️ Cheltenham Responsible Gambling Tips:
  • Set a festival budget before Day 1 and stick to it — treat it as entertainment spending
  • Don't chase losses from one day's racing into the next day's card
  • Use deposit limits on your GRAI-licensed bookmaker account ahead of the festival
  • Remember ante-post non-runner risk — only stake what you can afford to lose entirely

Frequently Asked Questions

When is Cheltenham Festival 2027?

Cheltenham Festival 2027 runs from Tuesday 16 March to Friday 19 March 2027. The Gold Cup — the festival's flagship race — takes place on Friday 19 March. The four days are Champion Day (Tue), Ladies Day (Wed), St Patrick's Thursday (Thu), and Gold Cup Day (Fri).

What is each-way betting at Cheltenham?

An each-way bet is two bets in one: a win bet and a place bet at equal stakes. For races with 8+ runners, the place part typically pays at 1/4 odds for first, second and third (sometimes four places in large-field handicaps). If your horse wins, both bets pay. If it finishes placed, only the place part pays. Each-way is popular at Cheltenham's large-field handicap hurdles and chases where even a placed finish at long odds can return a meaningful sum.

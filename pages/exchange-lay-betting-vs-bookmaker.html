How Lay Betting Works: The Maths

When you lay a selection at 5.0 for a liability of €40 (backing €10 at 5.0): if the selection loses, you win €10 (the backer’s stake). If it wins, you pay €40 (4 × €10 profit to the backer). Your maximum liability is always (odds − 1) × stake. At low exchange odds, liability is manageable; at high odds, liability can be many multiples of the stake. Never lay without calculating the full liability first.

When Laying Gives an Advantage

Laying is advantageous in three scenarios: (1) Matched betting — laying the same selection as a free bet back bet eliminates event risk; (2) Trading — back a selection pre-match, lay it in-play if odds shorten, locking in a guaranteed profit; (3) Standalone lay strategy — identifying overpriced favourites and laying them at odds you believe understate the true probability of them losing.

Liability Management

Liability is the amount you lose if the lay bet wins. At 2.0 (evens), laying €10 means a €10 liability. At 10.0, laying €10 means a €90 liability. Never commit to a lay bet without confirming you have sufficient exchange balance to cover the liability. Most exchanges will not accept a lay if your balance is insufficient. When trading in-play, run the hedging maths in advance to know exactly what stake to lay for a guaranteed profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is lay betting?

Lay betting means betting that a selection will NOT win — you act as the bookmaker for that outcome. If the selection loses, you keep the backer’s stake; if it wins, you pay the payout. Only available on betting exchanges.

How do I calculate lay liability?

Liability = (lay odds − 1) × lay stake. Example: lay 5.0 for €10 = liability of €40. Always confirm your exchange balance covers the full liability before placing a lay bet.

Do I need a betting exchange account to lay bets?

Yes. Lay betting is only possible on betting exchanges. Fixed odds bookmakers only allow back bets. Betfair is the most liquid exchange and holds a GRAI RI licence for Irish players.

Can I trade horse racing by back and lay?

Yes. Trading — backing at high odds, laying at lower odds to lock in profit — is a standard exchange strategy on horse racing. It requires watching the market in real time and accepting exchange commission on net profits.

Is lay betting risky?

Lay betting carries high liability on long-priced selections. At low odds (1.5–3.0), liability is manageable. At high odds (10.0+), one win can result in a large loss. Always calculate liability before placing.

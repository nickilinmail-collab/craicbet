Progressive jackpot slots offer life-changing prizes — some exceeding €10 million. But the trade-off is a lower base RTP. Understanding how jackpots work helps you choose the right casino and manage expectations.

Types of Jackpots

  • Standalone/Local jackpots — Funded only by bets on that specific game at that casino. Smaller prizes, more frequent hits.
  • Network/Linked jackpots — Funded by bets across all casinos running the same game. Larger prizes (millions), less frequent hits.
  • Fixed jackpots — Set prize amounts that don't grow. High hit frequency but limited upside.

How Progressive Jackpots Work

A percentage of every bet placed on a progressive slot feeds the jackpot pool. This jackpot contribution is funded from the game's RTP budget — meaning the base game RTP is lower than a standard slot. A game with a 94% published RTP might include 2–4% going to the jackpot, with only 90–92% returned through base game wins.

Must-Drop Jackpots

Must-drop jackpots are guaranteed to pay out before reaching a specified amount or by a set time. These include "daily must-drop" and "hourly must-drop" jackpots. Because they must pay out, they hit more frequently and predictably — popular with players who prefer higher hit frequency over massive jackpot sizes.

Jackpot RTP: What Casinos Don't Always Tell You

Most jackpot games display an RTP that includes the jackpot contribution — achievable in theory but effectively meaningless unless you hit the jackpot. The "base game RTP" (excluding jackpot contribution) is what you'll actually experience across normal sessions. Some regulators require this to be disclosed; check the game's information panel.

Jackpot Verification

Legitimate jackpots are audited by independent testing laboratories (eCOGRA, iTech Labs, GLI). If a casino claims huge jackpot wins, look for verified jackpot logs on the provider's website — Microgaming, for example, publishes its jackpot winners publicly.

Odds of Winning a Progressive Jackpot

Network progressives like Mega Moolah have jackpot odds commonly cited around 1-in-50 million. Smaller must-drop jackpots can be won far more frequently — sometimes multiple times per day across thousands of players. The key facts to understand before playing:

  • Jackpot contribution comes from your stake — typically 1–3% of every spin goes into the jackpot pool
  • Base game RTP is usually lower on jackpot slots (88–94%) than non-jackpot titles (95–97%)
  • Must-drop jackpots guarantee a winner before a time or value threshold — better odds than pure progressives
  • Jackpot triggers are random and not affected by bet size on most titles (though some require max bet)

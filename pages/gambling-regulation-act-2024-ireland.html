Gambling Regulation Act 2024 Ireland: Complete Player Guide

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the most significant overhaul of Irish gambling law in almost a century. Signed by the President on 23 October 2024, it created Ireland's first dedicated gambling regulator, introduced mandatory player protections, and set the roadmap for a fully licensed online gambling market. Here is everything Irish players need to know.

What Is the Gambling Regulation Act 2024?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the primary legislation establishing a modern, comprehensive regulatory framework for gambling in Ireland. Before this Act, Ireland relied on laws that were decades out of date — chiefly the Betting Acts of 1931 and the Gaming and Lotteries Acts of 1956 — none of which were designed with online gambling in mind.

The Act was years in the making. Work on reform began in earnest in 2019, the Gambling Regulation Bill was passed by the Oireachtas in 2022, and the final Act received presidential assent on 23 October 2024. It is enabling legislation: it creates the GRAI and sets out its powers, with many specific rules and codes developed by the GRAI as a secondary step.

What Changed for Irish Players?

The Act introduced several concrete protections for gambling customers in Ireland. These are not voluntary — they are statutory requirements that every GRAI-licensed operator must comply with.

Credit Card Ban

GRAI-licensed operators are prohibited from accepting credit cards as a funding method for gambling accounts. This is a significant consumer protection measure: credit card gambling can mask the true cost of losses and lead to debt. Irish players must use debit cards, bank transfers, e-wallets or other approved payment methods.

Mandatory Deposit Limits

All GRAI-licensed operators must offer deposit limit tools and must actively prompt new customers to set them during registration. Players can set daily, weekly or monthly caps on the funds they deposit. The key distinction from previous voluntary schemes is that operators must provide these tools prominently, not bury them in account settings.

National Gambling Exclusion Ireland (NGEI)

One of the most significant innovations is the National Gambling Exclusion Ireland (NGEI) — a single national self-exclusion register. When fully operational, registering on the NGEI will automatically exclude a player from every GRAI-licensed operator in Ireland. This replaces the previous fragmented system where players had to contact each operator separately to self-exclude.

Advertising Restrictions

The Act introduced a statutory advertising watershed for gambling. Gambling advertisements cannot be broadcast on television or radio before the watershed. Additional restrictions limit the use of celebrities, sportspeople and social media influencers in gambling promotions, to reduce the appeal of gambling advertising to younger audiences.

Age Verification

The Act mandates robust age verification requirements. GRAI-licensed operators must verify that customers are aged 18 or over before allowing them to open an account or make a deposit. The standard of verification is significantly higher than the previous self-declaration approach.

⚠️ Important: These Rules Apply to GRAI-Licensed Operators

The protections above apply to operators holding a GRAI licence. If you use an offshore casino or sportsbook that has not applied for a GRAI licence, those statutory protections do not apply — you are relying on that operator's home jurisdiction rules. This is one reason to choose a GRAI-licensed betting site for your sports betting.

Gambling Advertising: What the Act Restricts

Advertising rules are among the most visible changes the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 brings to everyday life in Ireland. The GRAI has the power to issue statutory advertising codes, moving beyond the voluntary self-regulation that preceded this Act.

  • Broadcasting watershed: Gambling advertising cannot air on TV or radio before the watershed. The specific times are set out in the GRAI's advertising codes.
  • Celebrity and influencer restrictions: Gambling operators cannot use celebrities, sports stars or social media influencers who are likely to appeal to under-18s. This targets the common practice of using footballers or reality TV stars in betting promotions.
  • Mandatory responsible gambling messaging: All gambling promotions must include responsible gambling messages and signpost support resources.
  • Inducement rules: The GRAI can regulate bonus offers and free-bet promotions, particularly those that target problem gamblers or encourage unsustainable behaviour.
  • Proximity restrictions: Restrictions on advertising near schools, playgrounds and other places frequented by young people.

What Does the Act Mean for Online Casino Players Right Now?

If your primary interest is online casino gaming — slots, live dealer tables, roulette — the picture in November 2026 is straightforward: the GRAI has not yet issued any casino or gaming licences. Zero. Any site claiming a "GRAI casino licence" is misleading you, because those licences do not yet exist.

Irish players can and do legally play at internationally licensed online casinos. The most reputable offshore licences are from the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC), and Gibraltar. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 does not make it illegal for Irish players to use offshore sites — it sets up the framework so that, once GRAI casino licences are issued (expected 2027–2028), operators who want to serve Ireland officially will need to hold one.

When casino licences do arrive, the same player protections that apply to GRAI-licensed betting — deposit limits, NGEI self-exclusion, credit card ban, advertising codes — will extend to casino operators too. That is a substantial step forward for Irish casino players.

The Social Impact Fund

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established a Social Impact Fund, financed by a levy on gambling operators. This fund supports research into problem gambling, treatment services, and public awareness campaigns. The Gambling Awareness Trust and similar organisations benefit from this framework. It represents a move away from relying on operator-funded voluntary contributions towards a statutory obligation.

GRAI Complaints: What the Act Gives You

For the first time, Irish players who have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator have a formal regulatory path to resolution. Before the Act, a complaint against an online betting or gambling operator in Ireland had no domestic regulator to escalate to. Now, the GRAI operates a formal complaints process for disputes with licensed operators. This is a significant shift in player power.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Gambling Regulation Act 2024?▾
The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the primary legislation that overhauled Ireland's gambling laws. Signed into law on 23 October 2024, it created the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as an independent statutory body with powers to license, regulate, and enforce gambling rules across all sectors in Ireland.
When did the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 come into effect?▾
The Act was signed into law on 23 October 2024. The GRAI became operational shortly after, with the first remote betting licences issued from 1 July 2026. Casino and gaming licences are expected to follow in 2027 or 2028.
Does the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 ban credit cards for gambling?▾
Yes. One of the key consumer protections introduced under the Act is a ban on using credit cards to fund gambling accounts at GRAI-licensed operators. Debit cards, bank transfers, e-wallets and other payment methods remain available.
What is the NGEI under the Gambling Regulation Act?▾
The NGEI (National Gambling Exclusion Ireland) is the national self-exclusion register established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. It allows Irish players to exclude themselves from all GRAI-licensed gambling operators with a single registration, rather than having to contact each operator individually.

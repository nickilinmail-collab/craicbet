Gambling Regulation Act 2024 Ireland — What It Means for You as a Player

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the most significant overhaul of Irish gambling law since the Betting Act 1931. Signed into law in October 2024 and coming into force in stages through 2025–2026, it created a new regulator (the GRAI) and introduced real protections for Irish players. This guide explains what it means for you as a bettor — not for operators.

The 5 Things It Actually Changes for You

1. A Real Regulator With Powers

Before 2024, online gambling in Ireland was largely regulated by the Revenue Commissioners, primarily through a licence-fee regime rather than active consumer protection. The GRAI has actual enforcement powers: it can investigate operators, impose fines, suspend or revoke licences, and receive your complaints.

2. Bookmakers Cannot Withhold Your Winnings

GRAI-licensed operators are explicitly prohibited from "unreasonably withholding the payment of a consumer's winnings" (with limited exceptions, e.g. if a suspicious gambling pattern is identified). This is now a regulatory obligation, not just a contractual one — meaning GRAI can investigate and act if a licensed bookmaker refuses to pay out on a legitimate winning bet.

3. A National Self-Exclusion Register

The Act creates the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) — Ireland's equivalent of the UK's GAMSTOP. Once the register is fully operational, a single self-exclusion request will bar you from all GRAI-licensed operators, instead of having to contact each bookmaker individually. This closes a major gap in Irish player protection.

4. Credit Card Deposits Banned

Operators cannot accept credit card gambling deposits from Irish players. This has been in force since 2026 as part of the GRAI licensing conditions. Debit cards, bank transfer, and e-wallets are unaffected.

5. Stricter Rules on Bonus Offers

The Act introduces restrictions on "inducements" — targeted bonus offers, free bets, and promotions. Operators cannot send targeted bonus offers to players who have set deposit limits, self-excluded, or are otherwise identified as potentially vulnerable. General public advertising of bonuses continues, but the aggressive retargeting of problem gamblers with offers is prohibited.

What Didn't Change

  • Tax on winnings: Irish punters still pay zero tax on gambling winnings. The Act did not change this — see our gambling tax guide.
  • Bookmakers' right to restrict accounts: The Act did not give players the right to be accepted as a customer or prevent commercial stake restrictions. Bookmakers can still limit your stakes.
  • Casino licences: Zero GRAI casino licences have been issued. Casino/gaming licensing is expected 2027–2028. Online casinos operating in Ireland in 2026 are licensed elsewhere (typically MGA Malta).

Where Does This Leave Unlicensed Operators?

Operating without a GRAI licence after 1 July 2026 is a criminal offence in Ireland, with penalties of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover. Several major operators (including Stake) geo-blocked Irish users rather than apply. Others (such as Betfred) temporarily paused Irish operations to align with the new requirements. As a player, betting with an unlicensed operator means you have no recourse to GRAI if something goes wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 Ireland?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is Ireland's comprehensive new gambling law, replacing legislation dating to 1931. It established the GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) as the new regulator, made GRAI licences mandatory for all online bookmakers serving Irish customers from 1 July 2026, banned credit card gambling deposits, and created a national self-exclusion register.

What does the GRAI do for players?

GRAI licenses and regulates betting operators in Ireland. Its practical impact on players includes: licensed operators must pay settled winnings (no unreasonable withholding), a national self-exclusion register makes it easier to self-exclude, credit card deposits are banned, and targeted bonus offers to vulnerable players are restricted. Players can complain to GRAI at grai.ie/complaints if a licensed operator breaches these rules.

Do casinos have a GRAI licence in Ireland?

No. As of November 2026, GRAI has only issued remote betting licences. Zero casino or gaming licences have been issued. Casino licensing under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is expected to begin in 2027–2028. Online casinos available to Irish players in 2026 hold licences from other regulators, typically the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA).

Is it illegal to use an unlicensed betting site in Ireland?

No — there is no criminal offence for a player using an unlicensed site. The criminal offence under the Act applies to the operator for serving Irish customers without a licence (fines up to €20 million). For players, the practical risk is that you have no recourse to GRAI if the unlicensed operator withholds your winnings or closes without warning.

When will Ireland's National Gambling Exclusion Register be available?

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) is under development as part of GRAI's regulatory infrastructure (built on Salesforce, contracted to Deloitte for €10.4 million in February 2025). As of November 2026, a public launch date has not been announced. When launched, it will allow a single self-exclusion to apply across all GRAI-licensed operators.

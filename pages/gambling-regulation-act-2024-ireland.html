Gambling Regulation Act 2024 Ireland — Plain English Guide for Bettors

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the most significant overhaul of Irish gambling law in almost a century. It replaced the Betting Act 1931 and created a modern, comprehensive framework for regulating gambling in Ireland — covering everything from online betting to betting shops to gaming machines. Here is what it actually means in plain English for Irish players.

Why Was a New Gambling Law Needed?

Ireland's gambling sector had been governed primarily by the Betting Act 1931 — legislation written before television existed, let alone smartphones and online betting. By the 2020s, almost half of all Irish gambling money flowed through online platforms operating under laws designed for a completely different era. The 2024 Act was designed to bring Ireland in line with modern international standards.

Key Dates — Gambling Regulation Act 2024 Timeline

  • October 2024: Gambling Regulation Act 2024 signed into law
  • 5 March 2025: GRAI formally established as Ireland's gambling regulator
  • 5 February 2026: Minister signs commencement order activating licensing and enforcement powers
  • 9 February 2026: GRAI opens remote betting licence applications
  • 1 July 2026: Remote betting licences take effect; unlicensed online operators face enforcement
  • 1 December 2026: In-person (retail) betting licences take effect
  • 2027–2028: Remote gaming (casino) licences expected to be introduced

What the Act Requires from Licensed Operators

  • Mandatory deposit limits: Licensed operators must offer deposit limits and cannot prevent players from setting them
  • Age verification: Stricter identity checks required before allowing any gambling activity
  • Self-exclusion: All licensed operators must participate in the national self-exclusion register
  • Advertising restrictions: Caps on gambling advertising hours, content standards, and promotion of responsible gambling
  • Social Impact Fund: All licensed operators contribute a levy to fund gambling harm services in Ireland
  • Complaints mechanism: Players can escalate unresolved disputes to the GRAI
  • Financial reporting: Operators must report gambling revenue and player activity data to GRAI

What the Act Means for Unlicensed Sites

The Act gives GRAI significant enforcement powers against operators that target Irish customers without a licence:

  • Criminal prosecution of unlicensed operators
  • Court orders requiring sites to cease operations or be blocked
  • Coordination with payment processors to block transactions to unlicensed sites
  • Fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover for licensed operators that breach rules

Since July 2026, Stake, Polymarket, Kalshi and multiple other operators have been blocked or have voluntarily restricted access for Irish users following GRAI engagement.

What It Does NOT Do (Yet)

  • Casino licensing — remote gaming licences are not expected before 2027
  • Individual criminal liability for players using unlicensed sites — the law targets operators, not punters
  • Mandatory stake limits on online betting (under review)

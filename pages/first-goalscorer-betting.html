Goalscorer Markets Explained: First, Anytime and the Small Print

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Goalscorer markets are the most personal bets in football — back your striker, cheer every touch. They are also markets where the settlement rules and the substitution board quietly decide more outcomes than form does. Before putting a cent on first goalscorer, it pays to know exactly what you are buying.

First vs anytime: the gap in your favour (sometimes)

First goalscorer pays a much bigger price than anytime goalscorer on the same player, because the player must score the opening goal, not just any goal. Here is the honest-maths check: a striker who takes a big share of his team’s goals in a side likely to score first has his first-scorer chance driven by two things you can reason about. If the first-scorer price is not meaningfully better than the anytime price adjusted for that, you are paying for the glamour word “first”. For most punters, most of the time, anytime is the better-value shape of the same opinion — it wins in every scenario first-scorer wins, plus plenty more.

Penalty takers move the whole market

The single biggest edge in goalscorer pricing is knowing who is on penalties and set pieces this week — not last season. A designated penalty taker picks up a large slice of goal probability that has nothing to do with open play. Prices usually reflect the regular taker; they adjust slower when duties change after a transfer, a missed spot kick, or a manager’s whim mentioned once in a press conference. That is one of the few places in this market where paying attention beats the price.

The 60th-minute problem

First goalscorer bets on a starting striker carry a hidden clock. If the match is still 0–0 past the hour and your man is tiring, he gets substituted — and your bet dies with him on the bench while the price you took assumed ninety minutes. Modern squad rotation makes this worse: five substitutions mean attackers are hooked earlier and more often. Anytime bets suffer the same truncation, but first-scorer bets feel it hardest because the longer the match stays scoreless, the more of your remaining chance was loaded onto minutes your player will not play.

Own goals and settlement small print

The near-universal rule: own goals do not count for goalscorer settlement. If the opening goal is an own goal, first goalscorer markets settle on the next legitimate scorer — your bet usually survives an own goal rather than losing to it. But rules on players who come on after a goal, abandoned matches, and dead-heat terms differ between books. Reading the settlement rules once takes five minutes and is worth more than any tipster thread.

Where the value actually hides

Midfielders and defenders on set-piece duty are chronically underpriced relative to their real chance in matches their team should dominate, because casual money piles onto famous strikers and shortens them past fair. The market’s shape is driven by shirt names; the goals are increasingly shared around. If you want the fun of a scorer bet with better maths, the unfashionable second striker or the corner-taking midfielder is usually where the price is honest.

The honest summary

Goalscorer betting is a fun market wearing a few traps: a first/anytime gap that flatters “first”, penalty duties that matter more than form, substitutions that amputate your bet after an hour, and settlement rules worth reading before — not after — the own goal goes in. Know those four things and it is honest entertainment; skip them and you are donating the margin plus your blind spots.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do own goals count in first goalscorer betting?

Almost never. The standard rule is that own goals are ignored for goalscorer settlement: if the opening goal is an own goal, first goalscorer settles on the next legitimate scorer, so your bet usually stays alive rather than losing.

Is anytime goalscorer better value than first goalscorer?

Usually, for casual punters. Anytime wins in every scenario first-scorer wins, plus many more — and the premium on “first” often overstates the true difference, especially for starters likely to be substituted if the match stays scoreless.

What happens to my goalscorer bet if the player is substituted?

If your player started and goes off before scoring, the bet runs to settlement and loses — no refund. Players who are named but never come on are treated differently (often refunded), and exact terms vary by bookmaker, so read the settlement rules.

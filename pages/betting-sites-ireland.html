What Changed on 1 July 2026

Before July 2026, online bookmakers in Ireland operated under Revenue Commissioners permissions dating back to the Betting Act 1931 — a framework designed for on-course bookies, not global platforms. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 replaced that with the GRAI, which began issuing modern remote betting licences on 1 July 2026. All previous Revenue permissions expired on 30 June 2026. Any operator taking bets from Irish residents without a current GRAI licence is now committing a criminal offence — not a civil matter, a criminal one, with fines of up to €20 million.

This matters to you because a GRAI licence is not just a badge. It is a legal commitment: the operator must verify your age, pay out winnings as a licence condition, follow responsible gambling rules, ban credit card deposits, and answer to the GRAI's formal complaints process. None of that applied under the old Revenue system.

How to Check Any Betting Site in 2 Minutes

The GRAI publishes a public register at grai.ie that is updated in real time. Here is the fastest way to use it:

  1. Scroll to the footer of the betting site and find the legal company name (not the brand name — something like "XYZ Limited").
  2. Open the GRAI register and search for that company name.
  3. Look for RB in the licence number — that stands for Remote Betting. A licence with RI is a betting intermediary (exchange), not a bookmaker.
  4. Check the status is Active and the expiry date is in the future.

Betting vs Casino: Where Ireland Stands Right Now

Sports betting is now Ireland's only fully domestically regulated gambling vertical. Casino licensing is separate — the GRAI has issued zero casino licences (formally: remote gaming licences) and does not expect to open applications until 2027–2028. The Irish sports mix: The Irish market has its own shape. Horse racing is the historic heart — Cheltenham week is effectively a national holiday, and the Irish Grand National draws the biggest single-day betting volumes of the year. GAA is the uniquely Irish corner that global guides handle badly; markets are thinner and the draws more significant than in any football equivalent. Premier League football dominates volume year-round, with football betting accounting for the majority of online turnover.

Payments: Under GRAI rules, credit cards are banned for all gambling deposits. Revolut, debit cards (Visa, Mastercard), and bank transfer are the standard routes. Most licensed operators process withdrawals within 1–3 business days; some e-wallet routes are same-day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is online betting legal in Ireland?

Yes — fully regulated since 1 July 2026. Bookmakers serving Irish customers online must hold a GRAI remote betting licence; operating without one is a criminal offence under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

How do I know if a betting site is licensed in Ireland?

Go to the official GRAI register at grai.ie and search the legal company name from the site's footer. Look for RB in the licence number (Remote Betting) and confirm the status is Active. Or use our free GRAI checker — updated daily.

Do I pay tax on betting winnings in Ireland?

No — betting winnings are completely tax-free for players in Ireland. The 2% betting duty is paid by licensed operators, not you. Full details in our gambling tax guide.

Can I use Revolut to bet online in Ireland?

Yes. Revolut is widely accepted at GRAI-licensed betting sites for both deposits and withdrawals. Under GRAI rules, credit cards are banned for gambling deposits — Revolut (as a prepaid/e-money card) is allowed. Deposits are instant; withdrawals typically take 1–2 business days.

What is the minimum age to bet online in Ireland?

18. GRAI-licensed operators are required to verify age before allowing any betting activity. You will need to provide valid ID (passport, driving licence) before your first withdrawal.

Are free bets still legal in Ireland after the GRAI?

Yes — free bets and welcome offers are still legal. The GRAI's inducements rules restrict where and how they can be advertised (no TV ads before the 9pm watershed, no targeting of self-excluded players), but the offers themselves remain available. Always check the wagering requirements before claiming.

Can I use bet365 in Ireland?

Yes — bet365 holds a GRAI remote betting licence and is legally available to Irish customers. As with all GRAI-licensed operators, credit card deposits are banned, age verification is mandatory, and you can escalate disputes to the GRAI. Check grai.ie for the current licence status before depositing.

What is the GRAI licence number format?

GRAI licences follow the format GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-NNNN, where XXXX is a four-digit operator identifier, RB stands for Remote Betting (RI would be a Remote Intermediary / exchange), YY is the two-digit year of issue, and NNNN is a sequential number. For example, our partner's licence is GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 — operator 191, remote betting, issued 2026, first licence of that series. You can verify any licence at grai.ie.

What happens if a betting site loses its GRAI licence?

If a GRAI licence is suspended or revoked, the operator must immediately stop accepting bets from Irish customers. Player balances must be returned — this is a licence condition, not just good practice. The GRAI publishes all licence changes on its public register at grai.ie. CraicBet monitors the register daily and flags any changes on our GRAI tracker page. If you have funds with an operator that loses its licence, contact the GRAI directly.

Is online betting taxed in Ireland?

Irish players pay no tax on betting winnings — there is no equivalent of the UK's old betting duty on punters. Operators pay a 2% betting duty on turnover (gross stakes received from Irish customers). Some operators absorb this cost; others pass it on through slightly lower odds. Either way, you keep 100% of your winnings. See our gambling tax guide for full details including the distinction between casual betting and professional gambling income.

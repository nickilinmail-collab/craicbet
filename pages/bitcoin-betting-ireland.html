Bitcoin Betting Ireland 2026 — Is It Legal? GRAI's Position Explained

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Bitcoin betting is a topic Irish punters search for regularly — but the situation in Ireland in 2026 is more nuanced than a simple yes or no. This guide breaks down what GRAI says, the legal position for Irish players, and the practical risks of using Bitcoin at offshore sportsbooks. See also our broader crypto betting guide for ETH and other currencies.

Is Bitcoin Betting Legal in Ireland?

Bitcoin itself is a legal asset to hold and trade in Ireland — the Revenue Commissioners have issued guidance on its tax treatment. The question for betting is more specific: can you use Bitcoin to fund a betting account?

For individual Irish players, there is no law that explicitly makes Bitcoin deposits at betting sites illegal. However, the key concern is where you are betting:

  • GRAI-licensed bookmakers: These operate under Irish law and must comply with strict AML/KYC rules. Most do not currently accept Bitcoin deposits.
  • MGA or UKGC-licensed operators: Some accept crypto. These are legitimate operators with consumer protection.
  • Unlicensed offshore Bitcoin-only sportsbooks: No consumer protection. If a dispute arises or the site disappears, you have no legal recourse.

GRAI's Position on Crypto Payments

GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026. GRAI's framework requires licensees to have robust Know Your Customer (KYC) and Anti-Money Laundering (AML) controls. Anonymous crypto transactions are fundamentally at odds with these requirements.

As of November 2026, GRAI has not issued specific guidance permitting or banning Bitcoin payments at licensed bookmakers. In practice, the licensed operators under GRAI's framework use traditional, traceable payment methods (debit cards, bank transfers, e-wallets) that satisfy AML obligations.

Tax Implications of Bitcoin Betting in Ireland

Revenue Ireland classifies cryptocurrency as a chargeable asset. If you deposit Bitcoin to a betting site and later withdraw more Bitcoin than you deposited, the increase in BTC value could be treated as a capital gain — separate from any winnings. Betting winnings themselves are not subject to income tax for Irish residents, but the crypto component of gains may be. This is an area where professional tax advice is recommended.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Bitcoin betting legal in Ireland?

Using Bitcoin to fund a betting account is not explicitly illegal for individual Irish players, but it sits in a regulatory grey area. GRAI-licensed bookmakers currently use traditional payment methods. The key risk is using unlicensed offshore Bitcoin-only sportsbooks, which offer no consumer protection.

Does GRAI allow crypto payments at licensed bookmakers?

GRAI has not specifically approved or banned crypto payments. In practice, GRAI-licensed bookmakers use traditional, AML-compliant payment methods. Anonymous crypto transactions are difficult to reconcile with GRAI's KYC requirements.

What are the risks of Bitcoin betting sites for Irish players?

The main risks: no consumer protection if unlicensed, difficulty withdrawing winnings, no access to formal complaints procedures, and crypto price volatility affecting the real value of your deposits and withdrawals.

Are there GRAI-licensed bookmakers that accept Bitcoin?

As of November 2026, GRAI-licensed bookmakers accept traditional payment methods. If crypto payment support is important to you, some MGA-licensed operators may offer crypto options — always verify the licence before depositing.

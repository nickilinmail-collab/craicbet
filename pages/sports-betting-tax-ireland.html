Sports Betting Tax in Ireland: Do You Pay Tax on Gambling Winnings?

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read
Important disclaimer: This page provides general information about the publicly known Revenue position on gambling taxation in Ireland. It is not tax advice. Tax law can change and individual circumstances vary. Always consult a qualified tax adviser (such as a Chartered Accountant or Tax Consultant registered with the Tax Advisers Ireland body) if you have specific questions about your personal tax position.

One of the most common questions from Irish sports bettors is whether they are required to pay income tax on their winnings. The short answer, based on Revenue’s long-standing position, is no — for recreational bettors. But it is worth understanding the full picture: who actually pays the tax, what changes for professional gamblers, and how the new GRAI licensing regime affects the broader regulatory and tax landscape.

Are Gambling Winnings Taxable in Ireland?

Under Irish tax law as applied by Revenue, gambling winnings are not treated as taxable income for recreational bettors. Ireland does not impose a specific “gambling winnings tax” on the person placing bets. This is consistent with the approach taken in the United Kingdom and differs from some other jurisdictions (notably the United States, where gambling winnings are taxable income).

The rationale is that betting is treated as a game of chance rather than a trade or profession. Because gambling is not considered a systematic business activity for recreational bettors, the winnings are not assessable as income under Schedule D (which covers trading income) or under any other income tax head.

This applies to winnings from sports betting, horse racing, casino gambling and other gambling activities. It does not depend on the amount won: even a large accumulator payout is not subject to income tax for a recreational bettor under Revenue’s current position.

Who Pays the Betting Duty in Ireland?

The tax on betting in Ireland is collected from licensed bookmakers, not from bettors. This is known as betting duty and is administered by Revenue under the Betting Acts. Bookmakers operating in Ireland — both retail shops and remote (online) operators — pay duty on their gross betting revenue (essentially the margin they retain across all bets).

The betting duty rate has been set at 2% of gross betting receipts for licensed remote operators. For bettors, the practical effect is that this cost is built into the odds the bookmaker offers — the margin you bet against already accounts for the bookmaker’s duty obligation. You pay the tax indirectly through the odds, not directly out of your winnings.

Retail bookmakers also pay duty on their shop revenues. The structure has evolved over the years as online betting has grown. The 2015 Betting (Amendment) Act extended the licensing and duty regime to remote operators targeting Irish customers, requiring online bookmakers serving Irish bettors to hold a Remote Bookmaker’s Licence from Revenue.

GRAI Licensing and Tax Compliance

Since 1 July 2026, remote betting in Ireland has also been regulated by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI). A GRAI remote betting licence (such as licence GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001) is a consumer protection and regulatory requirement, separate from and in addition to the Revenue betting licence and duty obligations.

Bookmakers operating in Ireland must hold both authorisations: a Revenue Remote Bookmaker’s Licence (covering tax registration and duty) and a GRAI remote betting licence (covering conduct, advertising, responsible gambling obligations and consumer protection). The two regimes are complementary rather than alternatives.

For bettors, using a GRAI-licensed bookmaker does not change your personal tax position on winnings. GRAI licensing is relevant to the bookmaker’s legal right to operate and to your consumer protections, not to the tax treatment of your personal winnings.

You can verify GRAI-licensed bookmakers on the GRAI website (grai.ie), which maintains a public register of all issued licences. CraicBet tracks the register and publishes updates at our GRAI licence register tracker.

Keeping Records of Your Betting

Even though recreational betting winnings are not taxable in Ireland, there are situations where keeping records of your betting activity is sensible:

  • If you bet heavily or professionally. Revenue’s exemption from income tax applies to recreational bettors. If you bet as a systematic business — managing a large portfolio of bets, trading on betting exchanges as a primary income source, or running a tipping or advisory service — Revenue may treat this as a trade and assess the profits accordingly. The line between “recreational” and “professional” is not precisely codified in Irish tax law; it depends on facts and circumstances, and has been the subject of limited Irish case law. If you are in any doubt about your position, consult a tax adviser.
  • For exchange trading. Betting exchange trading (buying and laying prices to generate a profit regardless of match outcome) can more closely resemble a financial trading activity than recreational gambling. Revenue has not issued specific guidance on this point for Ireland, but it is an area of potential complexity.
  • For foreign winnings. If you bet with operators based outside Ireland and generate significant winnings, the tax treatment may be more complex, particularly if any withholding tax is applied at source in the operator’s home jurisdiction.
  • For general financial records. Maintaining records of deposits, withdrawals and significant winnings from betting accounts is good financial practice and may be relevant if your bank queries large account movements.

None of this advice substitutes for professional guidance. Tax law can change; Revenue’s administrative approach can evolve; and individual circumstances vary significantly. If your betting activity is substantial, irregular, or involves complex arrangements, speaking to a qualified accountant or tax adviser is the appropriate course of action.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I pay tax on betting winnings in Ireland?

For recreational bettors, gambling winnings are not subject to income tax in Ireland under Revenue’s current position. The tax is levied on the bookmaker (betting duty), not on the winning bettor. If you are unsure about your individual circumstances, consult a qualified tax adviser.

What is the betting duty in Ireland and who pays it?

Betting duty is levied on licensed bookmakers, not on bettors. Remote operators pay duty on their gross betting revenue. This cost is factored into the odds offered, so bettors bear it indirectly through the margins built into prices rather than through a direct tax on winnings.

Does using a GRAI-licensed bookmaker affect my tax position?

Using a GRAI-licensed bookmaker does not create any additional personal tax obligation for a recreational bettor. The GRAI licence is a regulatory requirement for the bookmaker. Under Revenue’s current position, recreational gambling winnings are not subject to income tax regardless of which licensed operator you use.

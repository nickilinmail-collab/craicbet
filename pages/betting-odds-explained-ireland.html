Betting Odds Explained — How to Read Fractions, Decimals and Calculate Winnings

Understanding odds is the most important skill a new punter can develop. Odds tell you two things at once: how likely the bookmaker thinks an outcome is, and how much you stand to win. This guide breaks down both formats used in Ireland — fractional and decimal — with worked examples using Irish betting contexts.

Fractional Odds

Fractional odds are the traditional format and still widely used in Ireland, particularly for horse racing and GAA. They are written as two numbers separated by a slash — for example, 4/1 (said "four-to-one") or 5/2 (said "five-to-two").

The first number tells you your profit. The second number is the stake required to earn that profit. Your original stake is always returned on top of any profit.

Worked example — GAA match:
You back Kilkenny to win at 5/1 with a €10 stake.
Profit = €10 × 5 = €50
Total return = €50 profit + €10 stake = €60
Worked example — horse racing:
You back a Cheltenham runner at 4/1 with a €10 stake.
Profit = €10 × 4 = €40
Total return = €40 profit + €10 stake = €50

Decimal Odds

Decimal odds express the total return per €1 staked — including your stake back. They are easier to calculate and compare across bookmakers, which is why they have become the default on most online platforms.

To convert fractional to decimal: divide the first number by the second, then add 1. So 4/1 becomes (4 ÷ 1) + 1 = 5.0. And 5/2 becomes (5 ÷ 2) + 1 = 3.5.

Worked example — decimal odds:
Decimal odds of 6.0 with a €10 stake.
Total return = €10 × 6.0 = €60
Profit = €60 − €10 stake = €50
This is the same as fractional odds of 5/1.

Implied Probability

Every set of odds implies a probability. To calculate it from decimal odds: divide 1 by the decimal odds and multiply by 100.

Example:
Decimal odds of 4.0 → implied probability = (1 ÷ 4.0) × 100 = 25%
Decimal odds of 2.0 → implied probability = (1 ÷ 2.0) × 100 = 50%

If you think the true probability of an outcome is higher than what the odds imply, the bet represents value. If lower, it doesn't.

The Bookmaker Margin (Overround)

If you add up the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a market, you will find they total more than 100%. The excess is the bookmaker's margin — also called the overround or "vig".

For example, in a two-outcome market (say, Team A vs Team B with no draw), if each side is priced at 1.90 (10/11), the implied probability for each is about 52.6%. Together that's 105.2% — the extra 5.2% is the bookmaker's built-in profit.

Typical margins are around 5–10% on football match results and major horse races, rising to 15–20% or more on longer-odds specials and coupon bets. This is why shopping for the best odds across GRAI-licensed bookmakers makes a material difference to long-term returns.

What do fractional odds mean?

The first number is your profit, the second is your stake. At 4/1, a €10 bet returns €50 (€40 profit + €10 stake back). At 5/2, a €2 bet wins €5, so a €10 bet wins €25 and returns €35 in total.

What is the bookmaker margin?

The margin (also called overround or vig) is built into odds so the bookmaker profits regardless of the result. Typical margins are 5–10% on football markets, higher on specials. It is why the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a market add up to more than 100%.

Which odds format is better — fractions or decimals?

This is personal preference. Decimal odds are easier to compare and calculate. Many Irish bookmakers let you switch format in account settings. Decimal odds are increasingly the default on online platforms.

Betting Odds Explained for Irish Punters

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Betting odds come in three main formats: decimal (the online standard in Ireland), fractional (the traditional Irish and British format) and American/moneyline (common in US markets). They all express the same thing — the implied probability of an outcome and the return on a winning bet — just in different ways. This guide walks through each format with clear worked examples, a conversion table and an honest explanation of why odds never add up to 100%.

Decimal Odds: The Online Standard in Ireland

Decimal odds represent your total return per €1 staked, including your original stake. This makes them the simplest format for calculating returns.

Formula: Total return = Stake × Decimal odds. Profit = Total return − Stake.

Worked example: You bet €20 on a team at 3.50.

  • Total return if you win: €20 × 3.50 = €70
  • Profit: €70 − €20 = €50
  • If you lose: stake is gone, return is €0

Decimal odds of 2.00 are even money (you win what you stake). Odds below 2.00 mean you are betting on a favourite (less profit than stake). Odds above 2.00 mean you are betting on an underdog (more profit than stake).

Fractional Odds: The Traditional Irish Format

Fractional odds (5/2, 7/4, 11/8) express profit relative to stake. The number on the left is the profit; the number on the right is the stake. Your original stake is returned separately.

Formula: Profit = Stake × (numerator ÷ denominator). Total return = Profit + Stake.

Worked example: You bet €20 at 5/2.

  • Profit: €20 × (5 ÷ 2) = €20 × 2.5 = €50
  • Total return: €50 + €20 = €70

5/2 is the same as 3.50 decimal. Fractional odds are still widely used by older Irish punters and in horse racing contexts, but most online bookmakers now default to decimal.

American/Moneyline Odds

American odds use plus (+) and minus (−) signs. They are less common in Ireland but appear on some bookmakers for US sports.

  • Negative number (−150): How much you must stake to win €100 profit. At −150, stake €150 to win €100 profit (total return €250).
  • Positive number (+200): How much profit you win on a €100 stake. At +200, stake €100 to win €200 profit (total return €300).

−150 converts to decimal 1.667; +200 converts to decimal 3.00. Use our odds converter to switch between formats instantly.

The Overround: Why Odds Never Add Up to 100%

The overround (also called the margin or vig) is the bookmaker's built-in edge. Here is the clearest way to see it, using a simple coin flip:

A fair coin has a 50% chance of heads and 50% of tails. Fair odds would be 2.00/2.00 for each outcome — the implied probabilities sum to exactly 100%.

A bookmaker prices the same coin flip at 1.90/1.90.

  • Implied probability of heads: 1 ÷ 1.90 = 52.6%
  • Implied probability of tails: 1 ÷ 1.90 = 52.6%
  • Total: 105.3%

That extra 5.3% is the overround — the bookmaker's margin. No matter which side wins, the bookmaker expects to retain approximately 5.3 cents from every €1 bet across both sides of the market. On a balanced book, the loser funds the winner's return and the margin stays with the bookmaker.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are decimal odds?

Decimal odds represent your total return per unit staked, including your stake. Odds of 3.00 on a €10 bet return €30 total (€20 profit + €10 stake). Decimal odds are the standard format on online betting sites in Ireland.

How do I convert fractional odds to decimal?

Decimal odds = (numerator ÷ denominator) + 1. For example, 5/2 fractional = (5 ÷ 2) + 1 = 3.50 decimal. Divide the top number by the bottom, then add 1.

What is implied probability in betting?

Implied probability is the chance of an outcome the bookmaker's odds suggest, calculated as 1 ÷ decimal odds. At 2.50, the implied probability is 40%. The sum of all implied probabilities in a market exceeds 100% — the excess is the bookmaker's margin.

What is the overround (bookmaker margin)?

The overround is the total of all implied probabilities in a market. On a fair coin flip, fair odds give a total of 100%. A bookmaker pricing it at 1.90/1.90 gives a total of 105.3% — that 5.3% excess is the margin, the bookmaker's built-in edge.

What do American odds (moneyline) mean?

American odds use plus (+) and minus (−) signs. A negative number (e.g. −150) shows how much you need to stake to win €100 profit. A positive number (e.g. +200) shows how much profit you make on a €100 stake.

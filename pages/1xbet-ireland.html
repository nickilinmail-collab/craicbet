1xBet Ireland 2026: GRAI Licence Status & What Irish Players Need to Know

1xBet is one of the world’s largest betting operators by volume, marketed heavily through football sponsorships and active across Africa, Eastern Europe, and Asia. But it holds no GRAI remote betting licence for Ireland, and its history includes the suspension of its UK Gambling Commission licence in 2020. Here is an honest, factual account of what that means for Irish players considering the platform.

What Is 1xBet?

1xBet is a large-scale international bookmaker operated by 1XCorp N.V., a company registered in Curaçao. It was founded in Russia in 2007 and has expanded aggressively into global markets, particularly across Africa, Eastern Europe, Asia, and Latin America. It is one of the most heavily marketed betting brands in sub-Saharan Africa and maintains a prominent presence through football sponsorship deals, including partnerships with European clubs and continental competitions.

In terms of scale, 1xBet offers an unusually wide range of betting markets — reportedly covering thousands of events daily across sports, esports, and virtual sports. Its platform is available in dozens of languages and accepts a broad range of payment methods targeted at emerging markets. This reach has made it one of the most searched betting brands globally.

However, 1xBet’s growth has not been without controversy. It has faced regulatory action in multiple jurisdictions, most notably the suspension of its UK Gambling Commission licence in 2020 following concerns about compliance with social responsibility and anti-money laundering requirements. Following that suspension, 1xBet exited the UK market. It operates in many countries without a local licence, relying on its Curaçao authorisation — a framework that provides considerably less player protection than regulators such as the MGA, UKGC, or GRAI.

Does 1xBet Have a GRAI Licence?

No. As of November 2026, 1xBet does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026 under Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Any operator that wishes to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

1xBet has not obtained a GRAI licence. It is not operating under Irish regulatory authorisation and does not appear in the GRAI’s licensing records. Its only current licence is a Curaçao eGaming licence, which is not recognised by Irish authorities as conferring any right to operate in the Irish market.

It is also worth noting that 1xBet’s situation differs meaningfully from that of operators such as bwin or Unibet, which hold licences from reputable EU or UK regulators despite not yet holding a GRAI licence. 1xBet’s primary licence is from Curaçao, and it has previously had a reputable licence (UKGC) suspended. For Irish players, this represents a meaningfully different risk profile.

Risks for Irish Players Using 1xBet

Using an operator without a GRAI licence always means forfeiting Irish-specific regulatory protections. With 1xBet, additional risk factors are relevant that do not apply to all unlicensed-in-Ireland operators. These are factual points, not opinions:

  • No GRAI licence: Irish players have no recourse to the GRAI, no GRAI-mandated deposit limit enforcement, and no coverage under Ireland’s National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register. A self-exclusion registered with NGEI would not bind 1xBet.
  • Curaçao licensing is considered weak: Curaçao eGaming has historically been criticised by regulators and consumer groups for limited enforcement and low barriers to licensing. The MGA, UKGC, and GRAI operate with significantly stricter player protection requirements.
  • UKGC licence suspended in 2020: The UK Gambling Commission suspended 1xBet’s UK operating licence in 2020. The UKGC cited failures relating to social responsibility obligations and anti-money laundering compliance. This is documented in UKGC enforcement records. 1xBet ceased UK operations following the suspension. This regulatory history is a material fact for any player assessing the operator.
  • Reported controversies in multiple markets: 1xBet has been reported to operate in countries without obtaining local licences. Several national regulators across Europe and Africa have issued warnings or taken enforcement actions against the brand. These are reported facts from regulators and media; CraicBet cannot independently verify every individual claim.
  • No formal Irish complaints route: If a dispute arises over a withdrawal, account closure, or bonus terms, Irish players have no formal route to escalate the matter to an Irish regulator. Any complaint would need to go through Curaçao’s licensing body, which has limited formal enforcement mechanisms compared to the GRAI or UKGC.

What Does This Mean Practically?

Concretely, an Irish player using 1xBet in 2026 is operating outside the protections of Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The specific practical consequences are:

  • No mandatory deposit limits under Irish law: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required by Irish regulations. 1xBet, operating under Curaçao rules, is not bound by these requirements.
  • No NGEI integration: The National Gambling Exclusion Identification register is Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. GRAI licensees must check and honour NGEI registrations. 1xBet has no obligation to do so.
  • No Irish complaints escalation: Disputes with GRAI-licensed operators can be escalated to the GRAI itself if unresolved. With 1xBet, no equivalent Irish route exists.
  • Credit card ban may not apply: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by this ban — meaning you could potentially use a credit card, gambling with borrowed money in a way Irish regulators have specifically prohibited for licensed operators.
  • Withdrawal timelines at operator discretion: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. 1xBet withdrawal timelines are governed only by its own terms and its Curaçao licence, which has limited formal enforcement track record.

This is not an abstract list of regulatory distinctions. Each item above represents a protection that Irish regulators considered important enough to mandate by law — and that 1xBet is not required to provide.

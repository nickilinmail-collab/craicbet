Betting Tax in Ireland 2026: Do You Pay Tax on Gambling Winnings?

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 3 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Short answer: Irish bettors do not pay personal tax on gambling winnings. Winnings from sports betting, casino games, poker and other gambling are not considered taxable income under Irish tax law. The 2% betting duty is paid by licensed bookmakers, not by you. This guide explains the full picture.

Key facts at a glance
  • Individual gamblers in Ireland pay no income tax on betting or casino winnings
  • Operators pay a 2% duty on gross gaming revenue from Irish customers
  • Remote (online) betting duty has applied to online operators serving Ireland since 2015
  • This page is general information only — not tax advice. Complex situations: consult a qualified Irish tax professional.

Do Irish Gamblers Pay Tax on Winnings?

No. Under established Irish tax law, gambling winnings are not subject to income tax, capital gains tax or any other personal tax for Irish residents. This applies whether you win from:

  • Sports betting (football, horse racing, GAA, rugby, etc.)
  • Online casino games (slots, roulette, blackjack, live casino)
  • Poker — online or live tournament
  • Lotto and scratch cards (National Lottery)
  • Betting exchanges

The Revenue Commissioners have historically treated gambling winnings as outside the scope of income tax for ordinary punters. The rationale is that gambling is not a trade or business for the typical bettor, and winnings are therefore windfall gains rather than earned income.

The 2% Betting Duty: What It Is and Who Pays It

Ireland levies a 2% betting duty on licensed bookmakers and betting exchanges. Here is exactly how it works:

Who pays it?The licensed bookmaker or exchange, not the bettor
What is it applied to?Gross gaming revenue (stakes received minus winnings paid out)
Rate2% of gross gaming revenue from Irish customers
Does it affect my odds?Indirectly — it is factored into the bookmaker's margin

The duty does not come off your stake or your winnings directly. You will never see a "tax deduction" on your betting slip or withdrawal. The practical effect is that it is baked into the odds you are quoted — one of the reasons bookmaker margins exist.

How the 2% Duty Affects Your Odds

Understanding the duty's effect on odds is part of being a sharper bettor. A bookmaker operating in Ireland must cover this duty as part of their cost base, which means their odds are slightly less generous than they would be in a zero-duty environment. This is one of the reasons why comparing odds across different operators — particularly those licensed in different jurisdictions — can produce meaningfully different prices on the same event.

Remote Betting Duty: Online Operators Since 2015

Before 2015, online operators could serve Irish customers from offshore locations without paying Irish betting duty. The Finance (No. 2) Act 2013 introduced remote gambling duties, which took effect in 2015. This means that any operator accepting bets from Irish customers — regardless of where the operator is based — must register with Revenue and pay the 2% duty on gross gaming revenue from those Irish customers.

This was a significant change that levelled the playing field between Irish-based bookmakers and large international operators. It also means that the duty is relevant whether you bet with a GRAI-licensed operator, a UKGC-licensed operator, or an MGA-licensed operator — all must pay it if they serve Irish customers.

What About Professional Gamblers?

The Revenue Commissioners have not historically sought to treat gambling as a trade for tax purposes in Ireland, even for habitual or professional gamblers. This distinguishes Ireland from some other jurisdictions where professional gamblers can face income tax on their winnings.

However, this is a nuanced area. If your gambling activity is very substantial, highly organised, and generates consistent significant income, the position is less clear-cut. Revenue reserves the right to assess individual circumstances. If you are in any doubt about your personal tax position, you should seek independent advice from a qualified Irish chartered tax adviser (CTA). Nothing on this page constitutes tax advice.

Poker Winnings: Is Poker Different?

No. The same principles apply to poker winnings for Irish residents — whether from cash games online, live tournaments, or online poker rooms. Revenue has not historically pursued poker winnings as taxable income in Ireland. The position may theoretically differ for someone who derives their primary livelihood from professional tournament poker and runs it as a business, but there is no established precedent for Revenue treating ordinary poker players' winnings as taxable.

Lotto and Scratch Cards

National Lottery prizes — including the main Lotto jackpot — are entirely tax-free for Irish residents. The National Lottery itself pays a levy to the state, but individual winners keep 100% of their prize. This is the same principle as sports betting and casino winnings: the levy is paid by the operator, not the winner.

What Changed Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 restructured the licensing and regulatory framework for gambling in Ireland, creating GRAI as the independent regulator. It did not change the betting duty rate or the tax treatment of winnings for individual players. The 2% duty rate and the personal tax exemption on winnings remain unchanged.

What did change is the regulatory and compliance environment for operators: advertising restrictions, player protection requirements, and mandatory GRAI licensing for operators serving Irish customers. These changes improve player safety but do not affect the tax treatment of your winnings. See our GRAI licence guide for full details on the regulatory changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I have to pay tax on gambling winnings in Ireland?

No. Irish residents do not pay income tax, capital gains tax or any other personal tax on gambling winnings, whether from sports betting, casino games, poker, or other gambling. Winnings are not considered taxable income for the ordinary punter under established Irish tax law.

Who pays the 2% betting duty in Ireland?

Licensed bookmakers and betting exchanges pay the duty to Revenue, not the individual bettor. The rate is 2% on gross gaming revenue from Irish customers. This cost is absorbed into the bookmaker's odds margin, which is why comparing odds between operators using our margin calculator is worthwhile.

Is online gambling taxed differently to in-shop betting in Ireland?

For the individual player, the result is the same: no personal tax on winnings in either case. The betting duty framework applies to both licensed remote (online) operators and retail (in-shop) bookmakers. Remote betting duty has applied to online operators accepting Irish bets since 2015.

Does the 2% duty apply to my stake or my winnings?

Neither directly. The 2% duty is paid by the operator on their gross gaming revenue (roughly, total stakes minus total winnings paid out). You as the punter do not pay it on your stake or your winnings. The effect on you is indirect: the duty is factored into the bookmaker's margin, slightly affecting the odds you are offered.

What if I am a professional gambler in Ireland?

Revenue has historically not treated gambling winnings as trading income for tax purposes in Ireland, even for professional or habitual gamblers. However, this is a complex area that depends on individual circumstances. If you earn significant consistent income from betting, seek independent advice from a qualified Irish chartered tax adviser. Nothing on this page constitutes tax advice.

Is it different for poker winnings in Ireland?

The general rule — gambling winnings are not taxable for Irish residents — applies to poker too. Revenue has not historically sought to tax poker winnings as income. If your circumstances are unusual (very large or consistent winnings that form a primary income), seek professional tax advice.

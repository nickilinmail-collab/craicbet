Are Betting Winnings Taxable in Ireland? The Revenue Position

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Betting winnings are not subject to tax in Ireland for the vast majority of punters. Irish Revenue does not treat gambling winnings as income for tax purposes. Here is a detailed look at the rules, including the edge case of professional gambling.

Revenue's Position on Gambling Winnings

Irish Revenue Commissioners treat gambling winnings as exempt from Income Tax, USC (Universal Social Charge), and PRSI. This is long-standing Irish tax policy predating the GRAI and was not changed by the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Whether you win €50 on a weekend accumulator or €50,000 on a Cheltenham Festival bet, the amount is yours to keep without any tax liability.

This applies equally to: sports betting, horse racing, casino games, poker, lotteries, and any other form of gambling.

Professional Gambling: The Exception

Revenue could in theory treat gambling as a trade if you operate in a systematic, commercial manner — using methodologies, running it as a primary livelihood, keeping professional records. Under s.18 Taxes Consolidation Act 1997, trading profits are taxable. However, there is no published Revenue guidance identifying specific criteria, and Revenue has very rarely pursued professional gambler cases in practice. The Courts have set a high bar for what constitutes a gambling “trade.”

Keeping Records: Is It Necessary?

For casual punters, there is no legal requirement to keep records of betting activity for tax purposes. If you are a high-volume bettor earning substantial sums systematically, it would be prudent to keep records and potentially seek professional tax advice, but this is not a legal obligation for recreational gamblers.

The 2% Operator Betting Duty

Operators pay a 2% betting duty on gross stakes from Irish customers. This is entirely the operator’s liability. You may notice marginally different odds at different bookmakers partly as a result of how they handle this duty — some absorb it, some reflect it in slightly lower odds — but you pay no betting duty directly.

What the 1% Betting Duty Means for You

Ireland imposes a 1% betting duty on the total amount staked. This is paid by the bookmaker, not deducted from your winnings. However:

  • Some operators (mainly retail shops) pass the duty on to the customer — read the terms
  • Online bookmakers licensed by GRAI typically absorb the duty within their margin
  • No tax return is required for betting winnings in Ireland under current Revenue Commissioners guidance
  • If gambling is your primary income source or constitutes a business, different rules may apply — consult a tax adviser

Can I Bet with Crypto in Ireland Legally

Betting with cryptocurrency is a legal grey area in Ireland. No GRAI remote betting licence explicitly authorises crypto-only betting sites — licences are issued to operators handling regulated currency (EUR). Crypto betting platforms operating without a GRAI licence are unlicensed from an Irish regulatory perspective, meaning no consumer protection, no dispute resolution, and no KYC guarantees. Irish residents can technically access them, but do so without the safeguards that apply to GRAI-licensed sites.

Tax note: any winnings from betting — crypto or fiat — are not subject to income tax in Ireland if betting is recreational. However, converting crypto winnings to EUR may trigger a Capital Gains Tax event on any gain from the crypto itself.

Can I Bet on Credit Card in Ireland

Since April 2020, most major GRAI/Revenue-licensed operators have voluntarily removed credit card deposits following the UK's ban, and some Irish banks block gambling transactions on credit cards regardless. Debit cards (Visa Debit, Mastercard Debit) remain the standard — they draw from your account balance, not borrowed credit. If your credit card is declined at a betting site, switch to your debit card or use Revolut, PayPal, or a bank transfer.

Can I Have Two Betting Accounts in Ireland

Having two accounts at the same operator is a breach of their terms and conditions and can result in both accounts being closed and funds confiscated. Having accounts at two different operators is permitted and common — there is no legal restriction. Many Irish bettors hold 3–5 accounts to access the best odds across operators (line shopping) and to claim multiple welcome bonuses.

Can I Use a VPN to Access Blocked Betting Sites Ireland

Using a VPN to access a site that is blocked in Ireland, or that does not accept Irish customers, violates the site's terms and conditions. If your account is discovered to be using a VPN, it will be closed and winnings forfeited. Additionally, an unlicensed site operating outside GRAI oversight offers no consumer protection — disputes cannot be escalated to the GRAI or any Irish authority. The risk is entirely on the player.

Can I Withdraw to a Different Card Than I Deposited Ireland

Under anti-money laundering (AML) rules, most operators require withdrawals to return to the same payment method used to deposit. If you deposited with Visa Debit, the withdrawal must go back to that card. Only after the deposit amount is returned can you withdraw remaining winnings to a bank account or e-wallet. This is a regulatory requirement, not an operator choice — it prevents money laundering through betting accounts.

Can I Keep My Winnings If My Account Is Closed Ireland

If an operator closes your account for breaching their terms (bonus abuse, multiple accounts, matched betting flagged as abusive), they may void bets and confiscate funds. If closure is at your request (self-exclusion or cooling-off), your balance is returned minus any pending bonuses with active wagering requirements. For disputes, the GRAI provides a formal complaints process — file at grai.ie before escalating further.

How Much Can You Win on a Bet Ireland Tax Free

Betting winnings in Ireland are not subject to income tax, Capital Gains Tax, or any other tax when betting is recreational. There is no upper limit — you can win €1,000, €100,000, or €1 million on a single bet and owe zero tax to Revenue. The only tax on betting is the 1% betting duty paid by the operator (included in the margin), not the punter. This is one of the most punter-friendly tax regimes in Europe.

Exception: if the Revenue Commissioners determine that betting is your primary business activity (professional gambler), winnings may be treated as income. This is rare and applies to very few individuals. For the vast majority of recreational bettors, all winnings are completely tax-free.

How Long Does KYC Verification Take Betting Ireland

KYC (Know Your Customer) verification at Irish betting sites typically takes 24–72 hours for standard documents (passport or driving licence + proof of address). Some operators use automated ID verification that completes in under 10 minutes. GRAI-licensed operators must complete KYC before allowing withdrawals. If KYC is taking longer than 5 business days, contact customer support directly — common delays are caused by blurry document photos or mismatched address details.

Are betting winnings taxable in Ireland?

No — betting and gambling winnings are not subject to income tax, USC, or PRSI for recreational punters in Ireland. You keep 100% of your winnings.

Does Revenue know about my betting winnings?

Revenue has access to financial data, but gambling winnings are not reportable income and there is no tax return requirement for recreational gamblers. Keep records if you bet professionally.

What counts as professional gambling for tax purposes in Ireland?

Revenue looks at systematisation, regularity, and whether gambling is your primary commercial livelihood. This is a high bar rarely applied. Most regular bettors are not affected.

Do I need to declare poker winnings in Ireland?

No — poker winnings are treated the same as sports betting. Tax-free for recreational players. Only if poker is a systematic, commercial primary income might Revenue consider it taxable.

Is the 2% betting duty taken from my winnings?

No — the 2% betting duty is paid by licensed operators on stakes received. It is their liability, not yours. Some operators reflect it in their pricing (lower odds); others absorb it. You keep 100% of your winnings either way.

Best Odds Guaranteed Explained

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

Best Odds Guaranteed — BOG — is the most genuinely punter-friendly concession in racing betting, and one of the few promotions whose value survives the honest-maths test. It is also hedged with conditions that quietly limit what it is worth. Here is the full picture.

What BOG actually is

Take an early price on a horse — say 4/1 at 10am. If the starting price (SP) at the off is bigger — say 6/1 — a BOG bookmaker pays you at 6/1. If the SP is shorter, you keep your 4/1. You get the better of the two prices, automatically. It removes the classic punter's dilemma: take the morning price and watch it drift, or wait for SP and watch it crash.

When it applies — and when it doesn't

BOG is typically offered on UK and Irish horse racing (sometimes greyhounds), usually only from a set time on the day of the race — commonly from 8am or from when final declarations are priced. It almost never applies to ante-post bets struck days or weeks out, and often excludes specials, enhanced prices and some each-way terms. Every bookmaker writes its own rules, so the phrase “BOG applies” always has a footnote worth reading.

The expected value: why BOG is real money

BOG is a free option. Your payout is max(price taken, SP) — you can only gain. On a horse that drifts from 4/1 to 6/1, a €10 win bet pays €70 instead of €50: €20 of pure concession. Across a season of day-of-race bets, drifters are common enough that BOG adds a measurable percentage to returns — one of the few promos whose expected value is unambiguously positive rather than cleverly disguised.

The catches

First, maximum stakes and maximum extra winnings: BOG terms usually cap the stake or the additional payout the concession can generate, so it protects the recreational punter, not the big hitter. Second, it does not make a bad price good: BOG on a horse you took at 2/1 that should have been 3/1 still loses money in expectation — the guarantee only compares your price with SP, not with the true odds. Third, accounts that use BOG very profitably can find themselves restricted, a commercial reality of modern bookmaking. Fourth, timing: bets struck before the BOG window opens get no protection at all.

BOG versus taking SP

With BOG in place the decision is simple: if you fancy a horse, take the current price — you hold the early price if it shortens and SP if it drifts. Betting at SP by choice surrenders the option for nothing. Without BOG (ante-post, overseas racing), the old trade-off returns and taking a price is a genuine judgement call.

Using it well

Bet within the BOG window, read the stake caps, and treat the concession as a bonus on bets you would make anyway — never as a reason to bet more. And as with all racing betting: set a budget that belongs to your entertainment money.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does Best Odds Guaranteed mean?

You get the better of the price you took and the starting price. Take 4/1 and the horse goes off 6/1, you are paid at 6/1; if it goes off 2/1, you keep your 4/1.

Does BOG apply to ante-post bets?

Almost never. BOG windows typically open on the morning of the race; ante-post bets struck earlier are excluded, along with many specials and enhanced prices.

Is Best Odds Guaranteed really worth anything?

Yes — it is one of the few promotions with unambiguously positive expected value, since it can only improve your payout. The terms cap stakes and extra winnings, so read them.

Key BOG Terms and Conditions

  • BOG applies to Irish and UK horse racing at most major operators
  • BOG applies to pre-race bets only — not in-play
  • Free bets may not be eligible for BOG upgrades — check T&Cs

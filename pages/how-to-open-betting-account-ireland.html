Opening a Betting Account in Ireland: What the GRAI Era Asks of You

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

Opening a betting account in Ireland changed with the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and the arrival of the GRAI as regulator. Verification is no longer an afterthought done at your first withdrawal: licensed bookmakers must confirm who you are and that you are 18 or over before you gamble. Here is the process step by step, what documents to have ready, and what your rights are once the account is open.

Step 1: Check the licence before anything else

Before you type a single detail, confirm the operator actually holds a GRAI licence — the register is public and the check takes two minutes (our licence check guide walks through it). Every protection on this page applies only at licensed operators. An unlicensed site can take your documents and your deposit and owe you nothing.

Step 2: The sign-up form

Expect the standard set: full name exactly as on your ID, date of birth, home address, email and mobile number. Use your real details — mismatches between the form and your documents are the single most common cause of verification delays. You will also set deposit limits at many operators during sign-up; set them honestly rather than at the maximum, because the calm version of you is the one filling in the form.

Step 3: Identity and age verification (KYC)

Licensed operators must verify age and identity before you bet, not after you win. Typically you will photograph or upload one government photo ID — a passport, Irish driving licence or Public Services Card — and the operator runs an electronic check. Where the electronic check cannot confirm your address, you will be asked for a proof of address: a utility bill, bank statement or official letter, generally dated within the last six months. Automated checks often clear in minutes; manual document review can take longer, and you should hear back within a few days at most.

Step 4: Questions about your money

Do not be surprised by questions about occupation or source of funds, particularly around large deposits — anti-money-laundering rules require operators to understand where significant sums come from, and a bookmaker may request payslips or bank statements before accepting or releasing large amounts. It feels intrusive; it is also what separates a licensed operator from a site that asks nothing because it never intends to pay out.

Your rights once the account is open

Under the Act, a licensed bookmaker must keep your funds separate from company money, pay out what you win, and close your account whenever you ask without a runaround. You cannot deposit with a credit card — the ban covers cards directly and credit-funded e-wallet payments (see our wider rules coverage and punter rules guide). If an operator mishandles your verification or your money, complain to the operator first, then escalate to the GRAI, which can sanction licensees. If verification goes wrong

Most failures are fixable: blurry photos, an address that differs from the form, a document outside the accepted list. Re-submit clean, current documents and the queue usually clears. If an operator will not verify you and will not return a deposit, that is a complaint, not a dead end — follow the route in our complaints guide. 18+. Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need to open a betting account in Ireland?

One government photo ID — a passport, Irish driving licence or Public Services Card — and, if the operator's electronic check cannot confirm your address, a proof of address such as a utility bill or bank statement, generally dated within the last six months.

How long does betting account verification take?

Automated electronic checks often clear in minutes. Manual document review takes longer — typically hours to a few days. Mismatched details between the sign-up form and your ID, and unclear document photos, are the most common causes of delay.

Can I bet before my account is verified?

No. Under the GRAI-era rules a licensed Irish operator must verify your age and identity before you gamble — not at your first withdrawal. A site that lets you deposit and bet with no checks at all is a warning sign, not a convenience.

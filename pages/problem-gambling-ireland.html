Signs of Problem Gambling

Problem gambling does not always look the same. Some people gamble large amounts rarely; others gamble small amounts constantly. What matters is the effect on your life. Common signs include:

  • Thinking about gambling frequently, or feeling restless when you try to stop
  • Chasing losses — betting more to try to recover money already lost
  • Gambling with money set aside for rent, bills, or food
  • Hiding gambling activity from family or friends, or lying about it
  • Borrowing money or selling possessions to fund gambling
  • Feeling anxious, irritable, or depressed when not gambling
  • Missing work, study, or family commitments because of gambling
  • Feeling that gambling is out of your control, even when you want to stop

Experiencing some of these does not mean you have a clinical disorder — but it is a signal worth taking seriously. The earlier you seek help, the more options are available.

Self-Help Tools at Irish Gambling Sites

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, all GRAI-licensed operators must provide a range of responsible gambling tools. You do not need to be in crisis to use them — they are there for everyone:

  • Deposit limits — cap daily, weekly, or monthly spending (reductions take effect immediately)
  • Self-exclusion — block access to your account for a minimum of 6 months or permanently
  • Reality checks — on-screen reminders of how long you have been playing and how much you have spent
  • Session time limits — set a maximum session length before a prompt appears
  • Cooling-off periods — if you request a limit increase, a mandatory wait applies before it goes through

All these tools are found under Responsible Gambling or Account Settings. Setting a deposit limit takes under two minutes. Read our deposit limits guide → or self-exclusion guide → for step-by-step instructions.

Supporting Someone with a Gambling Problem

If someone you care about is struggling with gambling, it can be difficult to know what to do. A few principles that tend to help:

  • Stay calm and non-judgmental. Shame and blame rarely help and often push people further away from seeking help.
  • Listen more than you advise. Sometimes being heard is what a person needs first.
  • Share information, not ultimatums. Let them know that help is available — 1800 753 753 or gamblersanonymous.ie — without making it conditional on their choices.
  • Get support for yourself. Gam-Anon (linked from gamblersanonymous.ie) exists specifically for families and partners of people with gambling problems. The Gambling Awareness Trust can also advise family members directly.
  • Protect your own finances. If gambling is affecting shared money, Citizens Information and MABS can advise on steps to take.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is problem gambling?▾
Problem gambling is gambling behaviour that disrupts personal, family, or vocational pursuits. It exists on a spectrum — from mild concern to a serious compulsive disorder. The key signs are preoccupation with gambling, chasing losses, gambling with money needed for essentials, hiding gambling from others, and feeling unable to stop.
Where can I get free, confidential gambling help in Ireland?▾
The Gambling Awareness Trust helpline is 1800 753 753 — free, confidential, and available 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Gamblers Anonymous Ireland runs peer-support meetings nationwide (gamblersanonymous.ie). The HSE's gamblingcare.ie provides professional counselling referrals. All services are free.
What tools do Irish gambling sites offer to help with problem gambling?▾
GRAI-licensed operators must provide: deposit limits (daily, weekly, monthly), self-exclusion (minimum 6 months, up to permanent), session time limits and reality checks, and cooling-off periods before any limit increase. These tools are found in account settings under Responsible Gambling.
How can I help a family member with a gambling problem?▾
Start by speaking calmly and without judgment — avoid ultimatums. Encourage them to contact the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753 or Gamblers Anonymous (gamblersanonymous.ie). Gam-Anon supports family members and partners. The Gambling Awareness Trust can also advise family members directly.
What protections does the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 provide?▾
The Act requires all GRAI-licensed operators to offer mandatory responsible gambling tools, comply with strict advertising codes, contribute to a Social Impact Fund for treatment services, and honour a national self-exclusion register once it launches. It also strengthens the GRAI's enforcement powers against non-compliant operators.

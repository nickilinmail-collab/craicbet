Football All-Ireland Quarter-Final Betting Ireland 2026

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 CraicBet Editorial Team 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

The All-Ireland football quarter-finals at Croke Park mark the business end of the championship, with the eight best counties competing for a semi-final place. For punters, the quarter-finals are one of the highest-volume GAA betting weekends of the year, with strong markets and competitive odds at GRAI-licensed bookmakers.

Quarter-Final Format

The Super 8s format was replaced by straight quarter-finals in a restructuring of the football championship. Provincial champions enter the semi-finals directly, while the other eight qualifiers play off in quarter-finals. The draw determines fixtures and can produce high-profile early clashes between All-Ireland contenders.

Key Factors for Quarter-Final Research

Injury news from county management press conferences, the route each team took through qualifiers (how many games played, fitness concerns), and historical Croke Park performances are all relevant. Some counties perform above expectations on big match days at headquarters; others struggle with the occasion.

Frequently Asked Questions

When are the All-Ireland football quarter-finals?

The quarter-finals take place in late July, usually two weekends before the All-Ireland semi-finals in August. Dates are published by the GAA on gaa.ie.

Do I need to bet before the quarter-finals start?

No. GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer in-play betting during all major All-Ireland quarter-final games. You can wait for the game to start before betting if you prefer to assess early momentum.

How does the draw affect quarter-final betting?

The quarter-final draw is made publicly and immediately affects outright All-Ireland prices. A county drawn against a weaker opponent may shorten in the outright market; one drawn against a strong side may ease. Act quickly after the draw if you spot a price movement you want to take.

What is handicap betting in GAA football?

A handicap bet applies a points start to the perceived weaker team. For example, if Leitrim receive a 12-point start against Kerry, a bet on Leitrim wins if Kerry win by 11 points or fewer, or if Leitrim win outright.

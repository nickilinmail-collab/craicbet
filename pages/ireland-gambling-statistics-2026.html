Irish Gambling Market Size

The Irish licensed gambling market generated €911.2 million in Q1 2026, covering revenue from licensed remote and in-person operators. At this quarterly rate the annualised market exceeds €3.6 billion — one of the highest per-capita gambling markets in the EU relative to population.

The Irish market is dominated by remote (online and telephone) betting. Horse racing and football are the two largest by volume, with GAA and rugby carrying significant betting interest during their respective seasons. The Cheltenham Festival in March and the Grand National in April represent the two biggest single-week spikes in Irish betting activity annually.

Regulation & Licensing

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) is the independent statutory body established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. GRAI has responsibility for licensing, compliance monitoring, advertising standards, and consumer protection for all gambling in Ireland.

GRAI began issuing remote betting licences following the Act's commencement on 1 July 2026. The licence number format for remote betting is GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-NNNN — where RB denotes Remote Betting. Licences are verifiable on grai.ie.

The Black Market Problem

Ireland's new regulatory framework is partly a response to the scale of unlicensed gambling activity. Industry research estimates that across Europe, approximately 70% of online betting volume passes through unlicensed operators, representing around €80 billion annually and involving an estimated 6,000 unlicensed operator sites.

Irish consumers who bet with unlicensed sites:

  • Have no access to GRAI's consumer complaints process
  • Have no statutory protection if the operator refuses to pay
  • May have no recourse for responsible gambling tools (deposit limits, self-exclusion)
  • Risk using sites with no independent game fairness verification

Frequently Asked Questions

How big is the Irish gambling market?

The Irish licensed gambling market generated €911.2 million in Q1 2026 alone, putting the annual run rate above €3.6 billion. This covers bets placed through licensed operators and does not include estimated black market activity. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 commenced on 1 July 2026, bringing the market under GRAI as the statutory regulator.

How many GRAI licences have been issued?

As of September 2026, GRAI had issued 32 remote betting licences. These are remote BETTING licences only (licence category GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-NNNN). Zero casino licences have been issued; GRAI has not yet opened applications for remote casino licences, which are expected to become available in 2027–2028.

What is the black market gambling problem in Ireland?

An estimated 70% of European betting volume passes through unlicensed operators — approximately €80 billion annually across ~6,000 unregulated sites. Irish consumers using unlicensed sites have no GRAI consumer protection and no recourse if disputes arise. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced penalties of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover for unlicensed operators targeting Ireland.

How Snooker Betting Works

Snooker offers a wider range of markets than many punters realise. Here is a plain-language breakdown of the most common bet types.

Market What You’re Betting On Typical Availability
Match Winner Which player wins the match outright. The simplest and most liquid snooker market. All tournaments, all rounds
Frame Handicap A virtual frame advantage applied to the underdog. Bet on the adjusted frame score, not the raw result. Main tour events; best-of formats of 11+ frames
Correct Frame Score Predict the exact frames won by each player (e.g. 10–6). Longer odds reflecting the precision required. Longer matches; World Championship from second round
Century Break in Match Will at least one century (100+ break) be made during the match? Yes/No market. Main tour events
Highest Break Which player will record the highest single break in the match? Or outright highest break in a tournament. Main tour events; major tournaments

Most GRAI-licensed sites also offer in-play markets — typically next frame winner and total frames in match — with odds updating between frames. Settlement rules for snooker bets vary between operators; always check whether a walkover or retirement mid-match voids your bet or stands at the point of abandonment.

Snooker’s Big Events

These are the tournaments that generate the most betting interest among Irish punters. No invented dates are listed below — event windows are based on the sport’s standard annual calendar.

World Championship — The Crucible Held at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield each April and May. The sport’s most prestigious event, run over 17 days with the final played over 35 frames. Outright and round-by-round markets open months in advance.
UK Championship The second-biggest ranking event, typically held in York in December. Full-frame best-of formats and a large draw make it a favourite for frame handicap bettors.
Masters An invitational event held at Alexandra Palace in London, usually in January or February. Restricted to the world’s top 16 players, which concentrates betting interest and tightens match markets.

Beyond the Triple Crown, the World Snooker Tour calendar includes numerous ranking events throughout the season where outright, match winner and frame markets are available on GRAI-licensed sites.

Why Snooker Is Popular with Irish Bettors

Snooker has consistently drawn strong betting interest in Ireland for several reasons:

  • TV coverage: Major snooker events have long had extensive free-to-air and subscription TV coverage accessible in Ireland, keeping the sport in front of a large audience.
  • Well-known players: Household names like Ronnie O’Sullivan and Mark Selby — among the most recognised figures in the sport — give casual bettors a recognisable point of entry when picking match winner bets.
  • Frame-by-frame structure: The natural pause between frames suits in-play betting, allowing punters to assess momentum without the continuous pressure of fast-moving sports.
  • Long tournament formats: Best-of-25 or best-of-35 World Championship matches generate sustained interest across days, not just 90 minutes.
  • Wide market availability: Most GRAI-licensed sites list snooker across the main tour season, with enhanced coverage for the Triple Crown events.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the most popular snooker bet type?

Match winner is the most common snooker bet: you simply pick which player wins the match. Frame handicap betting is a popular alternative when there is a clear favourite — the handicap levels the market and produces more competitive odds. See our snooker betting guide for a full market breakdown.

How does frame handicap betting work?

The bookmaker gives the underdog a virtual frame advantage and the favourite a deficit. For example, if Player A is listed at −3.5 and Player B at +3.5, Player A must win by 4 or more frames for a bet on them to pay out. The handicap is applied to the final frame score. This market is most commonly offered in longer best-of formats. Full examples are in our snooker betting guide.

What is the Snooker World Championship?

The World Snooker Championship is the sport’s most prestigious ranking event, held annually at the Crucible Theatre in Sheffield each April and May. The tournament runs across 17 days in a knockout format, with the final decided over 35 frames. It carries the sport’s largest prize fund and generates the most betting activity of any snooker event.

Can I bet live on snooker?

Yes. Most GRAI-licensed betting sites offer in-play markets on major snooker tournaments. Common live markets include next frame winner, total frames in match, and century break in match.

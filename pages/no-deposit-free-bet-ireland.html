What Changed with GRAI Bonus Rules?

When GRAI became Ireland's gambling regulator on 1 July 2026, it introduced new transparency requirements for bonus advertising. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, licensed operators must:

  • Display all material terms clearly and prominently alongside any bonus offer
  • Include minimum odds, wagering requirements and expiry dates in the main offer text — not buried in small print
  • Avoid "misleading" bonus descriptions (GRAI's exact standard)
  • Not target bonus promotions at players who have set deposit limits or requested marketing opt-outs

These rules have made some types of no deposit offer less commercially attractive to operators, which is why many have shifted towards deposit match bonuses with clearer value propositions.

Reading the Small Print: What to Check

Under GRAI rules, the terms must be clear — but you should still check:

  1. Wagering requirement — how many times must you bet the bonus before withdrawing? (1x = bet it once; 5x = bet it 5 times)
  2. Minimum odds — most free bets must be placed at odds of at least evens (2.0 / 1/1) or higher
  3. Stake-not-returned (SNR) — most free bet winnings exclude the stake value from your payout
  4. Expiry — no deposit free bets typically expire within 7 days if unused
  5. Market restrictions — some are valid only on specific sports or events
⚠️ Avoid unlicensed sites for bonuses. Unlicensed operators targeting Irish players often advertise huge no deposit bonuses but have no obligation to pay them out. Always verify GRAI licence status at grai.ie before depositing.

Honest Maths: Is a No Deposit Free Bet "Free"?

Yes and no. The bet itself costs you nothing. But if the offer has a 3x wagering requirement and minimum odds of 2.0, here's what that actually means for a €10 free bet:

  • You must wager €10 × 3 = €30 in total before withdrawing winnings
  • At 2.0 odds, the implied probability is 50% — so the expected return on €30 wagered is roughly €30 (break-even before margin)
  • The bookmaker's margin (typically 5–8%) means your expected value is slightly negative overall
  • The free bet gives you a chance at profit, but it's not a guaranteed win

This is not to say free bets have no value — they absolutely do. But honest maths means understanding what you're actually getting.

Expected Value of a No Deposit Free Bet

A €10 SNR no-deposit free bet placed at minimum odds of Evens (2.0 decimal) has an expected value of approximately €5 — if you win (50% probability), you receive €10 in winnings (stake not returned). After 1× wagering on the €10 winnings (required by most sites), the realistic take-home is €10 less the expected loss from the wagering. For a quick calculation: EV = bet_amount × (probability_of_win × odds − 1). At Evens with 50% probability: EV = €10 × (0.5 × 2.0 − 1) = €0 — meaning the EV is neutral before wagering, slightly negative after. The main value is exploring the site at zero personal cost.

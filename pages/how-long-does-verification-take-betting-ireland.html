How Long Does KYC Verification Take on Irish Betting Sites?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

KYC (Know Your Customer) verification is mandatory at every GRAI-licensed bookmaker. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, operators must verify your identity before allowing withdrawals — and usually before you can place larger bets. Here is what to expect and how to get through it quickly.

Typical KYC Timelines in 2026

Most GRAI-licensed bookmakers complete standard identity checks within 24–72 hours during business days. Automated checks (passport scan plus selfie) can be instant or under an hour. Manual reviews take longer, particularly around big sporting events when volumes spike. Some operators now require verification before you can deposit at all.

  • Automated ID scan: Instant to 2 hours
  • Manual document review: 24–72 hours
  • Enhanced due diligence (large amounts): Up to 5 business days

What Documents Are Required

Standard KYC requires proof of identity and proof of address. Accepted identity documents: passport, national ID card, or Irish driving licence. Proof of address: utility bill, bank statement, or government letter dated within the past 3 months. Some operators also require a photo of your payment card (with middle digits covered) and a selfie holding your ID.

For withdrawals above a certain threshold (typically €2,000–€5,000), operators may request source of funds documentation — a payslip or bank statement showing where the money came from. This is a GRAI anti-money-laundering requirement.

Why GRAI Requires Identity Verification

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, GRAI-licensed operators are legally required to verify the age and identity of every customer. The minimum age to bet in Ireland is 18. Operators who allow underage betting can lose their licence. KYC also prevents money laundering and protects against identity fraud — if someone else tries to withdraw from your account, the name must match the verified identity on file.

Tips to Speed Up Your Verification

Use high-resolution photos with no glare. Make sure all four corners of the document are visible. Upload documents in JPEG or PDF format. Selfie photos should show your face clearly alongside the document. If using a utility bill, ensure the address matches your registered account address exactly. Any mismatch triggers a manual review.

Documents Accepted for Irish KYC

  • Photo ID (one required): Irish passport, Irish driving licence, EU national ID card, or UK passport.
  • Proof of address (one required): Utility bill (gas, electricity, water), bank statement, government letter — dated within 3 months, showing your name and address.
  • Source of funds (if requested): Payslips (last 3 months), bank statements, employment contract, pension statement, or proof of business income.
  • Tips for faster approval: Submit clear, full-page scans (not photos of a screen), ensure the document is in date, and use the site's secure upload tool rather than emailing.

What Happens If Verification Fails

  • The site will email you specifying what additional documents are needed — respond promptly.
  • Until verification is complete, withdrawals are typically blocked but you can continue to deposit and play (up to any deposit limits set).
  • If you believe the verification requirement is unreasonable or your account has been blocked without explanation, you can complain to the GRAI at grai.ie.
  • Accounts that cannot be verified after multiple attempts may be closed with the balance returned via the original deposit method.
Is KYC verification mandatory at Irish betting sites?

Yes. GRAI-licensed operators must verify your identity under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. This applies to all operators holding an Irish remote betting licence.

What ID do I need to verify my betting account in Ireland?

A passport, national ID card, or Irish driving licence for identity. A utility bill or bank statement from the last 3 months for proof of address.

Can I bet before my account is verified?

Some operators allow small bets before full KYC, but most require verification before your first withdrawal. Some now require it before any deposit.

What is enhanced due diligence on betting sites?

For large withdrawals or deposits (typically over €2,000–€5,000), operators may request source of funds documentation such as payslips or bank statements. This is a legal anti-money-laundering requirement.

What happens if my KYC fails?

The operator will tell you why and what to resubmit. Common failures include blurry photos, mismatched addresses, or expired documents. See our guide on KYC failures for the full appeal process.

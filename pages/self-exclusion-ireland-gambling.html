Self-Exclusion from Gambling in Ireland

Self-exclusion is one of the most effective tools available to anyone who wants to take control of their gambling. This guide explains how self-exclusion works in Ireland today, what the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 requires of operators, what Ireland's national register will look like when it launches, and where to find support right now.

What Is Self-Exclusion?

Self-exclusion is a formal request you make — either directly to an operator, or through a national register — asking to be blocked from accessing gambling services for a set period. During that period, the operator is required to close your account, return any remaining funds, and refuse any new registrations from you.

It is not a punishment or a sign of weakness. Self-exclusion is a recognised, evidence-based harm reduction tool, and all GRAI-licensed operators are legally required under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 to offer it. You can self-exclude for a minimum period (commonly 6 months) or permanently. Most operators will not allow you to reduce or end the exclusion before the chosen period is up — that delay is deliberate, giving you time to reflect.

Self-Exclusion Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced the most significant overhaul of Irish gambling law in decades. Among its core player-protection provisions, all GRAI-licensed operators must:

  • Provide a self-exclusion mechanism that is easy to find and use
  • Act on a self-exclusion request without delay — typically within 24 hours
  • Close the customer's account and return any balance held
  • Not send marketing communications to a self-excluded player
  • Refuse any new account opened by a self-excluded individual (identity checks are required)

Operators who fail to honour a self-exclusion request are in breach of their GRAI licence and can face enforcement action, including fines and licence suspension.

Ireland's National Self-Exclusion Register

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 mandates that the GRAI build and operate a national self-exclusion register. Once live, this single registration will exclude you from every GRAI-licensed operator simultaneously — you will not need to contact each site individually.

As of November 2026, the national register is not yet operational. The GRAI has not published a confirmed launch date. Until it goes live, the most reliable approach is to self-exclude from each operator you use directly (see below).

💡 GamStop: Not Available in Ireland

GamStop is the UK's national self-exclusion scheme and works only with UKGC-licensed operators. It does not cover Irish or international licences. If you are in the Republic of Ireland, GamStop does not apply to you. Ireland is building its own equivalent under the GRAI — until it launches, use the per-operator route below.

How to Self-Exclude from Individual Sites Right Now

Until the national register is live, here is how to self-exclude from each operator you use:

  1. Log in to your account and look for a Responsible Gambling or Account Settings section. Many operators allow self-exclusion with just a few clicks from there.
  2. If you cannot find it, contact customer support via live chat or email and state clearly: "I want to self-exclude my account." You do not need to give a reason.
  3. Choose your exclusion period — minimum is typically 6 months; most operators offer 1, 2, or 5 years, or permanent exclusion.
  4. Confirm your identity if asked. Keep a copy of the confirmation email or reference number.
  5. Repeat this process for every operator you have an account with or might be tempted to use.

Getting Support in Ireland

Self-exclusion works best alongside wider support. The following organisations can help — all services are free and confidential:

  • Gambling Awareness Trust — helpline 1800 753 753, available 24/7. Offers counselling and referrals.
  • Gamblers Anonymous Ireland — gamblersanonymous.ie. Peer-support meetings in most counties.
  • gamblingcare.ie (HSE) — gamblingcare.ie. Specialist gambling counsellors via the HSE.
  • Citizens Information — citizensinformation.ie. Guides to local support services and financial advice.

If gambling is affecting a family member, Gamblers Anonymous also runs Gam-Anon groups for families and friends.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is self-exclusion from gambling?▾
Self-exclusion is a formal agreement between you and a gambling operator (or a national register) that prevents you from accessing gambling services for a chosen period. It is a practical tool for anyone who wants to take a break or stop gambling altogether.
Is GamStop available in Ireland?▾
No. GamStop is a UK-only national self-exclusion scheme. It covers UKGC-licensed operators. It does not apply to gambling sites licensed in Ireland or internationally. Irish players cannot register with GamStop; Ireland is building its own national register under the GRAI.
When will Ireland's national self-exclusion register be ready?▾
The GRAI is developing a national self-exclusion register under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. As of November 2026, it is not yet live. Once operational, registering will exclude you from all GRAI-licensed operators automatically. Check grai.ie for the latest updates.
How do I self-exclude from an online casino right now?▾
Contact each operator's customer support and request self-exclusion. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, all GRAI-licensed operators must provide self-exclusion tools. You can typically self-exclude via account settings, live chat, or by emailing support. Keep a copy of any confirmation.
Who can I contact for gambling support in Ireland?▾
The Gambling Awareness Trust helpline is 1800 753 753 (free, confidential). Gamblers Anonymous Ireland runs peer-support meetings nationwide — visit gamblersanonymous.ie. The HSE's Problem Gambling service offers counselling referrals. Citizens Information (citizensinformation.ie) can guide you to local services.

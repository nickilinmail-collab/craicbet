GRAI and the Future of Casino Licensing in Ireland

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) represents the most significant change to Irish gambling law in decades. Established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2022, GRAI is tasked with creating a comprehensive regulatory framework for all forms of gambling in Ireland.

As of 2026, GRAI has begun issuing remote betting licences to qualifying operators. However, online casino and gaming licences are not yet available — these are expected to be introduced in 2027 or 2028, once GRAI completes its phased regulatory rollout. This means that currently, all online casinos accepting Irish players operate under international licences from jurisdictions like Malta (MGA), the United Kingdom (UKGC), Gibraltar, or Curacao.

What does this mean for Irish players right now? It means you should look for casinos with strong international licences — particularly MGA and UKGC — which provide robust player protections including segregated player funds, dispute resolution mechanisms, and mandatory responsible gambling tools. These standards are likely to form the baseline for future GRAI casino licensing requirements.

When GRAI licensed casinos for online gaming do become available, we expect several changes: mandatory spending limits tailored to Irish players, enhanced identity verification, integration with Ireland's self-exclusion register, and stricter advertising standards.

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and Betting

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 rebuilt Irish gambling law from scratch, but its first real-world test is betting: remote betting licences were the first the new regulator issued, and bookmakers are the first operators living under the full regime. This guide covers the Act strictly from the betting side — who needs a licence, what the regulator can do, and how the rollout has actually gone. For the whole statute, see the full Act explainer.

The Licence Categories

The Act licenses gambling by activity. For betting that means three flavours: remote betting (online and telephone), in-person betting (shops and tracks), and remote betting intermediary — platforms that facilitate bets between parties rather than taking the other side themselves. Gaming (casino), lottery and business-to-business licences are separate categories on their own, later timetable.

Who Must Hold One

Anyone offering betting to people in Ireland needs the relevant licence — and the duty follows the customer, not the server. An operator with no Irish office offering odds to Irish residents is in scope; operating without a licence is a criminal offence. That is the legal basis for the GRAI's reported moves against offshore platforms, several of which have geo-blocked Irish users rather than apply.

What Licensees Sign Up To

Age verification, paying winnings, keeping customer funds separate from company money, closing accounts on request, no credit cards and no credit — plus the advertising watershed and the ban on inducements targeted at specific people. Enforcement Powers

The GRAI can investigate, suspend and revoke licences, and impose administrative sanctions up to €20 million or 10% of turnover, whichever is greater. Some breaches are criminal: the credit ban, for example, carries up to 12 months on summary conviction or up to five years on indictment. A three-year licence term (S.I. 38/2026) means every operator also faces a full re-examination at renewal.

The Rollout So Far

Signed into law in October 2024. GRAI established 5 March 2025. Remote betting applications opened 9 February 2026; the first licences took effect 1 July 2026, with the public register going live at grai.ie the same day. In-person betting licences can be granted from 1 December 2026. Gaming, lottery and B2B applications open through 2027 and 2028 — which is why no casino holds a GRAI licence today, as our casino timeline tracks.

What It Means in Practice

For the first time, "licensed Irish bookmaker" is a checkable claim with teeth behind it: a register you can search in two minutes (here is how), statutory conduct rules, and a regulator with fining power. The Act does not change the odds — it changes what happens when something goes wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 take effect for betting?

In phases: applications opened 9 February 2026, the first remote betting licences took effect 1 July 2026, and in-person betting licences can be granted from 1 December 2026.

Does an offshore bookmaker need a GRAI licence to serve Irish customers?

Yes — the duty follows the customer. Offering betting to people in Ireland requires a GRAI licence, and operating without one is a criminal offence.

What penalties can the GRAI impose on bookmakers?

Administrative sanctions up to €20 million or 10% of turnover (whichever is greater), suspension or revocation — and some breaches, like the credit ban, are criminal offences.

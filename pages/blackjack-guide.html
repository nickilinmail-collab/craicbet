Rules of Blackjack

Blackjack, also known as 21, is a card game played between you and the dealer. The objective is simple: get a hand value as close to 21 as possible without exceeding it, while beating the dealer's hand. If your hand goes over 21, you "bust" and lose immediately — regardless of what the dealer holds.

A standard game uses between one and eight decks of 52 cards. Each round begins with the player placing a bet, after which two cards are dealt to both the player and the dealer. One of the dealer's cards is face up, the other face down (the "hole card").

Card Values

A hand containing an Ace counted as 11 is called a "soft" hand (e.g., Ace + 6 = soft 17). When the Ace must count as 1 to avoid busting, the hand is "hard."

Gameplay Flow

  1. Place your bet — Choose your stake within the table limits.
  2. Cards are dealt — You receive two cards face up. The dealer gets one face up, one face down.
  3. Check for blackjack — If your first two cards total 21 (Ace + 10-value card), you have a natural blackjack and typically win 3:2.
  4. Player decisions — Choose from: Hit, Stand, Double Down, Split, or Surrender.
  5. Dealer plays — The dealer reveals their hole card and must hit on 16 or below and stand on 17 or above (rules vary by variant).
  6. Results — Closest to 21 wins. A tie (push) returns your bet.
Player Actions Explained:
  • Hit — Take another card.
  • Stand — Keep your current hand.
  • Double Down — Double your bet, receive exactly one more card.
  • Split — If you have two cards of the same value, split them into two separate hands (requires an additional bet).
  • Surrender — Forfeit half your bet and end the hand (not available in all variants).
  • Insurance — A side bet offered when the dealer shows an Ace. Pays 2:1 if the dealer has blackjack. Generally not recommended.

Tips for Beginners

  1. Learn basic strategy first — Memorise the chart above or keep it open while you play. It is the single most impactful thing you can do to improve your results.
  2. Start with low-stakes tables — Many online casinos offer blackjack from as little as €0.50 per hand. Build confidence before raising your stakes.
  3. Avoid insurance bets — Insurance carries a house edge of roughly 7.5%, making it one of the worst bets at the blackjack table.
  4. Never split 10s — A hand of 20 is strong. Splitting it is a common beginner mistake that hurts your expected value.
  5. Always split Aces and 8s — Two Aces give you two chances at 21. Two 8s total 16, the worst hand in blackjack — splitting gives you a better chance.
  6. Play European Blackjack for lower stakes practice — It uses fewer decks and has straightforward rules.
  7. Use free play modes — Most RNG blackjack games let you play for free. Take advantage of this to practise without risk.

Bankroll Management

Managing your bankroll is essential for enjoying blackjack long term. Here are some guidelines:

  • Set a session budget — Decide how much you can afford to lose before you sit down, and stick to it.
  • Use the 5% rule — Never bet more than 5% of your session bankroll on a single hand. If your budget is €100, keep bets at €5 or below.
  • Avoid chasing losses — Doubling your bet after a loss (Martingale) does not change the house edge and can drain your bankroll rapidly.
  • Set a win limit — If you double your starting bankroll, consider walking away. Locking in profits is a winning habit.
  • Track your sessions — Keep a simple log of wins and losses to understand your play patterns over time.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Playing without strategy — Relying on gut feeling instead of basic strategy adds 2–3% to the house edge.
  • Taking insurance — Statistically a losing bet in the long run.
  • Standing on soft 17 — You cannot bust with a soft hand. Always hit or double on soft 17.
  • Splitting 10s — 20 is the second-best hand possible. Do not break it up.
  • Ignoring table rules — Always check whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17, how many decks are in play, and whether surrender is available. These affect your strategy.
  • Playing at tables with 6:5 blackjack payouts — Always look for 3:2 payout tables. A 6:5 payout increases the house edge by roughly 1.4%.

House Edge Explained

The house edge is the mathematical advantage the casino has over the player, expressed as a percentage of each bet. In blackjack, the house edge depends on the rules and how well you play:

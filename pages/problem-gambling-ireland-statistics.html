Problem Gambling in Ireland — Statistics, Signs and Support

📅 Updated 5 November 2026 📝 CraicBet Editorial 🔍 Sources: GRAI Annual Report 2026, ESRI research, Gambling Regulation Act 2024
📞 Need help now? Call the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753 (free, confidential, 24/7). You can also visit gamblingcare.ie or problemgambling.ie. If you are in immediate crisis, contact your GP or the HSE Crisis Line. 18+ only. Gambling can be addictive.

Problem gambling is more widespread in Ireland than official figures have historically suggested. This page brings together the best available research, explains the signs to watch for, and lists every support service available to people in Ireland — whether you are concerned about yourself or someone you care about.

1. How Common Is Problem Gambling in Ireland?

~130,000
adults in Ireland experience problem gambling
Source: ESRI/GRAI research, cited in the GRAI Annual Report 2026

Research cited in the GRAI Annual Report 2026 — drawing on joint ESRI/GRAI research — estimates that approximately 130,000 adults in Ireland experience problem gambling. This figure represents a significant upward revision from earlier estimates, which were based on older survey methodologies that undercounted harms in the population.

The same ESRI research found that harmful gambling in Ireland is "about ten times higher than previously thought" — a finding that informed the Government's decision to frame the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 explicitly as a public health measure, as noted in analysis by McCann FitzGerald partner Alan Heuston in SBC News.

ESRI research commissioned by the GRAI also found that free bets and money-back guarantees increased gambling spending by over 10% in experimental conditions — one reason these inducements are now subject to restrictions under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

📌 Why the previous estimates were too low: Earlier Irish prevalence surveys often relied on self-reported problem gambling, which typically undercounts harm. The ESRI/GRAI research used a broader measure of harmful gambling — including people who experience harms without meeting clinical "problem gambler" criteria — which revealed the much larger true scale.

2. Signs of Problem Gambling

Problem gambling does not always look like extreme behaviour. Many people experiencing gambling harm continue to function at work and at home while the problem escalates. The following signs are drawn from recognised clinical frameworks (Gambling Awareness Trust, HSE, GamAnon):

  • Chasing losses — betting more to try to recover money lost in previous sessions
  • Hiding gambling activity — deleting betting app notifications, lying about time or money spent
  • Borrowing money or selling possessions to fund betting
  • Gambling to relieve stress, boredom, anxiety or low mood — using it as an emotional regulation tool
  • Missing work, family events or social commitments due to gambling or recovery from losses
  • Continuing despite wanting to stop — repeated unsuccessful attempts to cut back or quit
  • Preoccupation with gambling — constantly thinking about past bets or planning the next session
  • Financial consequences — unpaid bills, credit card debt, missing rent or mortgage payments
  • Relationship strain — arguments with partners, family withdrawal, isolation
  • Tolerance creep — needing to bet increasingly larger amounts to get the same excitement
⚠️ You do not need to tick every box. Experiencing two or three of these signs consistently is a reason to seek support. Problem gambling exists on a spectrum — you do not have to have lost everything to deserve help.

3. What Tools Do GRAI-Licensed Bookmakers Have to Offer?

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, all GRAI-licensed operators are legally required to offer a suite of responsible gambling tools. These are not optional features — they are mandatory licence conditions. They must be applied immediately when requested and must not require the player to contact customer support to enforce them.

Tool What it does Legal basis
Deposit limit Caps the amount you can deposit in a day, week or month. Must apply immediately when set; can be reduced at any time but increases require a waiting period. Gambling Regulation Act 2024, licence conditions
Session time limit Ends your session after a set time and prevents automatic re-logging. Reality check notifications at intervals. Gambling Regulation Act 2024, licence conditions
Cooling-off period Temporarily suspends your account for a set period (e.g., 24 hours, 7 days, 30 days). Cannot be cancelled once activated. Gambling Regulation Act 2024, licence conditions
Self-exclusion (operator level) Permanently or indefinitely blocks your account with a specific operator. Accounts cannot be reopened during the exclusion period. Gambling Regulation Act 2024, licence conditions
National Gambling Exclusion Register GRAI-operated register built on the Deloitte/Salesforce regulatory platform. A single self-exclusion applies across all GRAI-licensed operators simultaneously. Gambling Regulation Act 2024, Part 5

If a GRAI-licensed operator fails to offer these tools, makes them difficult to access, or does not apply a limit or exclusion immediately, this is a breach of their licence. See Section 6 on what to do.

5. What the New GRAI Rules Mean for Safer Gambling

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 represented the most significant reform of Irish gambling law since the foundation of the State. Among its harm-reduction provisions:

  • Credit card ban (1 July 2026): Deposits using credit cards are banned across all GRAI-licensed operators. This is a direct harm-reduction measure — research consistently links credit card gambling to higher rates of problem gambling because it removes the connection between gambling and real available money.
  • Mandatory responsible gambling tools: Deposit limits, session limits, cooling-off periods, and self-exclusion are no longer optional features — they are mandatory licence conditions. Operators must implement them immediately on request.
  • Restricted inducements: Free bets, bonus offers, and money-back guarantees are subject to restrictions. ESRI research commissioned by the GRAI found these promotions increased gambling spending by over 10% in experimental conditions.
  • Age verification mandatory: GRAI-licensed operators must verify that all players are 18 or over before allowing any gambling activity.
  • Player complaint rights: Under Parts 7 and 8 of the Act, players can file formal complaints with the GRAI against operators who fail to enforce responsible gambling obligations.
  • National Gambling Exclusion Register: Operated by the GRAI using a Deloitte/Salesforce system, the register allows a single self-exclusion to apply across every GRAI-licensed operator.

6. If a Bookmaker Ignores Your Self-Exclusion or Limit Request

Failure by a licensed operator to apply a self-exclusion, deposit limit, or cooling-off period immediately is a breach of their GRAI licence obligations under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

If this happens to you:

  1. Gather evidence — take screenshots of your request confirmation, your account activity after the exclusion should have applied, and any communications with the operator.
  2. Contact the operator first — write to their compliance or customer service team, specifically citing the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and their licence obligations. Keep a record of all responses.
  3. File a complaint with the GRAI under Part 7 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Submit via grai.ie. The GRAI has statutory enforcement powers including fines of up to €20 million or 10% of the operator's annual turnover, whichever is greater.
  4. Contact the Gambling Awareness Trust (1800 753 753) if you need emotional support during this process — they can also signpost you to legal advice if needed.
📌 Important: If a betting site is operating without a GRAI licence entirely, the GRAI complaint process still applies — operating without a licence is itself an offence under the Act. However, if you have deposited money with an unlicensed offshore operator, recovering funds may be significantly more difficult. Only use GRAI-licensed bookmakers.

7. Frequently Asked Questions

How many people in Ireland have a gambling problem?
Research cited in the GRAI's 2026 Annual Report suggests approximately 130,000 adults in Ireland experience problem gambling. ESRI research also found that harmful gambling in Ireland is about ten times higher than previously measured — a finding that influenced the framing of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 as a public health measure.
How do I self-exclude from all betting sites in Ireland?
Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, the GRAI operates a National Gambling Exclusion Register. Registering allows you to self-exclude across all GRAI-licensed operators simultaneously. Contact the GRAI at grai.ie or call the Gambling Awareness Trust on 1800 753 753 for step-by-step guidance.
What can I do if a betting site ignores my self-exclusion?
This is a breach of the licensee's obligations under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. You can file a formal complaint with the GRAI under Part 7 of the Act. Gather evidence (screenshots, account history) and submit via grai.ie. The GRAI has enforcement powers including fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover.

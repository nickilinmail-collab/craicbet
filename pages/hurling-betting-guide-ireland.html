Hurling Betting Guide — Markets, Tips and How to Bet on the All-Ireland

Hurling is unique to Ireland — the fastest field sport in the world and, for many punters, the most compelling betting event of the summer. From the provincial championships in May to the All-Ireland Final at Croke Park in September, there is extensive coverage from every GRAI-licensed bookmaker operating in Ireland. This guide covers everything you need to know to bet on hurling confidently.

Brief Introduction to Hurling

Hurling is a Gaelic sport governed by the GAA (Gaelic Athletic Association), played between teams of 15 on a large grass pitch. Players use a wooden stick (hurley) to strike a small leather ball (sliotar). A goal (ball into the net) is worth 3 points; a point (ball over the bar) is worth 1 point. Final scores are expressed as goals and points — for example, 1-18 to 0-14 means the winning team scored 1 goal and 18 points (21 total) versus 14 points.

The high scoring nature — totals of 30–40+ combined points are common — makes hurling particularly suited to over/under and handicap betting markets.

All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship Structure

The main competition runs from spring to September:

  • Provincial Championships (Leinster and Munster) — late spring/early summer
  • All-Ireland preliminary and quarter-finals — summer
  • All-Ireland semi-finals — July/August at Croke Park
  • All-Ireland Final — traditionally in late July or September at Croke Park, Dublin

The Leinster and Munster Championship winners progress, with other counties competing through a qualifiers route. The format has evolved over the years — always check the current season's draw structure with the GAA website or your bookmaker's competition pages.

How Hurling Odds Work

Hurling odds follow the same fractional and decimal format as any other sport — see our full odds guide for how to read and calculate them. The key difference from football is the high-scoring nature of hurling. Because scores can reach 20–25 points per side, over/under totals and handicap lines are typically set much higher than football. A line of 42.5 combined points in a Munster senior hurling clash, for example, reflects that typical games produce 40+ combined points.

Counties with the Strongest All-Ireland Hurling Records

The following counties have historically dominated the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship based on the official record of titles won:

  • Kilkenny — the most decorated county in the Championship's history
  • Tipperary — multiple All-Ireland titles across different eras
  • Limerick — dominant force in recent seasons, winning multiple All-Irelands in the 2018–2023 period
  • Cork — consistent All-Ireland contenders with a long winning record
  • Galway, Waterford, Wexford — regular contenders and occasional champions

Check current All-Ireland outright odds with GRAI-licensed bookmakers for the up-to-date market view on who is favoured for the upcoming season.

Tips for Betting on Hurling

These are general approaches — not specific bets. No betting outcome is guaranteed.

  • Home advantage matters less than you might expect — Croke Park is a neutral venue for most knockout games, but provincial semi-finals and local grounds can swing things. Check where the game is being played.
  • Weather impacts scoring — hurling in wet, windy conditions typically produces lower scores. If rain or strong wind is forecast, consider the under in total points markets.
  • Championship form vs National League form can diverge — counties sometimes treat league games as development opportunities. Championship form from the same season is a better indicator.
  • Manager tenure and squad continuity — a settled management team and core squad (factually verifiable from GAA sources) tends to correlate with consistent Championship performance.

In-Play Hurling Betting

All major GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer in-play (live) markets on senior All-Ireland hurling games. Because hurling is fast and goals can swing a game quickly, in-play prices move rapidly.

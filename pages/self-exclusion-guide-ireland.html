What Is Self-Exclusion?

Self-exclusion is a formal request to a gambling operator (or multiple operators via a central register) to close your account and block access for a set period. During the exclusion, you cannot deposit, wager or access your gambling account. The operator is required by law to enforce this.

Self-exclusion is not a "break" or a "cooling off" — those are separate tools. Self-exclusion is specifically designed as a protective measure for people who recognise they need a barrier between themselves and gambling activity. It is taken seriously by regulators; operators who fail to enforce self-exclusion face significant penalties from GRAI.

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER)

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) is the most powerful self-exclusion tool available to Irish players. Established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and managed by GRAI, it allows a single registration to exclude you from all GRAI-licensed gambling operators simultaneously.

Without NGER, self-exclusion from one site does not carry over to another. A player who self-excludes from Site A remains fully accessible to Sites B, C and D. NGER closes this gap by broadcasting your exclusion to all participating operators.

To register with NGER:

  1. Visit the GRAI website (grai.ie) and navigate to the NGER section.
  2. Create an account and verify your identity (required to prevent fraudulent exclusions and to ensure enforcement).
  3. Select your preferred exclusion duration and confirm your registration.
  4. GRAI notifies all participating licensed operators. Your accounts with those operators should be suspended promptly — typically within 24–72 hours.

Self-Excluding from Individual Sites

For immediate self-exclusion from a site you are currently using (while NGER covers all operators):

  1. Log in to your account.
  2. Go to Responsible Gambling / Safer Gambling in your account settings.
  3. Select Self-Exclusion or Close My Account.
  4. Choose your exclusion duration (minimum 6 months at GRAI-licensed sites).
  5. Confirm. The exclusion takes effect immediately or within a very short window.

Alternatively, contact the site's customer support directly — by live chat or email — and explicitly request self-exclusion. Put your request in writing (email is best) and keep a copy. If the site does not action your request promptly, file a complaint with GRAI.

Supplementing Self-Exclusion with Blocking Software

Self-exclusion from licensed operators does not cover unlicensed sites. For comprehensive device-level blocking, consider installing dedicated gambling blocking software:

  • Gamban (gamban.com) — blocks gambling across all devices (phone, tablet, desktop) using deep packet inspection. Very difficult to bypass. Available from €2.99/month or as a one-time annual fee.
  • GamStop (gamstop.co.uk) — multi-operator exclusion scheme covering UK-licensed sites, useful for sites that hold UKGC licences.
  • NetNanny / Circle — parental control software that can be configured to block gambling category sites, also useful for home Wi-Fi filtering.

What to Do After Self-Excluding

Self-exclusion is a protective tool, not a treatment. Many professionals recommend using the exclusion period to also access support:

  • Contact the Gambling Awareness Trust at 1800 753 753 for counselling referrals.
  • Consider attending Gamblers Anonymous meetings — peer support during the exclusion period significantly improves outcomes.
  • If debt is a problem, contact MABS (mabs.ie) for free, confidential money advice.
  • Inform a trusted person that you have self-excluded. Accountability support helps.

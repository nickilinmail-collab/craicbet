What Does NRNB Mean in Betting

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

NRNB stands for Non-Runner No Bet. If the horse you backed is withdrawn from the race before the off, your stake is returned in full. This is different from standard each-way betting, where a non-runner triggers a Rule 4 deduction on remaining runners.

How NRNB Works

Normally, when a horse is withdrawn, the bookmaker applies a Rule 4 deduction — a percentage reduction on winnings from other runners to reflect the reduced field. With NRNB, your stake is simply refunded if your horse does not run. No deduction, no complication.

NRNB is most commonly offered as a promotion on big ante-post markets (Cheltenham, Grand National, Irish National, Punchestown). It is designed to give punters confidence to bet early without the risk of their horse being scratched.

When to Use NRNB Promotions

NRNB is most valuable on ante-post markets for major festivals where non-runners are common. Horses entered for Cheltenham in October may not run in March due to injury, ground conditions, or trainer decisions. NRNB removes that risk.

Always check whether the NRNB applies to the win part only or both win and place parts of an each-way bet. Terms vary between bookmakers.

NRNB vs NR: what is the difference

NR (Non-Runner) with standard ante-post terms means your stake is lost if the selection does not run in the race. This is the default for most ante-post markets. NRNB (Non-Runner No Bet) means if your selection does not run, your stake is returned in full as cash — the bet is treated as void.

Which Irish bookmakers offer NRNB on ante-post bets

Most major GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer NRNB on selected races, particularly for the Cheltenham Festival, Grand National, Irish Grand National, and Punchestown Festival. NRNB prices are typically available on feature races (Champion Hurdle, Gold Cup, Champion Chase) well ahead of the festival. For less prominent races, standard ante-post terms (stake lost if non-runner) usually apply.

Always check the specific market terms before placing an ante-post bet. NRNB markets are usually labelled explicitly in the race listing or in the bet receipt. If in doubt, the operator's customer support can confirm whether a specific market is NRNB or standard ante-post.

Is NRNB the same as non-runner free bet?

Not exactly. NRNB returns your original stake. Some bookmakers instead offer a free bet of equal value. Check which applies — a cash refund is preferable to a free bet with wagering conditions.

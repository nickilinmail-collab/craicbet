Football Betting in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Football is the highest-volume betting sport in Ireland by online turnover. The Premier League dominates, with fixtures running from August to May and markets open year-round on outrights, next manager and player transfers. The Champions League adds a European dimension every Tuesday and Wednesday evening. This guide covers the competitions Irish punters follow most closely, the markets that matter, and where to bet with an Irish GRAI licence.

Competitions Irish Punters Follow

The Premier League is the foundation of football betting in Ireland. Weekly fixtures from August through May, 10 Saturday 3pm kick-offs, and the deepest market liquidity of any football competition mean you can get a bet on at competitive odds at any volume. English football's presence on Irish television and in Irish social media makes it the default for accumulators, bet builders and in-play betting.

The Champions League provides the marquee occasions — group stage midweek football through autumn, knockout rounds from February, and a final in May. Outright markets open in the summer and attract significant Irish interest, particularly when English clubs are in contention.

The League of Ireland (Premier Division and First Division) runs from March to November and is the domestic football competition. Markets are thinner than the Premier League, but local knowledge matters more in a less-efficiently-priced market. The FAI Cup adds knockout football into the mix.

The Republic of Ireland national team has its own dedicated following — qualifying campaigns and friendlies draw significant betting interest, and outright markets for major tournaments are available well in advance.

Main Football Betting Markets Explained

1X2 (Match Result): The basic three-way market — home win, draw, or away win. The most liquid and competitive market for each fixture. Margins on Premier League 1X2 markets typically run 4–6% at mainstream bookmakers.

Both Teams to Score (BTTS): Does each team score at least one goal? Independent of the result — a 2-1 either way is a yes. One of the most popular markets in Ireland for accumulators because it has a yes/no simplicity that makes combination bets easy to construct.

Over/Under Goals: The most common line is 2.5 goals — over means 3 or more goals in the match, under means 2 or fewer. Other lines (1.5, 3.5, 4.5) are available on most fixtures. For a fuller explanation, see our over/under guide.

Asian Handicap: Eliminates the draw by giving one team a head start. A -0.5 handicap on the favourite means they must win for the bet to land; +0.5 on the underdog means the underdog wins or draws. It can offer a better-value alternative to backing a favourite at short odds in a standard 1X2 market. Full explanation in our Asian handicap guide.

Correct Score: Predict the exact final score. Higher odds compensate for the difficulty. Most popular in lower-scoring games (Premier League tends towards tighter results than La Liga or Bundesliga). See our correct score guide.

In-Play Football Betting

In-play (live) betting during football matches has grown substantially and now accounts for a significant share of total football turnover. Odds move second by second based on events in the game — a goal, a red card or a missed penalty creates immediate price movement. The key skills in in-play football are reading the game flow and understanding how bookmakers price around goal timing. See our full in-play betting guide.

Accumulator Betting in Football

Football accumulators — combining multiple match results into one bet for a multiplied return — are the most popular bet type in Ireland across all bookmakers. The maths of accumulators work against the bettor: each selection carries the bookmaker's margin, and those margins compound with every leg added. A five-fold accumulator effectively multiplies the bookmaker's edge five times. That said, accumulators remain a legal, popular and affordable way to bet small amounts for a large potential return. Frequently Asked Questions

What football leagues can I bet on in Ireland?

GRAI-licensed bookmakers cover all major European leagues plus the Champions League, Europa League, League of Ireland and Republic of Ireland internationals.

What is BTTS (Both Teams to Score) betting?

A market where you bet on whether both teams will score at least once during the 90 minutes. Independent of the match winner. One of the most popular football betting markets in Ireland.

What is Asian handicap betting in football?

Asian handicap eliminates the draw by giving one team a head start. A -0.5 handicap on the favourite means they must win for the bet to land. It simplifies football to a two-way market and can offer better value than the standard 1X2.

Is League of Ireland betting popular?

League of Ireland betting has grown steadily. Markets are thinner than the Premier League, which can mean lower maximum stakes, but local knowledge can be a genuine edge in a less-efficiently-priced market.

Can I bet on the Republic of Ireland national team?

Yes. GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer markets on Republic of Ireland international fixtures including qualifying games, friendlies, and outright markets for major tournaments.

What If I Win Big Betting in Ireland?

Last updated: November 2026 — 18+ | Gambling Awareness Trust | 1800 753 753 | Not tax advice — consult a qualified Irish tax adviser for your specific situation.

The short answer: Betting winnings are completely tax-free in Ireland. There is no income tax, capital gains tax, or any other personal tax on sports betting winnings, regardless of the amount.

That said, winning a large sum triggers some real-world processes you should know about — KYC checks, bank queries, and bookmaker payout rules — none of which reduce your winnings, but all of which can slow things down if you're not prepared.

The tax position: winnings are yours in full

Ireland taxes the bookmaker, not the player. GRAI-licensed betting sites pay a 2% Betting Duty on turnover. As the customer, you receive your winnings in full with no deduction. This applies to:

  • All sports — horse racing, football, GAA, rugby, golf
  • All bet types — singles, accumulators, ante-post, in-play
  • All amounts — whether you win €100 or €100,000
  • Winnings from any licensed bookmaker, including offshore operators

You do not need to declare betting winnings on your tax return. There is no Revenue form to fill in.

What actually happens when you win a large amount

1. KYC verification (AML checks)

Under Irish and EU anti-money laundering law, bookmakers must verify large payouts. If you win significantly — there is no fixed legal threshold, but winnings over roughly €2,000–€10,000 in a single transaction commonly trigger checks — the bookmaker may ask for:

  • Photo ID (passport or Irish driving licence)
  • Proof of address (utility bill or bank statement, within 3 months)
  • Source-of-funds documentation for your original deposits (payslips, bank statements)

This is not an attempt to avoid paying you. It is a legal obligation. Once you provide the documents, the bookmaker must pay. If they still refuse after satisfactory verification, that is grounds for a GRAI complaint.

2. Withdrawal processing time

Large withdrawals often take longer than standard ones. Expect 1–5 business days for bank transfer. Card withdrawals are typically faster but some bookmakers only allow withdrawals up to a daily or weekly limit, requiring multiple transactions to clear the full amount.

3. Your bank may flag the deposit

When a large sum arrives in your bank account from a bookmaker, your bank's AML systems may flag it. This can result in:

  • A temporary hold while the bank verifies the source
  • A request to explain the funds
  • In rare cases, a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) to the Financial Intelligence Unit

Keep your bookmaker account statement showing the bet, the odds, and the payout — this is the documentation your bank will ask for.

Payout limits: know them before you bet

All bookmakers set maximum payout limits per event, per day, or per market. These are stated in their terms and conditions. If your winning bet exceeds the limit, the bookmaker pays up to their limit and no more — even if your calculated winnings are higher. For large accumulators, check the maximum payout before you place the bet.

Can a bookmaker void a winning bet?

Yes, in limited circumstances. A GRAI-licensed bookmaker can void a bet if:

  • The odds were offered in error (palpable error rule)
  • The bet breached bonus terms
  • The event was abandoned or declared void under their rules
  • Suspicious betting patterns were detected (match-fixing suspicion)

Arbitrary voiding of winning bets without a stated reason is not permitted for licensed operators. If this happens, escalate to GRAI.

Social welfare and mortgage implications

Betting winnings are not income for Revenue purposes, but the capital they add to your savings can affect means-tested social welfare payments. For mortgage applications, lenders look at bank statements and may view regular large gambling activity as a risk factor for income stability — even if you're winning overall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I pay tax on betting winnings in Ireland?
No. Betting winnings are completely tax-free for players in Ireland. The bookmaker pays Betting Duty — you receive your winnings in full with no deduction.
What happens if I win €10,000 on a betting site?
You keep all €10,000 tax-free. The bookmaker will likely conduct AML checks before paying — expect to provide ID and possibly source-of-funds documents. This is legal and standard; once verified, they must pay.
Can a betting site refuse to pay large winnings?
A GRAI-licensed bookmaker cannot refuse valid winnings. They can delay while completing AML checks. If they refuse after verification is complete, file a complaint with GRAI.
Does Revenue know about my betting winnings?
Revenue does not receive automatic reports of individual betting wins. Winnings are tax-free, so there is nothing to declare. Large deposits can trigger bank AML queries, but that is a banking matter, not a tax one.
Do I need to declare betting winnings when applying for a mortgage?
Not to Revenue. But lenders see your bank statements and may treat regular gambling activity as a risk factor, even if you are profitable. This is a lender's commercial decision, not a tax or legal requirement.
My bank froze my account after a large betting withdrawal. What do I do?
Contact your bank directly with your bookmaker account statement showing the bet and payout. Banks are required to unfreeze accounts once AML checks are satisfied. Winnings are legitimate and the freeze is a standard AML review, not a judgement on gambling.

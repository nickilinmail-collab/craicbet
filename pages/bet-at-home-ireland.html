What Is bet-at-home?

bet-at-home.com AG is a publicly listed German-Austrian company that operates an online sports betting and gaming platform. Founded in 1999, it was for a period one of the better-known European online bookmakers, particularly in German-speaking markets. The company is listed on the Frankfurt Stock Exchange and has historically held licences in markets including Germany and Malta.

In recent years, however, bet-at-home has been going through a significant restructuring of its market presence. The company has exited or reduced its operations in several markets due to the increasingly complex regulatory landscape across Europe, including the introduction of stricter requirements in Germany and other jurisdictions. This shrinking footprint means that bet-at-home is a less active presence in many markets than it was at its peak.

Ireland was never a primary target market for bet-at-home. The brand does not appear to have made any significant push into the Irish market, and the emergence of Ireland’s own GRAI regulatory framework in 2026 has added a further formal requirement for any operator wishing to serve Irish customers legally.

bet-at-home’s GRAI Licence Status

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, bet-at-home does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means bet-at-home has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation. Given the company’s trajectory of market exits, this is unlikely to change imminently, but the GRAI register is the definitive source.

bet-at-home in Ireland: What It Means for Players

Given bet-at-home’s market reduction trajectory, Irish players who encounter the brand should be aware of two separate concerns: first, the absence of a GRAI licence; and second, the broader question of the company’s operational stability in markets it has not historically prioritised.

When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds or has held — those Irish protections do not apply. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

Beyond these regulatory gaps, the fact that bet-at-home has been exiting markets introduces an additional element of uncertainty about continuity of service and long-term account security. CraicBet recommends Irish players stick to established, GRAI-licensed operators that are actively investing in the Irish market.

What Is a Free Bet?

A free bet is a promotional token that lets you place a bet without putting your own money at risk. The bookmaker stakes the bet on your behalf. If your selection wins, you keep the profit — but not the stake itself. If it loses, you lose nothing from your own funds (the token is simply used up).

That last point is the one most punters miss: free bets are almost always stake-not-returned (SNR). This is not a catch buried in the small print — it is the standard structure for virtually every free bet offer in Ireland and the UK.

📐 The SNR Maths, Simply Put

Say you have a €10 free bet and place it on a selection at odds of 3.0:

  • If it wins: Return = €10 × 3.0 = €30. Your payout = €30 − €10 stake = €20 profit. The €10 stake is not returned.
  • If it loses: You lose nothing from your own account. The free bet token is consumed.

Compare that to a real-money €10 bet at 3.0 that wins: you get back €30 (€20 profit + €10 stake). The free bet is worth €10 less in absolute terms — but it costs you nothing to place.

How Free Bets Actually Work in Ireland

Knowing the SNR structure is the first step. The other conditions you will almost always encounter are set out below — read these before you claim any offer.

Wagering Requirements

Some free bets come with wagering requirements: you must turn over the winnings a set number of times before you can withdraw. A "1x wagering" requirement on €20 winnings means you need to bet €20 more before cashing out. Requirements above 3x on a free bet offer are worth scrutinising carefully. Many straight free bets (as opposed to casino-style bonus funds) carry no wagering requirement on the winnings — the winnings are yours once the bet settles.

Minimum Odds

Nearly all free bet offers require your selection to be priced at a minimum of odds 1.5 (or evens/2.0 in some cases). Placing a free bet on a heavy favourite at 1.1 will usually violate the terms and the bet may be voided or the winnings forfeited. Always check the minimum odds stated in the offer.

Time Limits

Free bet tokens typically expire between 7 and 30 days after they are credited. Some operators give as little as 48 hours for in-play free bets. Check your account's promotions section or the original offer email for the exact deadline. An unused free bet simply vanishes when the clock runs out.

Getting Best Value: Use on Singles, Not Accumulators

Because the stake is not returned, accumulators reduce the effective value of a free bet. Each leg you add multiplies the odds but also increases the chance of losing entirely — and you are only ever getting the profit, not the return. A single selection at odds of 4.0–6.0 is typically where free bet value peaks: high enough to generate a meaningful payout, realistic enough to have a genuine chance of winning.

Types of Free Bet Offers

Not all free bets are created equal. Here are the most common formats you will encounter on GRAI-licensed betting sites in Ireland:

Welcome Free Bets (Deposit Match)

The most common format: you sign up, place a qualifying bet, and receive a free bet token of equivalent value. For example, "Bet €10, Get €10 Free Bet". The qualifying bet is usually settled before the free bet is credited. The qualifying bet itself is not a free bet — you stake your own money for that one.

Enhanced Odds Offers

Your first bet on a specific event is boosted to inflated odds. For example, a team priced at 2.0 is offered to new customers at 6.0 for a maximum stake of €5. The extra profit (€5 × 4.0 = €20 above the standard return) may be paid as a free bet token rather than cash. Read whether the enhanced return is paid in cash or in free bet credit.

Money-Back Specials

Your stake is refunded as a free bet if a specific event occurs: a horse falls at the last, a match finishes 0–0, or your team loses by a single goal. These are the safest free bet offers to claim because your downside is limited — you lose the value only if the event that triggers the refund does not happen. The refund is credited as a free bet, not cash.

Refer a Friend Free Bets

Some operators credit both you and a friend with a free bet when the friend signs up and places a qualifying bet using your referral link. These often have tighter T&Cs — check whether the friend must deposit, what the qualifying bet amount is, and how quickly the free bet is credited.

Acca Insurance (Accumulator Protection)

Place a qualifying accumulator of four or more legs, and if exactly one leg lets you down, your stake is refunded as a free bet. This is a popular offer for football accas. The key restriction: "one leg down" only — if two or more selections fail, there is no refund. The refund is stake-not-returned, so plan accordingly.

How to Get Maximum Value from a Free Bet

Five practical rules that make a material difference to how much you actually pocket:

  1. Check SNR vs SR before anything else. Stake-Not-Returned is standard, but Stake-Returned offers do occasionally appear on special events. An SR free bet is worth significantly more — do not treat them the same.
  2. Confirm the minimum odds requirement. Placing a free bet on a 1.3 shot and finding it voided because the minimum was 1.5 is a preventable loss. Read the T&Cs for the exact floor.
  3. Go for a single, not an accumulator. The SNR structure means every extra leg you add reduces your effective return per unit of risk. A single at 4.0–6.0 is usually the optimal free-bet sweet spot.
  4. Use it before the expiry date. Set a calendar reminder if the token expires in more than a week. Free bets that disappear unused are pure waste — there is no extension process.
  5. Read the withdrawable winnings cap. Some operators cap the amount of winnings from a free bet that you can withdraw — for example, "maximum withdrawal of €50 from free bet winnings." If you place a €10 free bet at 10.0 and win €90 profit but the cap is €50, you will only be able to withdraw €50. High-odds singles are great for free bets, but check the cap first.

Frequently Asked Questions About Free Bets in Ireland

Do free bets expire?▾
Yes. Almost all free bets carry an expiry window, typically between 7 and 30 days from the date they are credited to your account. The exact period is set out in the offer's terms and conditions. Using a free bet the day it arrives is a safe habit — unexpired free bets are simply lost if the deadline passes.
Can I withdraw a free bet?▾
No. A free bet token itself is never withdrawable. What you can withdraw are the winnings generated if your free bet selection wins. Because free bets are stake-not-returned (SNR), only the profit portion of a winning return is paid out. For example, a €10 free bet that wins at odds of 3.0 pays €20 profit — the €10 stake is not returned.
Are free bets taxable in Ireland?▾
Betting winnings, including winnings from free bets, are generally not subject to income tax for recreational bettors in Ireland. This is not financial or tax advice — if you are unsure about your individual circumstances, speak to a qualified tax adviser or visit the Revenue Commissioners at revenue.ie.
What does SNR mean?▾
SNR stands for Stake Not Returned. It is the standard structure for free bets. When you place an SNR free bet and it wins, you receive only the profit from the bet — the original stake (the free bet token) is not added to your payout. By contrast, an SR (Stake Returned) offer pays out the full return including the stake, though SR offers are uncommon.

Free Bet Maths: Calculating Real Value

A free bet (stake not returned) is worth less than its face value because you do not get the stake back when it wins. The formula:

Real value = Free bet amount × (Decimal odds − 1) / Decimal odds

Example: A €20 SNR free bet placed at 3.00 (2/1).

Potential win = €20 × (3.00 − 1) = €40. But you lose the €20 stake (not returned), so profit = €40. Real value at 50% probability = 50% × €40 = €20 expected profit — the same as the free bet value because you have broken even on the stake-not-returned element. At shorter odds the value reduces: at 1.50, profit is only €10 on a €20 SNR bet used at 50% implied prob.

Practically: get maximum value from SNR free bets by placing them at higher odds (3.00+). At shorter prices, more of the bet's theoretical value is consumed by the missing stake return.

Free Bet vs Cashback: Which Is Better?

Some operators offer cashback (a refund on a losing bet) instead of a free bet. The key difference:

  • Free bet: Given upfront or on a qualifying event. You choose when to use it. SNR terms apply.
  • Cashback: Usually returned as bonus cash or a free bet after a loss. The qualifying event is defined. Often has lower wagering on the returned amount (sometimes 1× playthrough).

Cashback with 1× playthrough on a qualifying loss is often the better value: a €20 cashback on a €20 loser gives €20 to bet once more, with the stake returnable if that bet wins. A €20 SNR free bet is worth less at the same odds because the stake is forfeited on a win.

What Happens if a Horse Is Withdrawn After I Bet Each Way Ireland

If a horse is withdrawn (non-runner) after you have placed an each-way bet, the standard rule depends on whether you have NRNB (Non-Runner No Bet) protection. Without NRNB: a Rule 4 deduction is applied to your winnings, reducing the odds to reflect the withdrawal. The deduction percentage is set by the bookmaker based on the horse's market price at the time of withdrawal -- typically 5-75% reduction. With NRNB: your stake is returned in full if the specific horse you backed is a non-runner.

Free Bets in Ireland: How They Actually Work in 2026

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Free bets are the most advertised feature in Irish sports betting — and the most misunderstood. This guide explains exactly how the maths works, what the GRAI's new rules changed, and how to use a free bet so you actually keep value from it.

The One Rule That Changes Everything

Almost every free bet in Ireland comes with stakes not returned. This means: place a €20 free bet at odds of 2.0 and win — you receive €20, not €40. The free stake itself is not paid out. That single mechanic cuts the headline value of most offers roughly in half, and it is buried in the terms every time.

How Free Bets Actually Work: The Full Mechanics

Step 1 — Qualifying bet: You place a real-money bet at minimum odds (usually 1.5–2.0) to unlock the offer. The qualifying bet is a real wager — it can lose, and often does. Never place a qualifying bet at odds you would not normally take.

Step 2 — Token credited: After the qualifying bet settles (win or lose), the free bet token appears in your account. Check the expiry — typically 7 days, sometimes as short as 48 hours.

Step 3 — Use the token: Select a market, apply the free bet token, and place the bet. If it wins, you receive the winnings only — not the stake. If it loses, nothing is deducted from your real balance.

Split tokens: A headline offer of €50 in free bets often means 5×€10 tokens, not one €50 token. This forces five separate selections and makes it harder to target higher odds across all of them.

The Smart Way to Use Free Bets

The table above shows it clearly: higher odds extract more expected value from a stakes-not-returned token. The sweet spot for most punters is 3.0–5.0 — high enough to boost EV without making it a pure lottery. Here is what that means in practice:

  • A horse at 4.0 each-way gives you two chances to win from one token.
  • An acca with three selections at 2.0 each gives combined odds of 8.0 — high EV from the token, but all three must win.
  • A short favourite at 1.5 returns very little on stakes-not-returned terms — avoid for free bets.

Never chase a qualifying loss with a bigger bet. The qualifying bet is a cost of the offer — it should be a selection you would make at those odds anyway.

What the GRAI Changed for Free Bets in Ireland

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 introduced Ireland's first restrictions on betting inducements. GRAI-licensed bookmakers are now prohibited from sending targeted free bet offers to self-excluded players or those who have set deposit limits. Unsolicited bonus push notifications face watershed restrictions. The offers themselves remain legal — the rules govern how aggressively they can be pushed, not whether they exist.

In practice this means: if you receive a free bet offer from a GRAI-licensed operator, it was sent within the legal framework. An unlicensed site has no such obligation. This is one more reason to check the GRAI register before opening an account anywhere. Full details: Ireland's inducements ban explained.

Free Bets in Ireland — November 2026 Update

Since GRAI remote betting licences became mandatory on 1 July 2026, the free bet landscape in Ireland has changed. Operators without a GRAI licence are no longer legally permitted to accept Irish customers for sports betting. This means the pool of legitimate free bet offers has narrowed — but the quality has improved. Every offer listed on this page comes from a GRAI-licensed bookmaker.

Key change for November 2026: GRAI rules now require that free bet terms are displayed clearly before you opt in. If a bookmaker's terms are buried or unclear, that is a red flag — and technically a breach of the new advertising standards under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

  3. What are the minimum odds on the free bet itself?

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I get my stake back with a free bet in Ireland?

Almost never. The standard mechanic is stakes not returned (SNR): if you place a €20 free bet at 2.0 and win, you receive €20, not €40. A handful of offers pay "free bet including stake" (FBIS) — these are rarer and worth more. The terms always specify which applies.

Are free bets legal in Ireland in 2026?

Yes. Free bet welcome offers remain legal under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The GRAI's inducements rules restrict targeted marketing (pushing offers to vulnerable players), but the offers themselves are permitted. Only GRAI-licensed operators can legally offer them to Irish customers from 1 July 2026.

What odds should I use a free bet at?

Higher than you would use with real money. Stakes-not-returned maths makes higher odds more valuable: a €20 token at 4.0 has an expected value of €15, versus €10 at 2.0. The practical sweet spot is 3.0–5.0 — high enough to maximise expected value without becoming a pure coin flip.

Why did my free bet expire before I used it?

Most free bet tokens expire 7 days after being credited, some as quickly as 48 hours. The clock starts when the token is credited to your account, not when you claim the offer. Check your account's promotions page or the bookmaker's terms as soon as you receive a token.

Can I withdraw a free bet without using it?

No — free bet tokens cannot be withdrawn directly. They must be used on a qualifying market, and only the winnings (not the stake) can then be withdrawn. There are no wagering requirements on the winnings themselves from a standard SNR free bet.

What is the difference between a free bet and a deposit bonus?

A free bet is a token used to place one bet — winnings are yours, stake is not returned. A deposit bonus (like a 100% match) credits bonus funds that typically carry a wagering requirement (e.g. 5–30x) before they can be withdrawn. Free bets are simpler and usually better value for straightforward sports betting.

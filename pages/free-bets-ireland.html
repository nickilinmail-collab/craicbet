Free Bets in Ireland: What You Need to Know

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Free bets are one of the most advertised features of online bookmakers in Ireland, and also one of the most misunderstood. The headline figure — "Get €50 in free bets" — and the actual value you can extract are rarely the same number. This guide breaks down how free bets really work, what the GRAI's rules changed, and how to calculate whether an offer is worth claiming.

Types of Free Bets Available in Ireland

Sign-up / welcome offers: The most common type. Usually triggered by opening an account and placing a qualifying bet. The free bet is credited after the qualifying bet settles. These are one-time offers per customer.

Reload offers: Ongoing promotions for existing customers. Often tied to specific events (enhanced odds on a weekend fixture, free bet for the Grand National). Lower headline value than welcome offers but available repeatedly.

Refer-a-friend: A free bet credited when you refer someone who opens an account and places a qualifying bet. The GRAI's inducements rules restrict how referral offers can be marketed.

Price boosts and enhanced odds: Not technically free bets, but effectively a one-time offer on a specific market. The enhanced price is offered on the first bet only, up to a maximum stake. The EV of a price boost depends on the size of the enhancement relative to the fair odds.

SNR vs SR: The Single Most Important Term

SNR (Stake Not Returned) is the standard for free bets at nearly all bookmakers. When you place a free bet and win, you receive the profit — not the stake plus profit. A €20 free bet at odds of 3/1 returns €60 profit (not €80). The difference matters: an SNR free bet is worth less than the same amount in real cash.

The expected value of an SNR free bet equals: Free bet amount × (odds − 1) / odds. At odds of 2/1 (3.0), a €20 SNR free bet has an EV of approximately €13. At 4/1 (5.0), it rises to approximately €16. Higher odds improve the value of an SNR free bet, up to a point where the probability of winning drops so low that the EV falls again.

SR (Stake Returned) free bets pay winnings plus the original stake. These are rarer and significantly more valuable than SNR. If you encounter an SR free bet offer, check whether there is a minimum odds restriction before the extra value is real.

Common Restrictions to Watch

Minimum odds: Most free bets require the selection to be at a minimum odds (often evens/2.0 or higher). A free bet placed on an odds-on favourite at 1/2 would not qualify. This restriction reduces how efficiently you can deploy the free bet value.

Single sport only: Some welcome offers restrict the free bet to specific sports (usually football or horse racing). A free bet restricted to horse racing cannot be used on Premier League football. Read the terms carefully before claiming.

Time limits: Free bets typically expire 7–30 days after being credited. Some require the qualifying bet to be placed within a shorter window. Expired free bets are forfeited with no recourse.

Maximum stake per free bet: Reload free bets often cap the free bet amount (e.g., free bet up to €10 on a nominated event). The maximum stake cap limits the absolute upside of the offer.

Wagering Requirements: When They Apply

Traditional sign-up free bets at most UK/Irish-facing bookmakers do not carry wagering requirements on the free bet itself — you place the free bet once and keep the winnings. However, some no-deposit bonus offers, casino-linked betting bonuses, or reload promotions may require you to wager the bonus amount a set number of times. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an SNR free bet?

SNR means Stake Not Returned. If you use a €20 SNR free bet and win at 3/1, you receive €60 profit — the stake is not added to winnings as with a cash bet. SNR free bets are worth less than the same amount in cash.

Are free bets still legal in Ireland in 2026?

Yes. Free bets remain legal under GRAI rules. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 restricts advertising (no TV before 9pm, no targeting self-excluded players) but the offers themselves remain available on GRAI-licensed sites.

What is a wagering requirement on a free bet?

A requirement to wager the bonus amount a certain number of times before withdrawing. A €10 bonus with 10x wagering means placing €100 in bets before the bonus converts to withdrawable cash. Always check terms before claiming.

What are the best odds to use a free bet at?

For SNR free bets, higher odds increase the expected value percentage. A £10 SNR free bet at evens returns £10 profit; at 3/1 it returns £30. Use our free-bet calculator to find the optimal odds range.

Do free bets expire?

Yes — most expire 7 to 30 days from when they are credited. Read the specific terms on each offer before claiming; expired free bets are forfeited.

What is a GRAI Licence? Everything Irish Players Need to Know

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 12 min read

💡 Key Takeaways

  • The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) is Ireland's first dedicated gambling regulator
  • Established under the Gambling Regulation Bill 2022, fully operational since 2024
  • Currently issues remote betting licences only — casino/gaming licences expected 2027–2028
  • All online casinos serving Irish players currently operate on offshore licences
  • GRAI licensing represents a major step forward in player protection for Ireland

What is the GRAI?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) is Ireland's first independent statutory body dedicated to regulating gambling activities. Before the GRAI's establishment, Ireland relied on outdated legislation — primarily the Betting Acts of 1931 and the Gaming and Lotteries Acts of 1956 — that was entirely unfit for the digital age of online gambling.

The GRAI was created to bring Ireland's gambling regulation into the 21st century. Its mandate covers licensing, compliance monitoring, player protection, and enforcement across all forms of gambling in the Republic of Ireland. For Irish players, this means a dedicated authority whose primary purpose is ensuring fair play, preventing problem gambling, and holding operators accountable.

What Licences Does the GRAI Issue?

As of October 2026, the GRAI issues remote betting licences only. This covers online sports betting and similar wagering activities. The licensing framework for online casino games, slots, and gaming has not yet been finalised.

This is an important distinction for Irish players. When you see a casino advertised as "GRAI licensed," it means the operator holds a remote betting licence — not a dedicated casino or gaming licence. The casino games those operators offer may still be provided under their existing offshore licences from jurisdictions such as Malta (MGA), Gibraltar, or Curaçao.

What the GRAI Licence Means for Irish Players

The GRAI licence introduces several concrete benefits for players in Ireland:

  • Deposit Limits: Licensed operators must offer mandatory deposit-limit tools, allowing players to cap their daily, weekly, or monthly spending.
  • Self-Exclusion: A national self-exclusion register is being developed. Once live, players will be able to exclude themselves from all GRAI-licensed operators with a single registration.
  • Reality Checks: Operators must implement session time reminders and activity statements so players can track their gambling habits.
  • Dispute Resolution: The GRAI provides a formal complaints procedure. If a licensed operator treats you unfairly, you have a regulatory path to resolution.
  • Advertising Standards: Strict controls on gambling advertising, including bans on targeting minors and requirements for responsible gambling messaging in all promotions.
  • Fair Play Assurance: Licensed operators must use certified random number generators (RNGs) and submit to regular audits.

How to Check if a Casino Has a GRAI Licence

Verifying a GRAI licence is straightforward:

  1. Visit the GRAI's official website at grai.ie
  2. Navigate to the Licensed Operators section
  3. Search for the operator by name
  4. Confirm the licence type (currently remote betting only) and its status (active/suspended/revoked)

Remember: as of October 2026, the GRAI only issues remote betting licences. If an online casino claims to have a GRAI "casino licence," that is not accurate — those licences do not yet exist. The Future of GRAI Licensing

The GRAI has confirmed that casino and gaming licences are on the roadmap, with applications expected to open in 2027 or 2028. When this happens, it will be a watershed moment for the Irish gambling industry:

  • Online casinos serving Irish players will need a GRAI licence to operate legally
  • Unlicensed operators may face DNS blocking or payment processing restrictions
  • Irish players will have the full protection of domestic regulation for casino games, not just sports betting
  • The tax framework for casino gaming revenue will be established

Frequently Asked Questions About GRAI Licences

What does GRAI stand for?▾
GRAI stands for the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland. It is Ireland's first independent statutory regulator for gambling, established under the Gambling Regulation Bill 2022.
Does the GRAI issue casino licences?▾
Not yet. As of October 2026, the GRAI issues remote betting licences only. Casino and gaming licences are expected to become available in 2027 or 2028. Until then, online casinos serving Irish players operate under international licences from jurisdictions like Malta, Gibraltar, or Curaçao.
Is it legal to play at a casino without a GRAI licence?▾
Yes. Irish players can legally play at internationally licensed online casinos. Since the GRAI does not yet issue casino licences, all online casinos serving Ireland operate on offshore licences. This is legal and normal. We recommend choosing operators licensed by the MGA, UKGC, or Gibraltar for the best player protections.
How do I check if a casino is GRAI licensed?▾
Visit the GRAI's official website at grai.ie and navigate to the Licensed Operators section. You can search for any operator by name to verify their licence status. Remember that GRAI currently only licenses remote betting — any casino games are covered by the operator's offshore licence.
What protections does a GRAI licence give me?▾
GRAI-licensed operators must provide deposit limits, self-exclusion tools, reality checks, and comply with strict advertising codes. The GRAI also has a formal complaints procedure, so if you have a dispute with a licensed operator, there is a regulatory path to resolution.
When was the GRAI established?▾
The legal framework was laid by the Gambling Regulation Bill 2022. The GRAI became fully operational in 2024, and issued its first remote betting licences in July 2026.
Will all casinos need a GRAI licence in the future?▾
Yes, that is the GRAI's stated goal. Once casino and gaming licences become available (expected 2027–2028), operators serving Irish players will need to hold a GRAI licence. Unlicensed operators may face enforcement measures including DNS blocking.
What is the difference between a GRAI licence and an MGA licence?▾
The GRAI is Ireland's domestic regulator, while the MGA (Malta Gaming Authority) is Malta's. Both regulate online gambling, but the GRAI focuses specifically on the Irish market with Irish player protections. Currently, many casinos serving Ireland hold MGA licences. Read our full comparison →
Does the GRAI replace the old betting licence system?▾
Yes. The GRAI replaces the previous system where betting licences were handled by Revenue Commissioners and District Courts. The new framework is comprehensive, covering online and offline gambling under a single modern regulator with proper enforcement powers.
What happens to casinos that don’t get a GRAI licence?▾
Once the full licensing framework is in place, operators serving Irish players without a GRAI licence may face enforcement actions. These could include DNS blocking (making the site inaccessible from Ireland), payment processing restrictions, and fines. Players are not penalised for using unlicensed sites, but they lose the protections that GRAI licensing provides.

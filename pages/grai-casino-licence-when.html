When Will GRAI Issue Casino Licences in Ireland?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) has issued zero casino licences as of November 2026. The authority began issuing remote betting licences in July 2026, but remote gaming licences — the type that covers online casino games, slots, and table games — are not expected until 2027 or 2028 at the earliest. This page explains why, what the law says, and what it means for Irish casino players right now.

💡 Key Facts

  • Zero GRAI casino or gaming licences have been issued as of November 2026
  • GRAI currently issues remote betting licences only (commenced July 2026)
  • Remote gaming (casino) licences are expected 2027–2028 under the Gambling Regulation Act timetable
  • No exact date for casino licences has been confirmed by the GRAI
  • Online casinos serving Irish players currently operate under international licences (MGA, Gibraltar, etc.)
  • Playing at internationally licensed casinos is legal for Irish players

Current Status: Betting Licences Only

The GRAI began issuing licences on 1 July 2026. At that stage, only one category of remote licence was ready: the remote betting licence. This covers online sports betting, in-play betting, and similar wagering products. All major bookmakers operating in Ireland were required to hold one to continue trading legally.

As of November 2026, the GRAI has issued 33 remote betting licences. The register is public and searchable at grai.ie. Our recommended partner holds licence number GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001, valid 16 September 2026 – 15 September 2029 — a remote betting licence, not a casino licence.

When Are Casino Licences Coming?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024, signed into law in October 2024, sets out a phased approach to rolling out GRAI licences. The Act grants the GRAI power to issue three categories of remote licence:

  • Remote betting licences — covering online sportsbooks and wagering
  • Remote gaming licences — covering online casino games, slots, table games, and live casino
  • Remote lottery licences — covering online lottery and lotto-type products

Phase 1, which began in July 2026, addressed remote betting. Phase 2 — remote gaming — is the next step. The GRAI has indicated this phase is expected to be ready in 2027 or 2028, but no specific date has been confirmed publicly as of this writing.

The delay between phases is partly practical: each licence type requires the GRAI to finalise technical standards, game-fairness requirements, advertising rules, and player-protection specifications that are specific to that category. Casino and gaming products are more complex to regulate than sports betting, with thousands of individual game titles, random number generators, and bonus structures to standardise.

What Does This Mean for Irish Casino Players?

Until GRAI gaming licences exist, online casinos serving Irish players are operating under international licences. The most common are:

  • Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) — widely regarded as the gold standard for European online casino regulation
  • Gibraltar Regulatory Authority — covers several well-known operators with strong player-protection track records
  • UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) — some operators hold dual MGA/UKGC licences; UKGC provides strong protections though it is a UK-specific regulator
  • Revenue Commissioners (legacy permits) — some operators may still hold old domestic permits, which continue in effect until replaced by GRAI licences or until they expire

Playing at a casino licensed by any of these bodies is legal for Irish players. Irish law does not criminalise players for using offshore-licensed sites. The gap in domestic regulation means players currently rely on the protections those offshore licences provide rather than the full GRAI framework.

Any reputable casino will display its licence number in the footer. Click the licence badge — it should link directly to the regulator's public register where you can verify the status. Frequently Asked Questions

Has the GRAI issued any casino licences?▾
No. As of November 2026, the GRAI has issued zero casino or gaming licences. The GRAI has only issued remote betting licences, which commenced in July 2026. Any site claiming to hold a GRAI casino licence is incorrect — those licences do not yet exist.
When will GRAI casino licences be available?▾
Remote gaming (casino) licences are expected to become available in 2027 or 2028 under the phased rollout of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. No exact date has been confirmed by the GRAI. Phase 1 (remote betting) commenced July 2026; Phase 2 (remote gaming) is the next planned step.
Is it legal to play at an online casino in Ireland without a GRAI licence?▾
Yes. Irish players can legally play at internationally licensed online casinos. Because the GRAI does not yet issue casino licences, all online casinos serving Ireland operate under licences from other jurisdictions such as Malta (MGA), Gibraltar, or Curaçao. This is legal and expected to remain so until GRAI gaming licences come into force.
What happens to online casinos once GRAI casino licences are introduced?▾
Once remote gaming licences become available, operators serving Irish players will need to apply for and hold a GRAI gaming licence. Those that do not may face enforcement measures under the Gambling Regulation Act, which gives the GRAI powers including DNS blocking and payment processing restrictions.
Are online casinos currently operating under old Revenue Commissioners permits?▾
Some operators may hold legacy permits from the Revenue Commissioners. These continue in effect until the GRAI issues corresponding licences or they expire. However, most online casinos serving Irish players are licensed through international regulators such as the MGA rather than under old domestic permits.

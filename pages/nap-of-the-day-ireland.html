DNB stands for Draw No Bet. It is a market where you back a team to win, and if the match ends in a draw, your stake is returned. Only a loss ends with you losing your money. DNB effectively removes the draw as an outcome, giving you a binary win/return scenario.

How Draw No Bet Works

Standard 1X2 market: back Team A, if they draw or lose you lose your stake. DNB: back Team A, if they win you win, if they draw your stake is returned, only if they lose do you lose.

This safety net comes at a cost: DNB odds are lower than the standard win odds, because you are buying protection against the draw.

DNB vs Asian Handicap 0

Draw No Bet and Asian Handicap 0 (AH 0) are mathematically identical. If a bookmaker offers AH 0 on a match, it is the same as DNB. The notation differs between European and Asian market styles, but the settlement rules are the same: draw = stake returned.

Calculating the Real Value of DNB

In a typical Premier League match, the draw probability is roughly 25–30%. By buying DNB odds instead of outright win odds, you are sacrificing roughly 25–30% of potential value in exchange for draw insurance.

Example: Team A wins at 2.0 (evens). Team A DNB at 1.60. The difference (2.0 − 1.60) represents the value of the draw insurance. Whether it is worth it depends on how likely you think a draw is for this specific match.

When to Use DNB

DNB makes most sense when: you are confident in one team’s quality but worried about defensive opposition that might hold for a draw; or when the win price is very short and a small extra safety net is worth the reduced return. DNB is less useful when draw probability is low (e.g., a heavy favourite against weak opposition).

DNB vs Asian Handicap: What Is the Difference

DNB (Draw No Bet) and Asian Handicap 0 are mathematically identical — both refund your stake if the match ends in a draw, and win/lose on the other results. The practical difference is presentation: DNB is listed as a separate market at most bookmakers, while AH 0 appears in the Asian handicap section. Odds may differ slightly between the two due to different margin structures applied to each market. Always compare both when placing a draw-no-bet style wager.

What Does DNB Mean in Betting Ireland

DNB stands for "Draw No Bet." If you place a DNB bet on Team A and the match ends in a draw, your stake is refunded in full. If Team A wins, you win at the stated odds. If Team A loses, you lose your stake. DNB effectively removes the draw outcome from the equation, giving you a two-way bet on the result. It is popular for betting on slight favourites in closely-matched games where a draw is a realistic outcome.

What Does Each Way Mean in Horse Racing Ireland

Each-way (EW) is two bets in one: a win bet and a place bet. Half your stake goes on the horse to win, half on it to place (finish in the top 2, 3, 4, or 5 depending on the race). The place part pays a fraction of the win odds — typically 1/4 or 1/5 depending on the number of runners and the operator's terms. Example: €10 EW on a horse at 10/1 with 1/4 odds, 3 places — if it finishes 3rd: win part loses (€5), place part wins €5 × (10/4 + 1) = €5 × 3.5 = €17.50 total return.

What Does DNB Mean in Betting Ireland

DNB stands for Draw No Bet. If you place a DNB bet and the match ends in a draw, your stake is returned in full -- you do not win, but you do not lose either. Only an outright loss costs you money. It is a popular market in Ireland for backing slight favourites in tight games where a draw is a realistic outcome.

DNB vs Asian Handicap Ireland: What Is the Difference

DNB and Asian Handicap 0 (AH 0) are mathematically identical -- if the match draws, your stake is returned. The difference is presentation: DNB is a named market with a single price, while AH 0 is part of the Asian Handicap framework which also includes quarter-ball and half-ball lines.

What Does Void Bet Mean Ireland

A void bet means the bet is cancelled and your stake is returned. Common reasons: the event was postponed or abandoned, a selection in an acca was not played, the horse was withdrawn (NRNB applies), or a bookmaker pricing error. In accumulators, a void leg is typically removed and the acca continues at reduced odds with one fewer selection.

What Does Push Mean in Betting Ireland

A push occurs when your bet neither wins nor loses -- the result lands exactly on the spread or total you bet. For example, if you bet Over 3 goals and the final score produces exactly 3 goals total, that is a push: stake returned. Pushes are more common in Asian handicap and total goals markets than in standard 1X2 betting.

What Does BOG Mean in Betting Ireland

BOG stands for Best Odds Guaranteed. If you take an early price and the starting price (SP) is higher when the race begins, you are automatically paid at the higher SP. For example: you back a horse at 5/1 early; it drifts to 7/1 SP. With BOG you get paid at 7/1. BOG is most valuable on ante-post racing bets and is commonly offered by Irish bookmakers for Cheltenham, the Grand National, and major Irish festival meetings.

What Does NRNB Mean in Betting Ireland

NRNB stands for Non-Runner No Bet. If a horse you backed is a non-runner before the race, your stake is returned in full. This applies to the entire bet. NRNB is particularly useful for ante-post betting on major festivals like Cheltenham -- where injury withdrawals are common -- because it removes the risk of losing your stake to a non-runner.

What Does Handicap Minus 1 Mean Betting

A -1 handicap means your selected team starts the game with a virtual one-goal deficit. For your bet to win, they must win the actual match by 2 or more goals. If they win by exactly 1 goal, the bet is a push (stake returned). If they draw or lose, you lose. This is a European handicap -- unlike Asian handicap where the -1 line creates no void outcome.

What does DNB mean in betting?

Draw No Bet — if you back a team and the match draws, your stake is returned. Only a loss results in losing your bet. The draw is removed as a result that costs you money.

Is DNB the same as Asian Handicap 0?

Yes — mathematically identical. AH 0 and Draw No Bet have the same settlement rules: draw = stake returned, win = win, loss = lose. The notation differs by market style.

Are DNB odds lower than outright win odds?

Yes — because DNB includes draw insurance. The odds are lower to reflect the fact that you can't lose your stake in the event of a draw.

When should I use Draw No Bet?

When you're confident in a team but worried about a draw. DNB reduces profit on a win but provides a safety net. Worth using when draw probability is high and the team's win margin is uncertain.

What happens to a DNB bet in an accumulator if one game draws?

The DNB leg is treated as a void — stake for that leg is returned and it's removed from the acca. The remaining legs continue at adjusted odds.

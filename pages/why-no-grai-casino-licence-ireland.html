There is no GRAI casino licence in Ireland because the GRAI has not yet opened casino licensing. As of November 2026, zero casino licences have been issued anywhere in Ireland under the GRAI framework.

What the GRAI Was Set Up to Do — and What It Has Done So Far

The GRAI was established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 as Ireland's first dedicated, independent gambling regulator. Before the GRAI, online betting in Ireland was overseen by Revenue Commissioners under a framework dating to 1931 — a law originally written for on-course bookmakers, stretched awkwardly to cover global digital platforms. The 2024 Act replaced that with a modern, purpose-built regulatory body with powers to license, audit, fine, and prosecute gambling operators.

The GRAI's first priority was remote betting — the category with the most market activity and clearest existing regulatory need. From 1 July 2026, any operator taking sports bets or other fixed-odds wagers from Irish residents requires a GRAI remote betting (RB) licence. Around 41 such licences had been issued by November 2026, covering most of the major bookmakers operating in Ireland.

Casino gaming — slots, roulette, blackjack, live dealer, poker — is a separate licence category under the Act. The GRAI has not yet opened applications for remote gaming licences. This is not an oversight; it reflects a deliberate phased rollout. Building a licensing framework for casino gaming requires additional technical standards, game certification requirements, and responsible gambling infrastructure that the regulator is still finalising.

The Expected Timeline: 2027–2028

The GRAI's published roadmap indicates that remote gaming (casino) licences are expected to become available in 2027–2028. No confirmed opening date has been announced as of November 2026. The timeline depends on several factors:

  • Completion of the GRAI's technical standards for casino game certification
  • Implementation of a national self-exclusion register covering casino products
  • Staffing and systems capable of processing gaming licence applications at scale
  • Secondary legislation (ministerial regulations) under the 2024 Act that will set specific casino licensing rules

The GRAI has said publicly that in-person (land-based) betting shop licences will open for applications in December 2026, suggesting the regulator is methodically working through licence categories in order of regulatory urgency. Casino gaming is expected to follow once the betting framework is fully bedded in.

How to Stay Safe Playing at Offshore Casino Sites

Until GRAI gaming licences are available, the best protection for Irish casino players is choosing sites licensed by reputable foreign regulators. Here is what to look for and how to verify it:

1. Check the licence footer

Every legitimate casino displays its licence information in the footer of the website. Look for a full licence number, the name of the issuing regulator, and usually a clickable seal that links to the regulator's verification page. If the footer is vague ("licensed and regulated") without naming the regulator and number, that is a warning sign.

2. Verify on the regulator's own register

Do not rely on the casino's word alone. Each major regulator publishes a public licence register:

  • MGA (Malta Gaming Authority): register.mga.org.mt — search by company name or licence number
  • UKGC (UK Gambling Commission): gamblingcommission.gov.uk/check-an-operator — note UKGC licences require the operator to have a UK address; Irish players are covered if the site explicitly services Ireland
  • Gibraltar Regulatory Authority: gra.gi/gambling-operators — a smaller register but strong standards
  • Estonian Tax and Customs Board: emta.ee — covers operators licensed in Estonia, an EU member state

3. Treat Curaçao licences with extra caution

Curaçao is a popular jurisdiction for online casino licensing, partly because it is cheaper and faster to obtain. However, Curaçao licences have historically offered weaker player protection: dispute resolution is slower, enforcement is limited, and the regulator has less leverage over operators. This does not mean all Curaçao-licensed casinos are unsafe, but you should apply higher scrutiny when a Curaçao licence is the only credential offered.

4. Test withdrawals early

One of the most reliable safety tests is to make a small withdrawal early in your time with a casino — before you have built up a large balance. Fast, friction-free withdrawals indicate an operator that is not making it difficult to get your money out. Casinos that delay withdrawals, request excessive documentation, or find reasons to confiscate balances tend to reveal this pattern before large sums are involved.

5. Use responsible gambling tools proactively

Even without an Irish licence, reputable casinos offer deposit limits, session time limits, and self-exclusion. Use them. Set deposit limits before you start playing, not after a session you regret. What Changes When GRAI Casino Licences Arrive (2027–2028)

When the GRAI does open casino licensing, Irish players can expect substantial improvements in consumer protection:

  • Payout obligation under Irish law: Casinos that hold a GRAI gaming licence will be required to pay winnings as a licence condition. Refusal would be a criminal breach.
  • Irish complaints route: Players will be able to escalate disputes directly to the GRAI rather than a foreign regulator.
  • National self-exclusion register: GRAI-licensed casinos will be required to check the national self-exclusion database, covering both betting and gaming products on a single register.
  • Advertising controls: The watershed and inducements rules that apply to betting advertising will also cover casino advertising.
  • Game certification: Casino games offered on GRAI-licensed platforms will need to meet Irish technical standards for fairness and RNG (random number generator) testing.

None of this applies yet — but it represents the direction of travel. If you are a regular casino player, the arrival of GRAI gaming licences will meaningfully strengthen your position.

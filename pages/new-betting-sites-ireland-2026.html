New Betting Sites Ireland 2026: Recently GRAI-Licensed Operators

Updated: November 2026 • Reading time: 10 min
Quick Summary: GRAI began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026. The first wave of approvals has seen multiple operators receive Irish licences during H2 2026. This guide explains why new GRAI-licensed sites are appearing, what their licence means for Irish bettors, and what to check before joining one.

Why New GRAI-Licensed Bookmakers Are Appearing in 2026

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 fundamentally changed the Irish betting landscape. Before GRAI, Irish residents could legally access offshore-licensed betting sites, but no dedicated Irish regulator existed with comprehensive oversight powers. The Act created GRAI and established a formal domestic licensing framework.

From 1 July 2026, GRAI began issuing remote betting licences. Operators who had previously served the Irish market under EU licences — primarily from Malta (MGA) or Gibraltar — now need a GRAI licence to continue operating legally in Ireland.

This has produced a continuous stream of new GRAI licence applications and approvals throughout H2 2026. Some are truly "new" brands; many are established operators newly obtaining Irish domestic certification. The distinction matters: a site newly GRAI-licensed in 2026 may have years of operational history under another licence.

The GRAI Public Register

The definitive list of GRAI licence holders is the GRAI public register, accessible at grai.ie. It lists every currently licensed operator with their:

  • Operating entity and trading name
  • Licence number
  • Licence type (remote betting, in this context)
  • Issue date and expiry date

You can verify any licence claim directly against this register. If a site claims to be GRAI-licensed but does not appear on the register, that is a serious red flag.

What to Check Before Joining a New Site

Whether the site is newly launched or newly GRAI-licensed, the same due diligence applies:

  1. Verify the licence on the GRAI public register. The licence number should be visible on the site's footer.
  2. Test the responsible gambling tools — are deposit limits, time limits and self-exclusion easy to find and use?
  3. Check deposit and withdrawal options — are your preferred Irish payment methods supported? What are the processing times and minimum/maximum limits?
  4. Read the bonus terms — headline offer aside, what is the wagering requirement, eligible market restriction, and time limit?
  5. Test customer support — send a question via live chat or email before depositing any funds. Response speed and quality are strong predictors of how disputes will be handled.
  6. Make a test withdrawal — before depositing significant funds, place a small bet and attempt a withdrawal. This reveals any friction before it becomes a problem.

Potential Advantages of New Sites

New market entrants sometimes offer advantages as they compete to build a customer base:

  • Enhanced welcome bonuses — higher than industry average to attract initial registrations. Always assess value net of wagering requirements, not gross.
  • Innovative features — some newer platforms invest in user experience improvements that established operators have not adopted: faster cash-out, expanded in-play markets, better statistics integrations.
  • Competitive odds — new entrants sometimes price markets keener to gain market share, particularly on major events.

Considerations with New Sites

  • Limited track record: established sites have years of verified payout history. New operators have not yet demonstrated how they handle edge cases, disputes or high-volume withdrawal requests.
  • Customer support capacity: new sites may have smaller support teams during early operation, leading to slower response times.
  • Technical stability: platforms new to high-traffic events (major tournaments, Grand National) occasionally experience outages. Check whether the site has a contingency plan for in-play bets placed during technical issues.

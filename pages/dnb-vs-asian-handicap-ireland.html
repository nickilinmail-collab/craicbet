DNB vs Asian Handicap: What Is the Difference

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Draw No Bet (DNB) and Asian Handicap 0 (AH 0) produce exactly the same result: if the match is drawn, your stake is refunded. If your team wins, you win. If your team loses, you lose. Despite being identical bets, they are presented differently on different platforms and can carry slightly different odds due to margin variations.

Key Differences in Practice

DNB is the standard label used by European and Irish bookmakers. AH 0 is the same bet expressed in Asian market notation, more common on betting exchanges and Asian-facing sites. The odds may differ slightly between the two labels even at the same bookmaker, due to how the margin is built into each market.

AH extends to half-goal and quarter-goal handicaps (AH 0.25, AH -0.5, AH -0.75 etc.), which DNB does not. DNB is a specific market; AH is a family of markets of which AH 0 is one option.

When to Use DNB vs AH 0

Use whichever label offers the higher odds for the same bet. If your bookmaker offers DNB at 1.72 and AH 0 at 1.75 for the same team, AH 0 is better value. DNB is more commonly available on standard bookmakers; AH 0 is easier to find on exchanges. At its core, it is the same insurance: draw protection at the cost of reduced win odds compared to 1X2.

DNB vs Asian Handicap 0: are they the same bet

Yes — mathematically, DNB (Draw No Bet) and Asian Handicap 0 (AH 0) are identical bets. Both eliminate the draw: if the match ends in a draw, your stake is returned. If your selection wins, you win at the stated odds. If your selection loses, you lose your stake. The only difference is presentation — different bookmakers label the same market differently.

When to use DNB vs Asian Handicap in Ireland

Use DNB when it is the market label offered on the specific event you want to bet on. Use AH 0 when you want to combine it with other Asian Handicap lines (e.g. a split bet of AH 0 and AH -0.5) in the same selection, or when the AH market offers slightly better liquidity or odds than the DNB market. For most recreational punters, the choice is simply whichever market your bookmaker displays.

AH 0 is more commonly offered on football markets and allows combination with half-line handicaps. DNB is more common in sports like tennis and basketball where draws are rare or impossible — in those sports, DNB simply means "back to win." In football, both terms appear frequently and can be compared directly.

Is DNB better than double chance?

Double chance (1X covers home win and draw) has different settlement to DNB. DNB backs a team and refunds on draw; double chance backs two outcomes simultaneously. DNB usually offers better odds than the double chance market covering the same team’s win/draw.

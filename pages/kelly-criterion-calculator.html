Kelly Criterion Calculator: Optimal Stake for Your Bankroll

Kelly Criterion Calculator

⚠ Kelly assumes you know your true win probability. Overconfidence leads to overbetting and large drawdowns. Half-Kelly is safer for most punters.

What is the Kelly Criterion?

The Kelly Criterion is a staking formula developed by mathematician John L. Kelly Jr. in 1956. It tells you the exact percentage of your bankroll to wager in order to maximise the long-run growth of your funds. Unlike flat staking, Kelly scales your bet size to your actual edge — when your edge is small, you bet less; when it is large, you bet more. It is used by professional bettors, poker players and even financial traders.

Kelly Formula Explained

The standard Kelly formula is:

f* = (bp − q) / b

where:
• b = net decimal odds (i.e. decimal odds − 1)
• p = your estimated probability of winning (as a decimal, e.g. 0.55)
• q = probability of losing = 1 − p
• f* = fraction of bankroll to bet

If f* is negative or zero, the Kelly Criterion says do not bet — there is no mathematical edge at the offered odds. The calculator above applies this formula and optionally divides the result by 2 (Half Kelly) or 4 (Quarter Kelly).

Full Kelly vs Half Kelly vs Quarter Kelly

Full Kelly maximises bankroll growth in theory, but it comes with extreme variance — drawdowns of 50% or more are common. Most professional bettors use Half Kelly or Quarter Kelly. Half Kelly reduces variance by roughly 75% while capturing around 75% of the growth rate. Quarter Kelly is very conservative and suits punters who want steady growth with minimal swings. The calculator defaults to Half Kelly for this reason.

Kelly Criterion Example

Suppose your bankroll is €500. You estimate a football match has a 55% chance of the home team winning, and you find odds of 2.10 (decimal) on that outcome. Using the Kelly formula: b = 1.10, p = 0.55, q = 0.45. f* = (1.10 × 0.55 − 0.45) / 1.10 = (0.605 − 0.45) / 1.10 = 0.155 / 1.10 ≈ 14.1%. Half Kelly = 7.05%, so the recommended stake is €500 × 0.0705 = €35.25.

Limitations of the Kelly Criterion

Kelly only works if your probability estimate is accurate. In practice, punters regularly overestimate their edge, which leads to overbetting and ruin. The formula also assumes bets are independent and that you can place any stake size — neither is fully true in real betting markets. Bookmakers impose maximum stakes, and odds can move before you get on. Use Kelly as a guide, not a guarantee, and always apply a fractional Kelly to protect your bankroll from estimation errors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Kelly Criterion in betting?

The Kelly Criterion is a formula that calculates the optimal fraction of your bankroll to bet based on your estimated edge and the offered odds, maximising long-run bankroll growth while avoiding ruin.

What is Half Kelly?

Betting half the Kelly-recommended stake. It significantly reduces variance and drawdowns while still capturing most of the long-run growth benefit. Most professional bettors use half or quarter Kelly.

What happens if Kelly gives a negative number?

A negative Kelly value means no mathematical edge — the bet has negative expected value at the offered odds. The correct action is not to bet.

Can I use Kelly for accumulator bets?

Kelly is designed for single bets. For accumulators you would need to chain the calculation across each leg, which compounds estimation error. It is best used for single-event wagers.

Is the Kelly Criterion used by professional bettors?

Yes — it is widely used in professional sports betting, trading and investment management. Most practitioners use fractional Kelly (half or quarter) to manage the high variance that comes with full Kelly staking.

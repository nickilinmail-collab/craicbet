Is Gambling Taxed in Ireland? Your 2026 Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 9 min read

The short answer: No. Gambling winnings are NOT taxed in Ireland. Whether you win €10 or €10 million, from slots, sports betting, poker, lottery, or any other form of gambling — you keep 100% of your winnings. No income tax, no capital gains tax, no special gambling tax for players.

This is one of the most frequently asked questions by Irish casino players, and the answer has remained the same for decades. In this guide, we explain exactly why gambling is tax-free in Ireland, how operator taxes work, what professional gamblers need to know, and how Ireland compares with other countries like the UK and the United States.

✅ The Key Fact

In Ireland, gambling winnings are completely tax-free for players. The tax burden falls on the gambling operators (bookmakers and casinos), not on the punters. This applies to all forms of gambling: casino games, sports betting, poker, lottery, and online gambling.

Why Are Gambling Winnings Tax-Free in Ireland?

Ireland's approach to gambling taxation is straightforward and has been consistent for decades: the tax is levied on the operators, not the players. This makes Ireland one of the most player-friendly jurisdictions in the world. Here's the full explanation:

  • Revenue (Irish Tax Office) classification: Gambling winnings are not considered "income" under Irish tax law. They're classified as windfalls — similar to gifts or lottery prizes — and are therefore outside the scope of income tax.
  • No capital gains tax: Since gambling is not considered an "investment activity" (it's entertainment), winnings are not subject to Capital Gains Tax (CGT) either.
  • Operator-side taxation: Instead, the Irish government collects revenue through a betting duty imposed on licensed operators. This ensures the state benefits from gambling revenue without burdening individual players.
  • Historical precedent: Ireland has never taxed gambling winnings. This position has been consistently upheld and is reflected in the Betting Acts and the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

The 2% betting duty is the primary gambling-specific tax. It's calculated on the total amount of bets placed, not on the operator's profit. This means even if a bookmaker has a losing month, they still owe betting duty on all wagers accepted.

Prior to 2015, Irish punters paid a 1% betting tax at the point of sale (deducted from bets placed in betting shops). This was abolished and replaced with the current operator-side model, making Ireland fully tax-free for players at all levels.

Does the Casino Withhold Any Tax?

No. Unlike the United States (where casinos withhold 24% on wins over $5,000), Irish-facing casinos do not withhold any tax from player winnings. When you cash out €10,000 from an online casino, you receive €10,000. No deductions, no forms to fill out, no reporting to Revenue.

The only deductions you'll see are:

  • Withdrawal fees: Some casinos charge a small fee for certain withdrawal methods (e.g., €2.50 for bank transfers). This is a service fee, not a tax.
  • Currency conversion: If playing at a casino in a different currency, your payment provider may charge a conversion fee. Again, not a tax.
  • Payment processor fees: Rare, but some e-wallets charge a percentage fee on gambling transactions. Revolut does not charge for standard casino withdrawals.

⚠️ Crypto Gambling Note

While gambling winnings are tax-free, cryptocurrency gains are subject to Capital Gains Tax (33%) in Ireland. If you deposit euro, gamble in crypto, and withdraw crypto that has increased in value, the increase in crypto value (not the gambling winnings) could theoretically be taxable. In practice, this is rarely enforced for gambling transactions, but it's worth noting. Consult a tax advisor if you gamble significant amounts in cryptocurrency.

What About Professional Gamblers?

This is the most common follow-up question, and the answer is still no tax — with a caveat.

Revenue (the Irish tax authority) does not currently classify gambling as a trade or profession, even if you do it full-time. This means even professional poker players and full-time sports bettors do not pay income tax on their winnings in Ireland.

However, there are edge cases to be aware of:

  • If gambling is your sole income: Revenue could theoretically argue it constitutes a trade, but this has never been successfully enforced in Ireland.
  • Sponsorship and appearance fees: If you earn money from sponsorship deals, streaming, coaching, or writing about gambling, that income is taxable as self-employment income. The gambling winnings remain tax-free; the business activities around gambling are not.
  • Interest on winnings: If you deposit large gambling winnings in a bank account and earn interest, the interest is subject to DIRT (Deposit Interest Retention Tax) at 33%. The original winnings remain untaxed.

US Gambling Tax — A Cautionary Comparison

To appreciate Ireland's position, consider what US players deal with:

  • All gambling winnings are considered taxable income by the IRS, regardless of amount.
  • Casinos are required to withhold 24% federal tax on slot wins over $5,000 and certain other wins.
  • Winners must report all gambling income on their Form 1040 tax return.
  • State taxes apply on top of federal tax — ranging from 0% to 13%+ depending on the state.
  • Professional gamblers face self-employment tax (15.3%) in addition to income tax.
  • Total effective tax rate on US gambling winnings can reach 37%+ federal plus state taxes.

An Irish player winning €100,000 keeps €100,000. An American player winning $100,000 might keep as little as $55,000–$63,000 after taxes. That's a massive difference and one of Ireland's genuinely underappreciated advantages for gamblers.

The GRAI and Future Tax Changes

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI), established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, is now the primary regulator for gambling in Ireland. While the GRAI's focus is on licensing, player protection, and responsible gambling, its establishment raises questions about potential future tax changes.

  • Current position: There are no proposals to introduce a tax on player winnings. The GRAI's focus is on regulation, not taxation.
  • Operator taxation may increase: The 2% betting duty could be revised upward as the GRAI establishes itself and the regulatory framework matures. Some industry experts predict a move towards a Gross Gaming Revenue (GGR) tax model, similar to the UK's 21% GGR tax.
  • Player impact: Even if operator taxes increase, this is unlikely to result in direct taxation of player winnings. Higher operator taxes may, however, lead to less generous bonuses and promotions as operators absorb the additional costs.

Practical Advice for Irish Gamblers

Even though winnings are tax-free, there are sensible steps every Irish gambler should take:

  1. Keep withdrawal records: Download transaction histories from your casino accounts. These prove the source of funds if your bank ever queries a large deposit.
  2. Use a dedicated bank account or e-wallet: Keeping gambling funds separate from your everyday banking makes record-keeping easier and helps with budgeting.
  3. Be aware of AML checks: Casinos are required to verify the source of deposits over certain thresholds. Cooperate with verification requests — it's for your protection as much as theirs.
  4. Understand crypto tax implications: If you gamble with cryptocurrency, be aware that gains on the crypto itself (not the gambling winnings) may be subject to 33% Capital Gains Tax. Keep records of crypto values at the time of deposit and withdrawal.
  5. Consult a tax advisor for large wins: If you win a life-changing amount (€50,000+), it's worth a one-off consultation with a tax advisor to understand any implications for investment income, social welfare, or estate planning.

Do You Need to Declare Gambling Winnings to Revenue?

No. Since gambling winnings are not taxable in Ireland, you do not need to declare them on your annual tax return. There is no reporting requirement for gambling income for individual players.

However, be aware of two practical considerations:

  • Large deposits may trigger bank queries: If you deposit large sums (e.g., €10,000+) into your bank account from gambling, your bank may ask about the source of funds under Anti-Money Laundering (AML) regulations. Keep records of your gambling activity and withdrawal confirmations.
  • Social welfare payments: If you receive social welfare and have significant gambling winnings, the Department of Social Protection may consider this when assessing your means. The winnings themselves aren't taxable, but they could affect means-tested benefits.

What About Inheritance and Gift Tax on Winnings?

If you win a large sum and want to give some to family or friends, be aware of Ireland's Capital Acquisitions Tax (CAT):

  • The gambling win itself: Tax-free. No CAT applies when you receive gambling winnings.
  • Gifting winnings to others: If you gift money from your winnings to family members, the recipient may be liable for CAT (33%) on amounts exceeding their lifetime tax-free threshold. As of 2026, the Group A threshold (parent to child) is approximately €335,000. Group B (siblings, grandparents) is approximately €32,500. Group C (all others) is approximately €16,250.
  • Inheritance: If gambling winnings form part of your estate, they're treated like any other asset for inheritance tax purposes. The winnings were tax-free when you received them, but your heirs may face CAT on their inheritance above the threshold.
  • Small gifts exemption: You can give up to €3,000 per person per year without any CAT implications. This is a useful way to share smaller wins with family.

FAQ — Gambling Tax Ireland

Do I pay tax on online casino winnings in Ireland?▾
No. Online casino winnings are completely tax-free in Ireland. This applies to slots, table games, live dealer games, and all other casino products. You keep 100% of what you win, and you do not need to declare it on your tax return.
Are sports betting winnings taxed in Ireland?▾
No. Sports betting winnings are tax-free for Irish punters. The 2% betting duty is paid by the bookmaker, not the player. Whether you win €10 or €100,000 on a football accumulator, the full amount is yours to keep.
What if I win the lottery — is that taxed?▾
No. National Lottery and EuroMillions prizes are completely tax-free in Ireland, no matter the amount. If you win the €200 million EuroMillions jackpot, you receive the full amount with zero tax deducted. However, any interest earned by investing or saving your winnings is subject to tax.
I gamble full-time — do I need to pay tax as a professional?▾
Under current Irish tax law, gambling is not classified as a trade or profession, so even full-time gamblers do not pay income tax on winnings. However, if you earn money from gambling-related activities (sponsorship, coaching, streaming, writing), that income is taxable as self-employment income. Consult a tax advisor if gambling is your primary income source.
If I win abroad, do I pay tax?▾
It depends on the country's rules. As an Irish tax resident, your gambling winnings are tax-free under Irish law. However, some countries (like the US) withhold tax on gambling winnings at source — meaning the casino deducts tax before paying you. Ireland does not have a tax treaty with the US covering gambling, so you cannot reclaim the withheld US tax. Within the EU, most countries do not tax non-resident gambling winnings.

Common Myths About Gambling Tax in Ireland

There's a lot of misinformation out there. Let's debunk the most common myths:

  • Myth: "You only pay tax on winnings over €10,000." — False. There is no threshold. Gambling winnings are tax-free regardless of the amount. Whether it's €10 or €10 million, you pay nothing.
  • Myth: "Online gambling winnings are taxed differently to betting shop winnings." — False. The tax-free status applies equally to all forms of gambling: online casinos, betting shops, poker rooms, lottery, and any other gambling activity.
  • Myth: "The casino deducts tax before paying you." — False in Ireland. Irish-facing casinos do not withhold tax on player winnings. Note: this may not apply to casinos based in countries with withholding tax (e.g., the US).
  • Myth: "Revenue will investigate you if you win a lot." — Unlikely. Revenue has no reason to investigate tax-free income. However, your bank may query large deposits under AML rules, which is a banking matter, not a tax matter.
  • Myth: "Professional gamblers pay tax." — Currently false under Irish law. Revenue does not classify gambling as a trade. However, income from gambling-adjacent activities (coaching, streaming, sponsorship) is taxable.

Impact on Social Welfare and Benefits

While gambling winnings aren't taxed, they can affect means-tested benefits:

  • Jobseeker's Allowance / Benefit: If you receive social welfare and have significant savings from gambling winnings, these savings may be assessed as part of your "means." The winnings themselves aren't income, but the resulting savings could reduce your payment.
  • Medical Card: Large gambling winnings deposited in your bank account could push your assessed savings above the medical card threshold, potentially affecting your eligibility.
  • HAP / Housing Assistance: Similar to above — accumulated savings from gambling could be assessed as capital and affect housing assistance eligibility.
  • Our advice: If you receive means-tested benefits and have a significant win, consult with a social welfare advisor before depositing the funds. The gambling win is tax-free, but how you hold the money matters for benefits purposes.

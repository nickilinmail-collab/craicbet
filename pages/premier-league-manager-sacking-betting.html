Premier League Manager Sacking Betting 2026/27 — Next Boss to Leave Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

Premier League manager sacking markets have grown in popularity among Irish punters, offering shorter-term action and regular market movement throughout the season. This guide explains how the sacking market works, what drives managerial dismissals, and how to approach this market responsibly.

How Manager Sacking Markets Work

Bookmakers typically run a rolling "next manager to leave" market throughout the season, listing all 20 Premier League managers with odds that shift based on results. Some operators also offer "to be sacked before Christmas" or "to leave before end of season" props on specific managers. Markets update rapidly after poor results, particularly runs of three or more defeats.

Historical Patterns

Premier League sackings cluster around predictable moments: after a run of three or more losses, following a heavy home defeat, or at the international break when clubs can install a new manager and give them time to settle before the next run of fixtures. The first managerial sacking of the season typically occurs between September and November.

Clubs that spend heavily in summer are least tolerant of poor starts — expectations are elevated and board patience is typically shorter when significant money has been invested in the squad.

Key Factors to Watch

  • Board backing statements: Publicly expressed confidence from the chairman often precedes a sacking by days rather than weeks — read these with appropriate scepticism.
  • Squad relationships: Reports of training ground unrest or player-manager friction are historically associated with imminent dismissals.
  • Table position at key dates: Clubs expect certain minimum points tallies at Christmas, the end of January, and the end of March. Missing these benchmarks typically triggers board review.
  • Dressing room leaks: Increased press speculation and player agents speaking to media are frequently precursors to change.

Responsible Betting on This Market

Sacking markets can move extremely quickly — a Saturday evening result can shift prices significantly by Sunday morning. Setting alerts and checking odds before major weekend fixtures is advisable. Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly are sacking odds updated after a result?

Major GRAI-licensed bookmakers update manager sacking markets within hours of a significant result — often within the hour for Premier League weekend games. Following sharp odds movement can indicate where professional money is going, though markets can also overreact to single results.

Are sacking bets void if a manager resigns rather than gets sacked?

Terms vary significantly. Most 'next to leave' markets settle on any departure — resignation, sacking by mutual consent, or dismissal. Some specific 'to be sacked' markets require a dismissal to settle positively. Always check the market terms before placing.

Which months see the most Premier League sackings?

October, November and January are historically the peak months for Premier League managerial changes. October after the first international break, November if the Christmas fixtures look difficult, and January when clubs can recruit a replacement immediately.

Can I bet on the next permanent manager after a sacking?

Yes — 'next manager' markets typically open within hours of a dismissal being confirmed. These markets are highly speculative and prices often reflect press speculation rather than firm information. Prices can move very rapidly as names emerge.

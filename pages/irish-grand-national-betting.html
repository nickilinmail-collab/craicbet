Irish Grand National Betting Guide — Everything You Need to Know

Race: Irish Grand National 2027
Date: Easter Monday, 5 April 2027
Venue: Fairyhouse Racecourse, County Meath
Distance: Approximately 3 miles and 5 furlongs (~3½ miles)
Fences: 30+ fences
Type: Handicap steeplechase (National Hunt)

The Irish Grand National is one of the oldest and most beloved events in Irish racing. Run on Easter Monday at Fairyhouse Racecourse in County Meath, it is Ireland's most prestigious steeplechase — a gruelling test over approximately 3½ miles and 30+ fences. For punters, it is also one of the year's most fascinating betting races: a large field, a handicap format and a long trip combine to make it genuinely competitive and hard to predict, which is exactly what makes each-way betting so popular here.

Race Overview and History

The Irish Grand National has been run at Fairyhouse on Easter Monday for well over a century, making it one of the fixtures most embedded in the Irish sporting calendar. The Easter Monday date is a tradition the race has maintained consistently — it brings large crowds to Fairyhouse and is a central event of the Easter weekend horse racing schedule in Ireland.

The race is run as a handicap, meaning each horse carries a weight assigned by the official handicapper to theoretically equalise their chances. Fields of 20–30 runners are typical, though the maximum is set by the race conditions. The combination of weight, distance and fences means that jumping ability and stamina are as important as pure speed — the winner must be a complete staying chaser.

Why Each-Way Betting Suits the Irish Grand National

With 20–30 runners in a 3½-mile handicap over fences, selecting the outright winner is very difficult. Even seasoned judges regularly get the race wrong. Each-way betting — especially with 4 places paid at 1/4 odds — offers a way to get a return if your selection runs well but falls just short of winning.

Worked example — Irish Grand National each-way:
You back a horse each-way at 16/1, 1/4 odds, 4 places.
Stake: €10 each-way = €20 total (€10 win + €10 place)

If the horse WINS:
Win part: €10 × 17 = €170
Place part: €10 × (16 ÷ 4 + 1) = €10 × 5 = €50
Total return: €220 (profit: €200)

If the horse PLACES (2nd, 3rd or 4th) but doesn't win:
Win part: €0 (lost)
Place part: €10 × 5 = €50
Total return: €50 (profit: €30 after the €20 total cost)

If the horse is UNPLACED:
Total return: €0 (€20 cost lost)

Ante-Post vs Race-Day Strategy

Ante-post markets for the Irish Grand National open many months before the race — sometimes as early as the previous summer. The appeal of ante-post betting is that early prices are typically more generous than what's available close to the race, particularly if a horse performs well over the winter and spring season.

The main risk with ante-post bets is non-runners: if your selection doesn't make it to the race, most standard ante-post bets are lost. Some GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer specific "non-runner no bet" terms on selected ante-post markets for the Irish Grand National — worth looking for if you want ante-post value with a safety net.

Race-day bets are safer (you can see the confirmed runners and weights) but prices are shorter. Many Irish punters compromise: take a small ante-post position early if they spot value, and supplement it with a race-day each-way bet once the field is confirmed.

Factors to Consider (General Approach)

These are broad considerations for how to approach the race — not specific tips, and no outcome is guaranteed:

  • Handicap weight: Lower-weighted horses have an objective advantage in terms of burden, but are often in that position because the handicapper regards them as less capable. The sweet spot is often horses rated in the mid-range of the field.
  • Trainer form in the spring: Willie Mullins and Gordon Elliott have trained many Irish Grand National winners. Their declarations and stable form at Easter are worth monitoring via Racing Post.
  • Going conditions: Fairyhouse in April can be soft or good depending on the spring. Ground conditions affect the type of horse suited — stamina becomes even more crucial in soft going.
  • Cheltenham form: The Cheltenham Festival in March directly precedes the Irish Grand National. Horses that ran well at Cheltenham but didn't win sometimes bounce back at Fairyhouse, though some need a rest and are not entered.
  • Jumping reliability: Over 30+ fences, clean jumping is essential. Horses with a good jumping record at the top level are worth prioritising over flat-track specialists.

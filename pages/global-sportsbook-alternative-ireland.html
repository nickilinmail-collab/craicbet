The UK High-Street Bookmakers in the Irish Market

The names William Hill, Coral, and Ladbrokes are part of Irish betting consciousness in a way that few other brands are. These three brands, now all operating under the umbrella of Entain plc (formed through the merger of GVC Holdings and Ladbrokes Coral in 2018), have a combined history in British and Irish betting stretching back over a century. William Hill was founded in 1934; Ladbrokes in 1902; Coral in 1926.

In Ireland, the Entain brands operate retail betting shops — you will see William Hill and Ladbrokes shops on Irish high streets, particularly in urban areas — and they also operate online platforms targeting Irish bettors. For much of the past two decades, the online arms of these businesses operated under their UKGC licences and their Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) licences, which provided the primary regulatory framework for Irish players.

That picture changed significantly on 1 July 2026 with the implementation of Ireland’s GRAI remote betting licensing regime.

What Changed on 1 July 2026 for UK Brands Operating in Ireland

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as Ireland’s dedicated gambling regulator. The first remote betting licences were issued beginning 1 July 2026. The critical point for Irish bettors: any operator targeting Irish customers online — regardless of whether it also operates in the UK, Malta, or anywhere else — must hold a GRAI remote betting licence to legally serve the Irish market.

A UKGC licence, an MGA licence, or a Gibraltar licence does not substitute for a GRAI licence for the purposes of serving Irish customers. The GRAI has jurisdiction over online gambling offered to persons in Ireland under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Operators without a GRAI licence are not authorised to target Irish customers under the new framework, regardless of their other regulatory standing.

This creates a meaningful distinction between:

  • A UK high-street bookmaker that has applied for and been granted a GRAI remote betting licence for the Irish online market
  • A UK high-street bookmaker that continues to operate under its UKGC or MGA licence without seeking GRAI authorisation for Ireland

The first scenario means the brand is fully compliant under Irish law and subject to GRAI oversight. The second means Irish players using that brand online would not receive Irish regulatory protections, even if the brand is entirely legitimate in its licensed UK market.

How to Check Whether a UK Brand Holds a GRAI Licence

The GRAI maintains a public register of licensed remote betting operators at grai.ie. This is the definitive, authoritative source. CraicBet’s own guide at check-bookmaker-grai-licence walks through the process in under a minute.

Important: within a corporate group like Entain, individual brands may hold GRAI licences separately. William Hill Ireland, Coral Ireland, and Ladbrokes Ireland are legally distinct businesses under the Entain group umbrella. The fact that one Entain brand holds a GRAI licence does not automatically mean all Entain brands do. Check each brand you intend to use individually against the GRAI register.

When you find an operator on the GRAI register, its licence reference will be in the format GRAI-XXXX-RB-26-XXXX. The “RB” stands for Remote Betting. This confirms it is a remote betting licence — the type required for online sports betting.

⚠️ Licence Statuses Change

This page reflects the position as of November 2026, but the GRAI continues to process licence applications and may grant or modify licences after this date. Always verify at the official GRAI register before depositing.

What Irish Bettors Should Look for Beyond GRAI Compliance

Once GRAI licensing is confirmed, the commercial comparison between UK-heritage bookmakers and their alternatives involves familiar factors: odds quality, market depth, bonus terms, payment methods, and mobile app experience. Some points specific to UK-heritage brands in Ireland:

Horse racing depth. UK-heritage bookmakers have historically been strong on UK racing — Cheltenham, Ascot, Royal Ascot, Newmarket. Their coverage of Irish racing (the Curragh, Leopardstown, Galway, Fairyhouse, Punchestown) is generally good but sometimes secondary to UK coverage in terms of best odds guaranteed availability, early prices, and place terms. Irish-focused competitors may price Irish racing more sharply.

GAA coverage. GAA betting is a distinctly Irish market. UK-heritage brands offer GAA markets but may not dedicate the same resources to pricing and in-play GAA markets as operators built specifically for the Irish market. Assess coverage of the All-Ireland championships, provincial championships, and club competitions if GAA betting is important to you.

Payment methods for Irish customers. Revolut has become a dominant payment method in Ireland. Some UK-heritage brands have been slower to integrate Revolut than newer market entrants. Check which payment methods are supported for both deposits and withdrawals before opening an account.

Withdrawal speeds. GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines set a floor for all GRAI-licensed operators. Within that framework, however, withdrawal processing speeds vary. If rapid access to winnings matters to you, check independent reviews of the specific brand’s withdrawal performance for Irish customers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do UK high-street bookmakers like William Hill, Coral and Ladbrokes operate in Ireland?▾
William Hill, Coral, and Ladbrokes are all part of Entain plc and operate retail betting shops in Ireland as well as online platforms targeting Irish customers. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, any operator targeting Irish customers online since 1 July 2026 must hold a GRAI remote betting licence. Whether each specific Entain brand holds a GRAI licence should be verified at grai.ie, as licence status can differ between brands within the same corporate group.
Does a UKGC licence cover Irish customers?▾
No. The UKGC licence covers operators targeting UK customers. Since the implementation of Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024, a UKGC licence does not authorise an operator to target Irish customers. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence. A UKGC-licensed operator without a GRAI licence is not bound by Irish-specific requirements including NGEI exclusion integration, deposit limit enforcement under Irish regulations, or the formal Irish complaints route via the GRAI.
What should Irish bettors check before using a UK-based bookmaker online?▾
Irish bettors should: (1) Check the GRAI public register at grai.ie to confirm the specific brand holds a current GRAI remote betting licence. (2) Note whether the licence is in the name of the specific brand they intend to use. (3) Set deposit limits before placing their first bet. (4) Confirm their preferred payment method is supported. (5) Be aware that credit card use for gambling is banned for GRAI-licensed operators under Irish law.
How do UK bookmaker retail shops in Ireland fit under the new GRAI regime?▾
GRAI licensing for remote (online) betting and retail (in-person) betting are separate regulatory matters. The GRAI remote betting licensing regime covers online and mobile betting. Retail betting shops in Ireland operate under existing Irish retail betting licences. When you bet online with a UK brand’s Irish website or app, the relevant licence is the GRAI remote betting licence, not the retail licence.

Relegation Outright Betting Ireland 2026/27: Teams to Go Down

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

Relegation outright betting is one of the most underexplored markets in European football. While the title race captures most of the outright market attention, the battle against the drop is often more genuinely uncertain and can offer better value — particularly in leagues where detailed knowledge of promoted clubs is not fully reflected in the opening prices. This guide explains how relegation markets work, what the honest maths looks like, and how to approach them systematically.

How Relegation Outright Markets Are Structured

A relegation outright bet is a bet on a named club to finish in the bottom three (or relevant number) at the end of the season. The market typically lists all clubs in the division, with implied probabilities that add up to well over 100% — that overage is the bookmaker's margin. For relegation markets, that margin is typically higher than for title winner markets, often 10–20%, because the field is wider and the probability is spread across more clubs with genuine uncertainty.

Many bookmakers also offer each-way terms on relegation, meaning a club that finishes in the danger zone but stays up through a playoff still generates a partial return. These terms vary by bookmaker and by league, so compare carefully before placing.

Promoted Clubs: The Structural Case for Relegation Betting

Newly promoted clubs are statistically the most frequent relegation candidates across all major European leagues. The gap in squad quality and tactical preparation between the Championship (or its equivalent) and the top division is significant, and most promoted clubs spend their first season fighting to survive. The market knows this — promoted clubs almost always open as favourites for relegation — but the market is not always correctly priced on which promoted club is most vulnerable.

The best-value relegation bets tend to be on promoted clubs that the market has underestimated. This can happen when: a promoted club has made significant summer investment that is not fully reflected in pre-season pricing; a promoted club's league record is misleadingly favourable because of a soft second-division draw; or a club that escaped relegation last season has been significantly weakened in the summer window.

Relegation Markets by League: What to Look For

Each major league has different relegation dynamics. The Premier League relegation market is the most liquid and competitive, with the greatest public money, which means prices on the obvious candidates can be tighter. La Liga and Serie A both relegate three clubs per season, and their lower levels of Irish betting turnover mean slightly wider markets with occasionally better prices on less-noticed candidates. The Bundesliga includes a relegation playoff, which introduces an additional outcome (playoff relegation) that affects how the bottom-of-table prices are set.

The Scottish Premiership also operates a playoff, with the 11th-placed side playing against the third-placed Championship club. This creates a specific secondary bet — to-be-relegated-via-playoff — that can be interesting on clubs that consistently hover in that danger zone.

Mid-Season Relegation Betting: When Prices Move

Relegation markets move dramatically mid-season. A club that starts the season as a mild survival candidate can be odds-on for the drop after a run of eight defeats. Conversely, a club priced as the most likely to be relegated can recover form and move out of the market quickly. The best mid-season opportunities are on clubs where the market has overreacted to a short-term run of results, without the underlying squad quality changing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do relegation outright bets work?

A relegation outright wins if the named club finishes in the bottom three and is relegated at the end of the season. Most bookmakers offer each-way markets on relegation too.

Are newly promoted clubs the best relegation bets?

Promoted clubs are the most common relegation candidates and are priced accordingly. The best value is often on promoted clubs whose squad investment or quality is underestimated by the market.

What margin do bookmakers take on relegation outrights?

Typically 10–20%, higher than match-odds markets due to the wider field and more complex uncertainty.

Can you bet on a team to avoid relegation?

Yes. Survival markets are available alongside relegation outrights, though prices on obvious survival favourites are very short.

Value Spots in Relegation Betting

  • Newly promoted teams: Championship sides promoted via play-off have higher relegation rates (~45%)
  • Manager instability: Teams entering the season with a new manager and new squad often underperform early
  • Fixture pile-ups: Teams also in cup competitions with small squads are higher risk
  • Historical patterns: In the Premier League, 1 of the 3 relegated teams is usually a newly promoted side
  • In-play value: After a 5-game losing run, prices shorten significantly — early season lays can be cashed out

Even Money Betting: What 1/1 Odds Really Mean

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 4 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

What Is Even Money?

Even money (1/1 in fractional, 2.0 in decimal) is the benchmark odds where your profit equals your stake if you win. A €10 bet at even money returns €20 if successful: €10 profit + €10 stake back. The name reflects this 1:1 ratio of profit to stake. Even money implies the market believes the selection has roughly a 50% chance of winning (the exact implied probability depends on the bookmaker margin applied).

Decimal vs Fractional Even Money

Even money is 1/1 in fractional odds and 2.0 in decimal odds. These are identical. Many Irish bookmakers display both formats and you can switch between them in your account settings. When comparing prices across bookmakers, always use decimal odds to avoid confusion: 10/11 is decimal 1.909, which is below even money despite looking superficially similar. The ‘best price’ is always the highest decimal number.

The Bookmaker's Edge at Even Money

In a true 50/50 market, both sides should be priced at 2.0 (evens). Bookmakers price each side slightly below true evens to build in their margin. A football 1X2 market at approximately 10% margin might price a coin-flip match at 1.91 on each side: the implied probability of each is 52.4% despite neither side being more likely. Over many even-money bets, this margin — about 4.5% per bet at 1.91 — erodes the bettor’s bankroll. The closer the price is to 2.0, the lower the margin applied.

When Even Money Bets Have Value

An even money bet has value when you believe the actual probability of your selection winning is above 50%. In practice, assessing whether a selection has >50% probability requires analytical work: form data, model outputs, comparative market analysis. Frequently Asked Questions

What does 1/1 mean in betting?

1/1 is fractional odds meaning you win €1 for every €1 staked. Also called evens or even money. The decimal equivalent is 2.0.

Is 10/11 the same as even money?

No. 10/11 is decimal 1.909, which is slightly below even money. You receive less than your stake as profit: a €11 win returns €21, not €22. It is close to evens but strictly below.

Why are both sides of a 50/50 bet usually priced below evens?

Bookmakers apply a margin to both sides. On a 50/50 market with a 5% margin, each side might be priced at 1.95 rather than 2.0, so both sides are marginally below true fair value.

When Bookmakers Offer Even Money

  • Even money typically means the bookmaker considers both outcomes roughly equal
  • True 50/50 probability at even money includes the bookmaker's margin – real probability is slightly below 50%
  • Coin flip bets (e.g. penalty shoot-out first scorer) may be priced at 10/11 or 5/6 to include margin
  • Betting just above even money (e.g. 11/10) can offer value if your assessed probability is above 50%

When Does Even Money Represent Value?

Even money (2.00 decimal) means the bookmaker is implying a 50% chance. After margin, the true implied probability is higher — typically 52–55%. To have value betting at evens, the event must have a greater than 50% chance of occurring. Consider:

  • Strong favourites at short odds — evens on a heavy favourite after line movement may represent genuine value
  • Exchange prices at 2.00 (Betfair etc.) carry no bookmaker margin — exact fair value at the market price
  • Avoid impulse evens bets on coin-flip events where you have no informational edge
  • Use the Kelly Criterion: bet a fraction only when you believe the probability exceeds 50% clearly

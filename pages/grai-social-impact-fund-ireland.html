GRAI Social Impact Fund Ireland — What It Is and Who Pays

One of the most significant new requirements in the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is Ireland's Social Impact Fund: a mandatory levy on all GRAI-licensed gambling operators that funds gambling harm research, treatment, and public awareness in Ireland.

This fund is a direct consequence of licensing — and one reason why the GRAI's enforcement against unlicensed operators matters beyond just consumer protection.

What Is the Social Impact Fund?

The Social Impact Fund is a ring-fenced fund financed by mandatory contributions from GRAI-licensed operators. It is designed to ensure that the gambling industry — not taxpayers — funds the services that address the harms gambling can cause.

This model follows similar funds in other regulated gambling markets, such as the UK's GambleAware funding model and Australia's mandatory treatment levies, but is administered directly under Irish law through the GRAI.

Who Pays Into the Fund?

All categories of GRAI-licensed operators must contribute:

  • Remote betting operators (online bookmakers) — licensed from 1 July 2026
  • In-person betting operators (betting shops) — licensed from 1 December 2026
  • Remote gaming operators (online casinos) — expected to be licensed from 2027
  • Lottery operators — covered under the Act

Unlicensed offshore operators that serve Irish customers without a GRAI licence do not contribute to the fund — a key reason the GRAI actively pursues unlicensed operators.

What Does the Fund Pay For?

  • Treatment services: Funding for problem gambling counselling and residential treatment
  • Research: Studies into gambling behaviour, harm rates, and the effectiveness of interventions in Ireland
  • Public awareness campaigns: National campaigns about responsible gambling and the risks of problem gambling
  • Support for affected families: Services for family members of people with gambling problems
  • Youth protection: Education and awareness programmes targeted at young people

Why This Matters for Irish Players

When you use a GRAI-licensed betting operator, a portion of the operator's revenue goes directly to fund gambling harm services in Ireland. When you use an unlicensed offshore site, nothing goes to the fund — and you also lose the other consumer protections that GRAI licensing provides.

📊 Ireland's gambling market context: Ireland's gross gambling revenue for Q1 2026 alone was estimated at €911.2 million — a significant market from which the Social Impact Fund levy will generate meaningful resources for gambling harm services.

How Is the Fund Different from Gambling Tax?

Ireland already levies betting duty on gambling turnover, which goes to general government revenue. The Social Impact Fund is entirely separate — it is a ring-fenced levy specifically for gambling harm purposes, administered by the GRAI, not the Revenue Commissioners. Licensed operators pay both betting duty and Social Impact Fund contributions.

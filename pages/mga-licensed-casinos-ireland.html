What Is the Malta Gaming Authority?

The Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) is a European Union gambling regulator operating under Maltese law. It has regulated online gambling since 2004, making it one of the longest-established licensing bodies in the world. The MGA is widely regarded as among the most rigorous regulators for online casinos — a reputation it has built through strict compliance audits, mandatory player fund segregation, and enforceable standards for game fairness.

An MGA licence is not simply a rubber stamp. Operators must pass a detailed application process covering company ownership, software audits, anti-money laundering procedures, and responsible gambling frameworks. They are subject to ongoing inspections and can have their licences suspended or revoked for non-compliance. The MGA also publishes its register of active licences, which you can check for free at mga.org.mt.

For Irish players in 2026, the MGA licence is the most meaningful player protection currently available from an overseas regulator. It is what we look for first when evaluating any casino recommended on this site.

Why MGA While GRAI Casino Licences Are Pending

As of November 2026, GRAI has issued zero online casino licences. The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland began granting remote betting licences from 1 July 2026, but casino (gaming) licences are a separate category and are expected to become available between 2027 and 2028.

Until GRAI begins licensing casinos, the choice of foreign licence is what determines how well you are protected. This is not an obscure technicality — it is the practical difference between having an independent disputes body you can escalate a complaint to, versus having no meaningful recourse if a casino refuses to pay you out.

MGA-licensed casinos are required to offer independent Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) services. This means if you have a dispute about a withdrawal, bonus terms, or account closure, you have an escalation path beyond the casino itself. Curaçao and Anjouan licences do not carry the same requirement, leaving players with far fewer options when things go wrong.

How to Check an MGA Licence

Verifying an MGA licence takes under two minutes. Here is how:

  1. Scroll to the footer of the casino website and look for the MGA logo or a licence number beginning with MGA/.
  2. Go to mga.org.mt and navigate to the licence checker or the public register.
  3. Enter the licence number. The result should show the company name, the licence type, and the status (Active/Suspended/Cancelled).
  4. If the number does not appear, or the casino name does not match the registered entity, the licence claim is false.

Some casinos display MGA logos as decoration without holding a valid licence. The 60-second check above is the only way to be certain. See our longer licence verification guide for more detail on each jurisdiction.

Is Arbitrage Betting Legal in Ireland

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Arbitrage betting — arbing — is legal in Ireland. It involves placing bets on all outcomes of an event across different bookmakers where the combined odds guarantee a profit regardless of result. No Irish law prohibits it, but bookmakers routinely detect and restrict arbers.

How Arbitrage Betting Works

Bookmaker A offers Home Win at 2.20. Bookmaker B offers Away Win at 2.20. If you calculate the combined implied probability: (1/2.20) + (1/2.20) = 0.455 + 0.455 = 0.909 (90.9%). Because the combined percentage is below 100%, there is a 9.1% arbitrage margin — meaning guaranteed profit regardless of which team wins, by splitting stakes proportionally.

Why Bookmakers Ban Arbers

Arbers are unprofitable customers for bookmakers. Bookmakers use software to flag accounts placing bets only on high-value or discrepant odds, often at maximum stake. Once flagged, accounts are typically restricted to very low stakes or closed entirely. GRAI-licensed bookmakers have the right to close accounts and refuse service under their terms and conditions.

Realistic expectation: arbing is a short-term strategy. Most serious arbers burn through new account bonuses quickly and exhaust available accounts within months.

Is arbitrage betting legal in Ireland 2026

Yes. Arbitrage betting (arbing) is completely legal in Ireland. No Irish law prohibits profiting from differences in odds between bookmakers. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI regulations do not restrict arbitrage betting as a practice. You are simply placing bets at different bookmakers at the same time — each bet is a standard, legal bet.

Can bookmakers close your account for arbitrage in Ireland

Yes, and they frequently do. While arbing is legal, bookmakers in Ireland can close or restrict accounts under their own terms and conditions without providing a reason. Operators are private businesses and can decline to offer their service to any customer. Arbers are often identified through betting patterns (always backing the highest available price, staking to identical margins across multiple operators, quick bet acceptance without shopping around).

Is arbing the same as matched betting?

They are related but different. Matched betting uses free bets to extract guaranteed profit from promotions. Arbing uses differing odds across bookmakers, with real money at stake on both sides. Both are legal; both risk account restrictions.

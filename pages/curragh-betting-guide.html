The Curragh Racecourse Betting Guide — Ireland's Home of Flat Racing

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

The Curragh is Ireland's premier flat racecourse, home to all five Irish Classic races and multiple Group 1 events throughout the flat season (April to October). Situated in County Kildare, it is the heartland of Irish thoroughbred breeding and training, with many of the world's leading horses competing here each summer. This guide covers The Curragh's betting character, key meetings and what Irish punters should know.

The Curragh's Race Calendar: Key Meetings

The Curragh hosts its biggest meetings between May and September:

  • Irish 2,000 Guineas (May) — Irish Classic for 3-year-old colts; first of the five Irish Classics.
  • Irish 1,000 Guineas (May) — Irish Classic for 3-year-old fillies.
  • Irish Derby (June) — The centrepiece of the Irish flat season; Group 1 for 3-year-olds over 1m 4f.
  • Irish Oaks (July) — Classic for 3-year-old fillies; 1m 4f.
  • Irish St Leger (September) — Stayers' Classic; 1m 6f.
  • Keeneland Irish Champion Stakes (September) — open Group 1 over 1m 2f; often attracts international fields.

Track Characteristics and Draw Bias

The Curragh is a wide, flat, right-handed course. At straight course distances (5f–7f), the draw can be significant in soft going — high draws have tended to perform better in certain ground conditions. At distances over 1 mile, the draw is less important as horses have time to find their position. The wide track generally favours prominent runners and those who travel smoothly through a race.

Betting at the Classics

Irish Classic races are conditions races (not handicaps) run with relatively small fields (8–14 runners typically). Each-way terms are usually 3 places at 1/4 odds. Because the fields are small and the form hierarchy for 3-year-olds changes rapidly through the season, ante-post markets open wide in winter and compress quickly once early season trials are run.

The English Classics (at Newmarket, Epsom, Ascot, Doncaster) are powerful form references. Horses with strong British Classic form often dominate the Irish equivalents — but the extra week of freshness sometimes gives Irish-trained horses a stamina advantage in the summer heat.

Each-Way Strategy at The Curragh

For Group 1 and Group 2 races with 8+ runners, standard terms are 3 places at 1/4 odds. For handicaps with larger fields (20+), terms extend to 5 or 6 places. Frequently Asked Questions

Where is The Curragh Racecourse?

The Curragh Racecourse is located in County Kildare, approximately 50km south-west of Dublin. It is Ireland's principal flat racing course and home to all five Irish Classic races.

Does The Curragh have a draw bias?

At straight course distances (5f–7f) in soft going, high draws have historically shown an advantage at The Curragh. At distances over 1 mile, the draw effect is minimal as horses settle early. Conditions on the day affect this — always check recent race results at the specific distance and going conditions.

What each-way terms are offered at The Curragh?

For Group 1 and Group 2 races with 8+ runners, standard terms are 3 places at 1/4 odds. For handicaps with larger fields, terms extend to 5 or more places. Always confirm terms before placing.

Which are the five Irish Classic races at The Curragh?

The five Irish Classics are: the Irish 2,000 Guineas, the Irish 1,000 Guineas, the Irish Derby, the Irish Oaks and the Irish St Leger — all run at The Curragh between May and September.

Accumulator Betting: The Honest Maths

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

The accumulator is Ireland’s favourite bet: a few selections, a small stake, a big number at the bottom of the slip. It is also the single most profitable product bookmakers sell, and the reason is pure arithmetic. This page shows exactly how the maths works — how the odds multiply, how the house edge multiplies with them, and the narrow set of cases where an acca still makes sense.

How acca odds are calculated

An accumulator multiplies the decimal odds of every leg. Four selections at 1.50, 1.80, 1.66 and 2.00 give 1.50 × 1.80 × 1.66 × 2.00 = 8.96 — a €10 stake returns €89.60 if all four win. One loser and the whole bet loses. That multiplication is also why the bet feels so attractive: modest prices compound into a headline figure no single bet offers.

The margin compounds too

Here is the part the adverts never show. Every leg’s price includes the bookmaker’s margin — say 5% on a typical football match market. On a single, you pay that 5% once. On an acca, the margin multiplies along with the odds: with 5% taken per leg, a double gives you about 90% of fair value, a treble about 86%, a 5-fold roughly 77%. In plain terms, a 5-fold built from 5%-margin markets carries a combined house edge of about 23% — several times dearer than a single, and in lottery territory. The same stake spread across five singles faces one-fifth of that drag.

A worked example

Suppose each of five legs has a true chance of 60% and is priced at 1.59 (fair odds 1.67 minus a 5% margin). The chance all five win is 0.65 = 7.8%. Fair odds for that are about 12.9; the acca pays 1.595 ≈ 10.2. You are being paid 10.2 for a 12.9 shot — around 21–22% of your expected return gone before kick-off. No staking plan, form study or gut feel recovers an edge that size.

Why bookmakers promote accas so hard

Free-bet offers, acca insurance, boosted trebles and “pick your five” adverts all point at multiples because multiples convert the shop’s tightest prices into its widest-margin product. Acca insurance — stake back as a free bet if one leg lets you down — is worth far less than it sounds: a free bet returns winnings only, and the offer usually requires minimum odds per leg that push you into bets you would not otherwise place.

When an acca makes sense

Honestly: as entertainment, deliberately priced as entertainment. A small, fixed stake on a weekend acca buys an evening’s interest across four matches for the price of a pint, and the lottery-style payoff is the product you are buying. What the maths rules out is accas as a serious attempt to grow a bankroll — for that, singles on low-margin markets are the only defensible version. Frequently Asked Questions

How is an accumulator's payout calculated?

Multiply the decimal odds of every selection together, then multiply by your stake. Four legs at 1.50, 1.80, 1.66 and 2.00 give combined odds of 8.96, so €10 returns €89.60 if every leg wins.

What is the real house edge on a five-fold acca?

If each leg carries a typical 5% margin, the edges compound to roughly 23% across five legs — several times the cost of betting the same matches as singles.

Is acca insurance worth it?

Less than it sounds. The refund is usually a free bet (winnings only, not the stake), capped, and conditional on minimum odds per leg. It softens one specific losing pattern while the compounded margin stays untouched.

How Does an Accumulator Work in Ireland

An accumulator (acca) combines multiple selections into one bet. All selections must win for the bet to pay out. The odds are multiplied together — a 4-fold acca at 2.0 / 2.5 / 3.0 / 1.8 pays: 2.0 × 2.5 × 3.0 × 1.8 = 27.0 (a €10 stake returns €270). This multiplication effect is why accas are popular despite having worse expected value than singles.

What Happens If One Leg of My Acca Is Voided Ireland

If one selection in your accumulator is voided — for example, a horse is a non-runner, or a match is abandoned — that leg is removed from the bet and the remaining legs carry on as a smaller accumulator. A 5-fold acca with one void becomes a 4-fold. Your stake does not change and the bet continues. The same rule applies to draws in DNB (Draw No Bet) markets included in an acca: a draw refunds that leg and the acca continues at reduced legs.

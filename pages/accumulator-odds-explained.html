Accumulator Odds Explained: How Your Acca Return Is Calculated

📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

Accumulators are the most popular bet type on Irish football. The appeal is obvious: combine several selections and a small stake can return a large amount. But many punters don't fully understand how the return is calculated, how often accas actually win, or why the bookmaker's margin makes long accas increasingly unfavourable. This guide covers all three honestly.

How Accumulator Odds Are Multiplied

An accumulator return is calculated by multiplying together the decimal odds of every selection, then multiplying that combined figure by your stake.

Worked example — 4-fold accumulator:
Selection A: 2.00 (evens) | Selection B: 1.80 | Selection C: 2.50 | Selection D: 3.00

Combined odds: 2.00 × 1.80 × 2.50 × 3.00 = 27.00
Stake: €10
Potential return: €270.00 (profit: €260.00)

The maths is exactly this simple. Use the Acca Calculator to do it instantly — enter each selection's decimal odds and your stake, and add legs to see how each one multiplies the combined figure.

One important point: the return includes your original stake. "Return" and "profit" are different. In the example above, the €270 return includes the €10 stake — the actual profit is €260.

The Real Probability of an Acca Winning

To understand the true probability of an acca winning, convert each selection's true odds to a probability and multiply those probabilities together.

If a football match has a true probability of a home win at 50% (0.50), and you pick the home team in four separate matches all with 50% true probability, the probability of all four winning is:

0.50 × 0.50 × 0.50 × 0.50 = 0.0625 = 6.25%

That means in 100 identical bets, you'd expect to win approximately 6 or 7 of them — and lose the other 93 or 94. The large return when it comes in partially compensates for this, but only if the odds offered are fair (no margin). Which brings us to the most important part.

How the Bookmaker Margin Compounds

Every market a bookmaker prices contains a built-in margin — the overround — that shifts the implied probabilities above 100%. A match market priced at 50% home / 25% draw / 30% away actually sums to 105%, meaning the bookmaker takes a 5% margin on each selection.

When you build an accumulator, this margin compounds across every leg. Using the Bookmaker Margin Calculator, you can verify the margin on each individual market. For a rough illustration of compounding:

Acca Legs Per-Leg Margin Approximate Total Margin
1 (single) 5% 5%
2 5% ~10%
4 5% ~19%
6 5% ~26%
10 5% ~40%

These figures are approximations. The key insight: a 10-leg acca at 5% margin per leg sees roughly 40% of its expected return eroded by the bookmaker's edge. The odds look large and exciting, but they are systematically less than the true probability of winning would justify.

When Accas Actually Make Sense

Accumulators are not mathematically irrational — but their value depends on context. Here are the situations where an acca is a reasonable choice:

Entertainment value is part of the equation. An acca gives you a reason to watch several matches at once. If you're betting for entertainment with a small stake you're comfortable losing, an acca extends the experience.

You have a genuine edge on each selection. If you believe you've identified value in each leg — odds that underestimate the true probability of that outcome — then compounding that edge across legs can amplify your advantage. This requires discipline and a genuine, well-evidenced belief in each selection, not just picking favourites.

Short accas with strong selections. A two or three-leg acca compounds the bookmaker's margin far less than a ten-leg bet. If you have high confidence in two or three outcomes, a short acca is a more defensible bet than a 10-fold with uncertain legs added to chase bigger odds.

Acca insurance promotions. Some bookmakers refund your stake if one leg of a 5+ selection acca lets you down. This effectively reduces the break-even requirement and can make the bet positive expected value in some cases — but always read the terms. Many acca insurance offers pay in free bets (stake-not-returned) rather than cash, which halves their practical value.

Frequently Asked Questions

How are accumulator odds calculated?
Accumulator odds are calculated by multiplying together the decimal odds of every selection. For example, three selections at 2.0, 2.5 and 3.0 produce combined decimal odds of 2.0 × 2.5 × 3.0 = 15.0. A €10 stake returns €150.
Why do accas lose more often than expected?
Because the bookmaker's margin compounds across every leg. If each selection carries a 5% margin, a 5-leg acca carries roughly a 23% compounded margin on the full bet. The true probability of all legs winning is higher than the implied probability the bookmaker's odds suggest, but you're paid the lower implied figure.
Are acca bonuses worth taking?
Acca bonuses (extra percentage added to winnings for 5+ leg accumulators) can partially offset the compounded margin, but rarely eliminate it entirely. The maths depends on the bonus percentage and how many legs are in the acca. Use the Acca Calculator to work out the return with and without the bonus before deciding.

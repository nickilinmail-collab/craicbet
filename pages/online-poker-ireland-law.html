Is Online Poker Legal in Ireland? The GRAI Era

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 3 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

Online poker sits in a strange position in Ireland right now: fully legal and widely played today, yet facing a regulatory question mark large enough that the game's future here is a live debate. Here is where things stand — written as of November 2026, on a topic that is still moving.

The position today: legal and open

Irish players can sign up and play on international poker sites without breaking any law. The market has operated openly for two decades, and poker winnings are not taxed in Ireland — gambling winnings generally fall outside income tax for recreational players. Nothing about the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 changed that overnight: the new regime is being switched on in stages, and online betting licensing only began in mid-2026.

The looming problem: stake and win limits

The Act as written contains maximum relevant payment and winning limits for gaming — widely reported as a €10 maximum stake and €3,000 maximum win. Applied literally to online poker, those numbers would make the regulated game unviable: tournament poker routinely has buy-ins above €10 and prizes far beyond €3,000, and cash-game pots blow through both figures as a matter of course. Industry commentators have described the limits, as drafted, as effectively a death knell for licensed online poker in Ireland.

Section 83: the escape hatch

The same Act contains its own fix. Section 83 allows the regulator, the GRAI, to vary those limits with the consent of the Minister for Justice. In other words, the €10/€3,000 figures are a default, not a destiny — the question is whether and when the power is used, and for which games. Poker advocates argue the limits were designed with slots-style gaming in mind and apply awkwardly to a peer-to-peer skill game; that argument is before the regulator, not settled law.

The consultation and the signals

The GRAI ran a consultation process touching gaming rules in August 2026 — one that drew criticism in poker circles for a very short response window. At least one major international poker operator has reportedly expressed interest in applying for an Irish licence, which suggests the industry expects a workable regime to emerge. None of this is guaranteed: gaming licensing itself is not expected before 2027–2028, so the practical status quo — an open international market — continues in the meantime.

What it means for an Irish player right now

Today, nothing changes: playing online poker remains legal, winnings remain untaxed, and no Irish licence yet exists for a poker site to hold — the GRAI has so far issued remote betting licences only. What is worth watching is the shape of gaming licensing when it arrives: if the limits are varied for poker, Ireland gets a regulated market with real player protections; if they are not, licensed poker may simply never launch here and the offshore status quo persists. Treat every date and figure in this debate as provisional — this page will age, and the regulator's own publications are the final word.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is online poker legal in Ireland in 2026?

Yes. Irish players can legally play on international poker sites, and recreational poker winnings are not taxed in Ireland. The GRAI has issued remote betting licences only so far; gaming (including poker) licensing is expected around 2027–2028.

What are the €10 stake and €3,000 win limits?

They are default maximum stake and winning limits for gaming written into the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Applied as drafted to poker they would make the regulated game unviable — but Section 83 of the Act lets the GRAI vary the limits with the consent of the Minister for Justice.

Are poker winnings taxed in Ireland?

Recreational gambling winnings, including poker, are generally not subject to income tax in Ireland. Professional circumstances can differ, so anyone playing at significant volume should take their own tax advice.

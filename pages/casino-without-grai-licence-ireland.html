Casino Without a GRAI Licence in Ireland: What Players Need to Know

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Why No Casino Has a GRAI Licence

The simple answer: GRAI has not issued any casino licences yet. The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland was formally established on 5 March 2025. It began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026 — but casino (gaming) licences are a separate category and are part of the next phase of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 rollout.

As of November 2026, that phase has not opened. Applications for online casino licences are expected to become available between late 2026 and 2028. This means every online casino that accepts Irish players today is operating under a foreign licence — not an Irish one. That is not a loophole or a scandal; it is simply where Irish regulation currently stands.

Any casino marketing itself as holding a GRAI gaming or casino licence is misleading you. If you see that claim, treat it as a red flag and do not deposit.

Is Playing Legal Without One?

For players, playing at an online casino is not a criminal offence under Irish law, even if the operator does not hold a GRAI licence. The licensing obligations under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 fall on operators, not on individual players. Irish residents have always had access to overseas-licensed gambling sites, and that remains the case while the domestic framework is built out.

That said, the absence of an Irish licence means the casino is not subject to GRAI oversight, GRAI complaints procedures, or Irish consumer protections specific to gambling. What protects you as a player is the quality of the foreign licence the casino holds. This is why choosing an MGA-licensed casino matters so much in the current environment.

What to Check Before Depositing

Before you put money into any casino that lacks a GRAI licence (which currently means all of them), run through this checklist:

  • Find the licence number in the footer of the casino site. It should link directly to the regulator's public register.
  • Verify it on the regulator's own website — for MGA, check mga.org.mt/check-player. If the number does not appear there, the casino is not MGA licensed regardless of what it claims.
  • Check responsible gambling tools: deposit limits, self-exclusion, and links to Gambling Awareness Trust (1800 753 753, gamblingcare.ie) should be easy to find.
  • Read the withdrawal terms: legitimate casinos publish payout times and verification requirements clearly. Vague or missing information is a warning sign.
  • Google the casino name + "withdrawal problem" or "not paying". A pattern of complaints is worth taking seriously.

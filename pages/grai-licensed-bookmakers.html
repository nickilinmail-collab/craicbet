GRAI-Licensed Bookmakers: Reading the Register Like a Pro

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Register Is the Only List That Matters

Since 1 July 2026 the GRAI has been issuing remote betting licences, and the official register — not any affiliate list, ours included — is the source of truth. Dozens of operators have migrated from the old Revenue system, from household names to niche books. What matters is knowing how to read an entry.

Anatomy of a Licence Number

GRAI numbers are self-describing. Take a real one: GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001. The four-digit code identifies the operator account, RB means Remote Betting (IPB = in-person betting, and a future GM-type code will mark gaming), 26 is the year of issue. So when any site claims a "GRAI casino licence", one glance at the register answers it: no gaming codes exist yet.

Three Checks Before You Deposit

1. Match the company, not the brand — the register lists legal entities, and trading names can differ wildly. The company name lives in the site footer or terms. 2. Check the activity type — a betting licence covers the sportsbook only; the same operator's casino product runs under foreign licences until Irish gaming licensing opens. 3. Check the dates — licences run three years; an expired entry is as good as none.

How to Check if a Bookmaker Is GRAI Licensed

All legal online bookmakers serving Irish customers must hold a valid GRAI remote betting licence. The public register of licensed operators is maintained by GRAI at grai.ie. Before depositing, check:

  • Step 1: visit grai.ie and navigate to the licensed operators register
  • Step 2: search for the operator name or the licence number displayed on the site's footer
  • Step 3: confirm the licence is current and covers remote betting
  • Unlicensed sites: Irish law prohibits unlicensed sites from accepting Irish customers; using them means no consumer protection and no recourse for disputes
  • Casino note: GRAI has not issued any casino licences — casino sites claiming to be GRAI-licensed are making a false claim

Can a GRAI-licensed bookmaker also offer casino games?

Many do — but the casino side runs under foreign licences; the GRAI licence covers the sportsbook only.

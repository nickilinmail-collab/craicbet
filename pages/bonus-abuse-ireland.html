What Is Bonus Abuse on Irish Betting Sites?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Bonus abuse refers to using betting site promotions in ways that fall outside the operator’s intended terms — from obvious abuses like creating multiple accounts, to grey areas like systematically matched betting every offer. Here is what GRAI-licensed operators consider abuse and what the realistic consequences are.

Clear-Cut Bonus Abuse

These practices are unambiguously against operator T&Cs and can result in immediate account closure, bonus voiding, and in serious cases legal action:

  • Creating multiple accounts at the same operator to claim the welcome bonus more than once
  • Using stolen or borrowed identity documents to create new accounts
  • Colluding with others to systematically exploit referral bonuses (each person gets a referral bonus both ways)
  • Placing bets intended only to meet wagering requirements while covering positions elsewhere (wash wagering)

Grey Areas: Matched Betting

Matched betting — using a betting exchange to lay off a free bet and extract near-certain profit — is legal in Ireland. It is not fraud. However, operators consider it “bonus abuse” under their own T&Cs and may close or restrict accounts identified as matched bettors. The practical consequence is account restriction (“gubbing”) rather than legal action.

The matched betting guide covers this in detail, including how long accounts typically remain open before restriction.

Low-Margin Betting Patterns

Operators use software to identify customers who consistently bet at the highest available odds, use bonuses systematically, and have positive expected value. This is not illegal, but it triggers account restrictions at most bookmakers. If you only bet when you have a bonus or on best-odds markets, expect your account to be flagged eventually.

Can GRAI Rules Protect You From Bonus Abuse Accusations?

Not directly — GRAI rules cover consumer protection and fair dealing but do not prevent operators from exercising commercial discretion on who they offer bonuses to. If an operator closes your account without paying out legitimate winnings that are not from bonus activity, you can escalate to the GRAI. But operators are not required to continue offering you bonuses indefinitely.

What counts as bonus abuse on an Irish betting site?

Multiple accounts to claim bonuses twice, using fake identities, referral bonus collusion, and wash wagering are clear abuse. Matched betting is legal but most operators still restrict accounts that do it systematically.

Is matched betting considered bonus abuse in Ireland?

It is legal under Irish law but against most operators' T&Cs. Operators can close or restrict accounts they identify as matched betting. This is a commercial decision by the operator, not a legal offence.

Can a betting site confiscate my winnings for bonus abuse?

If you violated T&Cs (e.g., multiple accounts), yes — bonus winnings can be voided. Winnings from real-money bets not associated with bonus abuse cannot be withheld. If an operator withholds legitimate winnings, escalate to the GRAI.

How do betting sites detect bonus abuse?

Through KYC identity verification (catching duplicate accounts), device fingerprinting, IP tracking, betting pattern analysis (always betting on best-odds markets, using bonuses every time), and exchange layoff detection.

What should I do if I'm accused of bonus abuse unfairly?

Request the reason in writing. Submit a formal complaint. If the accusation relates to bonus activity, the burden is on the operator to show T&Cs were violated. Escalate to the GRAI if unresolved.

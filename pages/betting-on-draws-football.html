Betting on Football Draws in Ireland: When the Draw Has Real Value

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 4 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

Why Draws Are Often Overlooked

Most football bettors focus on team performance and neglect the draw as a genuine outcome. The result is that draws can be systematically mispriced in some markets, particularly when one team is a clear favourite. The favourite’s implied probability is often overestimated by casual bettors, leaving the draw and the away win with higher than fair value at certain bookmakers.

The draw also offers structural value because bookmakers tend to focus their pricing efforts on the win outcomes, where most money is wagered. Draw markets can carry higher residual value as a result.

Leagues with the Most Draws

Some leagues produce more draws than others due to tactical tendencies and competitive balance. The Italian Serie A and Spanish La Liga have historically produced relatively high draw rates in certain periods. Lower-division English football and some Northern European leagues also show higher draw frequencies. High-scoring leagues like the Netherlands tend to produce fewer draws.

Beyond league-level averages, specific matchups matter: evenly matched teams in tight title or relegation battles frequently draw. Teams managed defensively in home games against superior opponents are also more likely to produce draws than attacking head-to-head records suggest.

Draw No Bet vs Backing the Draw

Draw no bet (DNB) removes the draw from the market: if the game finishes level, your stake is returned. Backing the draw is different — you win only if the game finishes level and lose if either team wins. DNB is essentially a handicap of zero applied to a two-outcome market. The price for DNB on the favourite is higher than 1X2 win odds for that team because the draw protection is built in.

Backing the outright draw at the full 1X2 price gives potentially higher returns but requires the game to finish level. DNB gives insurance against the draw at the cost of lower odds on your win selection.

Using Draw Bets in Doubles

Combining draw bets in a double is high-variance but can offer strong value: two evenly matched games, each with a draw priced at approximately 3.0, produce a double at 9.0, returning €90 on a €10 stake if both end level. Frequently Asked Questions

What percentage of football matches end in a draw?

Across top European leagues, roughly 25-27% of matches end in a draw over a full season. The exact rate varies by league and season.

What is draw no bet?

Draw no bet (DNB) removes the draw outcome from a 1X2 market. If the match ends level, your stake is returned. You win if your chosen team wins and lose if they lose.

Is betting on draws profitable?

Draw betting can offer value in specific matchups, particularly evenly contested games where the market overweights the favourite. No single betting type is inherently profitable without an edge in selection.

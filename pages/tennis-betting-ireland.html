Tennis Betting Markets: What's Available

Match winner is the most straightforward tennis market: back one player to win the match in however many sets. For Grand Slam events (best of five for men, best of three for women), prices for heavy favourites can be very short, which is why set betting and handicap markets are popular alternatives.

Set betting lets you predict the exact scoreline in sets — for example, 2-0 or 2-1 in a best-of-three match. The correct score pays better than the straight match winner, but the bookmaker margin on this market is higher. For five-set men's matches, margins on set betting can reach 10–15%, so check carefully with a margin calculator before backing.

Game handicap gives the underdog a head start in total games across the match. If you back a favourite at -5.5 games, they must win by six or more total games across all sets. This is a practical alternative to accepting a very short price on the match winner market.

Total games over/under is independent of who wins: you bet on the combined total games played. A three-set match between closely-matched players might total 22–26 games; a straight-sets win by a dominant favourite might be 12–16. This market avoids taking a view on the result but requires accurate assessment of how competitive the match will be.

The Grand Slam Calendar

The four Grand Slams define the tennis betting year. Each has a distinct surface and character that shapes the betting market:

  • Australian Open (January): Hard court, Melbourne. The year's first major; form from the previous season is the main guide. Early rounds open in January and are well-covered by GRAI-licensed bookmakers.
  • French Open (May–June): Clay court, Paris. The slowest surface; clay specialists dominate and serve is less dominant, producing longer matches and higher total games totals on average.
  • Wimbledon (July): Grass court, London. The serve-dominant surface; first-set winners have a higher conversion rate here than on other surfaces. One of the highest tennis betting volumes of the year at Irish bookmakers.
  • US Open (August–September): Hard court, New York. Night sessions in front of a partisan crowd create conditions that differ from other hard court events. Upsets in early rounds are historically common.

Beyond the Grand Slams, the ATP Masters 1000 and WTA 1000 events (including the Masters in Paris, Miami Open, and Madrid Open) are well-covered with live betting. The year-end ATP Finals (November) also draws significant betting interest.

In-Play Tennis Betting: Why It Attracts Irish Punters

Tennis is structurally well-suited to in-play betting. Each match is a sequence of discrete scoring events — points, games, sets — that create frequent odds movements. When a player breaks serve, the market reprices immediately. When a player loses their own serve after a break, momentum shifts again.

Irish punters who bet in-play on tennis are often looking for a few specific situations: a heavy favourite who drops the first set (repriced longer than their true probability warrants), a known poor front-runner who leads but tends to fade, or a strong second-server who can hold under pressure. These patterns exist — but bookmakers are aware of them too, and in-play margins on live tennis are typically 5–8%, higher than the pre-match match winner margin.

One practical consideration: in-play tennis requires a fast interface and reliable live data. When comparing bookmakers for tennis specifically, check whether their in-play odds update rapidly and whether the streaming option (where available) has acceptable delay. A slow feed can mean you are reacting to events that the bookmaker has already priced.

ATP/WTA Tour: Lower-Profile Matches

Betting on ATP 250 and WTA 250 events carries additional risk. Markets are thinner, margins are wider, and the data available on players ranked outside the top 50–100 is considerably less than for Grand Slam contenders. The bookmaker's information advantage is larger in these markets. If you do bet lower-tier matches, focus on markets with the fewest outcomes — match winner or first-set winner — and avoid exotic markets such as correct score or player specials where the margin is highest.

Frequently Asked Questions

What tennis markets are available on Irish betting sites?

GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer match winner, set betting, game handicap, total games over/under, and first set winner for most ATP and WTA tour matches. Grand Slam events have deeper markets including correct score and player specials.

When are the four Grand Slams held each year?

The Australian Open is in January, the French Open runs May to June, Wimbledon is in July, and the US Open takes place August to September. These four events attract the highest tennis betting volumes of the year at Irish bookmakers.

Is in-play tennis betting available in Ireland?

Yes. Most GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer in-play betting on ATP and WTA matches, including Grand Slams and Masters events. Odds move quickly in tennis due to momentum swings — in-play tennis requires fast reactions and an understanding of how odds shift between points, games, and sets.

What is game handicap betting in tennis?

A game handicap gives one player a head start in total games. For example, if Player A is at -4.5 games, you need them to win by 5 or more games across the whole match. This is an alternative to backing a heavy favourite at short odds.

Why do tennis odds move so much during a match?

Tennis is inherently volatile at the micro level — a single break of serve can shift a match significantly. Bookmakers price in-play odds based on the current score and serve, and odds swing sharply when a player breaks serve, loses their own serve, or visibly fades physically. This volatility is what makes in-play tennis popular but also difficult to profit from consistently.

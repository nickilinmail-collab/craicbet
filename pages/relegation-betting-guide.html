Relegation Betting: Markets, Odds and What to Look For

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

How relegation betting works

Relegation outright markets predict which teams will be relegated from the top division at the end of the season. In the Premier League, three teams are relegated. Some operators offer a simple ‘to be relegated yes/no’ market on each team; others price all 20 teams in a combined market. Teams with the longest prices are perceived as most likely to go down; favourites for the title are typically 500/1 or longer to be relegated.

Factors that drive relegation odds

Squad depth and quality, manager experience at the level, budget relative to the league average, home record (critical for promoted sides playing in front of their own fans), and historical pattern of promoted teams (newly promoted sides are relegated more frequently than established top-flight sides). Early-season form is priced in rapidly — a promoted team losing its first four matches will see its relegation price fall dramatically in September.

Timing relegation bets

Relegation bets placed before the season begin at the longest prices. Prices for genuine relegation candidates can shorten from 4/1 to 1/3 by March or April when their fate looks increasingly likely. Betting on a team’s survival (the complement — not to be relegated) can offer short prices early-season but longer odds mid-season after poor form. The most dramatic price movements occur in October-December when early-season data is clearest.

Each-way relegation betting

Some operators offer each-way terms on relegation betting — for example, top 2 to be relegated in a market ranked by relegation probability. This is niche and not universally available. Frequently Asked Questions

How many teams are relegated in the Premier League?

Three teams are relegated from the Premier League each season to the Championship. The three teams finishing in positions 18th, 19th and 20th at the end of the season are relegated.

When is the best time to bet on relegation?

Pre-season offers the longest prices on potential relegation candidates, as uncertainty is highest. Prices shorten rapidly after poor early-season form. The best price-versus-certainty tradeoff for relegation betting is typically October to December, when enough data exists to identify genuine relegation candidates but prices haven’t collapsed yet.

Can newly promoted teams avoid relegation?

Yes, though newly promoted sides are statistically more likely to be relegated than established top-flight clubs. Some promoted teams with strong squads, experienced managers and good home form survive comfortably. Each promotion cohort produces at least one team that consolidates — identifying which one is the key judgement for relegation betting.

Relegation Betting Strategy

  • Pre-season value: newly promoted sides often 2.00–4.00 to be relegated — research their squad depth
  • Manager sacking: early relegation odds shorten significantly after a managerial change fails
  • Key player injuries: loss of a striker or goalkeeper can double relegation odds for small clubs
  • January window: clubs that do not strengthen at the winter window are vulnerable in second half
  • A team at 50/1 to be relegated after a strong start still represents a live position for the season

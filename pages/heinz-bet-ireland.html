Heinz Bet: Ireland's Complete Guide

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

A Heinz bet covers 6 selections in 57 individual bets — every possible combination of doubles, trebles, four-folds, five-folds and one six-fold accumulator. The name comes directly from the Heinz brand's famous “57 Varieties” slogan. At €1 per bet, a Heinz costs exactly €57 in stake. Here is everything you need to understand it before placing one.

What Is a Heinz Bet?

A Heinz is a full cover bet across 6 selections with no singles. It generates 57 bets by combining your selections in every possible grouping of two or more:

Bet typeNumber of betsSelections needed to win
Doubles15Any 2 of 6
Trebles20Any 3 of 6
Four-folds15Any 4 of 6
Five-folds6Any 5 of 6
Six-fold accumulator1All 6
Total57—

The maths behind the counts: for doubles, you are choosing 2 items from 6, which is 6×5÷2 = 15. For trebles, 6×5×4÷6 = 20. And so on down to the single six-fold.

Why Is It Called a Heinz?

HJ Heinz launched its “57 Varieties” advertising campaign in 1896, based on a train advertisement Henry Heinz saw for shoes with “21 styles”. The company adopted 57 as a marketing number even though it already made more products than that. The number stuck and became iconic. Bookmakers matched it to the 57-bet combination across 6 selections, and the name was born. It is one of the more memorable terms in betting, which is probably why full-cover bets on 7 and 8 selections are called Super Heinz and Goliath respectively, rather than anything more technical.

Minimum Wins Required and a Worked Example

The smallest bets in a Heinz are doubles, so at least 2 selections must win for any return. If only 1 wins, all 57 bets lose.

Here is a concrete example at €1 per bet (€57 total stake), with 4 horse racing selections winning at the following odds and 2 losing:

  • Horse A: 3/1 (wins) → decimal 4.0
  • Horse B: 2/1 (wins) → decimal 3.0
  • Horse C: 5/2 (wins) → decimal 3.5
  • Horse D: 4/1 (wins) → decimal 5.0
  • Horse E: 7/2 (loses)
  • Horse F: 5/1 (loses)

With 4 winners, you land: all doubles involving only the 4 winners (there are 6 such doubles), all trebles involving only the 4 winners (4 trebles), and the one four-fold of A×B×C×D. No five-folds or six-fold win because E and F lost. The five-folds and six-fold that include only winners from {A,B,C,D} do not exist — you need at least 5 of your 6 to win for a five-fold to land. Result: a decent profit driven mainly by the trebles and four-fold, but far short of an all-six winner. This is the classic Heinz experience: partial success pays, but the big return needs most selections to win.

All three share the same minimum threshold of 2 winners (because doubles are the smallest component in each), but the stakes scale dramatically. A Goliath at €1 per bet requires a €247 outlay. Most Irish punters who want more coverage beyond a Heinz move to 50p or 25p per bet to keep stakes manageable.

When to Use a Heinz

Horse racing is the natural home of the Heinz in Ireland. Cheltenham week, Punchestown, and the Galway Races all see heavy Heinz traffic because punters have multiple each-way fancies across a card. The full-cover structure means a few unexpected big-price winners can generate a significant return even if your other selections disappoint.

Football Heinz bets work well across a Saturday card where you have 6 confident match-result picks. The downside: a 0–0 or a late equaliser on one game kills a lot of your combinations. For football, many punters prefer a straight accumulator for the big return or a doubles and trebles combination for a lower stake.

Pros: you do not need all 6 to win; multiple small wins can combine into a worthwhile return; easier to manage than keeping track of individual accas.

Cons: €57 minimum stake for €1 bets; two or three winners at short odds may not cover the full outlay; the six-fold return requires all 6 selections to land.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many bets is a Heinz?

A Heinz consists of 57 bets from 6 selections: 15 doubles, 20 trebles, 15 four-folds, 6 five-folds and 1 six-fold accumulator.

Why is it called a Heinz bet?

The name comes from the Heinz food brand's famous “57 Varieties” advertising slogan, launched in 1896. The bet has exactly 57 components, hence the name.

How many selections need to win for a Heinz to pay out?

At least 2 of your 6 selections must win for you to receive any return, because the smallest bets in a Heinz are doubles. With only 1 winner you lose all 57 bets.

How much does a €1 Heinz cost?

At €1 per bet, a Heinz costs €57 in total. Each of the 57 individual bets costs €1. To reduce your outlay, use a smaller unit stake such as 10c per bet (€5.70 total) or 50c per bet (€28.50 total).

What is the difference between a Heinz and a Super Heinz?

A Heinz covers 6 selections in 57 bets. A Super Heinz covers 7 selections in 120 bets (21 doubles, 35 trebles, 35 4-folds, 21 5-folds, 7 6-folds and 1 7-fold). A Goliath covers 8 selections in 247 bets. All three require a minimum of 2 winners for any return.

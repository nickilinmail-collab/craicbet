How to Complain About a Bookmaker in Ireland — GRAI Dispute Guide 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Since 1 July 2026, Irish punters have a dedicated regulator — the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) — to handle complaints against licensed bookmakers. This guide walks through exactly how to escalate a dispute, what GRAI can and cannot do, and what to do if your bookmaker is not GRAI-licensed.

Step 1: Complain to the Bookmaker First

GRAI's complaint process requires you to raise the issue with the bookmaker first. Most GRAI-licensed operators are required to have an internal complaints procedure and must acknowledge your complaint within a set timeframe. Document everything: write by email rather than live chat so you have a record.

What to include in your complaint email:

  • Your account username or customer reference number
  • A clear description of what happened and on what date
  • Screenshots or transaction references if relevant
  • What outcome you are seeking (e.g. refund, account reinstatement)

Step 2: If the Bookmaker Doesn't Resolve It — Go to GRAI

If the bookmaker rejects your complaint or doesn't respond within a reasonable timeframe (typically 8 weeks for financial services, though GRAI's specific timeframes are set in their licence conditions), you can escalate to GRAI directly.

Common Dispute Types and How Each Is Handled

Disputed bet settlement

If you believe a bet was settled incorrectly (wrong score used, wrong rules applied), the bookmaker's rules document is the first reference point. In sports betting, bookmakers generally settle on official results from the governing body — this is written into their terms. GRAI does not arbitrate on the sporting merits of a decision, but if the bookmaker applied their own rules inconsistently, this is a valid GRAI complaint ground.

Account closure or suspended withdrawals

Bookmakers can close accounts or suspend withdrawals for KYC (Know Your Customer) checks under anti-money-laundering law. If your KYC documents were submitted and the bookmaker still won't release funds after an unreasonable time (typically more than 28–30 days), this is a reasonable complaint to raise with GRAI.

Bonus terms dispute

Bonus terms disputes are among the most common complaints. GRAI's advertising and inducements rules now require bookmakers to present terms clearly. If a term was not disclosed before you claimed the offer, document what you saw when you claimed and raise it with the bookmaker first. GRAI can investigate whether the licensed operator's terms met their disclosure obligations.

Responsible gambling failure

If a bookmaker continued to accept your bets after you had self-excluded via the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER), or failed to act on obvious signs of problem gambling, this is one of GRAI's highest-priority complaint types. Document the dates of exclusion, the subsequent bets accepted, and the amounts.

What If the Bookmaker Is Not GRAI-Licensed?

GRAI has no jurisdiction over unlicensed operators. If you are in a dispute with an operator that does not appear on the GRAI register, your options are:

  • Check the operator's actual licence (e.g. Malta MGA or UKGC) and file a complaint with that regulator directly
  • If the operator claims no licence at all — consider this a serious red flag; Small Claims Court or a chargeback with your card provider may be the only recourse
  • Report the operator to GRAI as a potentially unlicensed operator serving Irish customers

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I make a complaint about a bookmaker in Ireland?

First, raise the complaint with the bookmaker directly — in writing, by email, so you have a record. Include your account details, dates, and what you are seeking. If the bookmaker does not resolve it within a reasonable timeframe, escalate to GRAI at grai.ie. GRAI can only act on complaints against bookmakers that hold a GRAI licence.

What can GRAI do about my bookmaker complaint?

GRAI can investigate whether a licensed operator breached their GRAI licence conditions — such as responsible gambling failures, improper account closures, or undisclosed bonus terms. It cannot order a bookmaker to pay you a specific sum or adjudicate on the sporting merits of a bet settlement.

What if my bookmaker is not GRAI-licensed?

GRAI has no jurisdiction over operators without a GRAI licence. Identify which licence the bookmaker actually holds (usually Malta MGA or UKGC) and file your complaint with that regulator. You can also report the unlicensed operator to GRAI as a potentially illegal service targeting Irish customers.

How long does a bookmaker have to respond to my complaint in Ireland?

GRAI's licence conditions require licensed operators to have a formal complaints procedure and respond within set timeframes. Always check the bookmaker's own complaints policy in their terms. As a general guide, if you have not received a final response within 8 weeks, you have reasonable grounds to escalate to GRAI.

Can I get a chargeback from my bank for a betting dispute?

A chargeback (disputing a card transaction with your bank) is most relevant when an operator refuses to return your own deposit — i.e. you never received the service. For a disputed bet outcome, chargebacks are rarely successful because you authorised the original deposit and the bet settled. If the bookmaker is holding funds without explanation, document everything and contact both your bank and GRAI.

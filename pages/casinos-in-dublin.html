Casinos in Dublin: Where to Actually Play in 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

First, the Legal Quirk That Explains Everything

Ireland has never had a casino licence category — under the old Gaming and Lotteries Act 1956, commercial casinos are simply not provided for. Every "casino" in Dublin therefore operates as a private members' club: you join (membership is typically free and granted at the door with photo ID, sometimes after a 24-hour waiting rule), and the club offers gaming to its members. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 finally creates a licensing path for gaming venues, which the GRAI will roll out in phases through 2028.

What to Expect Inside

Smaller and more informal than a UK or continental casino: a few American roulette and blackjack tables, card-room poker (cash games most evenings, tournaments weekly), and banks of slots. Minimum bets are friendly — roulette from €1–€2, blackjack from €5. Dress codes are relaxed; drinks service varies by club. Most clubs run late — several effectively 24 hours.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are casinos legal in Dublin?

Commercial casinos have no licence category under current law, so venues operate legally as private members' clubs. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 creates a future licensing path.

Do I need to be a member to enter a Dublin casino?

Yes — but membership is typically free and arranged at the door with photo ID; some clubs apply a short waiting period.

What is the best casino in Dublin?

The Sporting Emporium is the closest to a full casino experience; D1 Club and the Carlton are the best-known city-centre alternatives.

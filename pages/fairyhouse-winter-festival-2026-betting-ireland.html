The Hatton's Grace: Teahupoo's Shot at History

No horse has ever won the Hatton's Grace four times. Teahupoo, winner of the last three renewals for Gordon Elliott, is entered to try — a feat that would put him past Honeysuckle and Apple's Jade, who each managed three in the past decade. The opposition is the deepest in years: 15 entries at the early stage, nine of them Grade 1 winners, including Willie Mullins' Lossiemouth and Jimmy Du Seuil, Henry de Bromhead's Slade Steel, and British champion trainer Dan Skelton's The New Lion, who would be the yard's first Irish runner since 2023 (Meath Chronicle, 6 Nov). Entries are not declarations — the final field firms up in race week.

The Drinmore and Royal Bond: First Grade 1 Tests for the Novices

Sunday's undercard is where Cheltenham Festival pictures start to form. The Drinmore Novice Chase drew 28 initial entries, half of them from the Elliott yard, with Skelton also entering Talk To The Man and Captain Teague. The Royal Bond Hurdle — listed by the racecourse as a Grade 2 for 2026 — has a long record of crowning top novice hurdlers. The winners here are routinely cut to short prices for March; the value question is whether a November price already carries a spring premium.

Betting Angles

  • Short-priced champions on seasonal debut: a horse going for a historic streak is often priced on reputation, not fitness. First-time-out record and any in-race market drift matter more than the career résumé — and at odds-on, the downside is all yours.
  • Entries ≠ runners: with 15 Hatton's Grace entries from a handful of powerful yards, several will be re-routed elsewhere. Ante-post prices before declarations carry non-runner risk unless the book offers NRNB — check the terms before you take a price.
  • Novice races reward the clock, not the name: in the Drinmore and Royal Bond the market leans on bumper and point-to-point reputations. Time figures from early-season Irish novice events have historically been the sharper guide.
  • One-yard depth again: when one trainer has half the entries, the stable's second string often outruns its price — the same angle we flagged for the Troytown.

Getting There / Watching

Fairyhouse Racecourse is at Ratoath, County Meath, about 25km north-west of Dublin, with free shuttle buses from Ashbourne, Ratoath, Dunshaughlin and Dunboyne on race days (fairyhouse.ie). The racecourse publishes race times in race week; feature races are live on Racing TV — see where to watch Irish racing.

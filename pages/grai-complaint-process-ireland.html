Step-by-Step: How to Make a Formal Complaint to GRAI

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) has powers to investigate complaints about GRAI-licensed operators — but the process is not as simple as firing off an email. There are steps to follow, limitations on what GRAI can do, and situations where a different route is more appropriate. This guide takes you through every stage.

Step 1: Exhaust the Operator's Internal Complaint Process First

Before GRAI will normally accept a formal complaint, you must have attempted to resolve the issue directly with the operator. GRAI-licensed operators are required to have an accessible internal complaints procedure. To do this correctly:

  • Submit your complaint formally in writing (email is fine) to the operator's support or complaints team.
  • Clearly state what happened, what you expected, what the operator did or failed to do, and what outcome you are seeking.
  • Keep copies of all correspondence.
  • Allow the operator a reasonable time to respond — typically 8 weeks, though many operators respond faster.

If the operator does not respond within 8 weeks, or responds with a final answer you disagree with, you are ready to escalate.

Step 2: Consider Whether GRAI or Another Body Is Right for Your Complaint

GRAI is a regulator, not a consumer ombudsman. This distinction matters:

  • GRAI investigates regulatory breaches — failures to comply with licensing conditions, responsible gambling obligations, advertising rules, etc.
  • GRAI cannot award you compensation directly or force an operator to pay you money it owes you.
  • For individual financial disputes (e.g. a withdrawal was refused without justification), the Financial Services and Pensions Ombudsman (FSPO) may have jurisdiction if the operator is also regulated for financial services.
  • For disputes about bonus terms, the operator's Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) scheme is typically the first route for individual resolution.

That said, if the operator's behaviour involves a breach of GRAI licensing conditions — for example, they refused to close your account after you invoked self-exclusion, or they targeted you with marketing after you had set deposit limits — that is squarely within GRAI's jurisdiction.

Step 3: Gather Your Evidence

Before submitting to GRAI, assemble the following:

  • Your account details with the operator (name, username, account number).
  • A chronological summary of events.
  • Copies of all correspondence with the operator (emails, chat transcripts, screenshots).
  • The operator's final response to your complaint (if received).
  • Any relevant terms and conditions you believe were breached.
  • Evidence of any financial loss if relevant (transaction history, screenshots).

GRAI investigations work better with organised evidence. A well-documented complaint is more likely to result in action than a general account of dissatisfaction.

Step 4: Submit Your Formal Complaint to GRAI

Formal complaints to GRAI are submitted via their official complaints process at grai.ie. The submission should include:

  • Your full name and contact details.
  • The name of the operator you are complaining about.
  • A clear statement of the issue and how it relates to a potential regulatory breach.
  • The evidence you have gathered.
  • What you have already done to resolve the matter with the operator.

GRAI will acknowledge your complaint and assess whether it falls within their jurisdiction and whether there is enough to investigate. Not every complaint results in a full investigation — GRAI prioritises cases involving systemic breaches or significant consumer harm.

Step 5: The GRAI Investigation Process

If GRAI decides to investigate, the process typically involves:

  • GRAI contacting the operator and requesting information and responses.
  • GRAI reviewing evidence from both sides.
  • If a breach is found: GRAI issuing a compliance notice, requiring corrective action, or commencing enforcement proceedings.
  • GRAI informing you of the outcome, though the detail shared with complainants can be limited by confidentiality requirements.

Timelines vary significantly. Straightforward cases may be resolved in weeks; complex regulatory investigations can take months. GRAI has committed to keeping complainants updated on progress.

What Happens If the Operator Is Not GRAI-Licensed?

GRAI can only regulate operators holding a GRAI licence. If you have a dispute with an unlicensed offshore site, GRAI has no jurisdiction. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I complain to GRAI about a betting site?

Yes, but only about GRAI-licensed operators. You must first exhaust the operator's internal complaint process. GRAI investigates regulatory breaches rather than individual commercial disputes.

How long does a GRAI complaint take?

Timelines vary by complexity. GRAI aims to acknowledge complaints promptly and will provide updates. Complex regulatory investigations can take several months.

What can GRAI actually do about my complaint?

GRAI can investigate regulatory breaches, issue compliance notices, impose fines, and in serious cases suspend or revoke licences. It cannot directly award individual compensation — that requires a civil claim or ADR process.

What if the betting site is not GRAI licensed?

GRAI cannot regulate unlicensed operators. If you have a dispute with an unlicensed offshore site, your options are very limited — there is no regulatory body to complain to. This is a key reason why betting only on GRAI-licensed sites matters.

Does GRAI handle disputes about bonus terms?

GRAI does not adjudicate individual bonus disputes directly. However, if bonus terms are deceptive under GRAI advertising rules, that is a regulatory matter GRAI can investigate. For individual resolution, the operator's ADR process is more appropriate.

How Long Does a GRAI Licence Last? The 3-Year vs 5-Year Question

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

The Current Answer: Three Years

GRAI B2C licences run for three years, set by the Gambling (Licensing of Gambling Activities) Regulations 2026 (S.I. 38/2026). That is already a significant upgrade on the old Revenue regime, where remote bookmaker licences lasted two years and in-person bookmaker licences had shrunk to one.

The Five-Year Debate

In its first public consultation (2025), operators pushed back on short terms: a five- or six-figure non-refundable application fee every three years is a heavy cycle, and short terms discourage long-term investment in compliance. The GRAI responded that it would consider five-year licence terms and look at a tiered fee model — a notable early example of the regulator adjusting to evidence rather than digging in.

Why Term Length Matters to Players

Licence renewal is the regulator's periodic deep-dive into an operator: finances, compliance record, complaint history. Shorter terms mean more frequent scrutiny; longer terms mean operators invest more in staying licensed. Either way, the renewal lever — backed by suspension and revocation powers between renewals — is what keeps a register meaningful. Check who currently holds a licence on our daily tracker.

What to Watch

If the GRAI adopts five-year terms, expect it via amending regulations with adjusted fees, likely before casino licensing opens — so the first casino licensees may sign up under the longer regime from day one.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a GRAI licence last?

Three years under the 2026 licensing regulations (S.I. 38/2026). The GRAI has said it will consider five-year terms following industry consultation.

Why do operators want five-year licences?

Application fees are non-refundable and can reach six figures, so a three-year renewal cycle is costly and discourages long-term investment.

Does a longer licence mean less oversight?

No — the GRAI retains suspension, revocation and sanction powers throughout the term.

GRAI Licence Terms: What the 5-Year Period Means

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, GRAI issues remote betting licences for a fixed term. The standard term for a remote betting licence is 3 years (not 5 years as in some earlier drafts). Our partner operator holds licence GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001, valid 16 September 2026 to 15 September 2029.

What GRAI Licensing Means for Players

  • GRAI-licensed operators must meet strict consumer protection standards including responsible gambling tools
  • Players can complain directly to GRAI about a licensed operator — complaints are investigated formally
  • Licensed operators must display their GRAI licence number on their website and in advertising
  • Funds protection: licensed operators must keep player funds separate from operating funds
  • Verification: check any operator's licence on the GRAI public register at grai.ie before depositing

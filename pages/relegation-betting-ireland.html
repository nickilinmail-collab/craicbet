Relegation Betting in Ireland: How to Bet on Teams to Go Down

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Relegation betting covers markets where you predict which teams will finish in the bottom positions of a league and be relegated to a lower division. In the Premier League, the bottom three teams are relegated at the end of each season. Similar markets exist in the Championship, League One, and major European leagues. Relegation betting can be placed as an outright selection (back a specific team to be relegated) or as a top/bottom finish market.

Market types in relegation betting

The most common formats are: a straight market to be relegated (any team to finish in the bottom three), to finish bottom of the league (wooden spoon), and bottom-half finish or relegation play-off markets in leagues with playoff formats. Some bookmakers offer each-way terms on relegation markets — for example, paying for the bottom four when three are actually relegated, giving a small return on a team that narrowly avoids relegation.

Factors that matter in relegation analysis

Newly promoted clubs are statistically the most likely to be relegated, particularly those promoted via the play-offs who may have fewer resources. Squad depth is a critical factor: teams that lose key players to injury or suspension for extended periods are significantly more vulnerable than their pre-season odds suggest. Managerial change mid-season is a double-edged signal: it may indicate panic from a board, but it can also trigger a turnaround. Points at Christmas is a reasonable proxy for final safety: in the Premier League, teams with fewer than 15 points by the December break have been relegated in the majority of historical seasons.

Timing and odds movement

Pre-season relegation prices for newly promoted clubs are often 1.5–2.5 (heavily expected to be relegated). These prices shorten further in early season when results confirm the difficulty of their position. Mid-season, clubs that have sacked a manager and are in poor form may drift to 1.2 or below, offering little value for new money. Frequently Asked Questions

How many teams are relegated from the Premier League?

Three teams are relegated from the Premier League each season, finishing 18th, 19th and 20th in the table. They are replaced by the top three finishers from the Championship (the top two automatically, the third via a play-off).

Can I bet each-way on relegation?

Some bookmakers offer each-way terms on relegation markets, paying for more teams than the actual number relegated. For example, paying four places when three teams are relegated. This can offer a small return on a team that finishes 4th bottom without being relegated. Always check the specific each-way terms available.

What is the survival betting market?

Survival betting is the inverse of relegation: you back a team to avoid relegation (finish 17th or higher in the Premier League). This market is usually available mid-season for clubs in the lower half of the table and is priced similarly to outright relegation betting.

Are play-off results relevant to relegation markets?

In leagues with relegation play-offs (such as some European leagues), play-off rules are specified in the market terms. In the Premier League, there are no relegation play-offs: the bottom three are automatically relegated by final standings. Championship markets may include play-off mechanics that affect final-position betting.

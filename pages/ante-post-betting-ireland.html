Ante-Post Betting in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Ante-post betting means placing a wager well before the day of the event — often weeks or months in advance. The word comes from Latin: ante (before) and post (the starting post in horse racing). For Irish punters, ante-post markets are most prominent around the festival calendar: Cheltenham, the Grand National, Punchestown and Galway. The appeal is longer odds — but the risks are real, and the rules around non-runners are what most punters get wrong.

Why Ante-Post Odds Are Better (and Why They Shorten)

Bookmakers price ante-post markets with less certainty than on-the-day markets. In a Cheltenham race months out, the trainer's plans are unclear, the going conditions are unknown, and entries may not even be confirmed. That uncertainty means the bookmaker builds in a wider margin — but it also means that for a selection you believe in, the early price can be considerably longer than what you will find on race day.

As an event approaches, information flows in. Horses enter specific races, trials run, declarations are made, ground reports arrive. Each piece of information narrows the uncertainty and the market tightens. The favourite for a Grade 1 at Cheltenham might be 8/1 (9.00) in October but 11/4 (3.75) by festival morning. If you backed it in October and it stays in the race, you have locked in exceptional value.

That is the core of ante-post logic: you are trading price certainty for event certainty.

The Critical Rule: Non-Runner No Bet (NRNB)

The biggest risk in traditional ante-post horse racing betting is the non-runner. Under standard ante-post rules, if your selection is withdrawn from a race — through injury, illness, the trainer changing plans, or the horse simply not making the cut — your stake is lost. You lose even though the race still takes place and you correctly identified a contender.

Non-Runner No Bet (NRNB) changes this. Under NRNB, if your selection does not run, your stake is returned in full. It is now widely offered by GRAI-licensed bookmakers on the major festival ante-post markets (Cheltenham, Grand National, Punchestown), but it is not universal. Check the market terms before betting:

  • NRNB markets are explicitly labelled — do not assume it applies
  • NRNB protects against withdrawal, not cancellation of the entire race
  • Standard each-way terms still apply even on NRNB markets
  • Some bookmakers apply NRNB only after a certain date (e.g. declarations close)

Void Bet Risk: When the Event Itself Is Cancelled

If the event itself is cancelled — a race meeting abandoned due to waterlogged ground, a football match called off indefinitely — most bookmakers void ante-post bets and return stakes. However, the specific treatment varies. Some bookmakers void only if the event is not rescheduled within a set period (typically 30 days). Others may settle on the rescheduled date using your original ante-post bet.

Cheltenham has never been cancelled since World War II, but the 2001 foot-and-mouth crisis came close. Severe weather has abandoned individual Punchestown cards. Reading the terms takes two minutes and can save significant frustration.

Popular Ante-Post Markets in Ireland: 2026–2027

Cheltenham Festival (March 2027): The biggest ante-post market for Irish racing. Willie Mullins and Gordon Elliott dominate the Irish ante-post landscape. Prices for the top novice chases and the Gold Cup open in summer and shorten dramatically by February. NRNB is standard on the main Cheltenham markets at most GRAI-licensed bookmakers.

Grand National (April 2027): The world's most bet-on race. Weights are published in February; this is when serious ante-post activity starts. The race has 40 runners, so non-runner risk is real for horses near the cut. NRNB is widely offered from weights publication onward.

Punchestown Festival 2027 (April/May): Ireland's own festival, often used by trainers to run horses that missed Cheltenham or were disappointing there. Ante-post prices open after Cheltenham. If a horse ran well at Cheltenham but was beaten in a photo finish, the Punchestown price may still represent value before the market re-prices.

Football league winner: Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga and Champions League outrights are live year-round. Betting early in a season or before the transfer window closes can capture significant value — but the event will not be voided if your team implodes, so risk management applies. Frequently Asked Questions

What does ante-post mean in betting?

Ante-post means betting before the day of the event — sometimes weeks or months in advance. The main appeal is longer odds before the market shortens as the event approaches.

What is non-runner no bet (NRNB)?

Non-runner no bet is a bookmaker rule where your stake is returned if your selection is withdrawn from a race before the off. Always check whether NRNB applies before placing an ante-post bet — not all bookmakers offer it on all markets.

What happens to an ante-post bet if the event is cancelled?

If the event is cancelled entirely, standard practice is to void all ante-post bets and return stakes. However, terms vary by bookmaker — always read the specific rules before betting.

Should I bet ante-post or on the day?

Bet ante-post when you have a strong view and the current odds represent genuine value versus what you expect on the day. Bet on the day when you want the security of current form, ground conditions and confirmed entries — but accept shorter odds in return.

What are the best ante-post markets in Ireland?

Cheltenham Festival (March), the Grand National (April), Punchestown Festival (April/May) and Galway Races (July/August) are the biggest ante-post horse racing markets for Irish punters. Football ante-post markets (league winner, top scorer, relegation) run across the season.

The Irish raiding party at Cheltenham is led by Willie Mullins (Closutton, Co. Carlow) and Gordon Elliott (Longwood, Co. Meath).

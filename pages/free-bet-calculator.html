Free bets come in two types: SNR (Stakes Not Returned) — the most common — where you only win the profit, not the stake back, and SR (Stakes Returned), where your full return includes the stake. A €10 SNR free bet at 3.0 returns €20; an SR free bet at 3.0 returns €30. Before claiming any offer, calculate the qualifying bet cost (the expected loss on the bet you must place first) against the free bet's expected value. If the net figure is positive, the offer has genuine value. If not, it may not be worth the effort — or the risk of placing a losing qualifying bet.

How Free Bets Actually Work

Qualifying bet: Most offers require you to place a real-money bet (the qualifying bet) at minimum odds before the free bet is credited. The qualifying bet is a real stake — you can win or lose it. The qualifying cost is the expected loss on that bet based on the bookmaker's margin.

SNR free bets are the most common. Your winnings are profit only — the stake is not returned. At €10 SNR and 3.0 odds, you receive €20 (the €10 profit × 2). An SR free bet at the same odds would return €30.

Net EV tells you whether the offer has positive value across all outcomes. Even if the net EV is slightly negative, the offer may still be worth taking — your qualifying bet might win, turning it into a large overall profit.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is SNR and why does it matter?

SNR stands for Stakes Not Returned. When you place a free bet, the stake is not included in your winnings — only the profit. At odds of 4.0, a €10 SNR free bet returns €30 (3 × €10), not €40. SR (Stakes Returned) includes the stake in the payout, making it worth more. Always check which type you are claiming.

What counts as the qualifying bet cost?

The qualifying cost is the expected loss you incur placing the bet required to trigger a free bet. It equals your stake multiplied by the bookmaker's margin. At a 5% margin on a €20 qualifying bet, the expected cost is €1. The higher the margin and the larger the stake, the more you are expected to lose on the qualifying bet.

Are free bets legal in Ireland after the GRAI?

Yes — free bets and welcome offers remain legal under the GRAI. The authority restricts advertising (no TV or radio before the 9pm watershed, no targeting of self-excluded players) but the offers themselves are permitted at GRAI-licensed bookmakers.

What odds should I use my free bet at?

For SNR free bets, higher odds increase the potential return but reduce the chance of winning. Many punters use free bets on odds between 3.0 and 6.0 to balance expected value with a reasonable winning probability.

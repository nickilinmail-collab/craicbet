Can You Bet on a Credit Card in Ireland? Ban Explained

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

No — you cannot use a credit card to deposit at any GRAI-licensed bookmaker in Ireland. The credit card ban came into effect alongside the GRAI framework on 1 July 2026. Here is what is banned, what is still allowed, and why the rule exists.

What the Credit Card Ban Covers

GRAI-licensed operators are prohibited from accepting credit cards for gambling deposits. This includes Visa Credit, Mastercard Credit, and any card product classified as credit under Irish banking definitions. The ban applies to all GRAI-licensed sites — sports betting, casino (when licensed), and any other form of gambling regulated by the GRAI.

This mirrors the UK ban introduced by the UKGC in 2020 and extends similar logic to the Irish market.

What IS Allowed for Deposits

  • Debit cards (Visa Debit, Mastercard Debit): Fully allowed. The key is “debit” — funds must come from money you already have.
  • Revolut: Allowed — classified as an e-money/prepaid debit card, not a credit instrument. Works at nearly all GRAI-licensed bookmakers.
  • E-wallets (PayPal, Skrill, Neteller): Allowed, subject to individual operator policies. Some operators exclude e-wallet deposits from bonus eligibility.
  • Bank transfer: Fully allowed. SEPA transfers are accepted at most operators.
  • Apple Pay / Google Pay: Allowed when linked to a debit card or current account. If linked to a credit card, that specific funding source is blocked.

Why Was the Credit Card Ban Introduced?

Betting with borrowed money (credit) is a recognised risk factor for problem gambling. The ban prevents people from funding gambling with money they do not have. Research from the UK post-ban showed a reduction in gambling-related debt problems among the population that had previously used credit cards for gambling deposits.

What Happens if You Try to Deposit by Credit Card?

Your deposit will be declined at the payment processor level. Most operators display a clear message explaining that credit cards are not accepted. Some card issuers also block transactions to gambling merchants at the card level regardless of the operator’s settings.

Credit Card Betting in Ireland: Current Status

As of 2024, credit card deposits are banned for gambling in the UK (since April 2020), but no equivalent blanket ban exists in Ireland. However, many Irish-facing operators have voluntarily removed credit card deposits, and individual card issuers (Bank of Ireland, AIB, etc.) may decline gambling transactions by default. Why Credit Card Betting Carries Extra Risk

  • Borrowing to gamble: Using a credit card means betting with borrowed money — losses must be repaid with interest (typically 20–25% APR).
  • Cash advance fees: Some banks treat gambling deposits as cash advances, charging a fee of 2–3% plus a higher interest rate from day one.
  • Credit score: Regular gambling deposits on a credit card can be flagged by lenders during mortgage or loan applications.
  • Responsible gambling: If you feel you need credit to fund gambling, speak to the Gambling Awareness Trust: 1800 753 753 or visit gamblingcare.ie. Gambling should only be funded from disposable income.

Can you bet with a credit card in Ireland?

No — credit card deposits are banned at all GRAI-licensed betting sites under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. Use a debit card, Revolut, e-wallet, or bank transfer instead.

Is Revolut allowed for betting in Ireland?

Yes — Revolut is classified as an e-money/prepaid debit card and is fully accepted at GRAI-licensed bookmakers. It is not a credit instrument and falls outside the credit card ban.

What payment methods can I use to bet in Ireland?

Debit cards (Visa/Mastercard Debit), Revolut, e-wallets (PayPal, Skrill, Neteller), and bank transfer are all allowed. Apple Pay and Google Pay are allowed when linked to a debit account.

Why are credit cards banned for betting in Ireland?

To prevent people from gambling with borrowed money, which is a risk factor for problem gambling. The ban mirrors the UK's UKGC credit card ban introduced in 2020.

Can I use a Monzo or N26 card for betting in Ireland?

These are classified as prepaid debit or e-money accounts, not credit cards. Most GRAI-licensed operators accept them. Check with your specific operator as acceptance varies.

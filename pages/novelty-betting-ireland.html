Last updated: 5 November 2026

Novelty betting covers wagering on outcomes outside traditional sports: TV show results, awards ceremonies, entertainment events and other cultural moments. It is fully legal under the GRAI framework and grows in popularity around major events like the Eurovision Song Contest, the Oscars and reality TV shows.

What is Novelty Betting?

Novelty betting — also called entertainment betting or non-sport betting — involves placing bets on the outcome of events that are not traditional sporting competitions. Common markets include:

  • Eurovision Song Contest: One of the most bet-on novelty events in Ireland; country winner, Irish entry performance
  • Oscar Awards: Best Picture, Best Actor/Actress and other major categories
  • BAFTA and Golden Globes: Film and TV awards markets
  • Reality TV: Who will win Love Island, Big Brother, Dancing with the Stars Ireland etc.
  • Christmas Number One: Which song will top the Irish or UK charts at Christmas

Novelty Betting Under GRAI

Novelty betting is legal under the GRAI framework. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 covers betting on any event of uncertain outcome. GRAI-licensed bookmakers are permitted to offer these markets. The same responsible gambling rules apply as for sports betting.

Tips for Novelty Betting

Novelty markets can be harder to research than sports betting because outcomes are more subjective (awards) or influenced by public voting (reality TV). Be aware that bookmaker margins on novelty markets are often higher than on sports. No strategy guarantees profit.

White Christmas Betting: Ireland-Specific

"White Christmas" betting is popular in Ireland each year. Key market terms:

  • Official measurement: Most bookmakers settle on whether a flake of snow (or sleet) falls at a specified Met Éireann weather station (commonly Dublin Airport) on 25 December.
  • Not "lying snow": The market usually requires a single snowflake to fall — not a white blanket. Check the specific settlement terms.
  • Historical frequency: Snow on Christmas Day at Dublin Airport is relatively rare — perhaps 10–15 times in the past 50 years, though the exact number depends on how "snowfall" is defined. The odds typically sit around 5/1 to 10/1, implying 9–17% — broadly consistent with historical frequency.

  • Novelty markets carry significantly higher margins than sports — often 15–30%

Is novelty and entertainment betting legal in Ireland?

Yes. Novelty betting on events such as the Eurovision, Oscar awards and reality TV is legal under the GRAI framework. Any event of uncertain outcome can be bet on through a GRAI-licensed bookmaker.

Can I bet on the Eurovision Song Contest in Ireland?

Yes. The Eurovision is one of the most popular novelty betting markets in Ireland. GRAI-licensed bookmakers open markets months before the contest, with ante-post prices for each competing country.

Do novelty betting markets have higher margins than sports betting?

Generally yes. Bookmaker margins on novelty markets like awards and reality TV tend to be higher than on competitive sports markets. Research the implied probability of each market before placing a bet.

Are reality TV betting winnings taxed in Ireland?

No. All betting winnings are tax-free for players in Ireland, regardless of whether the bet is on sport, elections or entertainment events.

Pay TV: Virgin Media Sport and Sky Sports

Live Champions League football in Ireland is primarily available through pay TV subscriptions. Virgin Media Sport and Sky Sports have been the main broadcast homes for the competition in recent seasons. Both offer extensive live coverage, including group-stage and knockout matches, with pre- and post-match analysis.

If you hold a subscription to either service, you can typically watch through their respective apps and online streaming portals as well as on TV. Check the official Virgin Media and Sky websites for current subscription prices and package details, as these change regularly.

Free-to-Air Options

Free-to-air access to live Champions League football in Ireland has historically been limited. RTÉ has at times held rights to show highlights or selected matches, but live broadcast rights for the full competition have typically sat with pay TV providers. For the 2026–27 season, check the latest broadcaster listings from RTÉ and Virgin Media to confirm what is free to air — rights arrangements do change between seasons and we recommend verifying directly rather than relying on any assumed carryover from previous years.

Streaming Services

Streaming access to the Champions League varies by market. DAZN holds Champions League rights in a number of European countries, but its availability and rights coverage in Ireland specifically should be checked on DAZN’s Irish website, as its market-by-market rights differ significantly. If DAZN holds Irish rights for select matches, it offers a streaming-first option without a traditional TV subscription.

Sky Sports subscribers can stream matches via the Sky Go app. Virgin Media subscribers have access via the Virgin Media TV app. Both require an active subscription.

For the most accurate picture of which service carries which match on a given night, check the listings on the UEFA Champions League website or the official schedule pages of the relevant broadcaster a few days before the fixture.

Watching in Pubs

Pubs across Ireland regularly show Champions League matches, particularly the high-profile Tuesday and Wednesday night fixtures. Big-screen showings are common in sports bars in Dublin, Cork, Galway, Limerick and other cities. Groups of friends watching in a pub is one of the most popular ways to follow the competition, especially for knockout matches or fixtures involving clubs with large Irish followings.

Calling ahead to your local pub to confirm they are showing a specific match is always a good idea, as not all pubs pay for the full commercial broadcast licence needed to show every fixture.

Mobile Apps and On Demand

Both Sky Sports and Virgin Media provide mobile apps that allow subscribers to watch live and on-demand content. If you have a qualifying subscription, downloading the respective app means you can follow the Champions League on a smartphone or tablet in Ireland, as long as you are connected to data or Wi-Fi.

RTÉ Player is the free streaming platform from RTÉ — if RTÉ holds any free-to-air rights for Champions League matches or highlights in a given season, those would be available there.

The 2026-27 Champions League Format

The 2026–27 UEFA Champions League uses the 36-team league phase introduced in the 2024–25 season, replacing the old group-stage format. Key points of the new structure:

  • All 36 clubs play eight matches in a single league table (four home, four away), against eight different opponents.
  • The top eight teams in the table advance directly to the Round of 16.
  • Teams finishing 9th to 24th enter a two-legged play-off round for the remaining Round of 16 places.
  • The bottom 12 (25th to 36th) are eliminated entirely from European competition.
  • The expanded format means more matches per week across the league phase and a wider spread of fixtures — better for broadcasters and punters alike in terms of variety.

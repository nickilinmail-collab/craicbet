Why GRAI Licensing Should Be Your First Check

The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) has been issuing remote betting licences since 1 July 2026 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. A GRAI licence is currently the gold standard for an Irish bettor — it means the operator is regulated under Irish law, with Irish legal protections applying to your account.

Specifically, GRAI-licensed operators must:

  • Offer mandatory responsible gambling tools (deposit limits, time limits, self-exclusion).
  • Participate in the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER).
  • Maintain segregated customer funds (player money is kept separate from operating funds).
  • Comply with GRAI advertising restrictions, including restrictions on targeting at-risk individuals.
  • Accept complaints via GRAI's formal dispute resolution process.

Sites without a GRAI licence may still be reputable if they hold a credible EU licence (MGA, UKGC), but they are not subject to Irish regulatory oversight. As more operators obtain GRAI licences in 2026 and beyond, prioritising domestic licence holders becomes increasingly practical.

Odds Quality and Markets

Odds quality is the biggest long-run determinant of your betting returns. A consistently better price — even a few cents in decimal odds — compounds over dozens or hundreds of bets. Always check odds at multiple licensed sites before placing a bet on a major event.

The markets available matter as much as the headline odds. Consider:

  • Irish sports: GAA (football and hurling), horse racing, greyhound racing, League of Ireland — these should be well-covered by any site targeting Irish customers.
  • Football: Premier League, Champions League, international tournaments including Republic of Ireland fixtures.
  • Live / in-play betting: if you enjoy betting during matches, check the in-play markets available and whether the platform remains responsive under load.
  • Niche sports: Rugby, darts, golf, cricket, MMA — look for the specific coverage you need.

Mobile Experience

Most Irish betting now happens on mobile devices. A good mobile experience includes a fast, responsive app or mobile site, easy navigation between markets during events, quick bet placement without excessive confirmation screens, and instant notifications for price changes and results.

Test the mobile experience before committing significant funds. Register, make a small deposit, place a test bet and attempt a withdrawal. This reveals any friction in the process that you'll otherwise discover at a worse time.

Payments for Irish Customers

Credit card gambling deposits are banned under Irish law. The most practical deposit methods for Irish bettors in 2026 are:

  • Revolut — fast, free and widely accepted; some sites treat Revolut as a prepaid card (check the specific site's policy).
  • Visa/Mastercard debit — universally accepted; instant deposits.
  • Bank transfer — slower but no fees for larger amounts.
  • PayPal, Skrill, Neteller — available at some sites; check for extra fees or excluded promotions.

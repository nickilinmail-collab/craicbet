Horse Racing Betting in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Horse racing is woven into Irish culture in a way that is unique even by global standards. Ireland punches far above its weight on the world stage — producing trainers, jockeys and owners whose horses dominate at Cheltenham, Royal Ascot and beyond. For Irish punters, horse racing betting runs year-round, with flat racing from spring through autumn and jumps racing filling the winter and providing the two great festivals: Cheltenham in March and Punchestown in April/May.

Key Irish Racecourses

The Curragh (Co. Kildare) is the home of Irish flat racing — the Irish Classics are run here, including the Irish Derby, Irish 2000 Guineas and Irish Oaks. It has a reputation for fair, galloping tracks that suit progressive horses.

Leopardstown (Dublin) hosts both flat and jumps racing, including the prestigious Christmas Festival in December and the Dublin Racing Festival in February, the last major jumps meeting before Cheltenham. Easy access from Dublin makes it the city's local track.

Punchestown (Co. Kildare) hosts the National Hunt Festival in late April/May, Ireland's national jumps festival, which closes the season. It features its own Grade 1 championship races that often attract top Cheltenham horses.

Galway hosts the famous Galway Races summer festival in July/August — a week-long celebration of horse racing and social life on the west coast. Betting volumes are significant and the competitive handicaps attract punters who enjoy finding value in busy markets.

Fairyhouse hosts the Irish Grand National on Easter Monday — the highest-profile jumps race in Ireland and one of the biggest single-race betting events of the year for Irish punters.

Main Horse Racing Betting Markets

Win: Your selection must finish first. The simplest and most liquid market.

Each-way: Two bets — win and place. The place part pays a fraction of win odds (typically 1/4 or 1/5) for finishing in the first few places (3 or 4 depending on field size). Each-way betting is especially suited to horse racing's larger fields. See our each-way guide and calculator for the maths.

Place only: Some bookmakers offer a place-only market where you bet on the horse finishing in the placed positions at the full win odds. Available on major races with large fields.

Forecast / Reverse Forecast: Predict the first and second in correct order (forecast) or either order (reverse forecast). Prices are set by the Tote forecast.

Festival Betting: Cheltenham and Punchestown

The two festival anchors of the Irish racing calendar attract the most betting activity of the year. Cheltenham Festival in March is effectively a national event for Irish punters, with Irish-trained horses historically dominant. The ante-post markets open months in advance and attract significant money. See our full Cheltenham betting guide.

Punchestown Festival runs five weeks after Cheltenham and closes the jumps season. Many Cheltenham winners return here, sometimes with additional weight, which makes the form translation between the two meetings a central betting question each spring. Our Punchestown 2027 guide covers the key races and historical form.

Best Odds Guaranteed and BOG

Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG) means that if the Starting Price is higher than the early price you took, you are paid at the SP. For horse racing, BOG is one of the most valuable features a bookmaker can offer — it removes the risk of taking a price that shortens significantly by race time. Many GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer BOG on UK and Irish racing, typically for bets placed before 10am on race day. Check the specific terms on each site, as BOG rules vary.

Irish Racecourses: Flat vs Jumps

Ireland has two distinct racing seasons and venue types:

  • Flat racing (spring–autumn): The Curragh (home of Irish Classics — 1,000 Guineas, 2,000 Guineas, Oaks, Derby, St Leger), Leopardstown, Naas, Tipperary, Galway (famous summer festival).
  • National Hunt / jumps (autumn–spring): Punchestown (May festival, Grade 1 chases), Leopardstown (Christmas and February), Fairyhouse (Irish Grand National, Easter), Navan, Thurles.
  • Mixed: Leopardstown and several regional courses host both disciplines across the calendar.

For betting purposes, knowing the type of racing matters: flat racing is about pure speed and form, jumps add variables (falls, jumping errors, stamina in poor ground) that increase uncertainty — particularly relevant for place betting terms (more places offered in jumps handicaps) and ante-post risk.

What are the main horse racing racecourses in Ireland?

Key Irish racecourses include Leopardstown (Dublin), The Curragh (Co. Kildare, home of Irish flat racing), Punchestown (jumps festival), Fairyhouse (Irish Grand National), Galway (summer festival) and Tipperary, Cork and Limerick.

What is an each-way bet in horse racing?

An each-way bet is two bets in one — a win bet and a place bet. The place part pays a fraction of the win odds (typically 1/4 or 1/5) for finishing in the placed positions. It costs twice the stated stake. Use our each-way calculator for the exact maths.

What is Best Odds Guaranteed (BOG)?

BOG means if you take an early price and the SP on race day is bigger, you are paid at the SP. Many GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer BOG on UK and Irish racing — check current terms on each site.

What is the difference between flat and jumps racing for betting?

Flat racing focuses on speed; jumps racing has horses jumping hurdles or fences. Jump racing has more variables (falls, errors) and tends to have more non-runners, which affects ante-post betting risk.

When is Punchestown Festival 2027?

Punchestown Festival typically runs in late April/early May, approximately five weeks after Cheltenham. Exact 2027 dates will be confirmed by Punchestown Racecourse — check their official website.

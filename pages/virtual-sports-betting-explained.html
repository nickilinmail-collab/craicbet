Virtual Sports Betting Explained: RNG Races and the Real Margin

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Virtual sports are computer-generated races and matches — animated horses, greyhounds or footballers whose results are decided by a random number generator (RNG), not by anything happening on a real pitch or track. Licensed bookmakers run them around the clock, with a new "event" every few minutes. This guide explains how they actually work and, in our usual honest-maths style, what that means for the value you get.

How virtual events are decided

Each virtual runner carries fixed odds set by the software. When the event starts, the RNG picks a winner using probabilities tied to those odds, and the animation simply plays out the result that has already been decided. There is no going, no draw bias, no team news — the graphics are presentation, not information.

No form to study — by design

In real racing, a sharp punter can disagree with the market: read the form, spot a horse priced wrong, and bet with an edge. Virtuals remove that possibility entirely. The "form figures" shown on screen are decorative; the true probabilities are the ones the software uses, and they are never published. You cannot out-research an RNG.

The margin is fixed — and usually bigger

Every book carries a margin (overround), but in real sports it varies and competition squeezes it. In virtuals the margin is baked into the product and typically sits well above a real-event book — closer to what you would see on a fixed-odds gaming machine than on a Premier League match. Over hundreds of fast-repeating events, that fixed house edge grinds in a way a Saturday acca never could. Virtuals vs real-event betting

Real-event betting is a market: prices move, opinion matters, and a disciplined punter can occasionally find value. Virtual betting is a casino-style game wearing a sports costume: fixed edge, no information, and an event every three minutes. Neither is "rigged" at a licensed operator — RNGs are tested — but they are different products, and virtuals are closer to RNG casino games than to a real race.

Our honest take on the value

Treat virtuals as paid entertainment, never as a way to make money. The speed of play is the real cost: a fixed edge applied every few minutes adds up far faster than one afternoon bet. If you play them, set a small fixed budget, decide your session length in advance, and skip any temptation to chase a losing run — each event is independent, so no result is ever "due".

Frequently Asked Questions

Are virtual sports fixed?

Not in the sense of predetermined against you personally — licensed operators use tested RNGs. But the odds include a fixed margin, so over many events the house edge is a mathematical certainty.

Can you study form in virtual sports?

No. The on-screen form figures are decoration. The true probabilities live inside the software and are never published, so unlike real racing there is no information edge to find.

Is the margin on virtuals higher than on real sports?

Typically yes. Real-event margins are squeezed by competition; virtuals carry a fixed margin that is usually well above a big football match — and a new event every few minutes multiplies its effect.

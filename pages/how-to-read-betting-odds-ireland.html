How to Read Betting Odds in Ireland

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Betting odds tell you how much you will win relative to your stake. In Ireland you will encounter three formats: fractional (traditional), decimal (now most common online), and American moneyline (less common, used by US sports sites). All three describe the same probability — they are just presented differently.

Fractional Odds (Traditional Irish Format)

Written as X/Y (e.g. 5/1, 7/2, 11/4). The left number is profit; the right is stake. At 5/1: stake €10, win €50 profit + €10 stake back = €60 total return. At 7/2: stake €10, win €35 profit + €10 back = €45 total return. If the right number is bigger than the left (e.g. 1/2), the selection is odds-on — you risk more than you win.

Decimal Odds (Standard Online Format)

Written as a single number (e.g. 6.00, 4.50, 1.50). Multiply your stake by the decimal to get total return including stake. At 6.00: €10 × 6.00 = €60 total (€50 profit). To convert from fractional: (5/1) = 5 ÷ 1 + 1 = 6.00. Decimal odds include your stake in the figure; fractional odds do not.

Implied Probability and Value

Implied probability = 1 ÷ decimal odds × 100. At 6.00 decimal, implied probability = 1/6 = 16.7%. If you believe the true probability is higher than 16.7%, you have a value bet. If you think the true probability is lower, the bet has negative value. The bookmaker builds in a margin (usually 4–8% on main markets), so implied probabilities across a market add up to more than 100%.

Why do some sites use decimal and some fractional?

Decimal odds are now standard across most online bookmakers. Fractional odds remain common in Irish and UK horse racing tradition. Most apps let you switch between formats in settings.

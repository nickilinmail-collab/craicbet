How to Read Betting Odds: Fractional, Decimal and American Explained

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Odds are the language of betting, and Irish bookmakers offer three formats: fractional (the traditional Irish and UK format), decimal (increasingly default on most sites), and American (less common, used mainly for US sports). This guide explains all three, with conversion formulas and practical examples.

Fractional Odds (Traditional Irish/UK Format)

Fractional odds show profit relative to stake. The numerator is the profit; the denominator is the stake. So 5/2 means: stake €2, profit €5. Total return: €7 (€5 profit + €2 stake back).

Key examples: 1/2 (odds-on, profit half the stake), 1/1 (evens), 2/1 (double your stake), 5/1 (five times your stake as profit).

Decimal Odds (European Format)

Decimal odds show total return per unit staked, including your stake back. Total return = Stake × Decimal odds.

Decimal odds of 3.0 on a €10 bet: €10 × 3.0 = €30 total return (€20 profit + €10 stake). Note: fractional 2/1 and decimal 3.0 are identical.

Converting fractional to decimal: (Numerator ÷ Denominator) + 1. So 5/2 = (5 ÷ 2) + 1 = 3.5 decimal.

American (Moneyline) Odds

Positive (+) means profit on a $100 stake. Negative (−) means how much you must stake to profit $100.

  • +200: stake $100, profit $200 (same as 2/1 or 3.0)
  • −200: stake $200 to profit $100 (same as 1/2 or 1.5)

Implied Probability: The Most Useful Conversion

Implied probability = 1 ÷ Decimal odds × 100. This tells you what percentage chance the bookmaker is pricing in.

  • 2.0 (evens): 50% implied
  • 3.0 (2/1): 33.3% implied
  • 5.0 (4/1): 20% implied
  • 10.0 (9/1): 10% implied

Compare implied probability with your own assessment of likelihood. If you think there is a 40% chance of an event but it is priced at 25% (3.0), that is a value bet.

Reading Odds: Common Questions

  • Fractional odds show profit/stake: 2/1 means €2 profit for every €1 staked
  • Decimal odds include your stake: 3.00 returns €30 total on a €10 bet (€20 profit)
  • American/moneyline: minus = favourite (stake to win €100); plus = underdog (win on €100)
  • Implied probability: 1 ÷ decimal odds × 100 (e.g. 2.50 = 40%); sum across a market exceeds 100% due to bookmaker margin
What is the difference between fractional and decimal odds?

Fractional odds (5/2) show profit relative to stake. Decimal odds (3.5) show total return per unit staked, including stake. They convey the same information in different formats.

How do I convert fractional odds to decimal?

Divide the top number by the bottom, then add 1. Example: 5/2 = (5÷2) + 1 = 3.5. Or: 7/4 = (7÷4) + 1 = 2.75.

How do I calculate my return from decimal odds?

Stake × Decimal odds = Total return (including stake). €25 × 3.5 = €87.50 total (€62.50 profit + €25 stake).

What is implied probability in betting?

1 ÷ Decimal odds × 100. Tells you what chance the bookmaker is pricing in. If the implied probability is lower than your own estimate, it may be a value bet.

Which odds format do Irish betting sites use by default?

Many Irish sites default to fractional for horse racing and decimal for football, but most allow you to change the display format in account settings.

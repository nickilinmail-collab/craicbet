Stake.com Ireland 2026: Blocked by GRAI — What Irish Players Need to Know

What Is Stake.com?

Stake.com is one of the world’s largest online betting and casino platforms by volume, founded in Australia and owned by Stake.com N.V., a company incorporated in Curaçao. The platform covers a wide range of sports betting markets alongside casino products, and has built a substantial global following, particularly among younger bettors.

Stake.com has pursued high-profile brand ambassador partnerships with well-known sports figures and has been a significant advertiser in a number of international sports. It operates under a Curaçao eGaming licence, which is its primary regulatory authorisation. Curaçao licensing, while widely used in the online gambling industry, does not carry the same player protections as licences from regulators such as the UK Gambling Commission, the Malta Gaming Authority, or — crucially for Irish players — the GRAI.

That regulatory gap is precisely what brought Stake.com into conflict with Irish gambling law in 2026.

Why Was Stake.com Blocked in Ireland?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Under the Act, any operator wishing to lawfully target Irish customers must hold a GRAI remote betting licence — regardless of what licences it holds elsewhere.

Stake.com, holding a Curaçao licence but not a GRAI licence, was therefore operating outside Ireland’s new regulatory framework when it continued to accept Irish customers after July 2026. Following GRAI intervention in September 2026, Stake.com restricted access for users in Ireland rather than continue to operate without Irish regulatory authorisation.

The sequence is consistent with the approach the GRAI has taken towards a number of operators in the period since licences became mandatory: operators that have not yet obtained a GRAI licence, or that choose not to pursue one, are required to cease targeting Irish customers.

Does Stake.com Have a GRAI Licence?

As of November 2026, Stake.com does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means Stake.com has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

Stake.com has reportedly applied for a GRAI licence (reported by iGamingExpert, September 2026). If that application is successful, Stake.com could lawfully return to the Irish market. However, an application alone does not grant a licence: Stake.com must appear on the GRAI register before it is authorised to target Irish customers.

You can check the current state of Stake.com’s GRAI application — and whether a licence has been granted — directly on the GRAI public register at grai.ie. CraicBet will update this page if Stake.com’s status changes.

What Does This Mean for Irish Players?

In practice, Stake.com is currently not accessible to Irish users under its standard terms. The platform restricted Irish access in September 2026 as a direct consequence of the GRAI intervention.

Some Irish players have enquired about using a VPN to bypass the restriction. While a VPN can technically route traffic through a different country, doing so carries real risks:

  • No GRAI protections: If you access Stake.com via a VPN, you are operating outside Irish regulatory oversight. Deposit limits, NGEI self-exclusion integration, the credit card ban, GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines, and the right to escalate complaints to the GRAI — none of these apply to your account.
  • Terms & conditions breach: Stake.com’s terms almost certainly prohibit use from jurisdictions where the platform is restricted. Using a VPN to bypass a geo-restriction is typically a breach of those terms.
  • Account seizure risk: If Stake.com detects that an account has been accessed from a restricted jurisdiction via VPN, it may suspend the account and decline to pay out any balance or winnings.
  • No formal recourse: Because Stake.com does not hold a GRAI licence, an Irish player in dispute with Stake.com has no formal route to the GRAI for escalation.

CraicBet does not recommend circumventing regulatory restrictions. Irish players looking for a Stake.com alternative are better served by one of the growing number of GRAI-licensed operators.

If and when Stake.com obtains a GRAI licence and re-enters the Irish market, the position will change. Until then, the safest and most legally straightforward option for Irish bettors is to use a GRAI-licensed bookmaker.

GRAI Annual Report 2026 — What It Means for Irish Punters

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

On 30 July 2026 the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) published its first annual report, covering the period from its establishment on 5 March 2025. It is the most detailed public picture yet of how Ireland's new gambling regulator is built, what it has done, and where Irish punters stand under the new framework.

What Is the GRAI Annual Report?

The GRAI annual report is a statutory publication that sets out the Authority's activities, finances and regulatory milestones for the period under review. The first report, published 30 July 2026, covers the foundational period: how GRAI hired staff, designed its licensing systems, launched its consumer protection framework and developed its enforcement capabilities.

The report was produced under the oversight of GRAI CEO Anne Marie Caulfield and published under the auspices of the Minister for Justice, Jim O'Callaghan.

Key Licensing Milestones

The timeline in the annual report confirms the schedule Irish punters and operators have been working to:

  • March 2025: GRAI formally established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.
  • October 2025: Statement of Strategy 2025–2027 launched, setting out six objectives: licensing, monitoring & compliance, enforcement, consumer protection, governance, and a digital-first approach.
  • February 2026: Remote betting licence applications opened to operators.
  • 1 July 2026: First remote betting licences issued — the new Irish regulatory regime goes live for online operators.
  • December 2026: In-person (betting shop) licences expected.
  • 2027–2028: Gaming and casino licences scheduled — no GRAI casino licences have been issued to date.

Consumer Protections Introduced

The annual report confirms the consumer protections now in force under GRAI-licensed operators. These include:

  • Age verification required before any gambling activity
  • Credit card deposits banned
  • Operators must pay out winnings promptly
  • Operators must close accounts on a customer's request
  • Deposit limits must be effective immediately upon request
  • The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER), built on a Salesforce platform developed by Deloitte Ireland under a €10.4m contract signed in February 2025

GRAI also ran a brand awareness campaign in October 2025 to inform the public about the new framework.

Problem Gambling — ESRI Findings

The annual report draws on research by the Economic and Social Research Institute (ESRI) that provides a stark reassessment of problem gambling in Ireland. ESRI found that harmful gambling in Ireland is "about ten times higher than previously thought," with approximately 130,000 adults in Ireland experiencing problem gambling.

A separate ESRI/GRAI study found that free bets and money-back guarantees increased gambling spending by more than 10% in controlled experiments — a finding that is likely to inform future restrictions on promotional offers.

Enforcement Powers

The GRAI has significant enforcement tools under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024:

  • Fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover (whichever is greater)
  • Criminal penalties including prison sentences of up to 8 years
  • Compliance agreements, preliminary notices, notices of non-compliance
  • Directed investigations

GRAI has also built international cooperation agreements — with the UK, Belgium and Gibraltar in 2025, and with Denmark, Alderney and the Nevada Gaming Control Board in 2026.

Building the Regulator — Staff and Systems

At 31 December 2025, GRAI had 28 full-time staff, expected to grow significantly through 2026. The €10.4m Deloitte Ireland contract (February 2025) covers the entire regulatory technology stack: licensing, compliance, enforcement and the National Gambling Exclusion Register — all built on Salesforce.

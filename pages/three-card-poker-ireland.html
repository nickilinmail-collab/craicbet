Three Card Poker Ireland 2026: Rules, Strategy & Where to Play

Updated: November 2026 • Reading time: 8 min
Quick Summary: Three Card Poker is one of the fastest table games in any casino — online or live. With a simple optimal strategy (fold or raise on one rule) and a house edge around 3.4%, it sits squarely in the middle ground: easier than blackjack, with a lower edge than most slots.

How to Play Three Card Poker

Three Card Poker is a casino table game invented by Derek Webb in the 1990s. It pits your three cards directly against the dealer's three cards. Unlike Texas Hold'em, there are no community cards, no bluffing and no interaction with other players. It's purely you versus the house.

A round proceeds like this:

  1. Place your Ante bet (and optionally a Pair Plus side bet) before any cards are dealt.
  2. Three cards are dealt to you and three to the dealer, all face down.
  3. View your cards. You then choose to fold (lose your Ante) or place a Play bet equal to your Ante.
  4. Dealer reveals: the dealer needs at least Queen-high to "qualify." If the dealer doesn't qualify, your Ante is paid 1:1 and your Play bet is returned. If the dealer qualifies and you beat them, both bets pay 1:1.
  5. Ante Bonus: regardless of whether you beat the dealer, a Straight or better earns an Ante Bonus: Straight pays 1:1, Three of a Kind 4:1, Straight Flush 5:1.

Three Card Poker Strategy

The optimal Three Card Poker strategy is straightforward enough to memorise in under a minute:

Raise with Queen-6-4 or better. Fold everything else.

This means: if your hand is at least Q-6-4 (Queen high, with a 6 as the second-highest card and a 4 as the kicker), place the Play bet. If your hand is worse than Q-6-4 — including Q-6-3 — fold and lose your Ante. Following this rule brings the house edge on the Ante/Play combination to roughly 3.37%.

Deviating from this rule costs you. Folding hands you should raise gives the house an extra edge; calling with weak hands you should fold has the same effect.

Side Bets: Pair Plus

The Pair Plus bet wins if your hand contains a pair or better, completely independent of the dealer's hand and the Ante/Play bet result. The house edge on Pair Plus is approximately 2.32% at standard pay tables. It's a reasonable bet in isolation, but side bets in table games always carry a higher edge than the base game — treat them as entertainment spending rather than strategy.

Some casinos offer a 6-Card Bonus side bet combining your three cards with the dealer's three cards for the best five-card poker hand. The house edge varies widely by pay table; check the rules before placing it.

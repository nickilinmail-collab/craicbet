Is It Available in Ireland?

Yes. bet365 is available to Irish players and accepts registrations from Ireland. Unlike some offshore operators that have been geo-blocked by the GRAI for operating without a licence, bet365 holds a GRAI remote betting licence, which can be verified independently at the official GRAI public licence register on grai.ie. This means Irish players can open an account and bet on sports legally under Irish regulatory oversight.

The casino section of bet365 is a separate product and operates under a Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) licence. It is important to understand that the GRAI has issued zero casino or gaming licences as of November 2026 — these are expected to become available from 2027 or 2028. So while casino play at bet365 is covered by MGA regulation (a reputable and well-established framework), it is not yet covered by a GRAI casino licence.

What Does GRAI Licensing Mean for Irish Players?

The GRAI (Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland) began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. When a bookmaker holds a GRAI remote betting licence, Irish players benefit from a specific set of mandatory protections that do not apply at offshore-only operators:

  • Mandatory deposit limits: GRAI-licensed operators must offer deposit limit tools and, in some cases, enforce cooling-off periods before increases take effect.
  • NGEI self-exclusion: Ireland's National Gambling Exclusion Ireland (NGEI) register allows players to self-exclude across all GRAI-licensed operators simultaneously — one decision blocks you everywhere.
  • Advertising standards: GRAI-licensed operators are subject to Ireland's advertising rules, restricting how and when gambling is promoted.
  • Formal complaints route: If a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator cannot be resolved directly, you have recourse through the GRAI's alternative dispute resolution (ADR) process. See our GRAI complaints guide for step-by-step instructions.
  • Responsible gambling obligations: Operators must provide access to gamblingcare.ie and similar support resources.

For a deeper look at all the rights and protections GRAI licensing gives you, see our GRAI player protection guide.

Sports betting at bet365 is covered by a GRAI remote betting licence. This means the betting product is fully under Irish regulatory oversight. You can verify the licence at any time on the GRAI public register.

⚠️ Casino Product — MGA Licensed (Not GRAI)

Casino games at bet365 — slots, live dealer, table games — operate under a Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) licence. This is a reputable regulatory framework with strong player protections, but it is not a GRAI licence. The GRAI has issued zero casino licences as of November 2026. GRAI casino licences are expected from 2027 to 2028. No bookmaker or casino can legitimately claim to hold a GRAI casino licence at this point.

This two-licence structure is not unique to bet365. All major operators offering both betting and casino in Ireland currently have GRAI betting licences paired with MGA, UKGC, or Gibraltar licences for the casino side. Deposit Methods for Irish Players

Irish players have a range of payment options when depositing at bet365:

  • Revolut: Revolut is widely accepted and is one of the most popular deposit methods for Irish players, given the high uptake of Revolut banking in Ireland.
  • Visa and Mastercard debit cards: Standard debit cards work. Note that credit cards cannot be used for gambling transactions in Ireland under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — debit cards only.
  • Bank transfer: Standard bank transfers (including SEPA) are supported, though these typically take longer to clear than card or Revolut deposits.
  • PayPal and other e-wallets: PayPal is accepted at many major operators including bet365 — check the operator's current cashier for the full list at the time of deposit.

Minimum deposits are typically in the €5–€10 range. Withdrawal processing times vary by method; debit card and Revolut withdrawals are generally among the fastest.

How to Check Any Operator's GRAI Licence

One of the most useful things the GRAI has done is publish a freely accessible register of all licensed operators. You do not need to take any website's word for it — you can verify directly. See our full GRAI licence checker guide for a walkthrough, or follow these three steps:

  1. Go to grai.ie and navigate to the Licensing & Regulation section.
  2. Open the public licence register (the GRAI makes this available as a downloadable list or searchable page).
  3. Search by operator name or parent company name. A valid GRAI remote betting licence will appear in the format GRAI-XXXX-RB-26-XXXX.

If an operator claims to hold a GRAI licence but does not appear on the register, that is a serious red flag. If an operator claims to hold a GRAI casino licence, that is factually impossible at present — zero have been issued.

What the GRAI Rules Say About Free Bets

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI licensing conditions, free bets and bonus offers remain legal in Ireland. The regulations changed how they can be promoted, not whether they can exist. Key restrictions under GRAI inducements rules:

  • No pre-watershed TV advertising: Bookmaker TV ads showing free bet offers cannot air before 9pm.
  • No targeting of self-excluded players: Operators must remove self-excluded customers from all promotional communications immediately.
  • No bonuses to known problem gamblers: Operators must use responsible gambling signals to identify and stop bonus offers to customers showing distress patterns.
  • Transparent T&Cs: Bonus terms must be clearly communicated before acceptance — not buried in footnotes.

The offers themselves are legal. But the GRAI's framework means operators are, over time, moving toward more transparent bonus structures rather than headline-chasing. This is good for bettors.

Free Bet Types: Explained Honestly

No Deposit Free Bets

A no deposit free bet is credited to your account without requiring any deposit first. The offer sounds ideal — risk nothing, potentially win real money. The reality: no deposit free bets are almost always small (€5–€10), come with strict minimum odds requirements (evens or above, sometimes higher), must be used within 24–72 hours, and are Stake Not Returned (SNR). On a €5 no deposit free bet used at evens (1/1), a win returns exactly €5 — the stake is not returned. That is a maximum usable value of €5, with a probability (at evens) of roughly 50% (minus the bookmaker's margin). Expected value: approximately €2.25–€2.50 on a €5 no deposit bet. Useful for exploring a site; not a path to serious value.

Deposit-Matched Free Bets (Bet and Get)

The standard model in Irish betting: deposit and place a qualifying bet, receive a free bet in return. For example: deposit €20, bet €20 at minimum odds of 1/1, receive a €20 free bet. This is the most common form of welcome offer. The genuine usable value calculation:

  • Place the qualifying bet: expected loss from odds margin ≈ 5–8% of stake = €1–€1.60 cost
  • Receive €20 SNR free bet: used at 2/1 (3.00), a win returns €40 (winnings only). Probability ≈ 33% (at 2/1 with margin stripped). EV ≈ 33% × €40 = ~€13.20
  • Net expected value of the offer: approximately €11–€12 after qualifying bet cost

This is a reasonable return for opening a new account. It is not, however, €20 of free money. Never treat the headline figure as the real value.

Stake Returned (SR) Free Bets

Some offers credit a free bet where the stake is returned alongside winnings. Using the same example: a €20 SR free bet at 2/1. A win returns €60 (€40 winnings + €20 stake). The expected value is significantly higher — roughly €19–€20 at fair odds. SR free bets are worth twice the EV of SNR free bets at equivalent odds, all else being equal. If you see an SR offer, it is worth more than an SNR offer of the same headline size. Very few mainstream bookmakers offer SR; it is worth flagging when you find one.

Enhanced Odds / Price Boosts

Price boosts give you artificially inflated odds on a specific selection — for example, a football team to win at 5/1 instead of the standard 7/4. These are genuine value moments but come with restrictions: usually capped at a maximum stake of €5–€10, single bets only, and you must opt in before the game. The EV calculation is straightforward: at standard 7/4 (45% implied probability), if you think the team has a 50% chance to win, the EV at 5/1 is +€1.50 on a €5 stake. Worth taking; don't stake heavily.

GRAI Rules and Free Bets: What Changes for You

Post-GRAI, any bookmaker offering you a free bet as an Irish customer must hold a GRAI remote betting licence. This matters because:

  • If the bookmaker awards a free bet and then refuses to pay the resulting winnings, you can complain to the GRAI (not just the operator's foreign complaints department).
  • GRAI-licensed operators must credit winnings from free bets in a timely manner as a licence condition.
  • Bonus T&Cs must be communicated clearly; misleading terms can be a licence breach.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are free bets still legal in Ireland in 2026?

Yes. Free bets and welcome offers remain legal in Ireland under the GRAI framework. The GRAI's inducements rules restrict where and how they can be advertised — no TV ads before 9pm, no targeting self-excluded players — but the offers themselves are available at GRAI-licensed bookmakers.

What is a wagering requirement on a free bet?

A wagering requirement means you must meet certain conditions before the free bet or its winnings become available. For qualifying free bets (bet and get), you must typically place a qualifying real-money bet at minimum odds first. For bonus cash, you usually must wager it a set number of times. Always read the full T&Cs before accepting any offer.

What is the difference between a free bet and a matched deposit bonus?

A free bet is a token for a single wager where you risk no cash. A matched deposit bonus adds extra funds to your account proportional to your deposit. Free bets are usually simpler and quicker to understand. Matched deposit bonuses come with higher wagering requirements but can represent more total value for active bettors who meet the conditions.

What do 'stake not returned' and 'stake returned' mean?

Stake Not Returned (SNR) means that when your free bet wins, you get only the profit — not the free bet stake itself. Stake Returned (SR) means you get winnings plus the stake back. Example: €10 free bet, 3/1 winner. SNR = €30. SR = €40. SR free bets are significantly more valuable; most mainstream bookmakers offer SNR.

Can I use a free bet on horse racing or GAA?

Usually yes, but check the specific offer T&Cs. Most free bets can be used on horse racing, GAA, and football. Minimum odds restrictions apply — you typically cannot use a free bet on a very short-priced favourite. Some offers specify selected sports only. Always read the terms before placing.

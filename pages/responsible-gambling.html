Signs of Problem Gambling

Problem gambling doesn't always look the way you expect. It can develop gradually and affect anyone, regardless of income or background. Here are warning signs to be aware of — in yourself or someone close to you:

  • Spending more money or time gambling than you can afford
  • Feeling restless or irritable when trying to cut down or stop
  • Chasing losses — gambling more to try to win back money you've lost
  • Borrowing money or selling possessions to fund gambling
  • Lying to family, friends, or colleagues about how much you gamble
  • Neglecting work, education, or family responsibilities because of gambling
  • Feeling anxious, depressed, or guilty about your gambling
  • Gambling to escape problems or relieve negative feelings
  • Making repeated unsuccessful attempts to control or stop gambling
  • Needing to gamble with increasing amounts to feel the same excitement

If you recognise several of these signs, it doesn't mean you're weak or flawed. Problem gambling is a recognised condition, and effective help is available.

Self-Assessment: Is Gambling Becoming a Problem?

Answer these questions honestly. They are not a clinical diagnosis, but they can help you reflect on your relationship with gambling:

📋 Ask Yourself:

  1. Have you ever felt the need to bet more and more money?
  2. Have you ever lied to people important to you about how much you gamble?
  3. Do you ever gamble to escape worries or when you're feeling down?
  4. Have you ever tried to win back money you've lost by gambling more?
  5. Has your gambling caused financial problems for you or your household?
  6. Have you ever felt guilty about the way you gamble or what happens when you gamble?
  7. Have you ever borrowed money or sold anything to get money to gamble?
  8. Has gambling caused any health problems, including stress or anxiety?
  9. Have people criticised your gambling or told you that you have a problem?
  10. Have you ever felt you would like to stop gambling but didn't think you could?

If you answered "yes" to 3 or more questions, consider speaking to a professional. The services listed below are free and confidential.

Responsible Gambling Tools

Most licensed casinos offer built-in tools to help you stay in control. Here's what's available and how to use each one:

Deposit Limits

Set a maximum amount you can deposit per day, week, or month. Once the limit is reached, you cannot deposit more until the period resets. This is the single most effective tool for managing your spending. Tip: Set your limit before you start playing, not after a loss.

Loss Limits

A loss limit caps the total amount you can lose within a period. Unlike deposit limits, which control what goes in, loss limits control what goes out. If you reach your loss limit, you cannot place further bets until the period resets.

Session Time Limits

Set a maximum duration for your playing sessions. When the time is up, you're automatically logged out. This prevents the common problem of losing track of time while playing — something that happens more easily than you'd think.

Reality Checks

A pop-up notification appears at regular intervals (e.g., every 30 or 60 minutes) showing you how long you've been playing and your net win or loss for the session. It's a simple but powerful way to stay aware of time and money spent.

Self-Exclusion

If you need a complete break from gambling, self-exclusion lets you ban yourself from a casino for a set period — typically 6 months, 1 year, or 5 years. During this time, you cannot access your account, and the casino is obligated to refuse your bets. The GRAI is introducing a national self-exclusion register that will allow you to exclude yourself from all licensed operators in Ireland with a single request.

Account Closure

You can permanently close your account at any time. Any remaining balance will be returned to you. This is different from self-exclusion in that it's immediate and permanent.

Irish Support Services — Free & Confidential

Ireland has several excellent support services. All are free, confidential, and staffed by trained professionals.

Gambling Awareness Trust

Freephone: 1800 753 753

The national helpline for anyone affected by gambling. Provides information, support, and referrals. Available to gamblers, their family members, and concerned friends.

GamblingCare.ie

Website: www.gamblingcare.ie

A free, confidential counselling service funded by the HSE. Offers one-to-one counselling (in person and online), group therapy, and family support. Available nationwide.

Dunlewey Addiction Services

Location: Dublin

Provides professional addiction counselling and group therapy for people affected by gambling. Offers both individual sessions and structured programmes. Based in Dublin but also offers remote sessions.

Gamblers Anonymous Ireland

Website: www.gamblersanonymous.ie

A fellowship of people who share their experience, strength, and hope to solve their common problem. Free meetings held across Ireland, both in person and online. No referral needed — just turn up.

How the GRAI Protects Players

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI), established in July 2026, has introduced several measures to protect Irish players:

  • National self-exclusion register — one request to exclude yourself from all licensed operators
  • Advertising restrictions — limits on when and how gambling can be advertised, with specific protections for under-18s
  • Mandatory player protection tools — all licensed operators must offer deposit limits, reality checks, and self-exclusion
  • Complaints process — a formal mechanism to escalate disputes with operators
  • Operator accountability — licence conditions that require operators to intervene when they identify problem gambling behaviour

Tips for Staying in Control

  1. Set a budget before you play. Decide how much you can afford to lose and treat it as the cost of entertainment. When it's gone, stop.
  2. Set a time limit. Use session timers or set a phone alarm. It's easy to lose track of time.
  3. Never chase losses. If you've had a bad session, walk away. Trying to win it back almost always makes things worse.
  4. Don't gamble when upset, stressed, or under the influence. Emotional or impaired gambling leads to poor decisions.
  5. Use deposit limits. Set them at the start of the month and leave them in place. Reducing a limit takes effect immediately; increasing it typically requires a 24–72 hour cooling-off period.
  6. Take regular breaks. Step away from the screen. Walk, eat, drink water. A clear head makes better decisions.
  7. Remember: the house always has an edge. No system, strategy, or lucky streak changes the mathematical reality that casinos are designed to profit over time.
  8. Keep gambling separate from your finances. Never use rent money, bill money, or savings. Never borrow to gamble.
  9. Talk to someone. If gambling is causing you stress, talking helps. Whether it's a friend, family member, or one of the services above — don't carry it alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if I have a gambling problem?▾
Common signs include spending more than you can afford, chasing losses, lying about gambling, neglecting responsibilities, and feeling anxious or guilty about your gambling. Use the self-assessment questions above as a starting point. If you're worried, the fact that you're asking the question suggests it's worth speaking to a professional — call 1800 753 753 for free, confidential advice.
How do I self-exclude from online casinos?▾
Most casinos have a self-exclusion option in your account settings, usually under "Responsible Gambling" or "Player Protection." You can typically choose periods of 6 months, 1 year, or 5 years. The GRAI is also introducing a national self-exclusion register that will let you exclude yourself from all licensed operators at once.
Can I set deposit limits at online casinos?▾
Yes. All reputable licensed casinos offer deposit limits (daily, weekly, and monthly). You can set and reduce limits instantly in your account settings. Increases to limits usually require a 24–72 hour cooling-off period to prevent impulsive decisions.
Is help for problem gambling free in Ireland?▾
Yes. The Gambling Awareness Trust helpline (1800 753 753), GamblingCare.ie counselling, and Gamblers Anonymous meetings are all completely free and confidential. You don't need a referral from your GP — you can contact them directly.
Can I block gambling sites on my phone or computer?▾
Yes. Software tools like Gamban, BetBlocker (free), and GamBlock can block access to gambling websites and apps on your devices. Your internet service provider may also offer parental controls that can block gambling categories. These tools add an extra layer of protection alongside self-exclusion.

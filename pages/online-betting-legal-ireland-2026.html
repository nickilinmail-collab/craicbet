Is Online Betting Legal in Ireland in 2026? The Complete Answer

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Yes, online betting is legal in Ireland in 2026 — but the legal framework changed significantly on 1 July 2026 with the introduction of GRAI remote betting licences. This page gives you the complete answer: what the law says, what changed, what it means for players, and the important distinction between online betting and online casino gambling.

The Short Answer

Online sports betting by Irish players is legal. There is no Irish law that makes it a criminal offence for a player to place bets online. The legal obligations fall on operators, not players. Since 1 July 2026, operators must hold a GRAI remote betting licence to legally take bets from Irish customers. Players choosing to bet on unlicensed sites are not committing a criminal offence, but they lose significant consumer protections.

What Changed on 1 July 2026

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the GRAI as Ireland's gambling regulator and created the new licensing framework. The critical date was 1 July 2026, when GRAI began issuing remote betting licences. This was the moment the new framework became operational for online betting.

From 1 July 2026:

  • Operating an online betting service targeting Irish customers without a GRAI licence became a criminal offence.
  • Approximately 41 remote betting licences have been issued to operators who applied and met GRAI standards.
  • GRAI-licensed operators became subject to mandatory responsible gambling conditions, advertising rules, age verification requirements and consumer protection standards.
  • The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) became operational for GRAI-licensed operators.

The transition period between the Act's passage (2024) and licensing going live (July 2026) meant that operators were technically operating in a pre-licensing period. July 2026 is the definitive line after which unlicensed online betting is unambiguously illegal for operators.

What About Online Casino Gambling?

This is where the picture is more complex. As of November 2026, GRAI has not issued any casino gaming licences. Only remote betting licences have been issued. Casino gaming licences are expected to follow in a later phase, with GRAI and the government indicating a timeline of 2027–2028.

This creates a significant gap. Many operators offer both sports betting and casino games. An operator holding a GRAI remote betting licence can legitimately take sports bets from Irish customers, but their casino offering exists in a licensing grey area until GRAI issues gaming licences.

For players: placing bets on casino sites holding a reputable licence from another jurisdiction (such as the Malta Gaming Authority) is not explicitly illegal for the player in Ireland, but it falls outside the GRAI protection framework. You cannot complain to GRAI about such an operator's casino activities.

The Player's Legal Position

To be clear on a question many Irish bettors ask: you are not breaking Irish law by placing bets on a site that is not GRAI-licensed. The criminal liability in the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 falls on operators who provide services without a licence, not on customers who use those services.

However, "not illegal" is not the same as "as safe as licensed." Betting on an unlicensed site means:

  • No NGER self-exclusion coverage.
  • No mandatory deposit limits.
  • No formal complaint route to a regulator.
  • No fund protection requirements.
  • No age verification standards the site is held to by an Irish authority.

The risks are consumer risks, not criminal risks for the player. See our guide to unlicensed betting risks for a full breakdown.

What About Sports Exchange Betting and Peer-to-Peer Betting?

Betting exchanges and peer-to-peer betting platforms require GRAI licensing under the same framework as conventional bookmakers. An operator running a betting exchange and accepting Irish customers must hold an appropriate GRAI licence. The licence categories cover different types of gambling activity, and exchanges are included in the scope of the Act.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is online betting legal in Ireland in 2026?

Yes. Online betting by players is legal. Since 1 July 2026, operators must hold a GRAI remote betting licence to legally offer online betting to Irish customers. Players are not committing an offence by placing bets online.

Can I legally bet on a site without a GRAI licence?

As a player, there is no Irish law making it a criminal offence to place bets on an unlicensed site. However, you lose all GRAI consumer protections: no NGER coverage, no mandated deposit limits, no complaint route to GRAI, no fund protection.

What changed on 1 July 2026 for online betting in Ireland?

GRAI began issuing remote betting licences. Operating online betting targeting Irish customers without a GRAI licence became a criminal offence. Approximately 41 licences have been issued.

Is online casino gambling legal in Ireland in 2026?

GRAI has not issued any casino gaming licences as of November 2026 — only remote betting licences. Casino licences are expected from 2027–2028. Playing casino games on MGA-licensed sites is in a legal grey area for players.

Where can I check if a betting site has a GRAI licence?

Check the public register at grai.ie. GRAI-licensed operators must also display their licence number on their website in format GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-NNNN.

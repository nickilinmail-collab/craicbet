Top 5 Risks of Betting on Unlicensed Sites in Ireland in 2026

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

With approximately 33 GRAI remote betting licences now issued, there is no need to use an unlicensed offshore betting site in Ireland — but many players still do. Here are the five biggest practical risks of choosing an unlicensed site in 2026, with specific reference to what GRAI licensing actually protects you from.

Risk 1: No Recourse If Winnings Are Withheld

This is the risk most players think about when they think about unlicensed sites — and it is the most acute. When a GRAI-licensed operator withholds winnings, you have:

  • A formal complaint right to the operator.
  • Escalation to the operator's ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution) scheme.
  • Escalation to GRAI, which can investigate and take enforcement action including fines up to €20m or 10% of turnover.

When an unlicensed offshore operator withholds your winnings, you have:

  • Whatever internal complaints process they choose to operate (there is no requirement to have one).
  • Potentially a civil claim in the courts of whatever offshore jurisdiction the site operates from — which is typically slow, expensive and uncertain.
  • No GRAI complaint route, because GRAI has no jurisdiction.

Unlicensed sites have been known to void winning bets citing obscure terms, demand extensive documentation before paying, or simply become unresponsive. With no regulator holding them to account, there is no effective escalation path for Irish players.

Risk 2: Self-Exclusion Does Not Apply

The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER) — one of the most significant responsible gambling protections introduced by the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — only covers GRAI-licensed operators. If you self-exclude via the NGER to manage a gambling problem, that exclusion is invisible to any unlicensed offshore site. You can walk straight past the exclusion by simply logging in to an unlicensed site.

For people trying to manage gambling behaviour, this is not a theoretical risk — it is the specific pathway through which self-exclusion breaks down in practice. The gap between GRAI-covered and uncovered sites is exactly the gap that enables problem gamblers to circumvent their own self-exclusion.

See our page on GRAI self-exclusion for details of the NGER system.

Risk 3: Credit Cards Are Accepted (and Easy to Overspend)

GRAI-licensed operators cannot accept credit card deposits for gambling — this is a mandatory licensing condition. Unlicensed offshore sites face no such restriction. Many actively accept credit cards and some market to players using credit card rewards or cashback as an incentive.

When you gamble with credit, losses become debt repaid with interest. For players who struggle to control spending, the absence of the credit card ban on unlicensed sites makes it significantly easier to bet money they do not have.

The practical impact: a player who sets up a licensed account cannot deposit using credit. The same player on an unlicensed site faces no such barrier. See our page on the GRAI credit card ban.

Risk 4: Your Funds May Not Be Protected

GRAI licensing conditions require operators to maintain arrangements for segregating customer funds from the operator's own business finances. This matters if the operator becomes insolvent: your deposits and undrawn winnings should not simply become part of the general creditor pool in an insolvency proceeding.

Unlicensed offshore operators have no requirement to segregate customer funds. Some do, as good practice — but many do not. If a poorly-capitalised unlicensed operator fails, Irish players who have balances on that site may lose their money with no regulatory backstop and no practical enforcement route in an offshore jurisdiction.

High-profile collapses of offshore gambling operators (not Irish examples, but instructive) have resulted in players losing balances because funds were not segregated. With GRAI licensing, this is a regulated obligation.

Risk 5: Misleading Bonus Terms and No Accountability

GRAI advertising and bonus rules require licensed operators to present bonus terms clearly, including wagering requirements, minimum odds, time limits and restrictions, before a player accepts the bonus. Misleading bonus advertising is a GRAI breach that can result in substantial fines and enforcement action.

Unlicensed offshore sites face no such requirement. Headline bonus values can be advertised with extremely restrictive terms buried in full conditions accessible only via small-print links. Wagering requirements can be changed after you have accepted a bonus. Withdrawal of bonus funds can be blocked in ways not clearly disclosed upfront.

When this happens on a licensed site, you have recourse. When it happens on an unlicensed site, you have no regulator to complain to and limited practical options. See our page on GRAI bonus advertising rules for the standard GRAI-licensed sites must meet.

The Bottom Line

Betting on an unlicensed site in Ireland in 2026 is not illegal for the player, but it is a materially worse consumer experience than betting on a GRAI-licensed site. The five risks above are concrete, practical and regularly encountered by Irish players who use offshore sites. With roughly 33 GRAI-licensed operators now available, the case for using an unlicensed site is significantly weaker than it was before the GRAI licensing framework went live.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it illegal to bet on unlicensed sites in Ireland?

As a player, there is no Irish law making it a criminal offence. However, the operator is committing an offence, and you lose all GRAI consumer protections: no NGER coverage, no deposit limit mandates, no complaint route to GRAI, no fund protection.

Can an unlicensed site keep my winnings?

If an unlicensed site withholds winnings, you have very limited recourse. GRAI has no jurisdiction. Civil claims are slow, expensive and involve offshore courts. This is the most significant practical risk of unlicensed betting.

Does NGER self-exclusion cover unlicensed sites?

No. The NGER only covers GRAI-licensed operators. Self-exclusion via the NGER does not apply to any unlicensed offshore site.

Can unlicensed sites accept credit cards for gambling in Ireland?

Yes. The credit card ban is a GRAI licensing condition. Unlicensed offshore sites are not bound by it and many accept credit cards, making it easier to gamble with borrowed money.

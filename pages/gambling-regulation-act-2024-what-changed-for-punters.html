Gambling Regulation Act 2024: What Changed for You as a Punter

Last updated: November 2026 — 18+ | Gambling Awareness Trust | 1800 753 753

Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the biggest overhaul of Irish gambling law in decades. GRAI began operating on 2 October 2023 and issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Here's what it practically means for Irish players.

GRAI: the new regulator

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) is Ireland's first dedicated gambling regulator. Previously, online betting was overseen by Revenue Commissioners under the Betting Acts — a framework designed for high street bookmakers, not online gambling. GRAI has powers to license, monitor, and sanction gambling operators specifically.

What changed from 1 July 2026

Remote (online) betting licences began being issued by GRAI. Any bookmaker targeting Irish customers is now expected to hold a GRAI remote betting licence. The first licence issued was GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001. You can check any operator against the public GRAI register at grai.ie.

Player protection changes

The Act requires licensed operators to offer: mandatory deposit limits (you can set daily, weekly and monthly caps); self-exclusion tools linked to the upcoming national interoperable exclusion register; age verification before account creation or deposit; and access to Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) for complaints. These are legal obligations, not optional features.

Advertising rules

GRAI has introduced advertising watershed rules. Gambling advertising is restricted before 9pm on TV and radio. Bonus advertising is subject to new standards — no misleading terms, no targeting of vulnerable people. Operators who breach advertising rules face sanctions from GRAI.

What hasn't changed yet

Casino licences are not yet issuing — expected 2027-2028. The national interoperable exclusion register is in development. In-person betting shop licences move to GRAI from December 2026. So the full picture is still rolling out through 2026-2028.

What is the Gambling Regulation Act 2024?
It's the main piece of Irish legislation that overhauled gambling regulation — creating GRAI, introducing a new licensing system, and establishing player protection requirements including deposit limits, self-exclusion, and age verification.
When did GRAI start issuing licences?
GRAI began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026. Online casino licences are expected to follow in 2027-2028.
Do I need to do anything differently as a player?
No. Existing accounts remain valid. You may notice new features on your accounts — deposit limit prompts, clearer self-exclusion tools, and improved age verification for new accounts. These are protections for you.
Does the Act make unlicensed sites illegal?
It makes unlicensed operators liable to enforcement action by GRAI. As a player, you commit no offence by using an unlicensed site, but you lose all regulatory protection.

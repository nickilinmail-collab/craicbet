Clean Sheet Betting Ireland: How to Bet on Goalkeepers & Defences

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Clean sheet betting asks whether a specific team will prevent their opponents from scoring during the match. A clean sheet bet wins if the selected team concedes zero goals in 90 minutes. It is a binary market — yes or no — and it is available for home clean sheet, away clean sheet, or both teams to keep a clean sheet (which is equivalent to backing a 0–0 draw).

Market rules and settlement

Clean sheet markets settle on the 90-minute result only. If a team keeps a clean sheet in 90 minutes but concedes in extra time (in a cup tie), the clean sheet bet is still a winner. Own goals count — if a team scores an own goal, the opposing team is credited with a goal and the clean sheet is broken. This is a common and sometimes surprising rule: a goalkeeper who makes ten saves but sees his defender tap the ball into his own net has not kept a clean sheet for betting purposes.

Typical odds and frequencies

Clean sheet rates vary significantly by team and fixture context. In the Premier League, the best defensive sides keep home clean sheets in roughly 40–50% of home fixtures in a strong season. Away clean sheets are rarer, occurring in around 20–30% of away fixtures even for strong sides. This translates to typical clean sheet yes prices of 2.0–2.5 for a home clean sheet by a strong defensive side, and 3.0–4.5 for an away clean sheet. Teams with leaky defences may be priced at 3.5+ for a home clean sheet.

Identifying clean sheet value

Clean sheet betting rewards research on goalkeeper performance, defensive structure, and opponent attack. Expected goals conceded (xGC) is a useful metric: teams that consistently keep xGC below 1.0 per match have the structural basis to keep clean sheets at a higher rate than average. Fixture difficulty, opponent injury context, and in-form defensive players (particularly a goalkeeper in a strong run) are the most reliable predictors. Frequently Asked Questions

Does an own goal break a clean sheet in betting?

Yes. For betting purposes, an own goal counts as a goal conceded by the team that scores it, breaking their clean sheet. If a defender puts the ball in his own net, the opposing team is credited with a goal and any clean sheet bets on the defending team lose.

Do clean sheet bets include extra time?

No. Clean sheet markets settle on the 90-minute result only. If a team keeps a 0-0 score through 90 minutes but concedes in extra time (in a cup match), the clean sheet bet is still a winner. Extra time goals do not affect clean sheet betting settlement.

What is a typical clean sheet price in the Premier League?

For a strong home defensive side against mid-table opposition, clean sheet yes is typically priced around 2.0-2.8 (40-50% implied probability). Away clean sheet prices for the same team are usually 3.0-4.5 (22-33% implied probability). Teams with weaker defences may be priced at 4.0+ even for home fixtures.

Can I combine clean sheet and goalscorer bets?

Yes. Some bookmakers offer combination markets such as a team to keep a clean sheet and win, or clean sheet & over X.5 goals. Combining clean sheet yes for one team with a goalscorer from the same team can be a natural correlation: if the team scores, they may still keep a clean sheet. These combined markets are priced at the product of the individual probabilities.

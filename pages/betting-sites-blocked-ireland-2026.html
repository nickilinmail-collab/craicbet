Why are betting sites being blocked in Ireland?

Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024 created the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI), which launched its remote betting licensing regime on 1 July 2026. Any operator serving Irish customers online now needs a GRAI remote betting licence. Operating without one is a criminal offence under Section 36 of the Act.

GRAI's enforcement team is actively targeting unlicensed remote operators. When the regulator engages with a non-compliant site, the operator faces a choice: apply for a licence, withdraw from Ireland, or face High Court injunctions and fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual global turnover, whichever is higher.

Most operators served with GRAI notice have chosen to geo-block Irish IP addresses rather than face enforcement, while they either apply for a licence or exit the market permanently.

Stake, which describes itself as the world's largest online gambling platform with more than 44 million customers in 169 countries, blocked access for Irish users in September 2026 following engagement with GRAI. The move was reported by the Irish Times on 14 September 2026.

Irish users attempting to access Stake are shown the message: "Due to licensing restrictions, access to Stake from your current location is not available."

Notably, GRAI's remote betting licence requirement does not yet extend to online casino games — meaning Stake could legally have kept its casino offering live for Irish customers. A Stake spokesperson told SiGMA News: "Stake closed registrations in the Irish market, and we will look to apply for a licence to operate under the new regime."

Betfred paused its Irish operations from 30 June 2026 — the day before GRAI licensing took effect — citing the need to align with the new regulatory framework. Customers were asked to withdraw their balances before 30 June and not to leave bets settling after 29 June.

The suspension raised concerns for customers with ante-post bets on the 2026 FIFA World Cup; Betfred confirmed it would honour those wagers. The operator has indicated it hopes to return to the Irish market once it has completed the GRAI licensing process.

What does GRAI enforcement look like in practice?

GRAI's enforcement team has made clear it is using both soft and hard powers. The process generally follows this pattern:

1
GRAI contact

The regulator's enforcement team contacts the operator directly, informing them they appear to be operating in Ireland without the required licence and asking them to either apply or withdraw.

2
Voluntary geo-block

Most operators contacted to date have chosen to voluntarily geo-block Irish users while they assess their licensing options. This avoids immediate enforcement action.

3
Customer refunds

GRAI works with the geo-blocking operators to ensure Irish customers are refunded all money held on account. This is a consumer protection priority for the regulator.

!
Non-compliant operators: escalation

Operators that refuse to co-operate face High Court injunctions, fines of up to €20 million or 10% of global annual turnover, and criminal penalties for individuals involved in management. GRAI can also seek payment processing blocks.

Can I use a VPN to access blocked sites?

We do not recommend it. Using a VPN to circumvent a geo-block breaches the site's terms and conditions. Any winnings could be voided, and your funds carry no GRAI consumer protection whatsoever. If you have a dispute with an unlicensed operator, GRAI has no jurisdiction to help you.

Under Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024, it is an offence to supply remote gambling services to Irish customers without a licence. The Act does not criminalise individual bettors for using an unlicensed site — but you have no legal recourse if something goes wrong.

Your rights if you have money on a blocked site

  • Request a withdrawal immediately — contact the operator's support and withdraw all funds. Most sites that have voluntarily geo-blocked remain able to process outgoing withdrawals.
  • Use GRAI as leverage — if the site refuses to pay out, you can submit a complaint via the GRAI complaints process. GRAI has stated it is working with geo-blocking operators on refunds.
  • Payment provider chargeback — if you paid by debit card and cannot get a refund from the site, contact your bank about a chargeback under Section 75 / card dispute rules.
  • Keep records — screenshot your account balance and any correspondence before the site fully closes access.

Each-Way Betting on Horse Racing — The Complete Irish Punter's Guide

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

Each-way betting is one of the most used techniques in Irish horse racing betting. It allows you to back a horse to win and to place (finish in the top 2, 3, 4 or more positions) with a single bet split into two equal parts. This guide explains clearly how each-way betting works, what the terms mean, when it makes mathematical sense and how to calculate your returns before placing.

How Each-Way Betting Works

When you place a £10 each-way bet, you are placing two bets of £10 each — a total stake of £20:

  • The win part (£10): Wins if your horse finishes first.
  • The place part (£10): Wins if your horse finishes within the number of places specified by the bookmaker's terms (typically 2nd, 3rd, 4th, or 5th).

The place part pays out at a fraction of the win odds — usually 1/4 or 1/5 of the win price. If your horse wins, both parts pay out. If it places without winning, only the place part pays. If it finishes outside the places, both parts lose.

Understanding Place Terms

Place terms vary by race type and field size:

2–4 runnersWin only (no each-way)—
5–7 runners2 places1/4
8–11 runners3 places1/4
12–15 runners4 places1/4
16+ runners (handicaps)4–5 places1/4
Large handicaps 20+ (e.g. Grand National)5–7 places1/4

These are standard terms. Bookmakers can offer enhanced place terms as a promotion. Always confirm the specific terms for each race before placing.

When Does Each-Way Betting Make Mathematical Sense?

Each-way betting is most valuable when:

  • Large fields with extended place terms. In a 25-runner handicap with 5 places paid, a 20/1 each-way bet returns 5/1 (20 ÷ 4) on the place part for finishing 2nd–5th. The place return alone covers more than 5× your place stake.
  • Longer-priced selections. At prices of 10/1 and above, the place portion of an each-way bet carries significant standalone value. At 2/1 or 3/1, the place return at 1/4 odds is minimal.
  • Competitive handicaps. Where the form book is genuinely uncertain and the field is large, each-way is a natural fit.

Common Each-Way Mistakes

Irish punters commonly make these each-way errors:

  • Not checking the terms before placing. A 3-runner race has no each-way market; a 7-runner race has only 2 places.
  • Using each-way at short prices. At 2/1, the place part pays 1/2 (2 ÷ 4). You need the horse to win to generate a meaningful return — each-way adds little.
  • Forgetting the double stake. A £10 each-way bet costs £20. If you budget £10 for a race, your each-way stake is £5 each-way (£10 total).

Frequently Asked Questions

What does each-way mean in horse racing?

Each-way is a two-part bet: a win bet (the horse wins the race) and a place bet (the horse finishes within a set number of positions). Both parts cost the same stake, so a £5 each-way bet costs £10 total. If the horse wins, both parts pay; if it places without winning, only the place part pays.

What are the standard each-way place terms in horse racing?

Standard terms depend on field size: 2 places for 5–7 runners, 3 places for 8–11, 4 places for 12–15, 5 places for 16+ in large handicaps. The place fraction is usually 1/4 of win odds. Terms can vary — always confirm before placing.

When is each-way betting best value?

Each-way is most valuable on longer-priced horses (10/1+) in large-field handicaps with extended place terms (5+ places). At short prices (2/1–4/1) in small fields, the place return is minimal and each-way adds little value.

Does each-way betting cost more than a win bet?

Yes. An each-way bet is two separate bets at the same stake. A £10 each-way bet costs £20 in total (£10 win + £10 place). Always factor this into your budgeting.

In-Play Betting Rules Under the GRAI: What’s Allowed in Ireland?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

In-play (live) betting is legal in Ireland under the GRAI framework and offered by licensed operators. But the speed and continuous nature of in-play betting means specific responsible gambling and consumer protection rules apply. This page explains what GRAI-licensed operators can and cannot do with in-play betting, covering advertising, responsible gambling requirements, cash-out features and odds transparency.

In-Play Betting Is Permitted Under GRAI

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI licensing framework do not prohibit in-play betting. GRAI-licensed operators may offer live betting on sports, horse racing and other permitted betting activities. In-play betting is a standard feature of the licensed market and is offered by the majority of GRAI-licensed bookmakers.

The same licensing conditions that apply to pre-match betting apply to in-play betting, with some additional considerations arising from the specific characteristics of live wagering: faster pace, more frequent betting opportunities, and greater potential for impulsive decision-making.

Responsible Gambling in In-Play Contexts

In-play betting is associated with higher risk of problem gambling behaviour than pre-match betting. Research cited in the development of the GRAI framework identifies the continuous, rapid nature of in-play betting as a factor in excessive gambling spend. GRAI licensing conditions respond to this by requiring:

  • Session time limits and reality checks must be available and functional during in-play sessions. A reality check that only appears when you open account settings is not compliant — it must interrupt the in-play experience when triggered.
  • Deposit limits apply equally to in-play betting. An operator cannot allow in-play deposits or stakes to bypass the limits set at account level.
  • Self-exclusion via the NGER applies immediately to in-play markets, not just pre-match. An excluded customer must not be able to place any bet, pre-match or in-play.
  • Responsible gambling messaging must be accessible during in-play betting sessions — operators cannot make it easy to bet but hard to access support information during active play.

Advertising In-Play Betting

GRAI advertising rules apply to all gambling advertising including in-play betting promotions. Specific points that arise in the in-play context:

  • Push notifications promoting in-play opportunities cannot be sent to customers who have set responsible gambling limits, including deposit limits or session time limits.
  • In-play odds boosts and enhanced offers must be advertised with the same transparency requirements as pre-match bonuses: material terms must be disclosed prominently, not only in full terms.
  • Automated in-play bet suggestions (where platforms proactively suggest bets during a match) must not use manipulative dark-pattern techniques that pressure customers into placing bets they had not intended to make.
  • The broadcast watershed applies to in-play betting advertising on broadcast media — in-play odds or offers cannot be advertised on TV or radio between 5:30am and 9pm.

Cash-Out Features and GRAI Rules

Cash-out is a standard feature on in-play betting platforms that allows customers to settle a bet before the event ends, typically at a reduced value compared to the full win. GRAI licensing addresses cash-out in the context of fair terms and consumer protection:

  • Cash-out values must be calculated transparently. Operators must disclose how cash-out values are calculated. Customers have a right to understand what margin the operator is applying in the cash-out valuation.
  • Cash-out cannot be presented as risk-free when it is not. Some operators have marketed cash-out as a "safe" or "risk-free" option in ways that are misleading — GRAI advertising rules prohibit misleading representations.
  • Cash-out availability cannot be used as a manipulative retention tool in ways that encourage excessive betting. For example, using cash-out prompts specifically designed to trigger more bets from at-risk customers is a practice GRAI licensing conditions prohibit as a "dark pattern."

Odds Transparency in In-Play Markets

In-play markets move quickly, and odds change rapidly during matches. GRAI licensing conditions require that in-play odds are presented accurately and transparently. Specifically:

  • Operators cannot display odds that have already expired without clear indication that they are no longer current.
  • Bet acceptance delays must be communicated to customers — a customer should know if there is a delay between submitting a bet and it being accepted at the displayed odds.
  • If a bet is not accepted at the displayed odds and is instead offered at different odds, the customer must be clearly notified and must actively accept the revised offer — not automatically placed at worse odds without knowledge.

In-Play Betting on Unlicensed Sites

Unlicensed offshore sites offering in-play betting are not subject to any of the GRAI rules described above. The specific responsible gambling interventions during in-play sessions (reality checks, session limits applied to live betting) may be absent, poorly implemented, or easily disabled. Cash-out valuation methodology may be entirely opaque.

For bettors who enjoy in-play betting, the difference between GRAI-licensed and unlicensed in-play platforms is the same as for pre-match betting — with the additional factor that in-play betting's higher risk profile makes the responsible gambling safeguards more valuable, not less.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is in-play betting legal in Ireland under GRAI?

Yes. In-play betting is legal and offered by GRAI-licensed operators. The same responsible gambling, advertising and consumer protection standards that apply to pre-match betting apply to in-play betting.

Are there special responsible gambling rules for in-play betting?

Yes. Because in-play betting is faster-paced, GRAI requires that responsible gambling tools (session time limits, reality checks) function during in-play sessions, not just at account level. Deposit limits also apply fully to in-play markets.

Can operators push in-play betting notifications to customers who have set deposit limits?

No. GRAI prohibits sending marketing communications, including push notifications promoting in-play opportunities, to customers with active responsible gambling limits.

Are cash-out features regulated by GRAI?

Yes. GRAI requires cash-out terms to be clear and the value calculation methodology to be disclosed. Manipulative use of cash-out prompts targeting at-risk customers is prohibited.

Can in-play betting odds be manipulated on GRAI-licensed sites?

GRAI licensing prohibits unfair or misleading odds practices. Operators must present odds transparently and must clearly notify customers if a bet cannot be accepted at displayed odds before placing it at different odds.

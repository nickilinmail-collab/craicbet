Bookmaker Restricted My Account Ireland — Rights, GRAI Rules & What to Do

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

Having your betting account restricted or closed is frustrating — and more common than most punters realise. This guide explains why bookmakers restrict accounts in Ireland, what your rights are under the GRAI's new framework, and what you can actually do about it.

Why Do Bookmakers Restrict or Close Accounts?

Bookmakers restrict accounts primarily because a customer is consistently profitable. This is a commercial decision: bookmakers are not obliged to accept bets from customers who cost them money. The most common reasons for restriction:

  • Consistent winning: The account shows a positive return over time across pre-match markets
  • Sharp betting patterns: Always taking the best available odds, often just before line moves
  • Bonus exploitation: Using promotions in a systematic way (matched betting, qualifying bets)
  • Arbitrage: Backing all outcomes across multiple bookmakers to guarantee a profit
  • Early markets: Consistently betting early on markets where your selections move the line

Restriction typically means a stake limit (e.g. maximum €2 per bet on some markets) rather than a full closure. Bookmakers can apply restrictions under their terms and conditions without giving a reason.

What GRAI's Licences Change for Restricted Accounts

Since July 2026, GRAI-licensed operators in Ireland must comply with rules including:

  • Operators cannot unreasonably withhold the payment of winnings — this is an explicit obligation under the GRAI framework (per grai.ie/complaints)
  • Accounts must be closed when a customer requests it in writing
  • Account closure must happen within 13 months of inactivity
  • Suspicious activity must be reported — but this is separate from ordinary commercial restrictions

Importantly: the GRAI's rules on winnings focus on payment of settled bets, not on a bookmaker's commercial right to limit future stake sizes. A bookmaker can still restrict how much you can bet in future — but cannot withhold money already won on settled bets.

Key distinction: A bookmaker restricting your maximum stake is legal. A bookmaker refusing to pay out on a bet that was accepted and won is potentially a breach of GRAI rules (for licensed operators).

If Your Account Is Restricted: Step by Step

  1. Check your balance is accessible. Your funds should always be withdrawable regardless of any stake restrictions. If a bookmaker is blocking a withdrawal of funds already in your account, this is a separate and more serious issue.
  2. Identify the type of restriction. Is it a stake limit on specific markets? A maximum stake across the board? Or a full account closure? The distinction matters for what you can do next.
  3. Contact the bookmaker in writing. Request a clear explanation. Email or live chat transcript are preferable to phone — you need a written record. Ask specifically: "Is my account restricted, and if so, what are the current limits and reason?"
  4. Escalate if winnings are withheld. If the bookmaker is refusing to pay out on a settled, winning bet — not just limiting future stakes — this is where GRAI can help (see below).
  5. Lodge a GRAI complaint if warranted. GRAI accepts complaints about licensed operators withholding winnings, advertising breaches, and other specific obligations. See our full bookmaker complaint guide.

Can You Get a Restricted Account Unrestricted?

Sometimes. Bookmakers occasionally restore stakes after a period of losing activity, or when a customer's profile changes (e.g. betting on different markets). Some practical approaches:

  • Bet on accumulators or in-play markets rather than pre-match singles (restrictions often apply to single pre-match markets)
  • Use a betting exchange (Betfair Exchange) — exchanges cannot restrict winners commercially, they earn commission regardless
  • Open accounts with multiple operators — restrictions at one don't carry over to others

Operators That Have Left Ireland Since GRAI (2026)

Since GRAI's remote betting licensing regime launched on 1 July 2026, several operators chose not to apply for a licence and have instead geo-blocked Irish customers. If your account with one of these operators was closed, your balance should be refunded. GRAI stated it is "working closely with operators who have geo-blocked to ensure that consumers in Ireland are promptly refunded all monies deposited." If you have not received a refund from a geo-blocking operator, contact GRAI via their general enquiries form at grai.ie.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a bookmaker in Ireland restrict or close my account?

Yes. Bookmakers have the right to restrict your stake sizes or close your account under their terms and conditions. This is a commercial decision and does not require a reason to be given. GRAI-licensed operators cannot, however, unreasonably withhold payment of winnings on settled bets — that is a separate obligation under the GRAI framework.

Can I complain to GRAI if my account is restricted?

It depends on what 'restricted' means. If a GRAI-licensed bookmaker is withholding winnings on settled bets without good reason, you can complain to GRAI at grai.ie/complaints. If the bookmaker is simply limiting how much you can stake on future bets, that is a commercial decision GRAI does not regulate. See our complaint guide for step-by-step instructions.

Can a bookmaker keep my money if they close my account?

No. Your account balance belongs to you regardless of any restriction or closure. A bookmaker closing your account must allow you to withdraw your balance. If they are blocking a withdrawal of funds you deposited or won, this is a separate issue — contact the bookmaker in writing, then escalate to GRAI if the operator holds a GRAI licence.

What can I do if my bookmaker won't pay out my winnings?

Step 1: Complain to the bookmaker in writing (email) with your account details, the bet reference, and the amount disputed. Step 2: If unsatisfied, check whether the operator holds a GRAI licence at our GRAI checker. Step 3: If licensed, lodge a formal complaint at grai.ie/complaints. GRAI has powers to investigate and can require operators to comply. For unlicensed operators, options are more limited — seek legal advice or contact GRAI's general enquiries.

My account at a betting site was closed when GRAI came in — where is my money?

Several operators geo-blocked Irish customers rather than applying for a GRAI licence in July 2026. GRAI confirmed it is working with these operators to ensure balances are refunded. If you have not received your money back, contact GRAI via the general enquiries form at grai.ie with the operator name, your account details, and the balance amount. GRAI can engage with the operator on your behalf.

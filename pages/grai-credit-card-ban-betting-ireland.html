Credit Card Ban for Betting in Ireland — What GRAI Licensed Sites Must Do

⚠️ Betting only: GRAI has issued remote betting licences since 1 July 2026. Online casino licences are not expected until 2027–2028. This page covers the credit card ban as it applies to GRAI-licensed betting operators.
📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

The Short Version

From 1 July 2026, every operator that holds a GRAI remote betting licence is prohibited from accepting credit card deposits. This is a hard legal requirement — not an operator policy — embedded in the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and the conditions of the GRAI licence. If a licensed Irish betting site allows you to deposit with a Visa Credit or Mastercard Credit card, it is in breach of its licence.

Why the Ban Exists

Gambling with borrowed money is the strongest single predictor of gambling harm. Research from the UK, which introduced a similar ban in April 2020, found a substantial proportion of credit card gamblers were classified as problem gamblers. The logic is straightforward: if you lose money you have borrowed, you still owe it — with interest. GRAI's licensing conditions codify this by removing credit from the deposit equation entirely for licensed operators.

The ban also creates a bright-line rule that benefits responsible operators: a site cannot accidentally accept a credit payment that slips through. Consumer protection is baked into the payment infrastructure rather than relying on player disclosure.

Which Cards Are Affected

The prohibition covers all instruments that extend credit to the cardholder:

  • Visa Credit cards (standard, gold, platinum, rewards)
  • Mastercard Credit cards (standard, World, World Elite)
  • American Express cards (charge and credit variants)
  • Store cards and retail credit lines used via card networks
  • Revolut credit feature (where Revolut extends a credit line — not debit-funded balance)

The ban does not apply to debit instruments even where they carry a Visa or Mastercard logo. The distinction is the source of funds: your own money (permitted) versus borrowed money (prohibited).

Why This Matters for Bettors — The Honest Maths

If you were previously funding betting accounts with a credit card, the switch to a debit method changes your risk profile immediately. Consider: a €200 loss on a credit card costs you €200 plus interest (typically 20–25% APR if you don't clear the balance). The same loss from a debit card costs exactly €200. That difference in real cost is why regulators worldwide have moved to prohibit credit gambling.

The switch also makes it easier to track your gambling spend honestly: your bank statement reflects exactly what you have risked, with no interest accruing in the background.

Red Flag: A Licensed Site Still Accepting Credit Cards

If you encounter a site that claims to be GRAI-licensed but actively promotes or accepts credit card deposits, treat it as a serious warning sign. Either the licence claim is false or the operator is in breach of its conditions — both situations mean you should not deposit. Verify any licence claim at grai.ie's public register before depositing anywhere.

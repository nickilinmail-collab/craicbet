How to Play Pai Gow Poker

Pai Gow Poker is an Americanised version of the Chinese domino game Pai Gow, designed by Sam Torosian in the 1980s and adapted to use standard playing cards with poker hand rankings. It is played with a standard 52-card deck plus one Joker.

A round proceeds as follows:

  1. Place your bet.
  2. Seven cards are dealt to you and to the dealer.
  3. Set your hand: split your seven cards into a five-card "High Hand" and a two-card "Low Hand." The five-card hand must rank higher than the two-card hand using standard poker rankings (with one exception: A-2-3-4-5 is the second-highest Straight).
  4. The dealer sets their hand according to the House Way (a fixed set of rules).
  5. Comparison: your five-card hand vs. dealer's five-card hand; your two-card hand vs. dealer's two-card hand.
    • Win both: you win (minus 5% commission).
    • Lose both: you lose.
    • Win one, lose one: Push (no money changes hands).
    • Exact tie (a "copy"): dealer wins.

The Joker

The Joker is a semi-wild card. It can:

  • Complete a Straight, Flush, Straight Flush or Royal Flush.
  • Act as an Ace in any other situation.

It cannot be used as a fully wild card (e.g., to complete a pair of Kings). This matters most when you are setting a hand with the Joker — recognise whether the Joker helps your high hand, your low hand, or both.

Hand-Setting Strategy

The key decision in Pai Gow Poker is how to split your seven cards. There is a mathematically optimal way (the House Way) which you can copy or use as a reference:

  • No pair: put the highest card in the high hand, second and third highest in the low hand.
  • One pair: pair goes in the high hand; best remaining two cards in the low hand.
  • Two pair: generally split the pairs (one in high, one in low), unless you have an Ace pair — keep both pairs in the high hand and play the Ace in the low if possible.
  • Three pair: highest pair always goes in the low hand.
  • Three of a Kind: keep it in the high hand; play best two singles in the low hand. Exception: Aces — split the Aces, keeping a pair in high and one Ace in low.
  • Full House: generally split (pair in low, three of a kind in high), unless you have a second pair to play in low.
  • Four of a Kind: split if the quad is 7s or higher; keep together if 6s or lower.

Fortune Bonus Side Bet

The Fortune Bonus is a popular Pai Gow Poker side bet that pays based on your seven-card hand containing strong poker hands (regardless of how you set them). A Four of a Kind typically pays 25:1; Five Aces (four Aces plus the Joker) can pay up to 400:1. The house edge on the Fortune Bonus varies by casino but is typically 7–8%. Place it only for entertainment value, not as a core strategy.

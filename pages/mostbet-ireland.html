What Is Mostbet?

Mostbet is an online sports betting and casino operator that has built a significant presence across markets in the CIS region (the Commonwealth of Independent States, covering much of the former Soviet Union), as well as parts of Asia, Africa, and Latin America. The brand is known for a wide range of sports markets, high advertised bonuses, and an emphasis on mobile betting.

Mostbet operates under a Curaçao gaming licence issued by the Curaçao Gaming Control Board. Unlike the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) or the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC), the Curaçao framework is widely considered to impose less rigorous standards on operators, with fewer mandatory protections for players. It is used by many operators targeting markets without their own dedicated regulator, but it does not carry the same weight as an EU-regulated licence.

Mostbet is not a household name in Ireland or Western Europe more broadly. Its primary markets are elsewhere, and it does not appear to actively target the Irish market through licensed channels. That said, the question of its GRAI licence status is worth addressing clearly for any Irish player who has encountered the brand.

Mostbet’s GRAI Licence Status

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, Mostbet does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means Mostbet has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

Risks for Irish Players Using Mostbet

Using an operator without a GRAI licence carries meaningful risks for Irish players that go beyond those associated with using, say, an MGA-licensed operator that simply hasn’t yet obtained a GRAI licence. Mostbet’s Curaçao licence provides a lower baseline of protection in several important areas.

When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — and especially one operating only under a Curaçao licence — those protections do not apply. Specifically:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator — and with a Curaçao-licensed operator, your complaints route is particularly limited.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. A Curaçao operator is not bound by this rule, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator — and Curaçao oversight of payout disputes is limited.

Irish players considering Mostbet should be clear that they would be operating entirely outside Irish regulatory oversight, with weaker recourse than they would have using even a non-GRAI, MGA-licensed operator.

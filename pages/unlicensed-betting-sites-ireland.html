What Changed on 1 July 2026

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — passed in October 2024 — created GRAI and set a licensing deadline. From 1 July 2026, any operator offering remote gambling (online betting, online casino, prediction markets) to Irish residents requires a GRAI licence. This is the most significant change to Irish gambling law in decades.

Before July 2026, Ireland had no specific online gambling regulator. The Betting Acts covered licensed bookmakers, but the rapid growth of online-only operators — particularly offshore operators with Curaçao, MGA or no licence — happened largely outside any Irish regulatory oversight.

Major Sites Blocked or Investigated in 2026

Stake.com — Blocked September 2026

Stake.com is one of the largest online casinos globally, with reported revenues in the billions and sponsorships of major sports teams. Despite this scale, Stake did not apply for GRAI licensing and was geo-blocking Irish users by September 2026 following GRAI intervention.

What happened: GRAI issued a formal direction to Stake to cease offering gambling services to Irish residents. Stake complied by implementing geo-blocking. Players who had balances at the time of the block reported difficulties in some cases, though Stake did process most withdrawals.

The core problem with Stake for Irish players: Stake's primary licence is from Curaçao — one of the most loosely regulated gambling jurisdictions in the world. Curaçao e-gaming licences provide minimal player protection. Ireland's GRAI framework, by contrast, requires segregated player funds, mandatory deposit limits, and ADR access. Players on Stake had none of these protections.

Polymarket — Restricted July 2026

Polymarket is a prediction market platform that allows users to bet on real-world events (elections, sports outcomes, economic events). Despite describing itself as a "prediction market" rather than a gambling site, GRAI determined that its products constitute gambling under Irish law.

GRAI issued guidance in July 2026 that Polymarket requires a remote betting licence to serve Irish users. Polymarket geo-blocked Ireland in response. Players could no longer create new positions or add funds, though existing positions could be resolved.

Irony: Polymarket had seen huge traffic growth in Ireland during the 2024 US election cycle. Many Irish users first encountered it then and continued using it. The July 2026 block caught many regular users off guard.

Kalshi — Restricted July 2026

Kalshi, a US-regulated prediction market, was also restricted from accepting Irish users in July 2026, in the same GRAI guidance that covered Polymarket. Kalshi holds CFTC approval in the United States but that does not constitute Irish gambling authorisation.

Soft2Bet — 145 Sites Under Enforcement, 2026

Soft2Bet is a B2B gambling software provider that powers a large number of white-label casino and betting brands. GRAI identified 145 Soft2Bet-powered sites as operating in Ireland without GRAI licences. This is the largest single enforcement action GRAI has taken.

None of the 145 sites named in the enforcement action hold GRAI licences. Players on these sites face all the standard risks of using an unlicensed operator: unprotected funds, no ADR access, no mandatory responsible gambling tools.

The Scale of the Unlicensed Market

The problem is not specific to Ireland. Across Europe, the illegal gambling market is reported to represent approximately 70% of all online gambling, worth an estimated €80 billion annually (figures cited by GRAI in 2025). This is a larger market than the entire licensed sector in Europe.

The drivers of this are:

  • Higher bonuses on unlicensed sites: No responsible gambling obligations means unlicensed operators can offer bonuses that licensed operators cannot legally match
  • Easier account opening: Unlicensed sites often have lighter KYC requirements, attracting players who want quick sign-up
  • Products not available on licensed sites: Some unlicensed operators offer products (certain crypto gambling, some prediction markets) that licensed operators cannot legally offer under GRAI or UKGC rules
  • VPN accessibility: Even after geo-blocking, determined users can access blocked sites via VPN — with no additional legal risk to the player but significant fund-safety risk

Risks of Using an Unlicensed Betting Site

The risks are real and not theoretical. This section explains what can go wrong — and what cannot be fixed once it has.

💸 No Fund Protection

GRAI-licensed operators are required to hold player funds separately from operational funds. If an unlicensed operator becomes insolvent, your balance is an unsecured creditor claim — in practice, lost. This has happened with several offshore operators over the years.

⚖️ No Dispute Resolution

GRAI-licensed operators must provide access to an independent ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution) service. If an unlicensed site refuses to pay your winnings, you have no regulatory body to escalate to. GRAI cannot intervene on behalf of players on unlicensed sites.

🔒 No Data Protection Oversight

GRAI-licensed operators must comply with Irish and EU data protection law. Offshore operators may store your personal data in jurisdictions with weak data protection. Your ID documents, payment details and address can be used in ways you cannot control or challenge.

🛡️ No Responsible Gambling Tools

GRAI requires licensed sites to offer deposit limits, session limits, cooling-off periods and self-exclusion linked to the national self-exclusion register. Unlicensed sites have no obligation to offer these. High-risk players are especially exposed.

GRAI Enforcement Powers

GRAI is not a toothless regulator. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 gave it significant enforcement powers:

  • Fines: Up to €20 million or 10% of the operator's annual turnover — whichever is higher
  • Licence revocation: Can suspend or revoke GRAI licences for violations
  • ISP blocking: GRAI can apply to the courts to have unlicensed gambling sites blocked at the ISP level in Ireland
  • Criminal prosecution: Operating without a licence is a criminal offence under the Act; directors of companies can be personally prosecuted
  • Bank and payment processor orders: GRAI can direct payment processors to refuse transactions for unlicensed operators

The 100+ operator investigations announced in 2026 are expected to result in a wave of further blocks and potentially the first court-ordered ISP blocks, likely in 2027.

VPNs and Unlicensed Sites — What You Need to Know

Some players who have been geo-blocked from their preferred sites use VPNs to continue accessing them. Here are the key facts:

  • Not illegal for the player: Irish law does not criminalise gambling on an unlicensed site. The offence is on the operator side.
  • Terms of service violation: Almost all operators' terms prohibit using a VPN or accessing from a geo-blocked jurisdiction. This can be used to void winnings or close accounts.
  • Withdrawal risk: If a site identifies you as Irish via your payment method (Irish IBAN, Irish card, Revolut with an Irish registration), it may block your withdrawal even if you accessed via VPN. The geo-block was for a reason — the operator does not want to serve Irish accounts.
  • No protection whatsoever: Using a VPN to access a blocked site removes even the theoretical argument that you accessed in good faith. Your funds are entirely unprotected.

What Happens to Existing Balances When a Site Is Blocked

This is the most practical concern for players caught by a block mid-session or with a live balance on a site GRAI is investigating. The answer is uncomfortable:

  • GRAI has no jurisdiction over unlicensed operators. It cannot direct them to process refunds or withdrawals — it can only direct them to stop serving Irish players.
  • Operators typically provide a withdrawal window — Stake, for example, did process most withdrawals when it geo-blocked Ireland. But this is the operator's choice, not a legal requirement.
  • Credit card and banking reversal options are limited. If you funded the account with a card and the site refuses to withdraw, a chargeback may be possible — but gambling transactions are often excluded from card issuer chargeback protection.
  • Legal recourse against an offshore operator is impractical. Suing a Curaçao-licensed operator from Ireland, for a few hundred euros, is not viable.

The practical lesson: never maintain a large balance at any online gambling site. Withdraw winnings promptly. This is even more important on unlicensed sites but is good practice everywhere.

The Bigger Picture: 70% of European Betting Is Illegal

The scale of the unlicensed gambling market in Europe — reported at around 70% of all online gambling volume and €80 billion annually — reveals something important: regulation has consistently struggled to keep pace with an industry that moves fast, operates offshore, and benefits from relatively low legal risk for the player.

GRAI's approach in 2026 is to focus enforcement on the largest and most visible operators (Stake, Polymarket, Soft2Bet) while building the licensed market. The theory is that as GRAI-licensed sites offer better products, more regulated operators enter the Irish market, and consumer awareness of GRAI grows, the unlicensed share will shrink organically.

Whether this works depends heavily on what GRAI-licensed casinos can offer when the gaming licences finally arrive in 2027–2028. If the licensed market is competitive and the product is good, players who currently use unlicensed sites have reasons to switch. If licensed sites are over-restricted or the product is worse, the unlicensed market will persist regardless of enforcement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it illegal for me to use an unlicensed betting site in Ireland?
No, the player commits no offence. The law penalises the operator for providing unlicensed gambling services, not the player for accessing them. However, using an unlicensed site means your funds have no regulatory protection.
How do I know if a casino I'm using has an Irish licence?
As of November 2026, no online casino holds a GRAI licence — only betting sites do. Casino licences are expected in 2027–2028. The best current indicator for casino safety is an MGA or UKGC licence. Check the site footer and cross-reference on the regulator's public register.
What is GRAI and what does it do?
GRAI is the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland, created by the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. It issues gambling licences, enforces gambling laws, maintains the national self-exclusion register, and investigates unlicensed operators. It issued its first remote betting licences on 1 July 2026. Can GRAI block sites at the ISP level in Ireland?
Yes. GRAI can apply to the courts for an order requiring Irish ISPs (Eir, Vodafone, Sky, Virgin, etc.) to block access to unlicensed gambling sites. This power has not been used yet as of November 2026, but is expected to be deployed against persistent non-compliant operators in 2027.

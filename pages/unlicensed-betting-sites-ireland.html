GRAI Enforcement Tracker: Unlicensed Betting Sites in Ireland

Since online betting licensing began on 1 July 2026, the GRAI has acted against dozens of unlicensed operators — from small Irish bookmakers to the world's two largest prediction markets — and most stopped trading after a cease-and-desist warning, without any High Court action. In September 2026 the regulator said it was investigating more than 100 operators. GRAI Enforcement Powers

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 gives the regulator an escalating set of tools. In its first four months it has used the bottom of the ladder and, on its own account, not yet needed the top:

  • Cease-and-desist letters — the first step; the GRAI says most unlicensed operators stopped after this.
  • High Court orders — threatened against prediction markets in July 2026; none sought as of 4 November 2026.
  • Financial sanctions — up to €20 million or 10% of turnover for licensed operators, subject to court confirmation.
  • Criminal prosecution — operating without a licence is an offence under the Act; the GRAI has described "live investigations" that could lead to prosecutions.
  • Blocking and payment orders — provided for in the Act; no court-ordered ISP or payment block has been reported yet.

What This Means for Punters

  • You commit no offence by using an unlicensed site; the offence is the operator's. But you also have none of the protections the Act gives customers of licensed sites.
  • Refunds after a block are cooperation, not a right. The GRAI said it worked with geo-blocking operators so Irish customers were refunded — it cannot compel an operator it does not license. Withdraw balances promptly; never keep more on a site than you are playing with that week.
  • The credit card ban and age checks that apply to licensed operators are, in the regulator's words, ignored by many black-market operators — one reason it treats them as higher risk.
  • VPN use does not change your legal position, but it breaches operators' terms and gives a site grounds to void winnings or refuse withdrawal once it identifies an Irish payment method.
  • Complaints about a licensed operator can go to the GRAI's consumer-protection division; complaints about an unlicensed one cannot. See how to complain about a bookmaker.

How to Check a Site Before You Deposit

  1. Find the licence number on the site — the format is GRAI-XXXX-RB-26-0001 (RB = remote betting; RBI = betting intermediary). No number, or a number without "RB", is a red flag.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it illegal for me to use an unlicensed betting site in Ireland?
No — the player commits no offence. The Act penalises the operator for providing unlicensed gambling services. The practical cost is that your funds and your complaints have no Irish regulatory protection.
Has the GRAI published a list of blocked sites?
No. As of November 2026 the GRAI describes its actions in general terms ("dozens of operators", "two major prediction markets", "a major international platform") and has not named operators. The names in our log come from press reporting, as cited.
How do I know if a casino I'm using has an Irish licence?
No online casino holds a GRAI licence yet; only betting products are licensed. Casino licensing is expected to open in 2027. For casino safety today the relevant check is an MGA, UKGC or Gibraltar licence in the site footer, cross-referenced on that regulator's register.
Can the GRAI block sites at the ISP level in Ireland?
The Act provides for court orders against unlicensed operators; the GRAI threatened High Court action in July 2026 but said on 4 November 2026 that it has not yet gone to court. No ISP-level block has been reported.

What Is the GRAI and Why Does Licensing Matter?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) was established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. It is the independent statutory body responsible for regulating all commercial gambling in Ireland, including remote (online) betting.

GRAI remote betting licences became operational on 1 July 2026. From that date, any operator offering remote betting services to Irish customers must hold a valid GRAI licence. Without one, the operator cannot legally target Irish residents under Irish law.

A GRAI licence is not simply a registration fee — it comes with binding obligations: mandatory deposit limits, integration with the National Gambling Exclusion Interface (NGEI), compliance with the credit card betting ban, defined standards for withdrawal timescales, and access to the GRAI complaints and dispute resolution system for Irish players.

If a site operates without a GRAI licence, none of those obligations apply. That is the practical risk difference.

What You Risk Using a Non-GRAI Site

Placing bets with a site that lacks a GRAI licence is not a criminal offence for the individual. However, it removes a set of legal protections that apply specifically to GRAI-regulated operators. Here is what you give up:

  • 🚫
    No deposit limits enforcement. GRAI-licensed operators must offer mandatory deposit limits and comply with affordability checks. Non-GRAI sites are under no obligation to apply these protections to Irish users.
  • 🚫
    No NGEI self-exclusion integration. The National Gambling Exclusion Interface (NGEI) lets you self-exclude across all GRAI-licensed sites at once. Non-GRAI operators are not connected to NGEI, so a self-exclusion with GRAI-licensed sites does not apply there.
  • 🚫
    No credit card ban compliance. GRAI prohibits the use of credit cards for gambling deposits at licensed sites. Non-GRAI operators may still accept credit card deposits from Irish users, removing a key harm-reduction safeguard.
  • 🚫
    No Irish complaints route. If a licensed site refuses a withdrawal or mishandles a dispute, you can escalate to GRAI. With a non-GRAI site, your only recourse is the operator's own dispute process and, potentially, their home regulator — which may be in Curaçao, Malta or another jurisdiction.
  • 🚫
    No GRAI withdrawal standards. GRAI regulations set timescales and requirements for how licensed operators process withdrawals. Non-GRAI operators are not bound by these Irish-specific standards.

How to Check If a Bookmaker Has a GRAI Licence

The definitive source is the GRAI public register. Here is how to use it:

  1. Go to grai.ie in your browser. This is the official Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland website.
  2. Navigate to the Public Register section. This lists all operators that have been granted a licence under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.
  3. Search for the operator by name. If the brand appears with an active licence and a valid expiry date, it is GRAI-licensed. If it does not appear, it is not licensed for the Irish market.
  4. Note the licence type. GRAI currently issues remote betting licences (like GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001). Casino (gaming) licences are expected from 2027–2028. An operator holding only a betting licence is not licensed for casino products under GRAI.

Tip: a site displaying the GRAI logo does not automatically mean it is licensed. Always cross-check on the actual grai.ie register.

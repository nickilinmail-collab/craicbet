What Is Betboro?

Betboro is a relatively newer online sports betting operator that has focused its growth on Eastern European and international markets. The platform offers a range of sports markets including football, basketball, tennis, and other popular disciplines. It operates under a Curaçao gaming licence, which is a commonly used offshore jurisdiction.

Unlike operators licensed by the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) or the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC), Curaçao is not an EU or EEA regulator and does not carry the same level of player-protection requirements as those bodies. Betboro has built a following in markets where it has targeted its marketing, but it does not have a particular presence or track record in Ireland.

That said, holding any offshore licence does not automatically authorise an operator to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence, regardless of what other licences an operator holds.

GRAI Licence Status

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, Betboro does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means Betboro has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

Betboro in Ireland

Betboro may remain technically accessible to users with Irish IP addresses. However, accessible does not mean licensed, and there is a meaningful practical difference for players.

When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

Irish players considering Betboro should be clear that they would be operating outside Irish regulatory oversight, with recourse limited to Curaçao’s licensing regime, which offers considerably less player protection than Irish or EU regulators.

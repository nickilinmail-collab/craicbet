How Do Betting Odds Work in Ireland? Fractions, Decimals & More

Betting odds show how much you win relative to your stake and how likely the bookmaker thinks an outcome is. Fractional odds of 5/1 mean you win €5 for every €1 staked (plus your stake back). The same odds in decimal format are 6.00 — your total return per €1 staked. To convert odds to implied probability: divide 1 by the decimal odds, so 6.00 = 16.7%. Irish bookmakers show fractions by default, but you can switch to decimals in your account settings.

Betting odds are the language of wagering. They tell you how much you will win, they encode the bookmaker's view of probability, and they carry the hidden cost that bookmakers never make explicit. Understanding how they work — how to calculate a payout, how to compare fractional and decimal formats, and how to spot the margin — is the most important piece of knowledge for any Irish punter. This guide covers it all clearly.

Fractional Odds: The Traditional Irish Format

Fractional odds are still the default in Irish betting shops and horse racing. They are expressed as a fraction — 5/1, 2/1, 7/4, 11/8 — and they represent the profit you receive per unit staked.

  • 5/1 ("five-to-one"): €10 bet wins €50 profit + €10 stake back = €60 total
  • 2/1 ("two-to-one"): €10 bet wins €20 profit + €10 stake = €30 total
  • 1/2 ("one-to-two" or "odds-on"): €10 bet wins €5 profit + €10 stake = €15 total
  • Evens (1/1): €10 bet wins €10 profit + €10 stake = €20 total

Read the fraction as "profit / stake". 7/4 means €7 profit for every €4 staked. For a €10 bet: (10 ÷ 4) × 7 = €17.50 profit, returning €27.50 total.

Decimal Odds: The European and Online Standard

Decimal odds (also called European odds) have become the default on most Irish and international betting websites. They are simpler for quick calculation because they represent the total return per unit staked, including your stake.

  • 6.0: €10 bet returns €60 total (€50 profit) — same as 5/1 fractional
  • 3.0: €10 bet returns €30 total — same as 2/1 fractional
  • 1.5: €10 bet returns €15 total — same as 1/2 fractional
  • 2.0: €10 bet returns €20 total — same as evens

Converting: Decimal = (fractional numerator ÷ denominator) + 1. So 7/4 = (7÷4) + 1 = 2.75. Easy to calculate with on a phone.

Implied Probability: What Odds Are Really Saying

Every set of odds carries an implicit probability statement. To find it: divide 1 by the decimal odds. 6.0 → 1÷6.0 = 16.7%. 2.0 (evens) → 1÷2.0 = 50%. 1.5 → 1÷1.5 = 66.7%.

This is powerful because it lets you compare the bookmaker's implied probability against your own assessment. If you think a team has a 40% chance of winning but the bookmaker's odds imply only 30%, those odds are offering value from your perspective. If you think their implied 50% is too high, the bet is overpriced.

The Bookmaker Margin (Overround)

Add up the implied probabilities for all outcomes in a market. They should total 100% in a fair market — but they never do. In a typical football 1X2 market, they add up to around 105–110%. That excess is the bookmaker's overround, or margin, and it is how bookmakers guarantee themselves a profit regardless of the outcome.

Example: a coin-flip match where both teams have an equal 50% chance. Fair odds: 2.0 on each. But bookmakers might offer 1.90 on each — implying 1÷1.9 = 52.6% each, summing to 105.2%. That 5.2% above 100% is the margin. If you bet on both sides with equal stakes, you lose 5.2% of your money for certain. This is the fundamental truth of betting: the house has a structural edge built into every market.

Odds-On vs Odds-Against

When the decimal odds are below 2.0 (or the fractional numerator is smaller than the denominator, like 1/2 or 4/7), a selection is odds-on — you must stake more than you will profit. When odds are above 2.0 (or the numerator is larger, like 5/2 or 7/1), it is odds-against.

Odds-on does not mean "safe" — it means the market rates the selection as a probability greater than 50%. Heavy favourites lose regularly. Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between fractional and decimal odds?

Fractional odds (like 5/1) show profit relative to stake. Decimal odds (like 6.0) show total return including stake. Both describe the same price — 5/1 and 6.0 are identical.

What does 'evens' or 1/1 mean in betting?

Evens means your profit equals your stake. €10 at evens returns €20 total. In decimal odds, evens is 2.0.

What is implied probability?

Implied probability converts odds into the percentage chance implied for an outcome. For 5/1 (6.0 decimal): 1 ÷ 6.0 = 16.7%. For evens (2.0): 1 ÷ 2.0 = 50%.

Why do the implied probabilities of all outcomes add up to more than 100%?

The excess above 100% is the bookmaker's margin (overround) — typically 4–10% on major sports. It is how the bookmaker profits regardless of the outcome.

Can I convert fractional to decimal odds myself?

Yes. Decimal = (numerator ÷ denominator) + 1. So 5/2 becomes (5 ÷ 2) + 1 = 3.5. And 1/2 becomes (1 ÷ 2) + 1 = 1.5.

How to Calculate Returns from Odds

  • Fractional: (numerator ÷ denominator × stake) + stake = total return. E.g. 6/1 × €10 = €60 + €10 stake = €70
  • Decimal: decimal × stake = total return. E.g. 7.00 × €10 = €70
  • To find the implied probability from decimal: 1 ÷ decimal = probability. 7.00 → 1/7 = 14.3%
  • To convert fractional to decimal: (numerator ÷ denominator) + 1. E.g. 6/1 → 6 + 1 = 7.00

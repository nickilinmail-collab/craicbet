What Is a Push in Sports Betting?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

A push in sports betting occurs when the outcome exactly matches the line set by the bookmaker, resulting in neither a win nor a loss — your stake is returned. Pushes are most common in points spread betting, Asian handicap markets, and over/under totals. Here is a complete guide.

When Does a Push Occur?

Points spread (American football, basketball): If you bet Team A −3.5 and they win by exactly 3.5 (not possible with a half-point spread), it is a push. With whole-number spreads (Team A −3), if they win by exactly 3, the bet pushes.

Asian Handicap 0 (Draw No Bet): If the match ends in a draw, the bet pushes and stakes are returned. This is equivalent to Draw No Bet in European markets.

Over/Under totals: If you bet Over 2.5 goals and the game ends 1-1 (2 goals), you lose. But with a whole-number line (Over 2 goals), a game ending 1-1 is a push — stake returned.

Half-Points Eliminate Pushes

Bookmakers often set lines with half-points (−3.5, Over 2.5) specifically to eliminate the possibility of a push. When a half-point is included, one side must win. If you see a whole-number line, be aware that a push is possible.

Pushes in Accumulators

If one leg of an accumulator pushes, that leg is treated as a void/no-bet and removed from the accumulator. The remaining legs continue at adjusted odds. For example, a 4-fold acca where one leg pushes becomes a 3-fold. If all legs push, the entire stake is returned.

Irish Bookmaker Rules on Pushes

Under GRAI transparency requirements, operators must clearly state their rules on pushes and voids in their House Rules. Check the specific rules for each market at your operator — practices can differ between American-style and European-style markets. The standard industry practice is stake return, but always verify.

Push vs Void: What's the Difference?

  • Push: bet resolved at exactly the line – your stake is returned, no profit or loss
  • Void: bet is cancelled entirely (match abandoned, non-runner) – stake returned
  • Half-point spreads eliminate pushes entirely in American sports (e.g. -3.5 instead of -3)
  • Asian handicap uses half-lines or quarter-lines to handle push scenarios differently

How Pushes Affect Parlays and Accumulators

  • In US-style parlay betting: a push is treated as a void leg — the bet continues with one fewer selection
  • In standard UK/Irish accumulator rules: a void (push) leg is removed and the acca continues at adjusted odds
  • Asian handicap with 0 (level ball): guarantees a push or win/loss — no ambiguous result
  • To avoid push scenarios entirely: use half-point handicap lines (e.g. -1.5 instead of -1)

What is a push in sports betting?

A push is when your bet ties — the result exactly matches the line. Your stake is returned and you neither win nor lose. Common in points spread and Asian handicap markets.

What happens to my bet if it pushes?

Your stake is returned in full. No profit, no loss. If it was a leg of an accumulator, that leg is removed and the acca recalculates at adjusted odds.

Can a push happen in football betting?

Yes — with whole-number Asian handicap lines (AH 0 = Draw No Bet, AH 1, AH 2) or whole-number over/under lines. Half-point lines (AH 0.5, Over 2.5) eliminate pushes.

Is a push the same as a void bet?

Similar but not identical. A push is caused by the result tying the line. A void bet is usually caused by external factors (match abandoned, non-runner). Both result in stake return.

Do pushes affect accumulator odds?

Yes — a pushed leg is removed from the acca, and odds are recalculated. A 4-fold acca with one push becomes a 3-fold at adjusted odds.

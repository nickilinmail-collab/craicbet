What Does Void Bet Mean in Ireland

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

A void bet is a cancelled bet. When a bet is voided, your stake is returned in full as if the bet never happened. Void bets occur in specific circumstances — they are not the same as a losing bet.

Common Reasons a Bet Is Voided in Ireland

Irish bookmakers can void bets in several situations. The most common are: a match being abandoned before the minimum playing time required for settlement (usually 90 minutes for football, or the specific sport's rules); a horse declared a non-runner after bets were placed; a bookmaker displaying incorrect odds due to technical error; or a bet placed after an event has already started (palpable error).

What Happens to an Acca If One Leg Is Voided?

If one selection in an accumulator is void, that leg is removed and the acca continues with the remaining legs at recalculated odds. A 5-fold acca with one void becomes a 4-fold. The reduced odds are applied to your original stake.

This is different from a losing leg, which would lose the entire acca. A void leg simply does not count.

Why has my bet been voided in Ireland

A void bet is one where the bookmaker cancels the wager and returns your stake in full. No winnings are paid and no loss is incurred — the bet never happened from a financial perspective. Voids can occur at the bookmaker's discretion or as a result of specific circumstances in the market or event.

Under GRAI rules, bookmakers must return stakes on voided bets within a reasonable timeframe. If a bookmaker voids a bet you believe was placed correctly and at genuine odds, you can raise the dispute with GRAI at grai.ie if the operator's internal complaints process does not resolve it.

How long does a void bet refund take in Ireland?

Most bookmakers process void bet refunds instantly or within a few hours. If the event was recently abandoned or the non-runner just declared, it may take up to 24 hours while the event is officially settled.

Dublin Racing Festival Betting: Leopardstown’s Grade 1 Weekend

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

The Dublin Racing Festival at Leopardstown is the richest two days in Irish jump racing — a card stacked with Grade 1s run over the first weekend of February, five weeks or so before Cheltenham. For punters it is two things at once: a superb betting meeting in its own right, and the single best source of Cheltenham Festival clues. This guide covers both, with our usual honest look at the maths.

What the festival actually is

Since it was created in 2018 by merging Leopardstown's existing February fixtures, the DRF has packed the top of the Irish jumps programme into one weekend: championship-level hurdles and chases for the two-mile speedsters, the staying hurdlers, the novices and the top chasers, plus valuable handicaps underneath. Almost every leading Irish yard aims its best horses here, so fields are strong but often not large — and that matters for how you bet.

Small fields change the each-way maths

Grade 1s at Leopardstown can go to post with five, six or seven runners. With fewer than eight, standard each-way terms pay only two places — and in a five-runner race many firms offer win-only. An each-way bet at 1/5 the odds on a 7/2 shot in a six-runner Grade 1 is usually poor value: the place part returns little and the bookmaker's margin on it is large. In small graded fields, a win single on your selection, staked sensibly, is normally the cleaner bet. Run the numbers yourself on our each-way calculator before you part with the stake.

The handicaps are where the value hides

The big-field handicaps carry weaker favourites and genuine prices. They are harder puzzles — Irish handicaps are deep, and well-handicapped novices from powerful yards are perennially dangerous — but the market makes more mistakes in a 20-runner handicap than in a four-horse Grade 1 where everyone has seen the same form. If you want value rather than the thrill of backing a star at 4/9, this is where to look.

Reading it for Cheltenham

DRF form is the strongest single pointer to the Cheltenham Festival, simply because the best Irish horses meet each other here at full fitness. Two honest caveats. First, the market knows this too: a DRF winner is cut for Cheltenham within seconds, so the value has usually gone by Sunday evening. Second, Leopardstown and Cheltenham are different tests — a flat, left-handed gallop versus an undulating track with a stiff finish — so some DRF form simply does not transfer. If you fancy a DRF runner for March, the ante-post question is covered in our ante-post guide: you are betting money now, at the risk of a non-runner, for a bigger price.

Weather and the going

Early February in Dublin means the going is rarely quick — soft or heavy ground is common and it changes form lines. Check the official going on the morning of racing and be sceptical of horses whose best form is on better ground. Non-runners are also more common in testing conditions, which is another argument against tying money up ante-post on these races.

Betting on it sensibly

Treat the DRF like any big meeting: set a budget for the weekend before the first race, bet in level or near-level stakes, and do not chase Saturday losses on the Sunday card. Odds-on favourites win plenty of these Grade 1s — backing them all is a slow way to lose, because the one or two beaten favourites wipe out the small gains. 18+. Frequently Asked Questions

When is the Dublin Racing Festival?

It is run at Leopardstown over two days on the first weekend of February each year, roughly five weeks before the Cheltenham Festival. Check Leopardstown's official site for the confirmed dates and fixtures.

Is each-way betting worth it in DRF Grade 1 races?

Usually not. Many DRF Grade 1s have fewer than eight runners, so only two places are paid — or the race is win-only — and the place part of the bet carries a large margin. A win single is normally better value in small graded fields; save each-way for the big-field handicaps.

How reliable is DRF form at Cheltenham?

It is the strongest single guide to Cheltenham because the best Irish horses meet here at full fitness. But the market reacts within seconds, so the value goes quickly, and Leopardstown's flat, left-handed track is a very different test from Cheltenham's undulations and stiff finish — not all form transfers.

Welcome Bonus vs No Deposit Bonus — Which Offers Better Value?

📝 CraicBet Team 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

Every online casino tries to win you over with bonuses, but the two most common types — welcome bonuses and no deposit bonuses — work very differently. One requires your money upfront, the other doesn't. So which one actually gives you better value? Let's compare.

💰 Quick Answer

Welcome bonuses offer far more value in total bonus funds but require a deposit. No deposit bonuses are risk-free but much smaller with stricter terms. Smart players claim both — the no deposit bonus first (free money), then evaluate the welcome bonus on its terms.

What Is a Welcome Bonus?

A welcome bonus (also called a deposit bonus or sign-up bonus) is the main offer a casino gives new players when they make their first deposit. It typically matches your deposit by a percentage, giving you extra funds to play with.

For example, a 100% match up to €200 means if you deposit €200, you get an additional €200 in bonus funds, giving you €400 total to play with. Some casinos spread this across multiple deposits (e.g., bonuses on your first three deposits).

Welcome bonuses often include free spins as part of the package — typically 50 to 200 spins on a specific slot.

What Is a No Deposit Bonus?

A no deposit bonus is exactly what it sounds like — free bonus money or free spins given to you simply for registering an account. No deposit required. You sign up, the bonus is credited, and you can start playing immediately.

These typically come in two forms:

  • Bonus cash: Usually €5 to €20 in free bonus funds
  • Free spins: Usually 10 to 50 free spins on a specific slot

The catch? No deposit bonuses come with much stricter terms — higher wagering requirements, lower maximum withdrawals, and game restrictions. They're a taster, not a feast.

Wagering Requirements — The Critical Difference

The wagering requirement determines how many times you must bet through bonus funds before withdrawing. This is where the two bonus types differ dramatically.

The welcome bonus has lower wagering per euro of bonus (35x vs 60x), no withdrawal cap, and a much higher potential payout. The no deposit bonus needs less total wagering (€600 vs €7,000) but caps your winnings heavily.

Welcome Bonus — Pros & Cons

✅ Welcome Bonus Advantages

  • Much larger bonus amounts (€100–€500+)
  • Lower wagering requirements (25x–45x)
  • No maximum withdrawal cap (usually)
  • Longer time to clear (14–30 days)
  • Often includes free spins as well
  • Better overall expected value

❌ Welcome Bonus Disadvantages

  • Requires a real deposit — your money is at risk
  • Higher total wagering amount in absolute terms
  • Bonus abuse detection can void winnings
  • Can encourage depositing more than intended

No Deposit Bonus — Pros & Cons

✅ No Deposit Bonus Advantages

  • Completely free — zero financial risk
  • Great way to test a casino before committing
  • Lower total wagering in absolute terms
  • Can genuinely win real money (small amounts)

❌ No Deposit Bonus Disadvantages

  • Very small amounts (€5–€20)
  • Very high wagering multiplier (40x–99x)
  • Maximum withdrawal cap (€50–€200 usually)
  • Short time limit (3–7 days)
  • Often restricted to specific slots only
  • May require a deposit before withdrawing winnings

Realistic Scenarios — What Actually Happens

Let's walk through realistic outcomes for each bonus type to set proper expectations:

💲 Welcome Bonus Scenario

You deposit €100 and receive a 100% match (€100 bonus). Wagering: 35x on bonus only = €3,500 total wagering needed. Playing a 96% RTP slot, your expected loss over €3,500 in wagers is €140. Starting with €200 (€100 deposit + €100 bonus), you'd expect to have around €60 left after clearing. But variance means you could have €0 or €500+.

🆕 No Deposit Bonus Scenario

You receive €10 free, no deposit. Wagering: 60x = €600 total wagering needed. Max withdrawal: €100. Playing a 96% RTP slot, your expected loss over €600 in wagers is €24. Starting with €10, you'd statistically lose the bonus before clearing. But occasionally, a lucky streak will get you to the €100 cap.

The honest truth: Most players will not profit from either bonus type long-term. Welcome bonuses give you more play time and a realistic (if slim) chance of walking away ahead. No deposit bonuses are fun freebies that very occasionally pay out a small amount. The real value of both is extended entertainment.

Key Bonus Terms to Watch

Regardless of which bonus type you choose, always check these critical terms before claiming:

  • Wagering requirement — Lower is better. Below 35x is good for welcome bonuses. Below 50x is good for no deposit bonuses.
  • Wagering base — Is wagering calculated on the bonus only, or bonus + deposit? "Bonus only" is twice as good.
  • Max bet while wagering — Typically €5 per spin. Exceeding this voids your bonus and winnings.
  • Time limit — Check how many days you have to clear the wagering. 30 days is fair. 7 days is tight.
  • Game weighting — Slots usually count 100%. Table games may count 10–20%. Some games are excluded entirely.
  • Max withdrawal cap — Common on no deposit bonuses. Anything above €100 is reasonable.
  • Deposit requirement on no deposit bonuses — Some casinos require a deposit before you can withdraw no deposit bonus winnings. Check this before playing.

Which Is Better for You?

Claim the no deposit bonus first (if available) — it's free money with no risk, and it lets you try the casino's games, interface, and support before committing a penny.

Then evaluate the welcome bonus carefully. Check the wagering requirements, game contributions, and time limits. A 100% match with 35x wagering is a solid offer. Anything above 50x wagering is questionable value.

If you're a casual player just looking to have some fun with minimal risk, no deposit bonuses are perfect. If you're serious about maximising your bankroll and willing to put in a real deposit, welcome bonuses offer far more potential value.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I withdraw no deposit bonus winnings immediately?▾
No. You must first meet the wagering requirements, which are typically 40x to 99x the bonus amount. For example, a €10 no deposit bonus with 60x wagering means you need to wager €600 before you can withdraw. Additionally, most no deposit bonuses have a maximum withdrawal cap (usually €50–€200), and some require you to make a minimum deposit before you can cash out any winnings.
Can I claim both a no deposit bonus and a welcome bonus?▾
At most casinos, yes. The no deposit bonus is credited upon registration, and the welcome bonus is credited when you make your first deposit. However, always read the terms — some casinos require you to complete the no deposit bonus wagering (or forfeit it) before claiming the welcome bonus. Check the specific casino's bonus terms before signing up.
What is a good wagering requirement for a welcome bonus?▾
Anything at or below 35x is considered fair and competitive in the Irish market. Between 35x and 45x is average. Above 45x becomes difficult to clear profitably. Above 60x is poor value and best avoided. Also check whether the wagering applies to the bonus only or to the bonus plus deposit, as the latter effectively doubles the requirement.
Are no deposit bonuses really free?▾
Yes, they genuinely don't require a deposit. However, "free" comes with strict conditions: high wagering requirements, maximum win caps, short time limits, and sometimes a deposit requirement before withdrawal. You won't risk any of your own money, but converting a no deposit bonus into withdrawable cash is statistically difficult. Think of it as a free trial rather than free money.
Which bonus type has better expected value?▾
Welcome bonuses have better expected value in absolute terms because the bonus amounts are much larger and wagering requirements are lower per euro of bonus. However, no deposit bonuses have infinite expected value relative to your investment, since you invest nothing. For raw value, welcome bonuses win. For risk-adjusted value (return per euro risked), no deposit bonuses are technically superior since you risk €0.

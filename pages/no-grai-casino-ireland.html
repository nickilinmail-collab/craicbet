No GRAI Casino Licence in Ireland: What You Need to Know

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 3 min read

No online casino in Ireland holds a GRAI licence because the GRAI has not issued any casino licences yet. As of November 2026, only remote betting licences have been issued (since 1 July 2026). Casino and gaming licences are expected to open from 2027–2028. Irish players who want to play at licensed online casinos currently use sites regulated by the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) or the UK Gambling Commission.

What Is a GRAI Casino Licence?

GRAI — the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland — was established by the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and began issuing licences on 1 July 2026. Critically: GRAI has issued remote betting licences only. No GRAI casino (gaming) licences have been issued yet. These are expected sometime in 2027–2028.

This means every online casino currently serving Irish players operates without a GRAI casino licence — because none exist yet. A "no GRAI casino" is therefore the current status of every online casino in Ireland.

Is It Legal to Play at a Casino Without a GRAI Licence?

Yes, for now. Under the transitional provisions of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, online casinos that were operating before 1 July 2026 can continue serving Irish players while GRAI develops its casino licensing framework. Players are not breaking any law by playing at an MGA or UKGC-licensed casino from Ireland while GRAI casino licences are pending.

What Changes When GRAI Casino Licences Are Issued?

When GRAI starts issuing casino licences (expected 2027–2028), operators will need to apply. Sites that do not hold a GRAI casino licence at that point may be required to stop serving Irish players. This is exactly what happened with GRAI betting licences in July 2026 — Stake, Betfred, Kalshi and Polymarket chose to geo-block Ireland rather than apply.

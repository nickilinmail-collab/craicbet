Is Online Betting Legal in Ireland in 2026?

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Online betting is fully legal in Ireland and has been domestically regulated since 1 July 2026 when the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) began issuing remote betting licences under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. This is a landmark change: for the first time, bookmakers serving Irish customers online are licensed by an Irish authority.

What Changed on 1 July 2026

Before July 2026, online bookmakers operated in Ireland under Revenue Commissioners permissions derived from the Betting Act 1931 — a framework designed for on-course bookmakers, not digital platforms. Those permissions expired on 30 June 2026. Any bookmaker serving Irish customers online now requires a GRAI remote betting licence. Operating without one is a criminal offence under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, with penalties of up to €20 million.

What is Legal Under the GRAI Framework

  • Placing bets with GRAI-licensed operators: fully legal
  • Sports betting, horse racing, GAA, e-sports: all covered by remote betting licences
  • In-play (live) betting: legal
  • Welcome bonuses and free bets: legal, subject to advertising restrictions

What About Offshore Sites?

Placing bets with unlicensed offshore bookmakers is not currently a criminal offence for individual punters in Ireland. The criminal liability is on the operator, not the customer. However, using unlicensed sites carries significant practical risks: no GRAI complaints route, no Irish consumer protection, no payout obligation under Irish law, and potential issues with payment processors as banks increasingly block transactions to unlicensed gambling entities.

Casino: A Different Situation

Online casino (gaming) licences are separate from betting licences. The GRAI has issued zero casino licences and does not expect to open applications until 2027–2028. Online casino sites available to Irish players currently operate under foreign licences (MGA Malta, Estonian, Curaçao). This is a legal grey area for the sites — not for Irish players, who face no personal legal liability for playing.

What Changes Under the 2024 Gambling Act

  • GRAI replaces the Revenue Commissioners as the regulator for gambling licensing and enforcement.
  • Mandatory age verification: Operators must verify age before allowing any deposit or play — no more grace periods.
  • Advertising restrictions: Gambling advertising watershed rules came into force, restricting TV and radio ads before 9pm.
  • National Self-Exclusion Register: A centralised register allowing problem gamblers to exclude from all GRAI-licensed operators simultaneously.
  • Operator liability: Enhanced duty of care obligations — operators must monitor for signs of problem gambling and take action.

Using Unlicensed Sites: What Irish Punters Should Know

  • Unlicensed sites are not regulated by the GRAI and offer no consumer protections under Irish law.
  • If an unlicensed site refuses to pay out, there is no Irish regulatory body to escalate to.
  • Using a VPN to access blocked sites may void your bets and account under the site's T&Cs.
  • Stick to GRAI-licensed operators — verify licence status at grai.ie.
Is online betting legal in Ireland in 2026?

Yes — fully legal and domestically regulated since 1 July 2026. Operators need a GRAI remote betting licence to legally serve Irish customers. Using a GRAI-licensed bookmaker is entirely legal.

What is the GRAI?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland — Ireland's first dedicated gambling regulator, created by the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. It issues licences, enforces rules, and handles consumer complaints.

Can I bet on offshore sites in Ireland?

It is not a criminal offence for individual punters to use offshore sites. However, you lose all Irish consumer protections: no GRAI complaints route, no payout obligation under Irish law, and payment processing risks.

Is the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 the same as the GRAI framework?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the law that created the GRAI and set the new framework. The GRAI is the authority established under that Act to implement and enforce the rules.

Are online casinos legal in Ireland?

Online casino sites are available to Irish players but are not yet licensed by the GRAI. Casino licences are expected 2027–2028. Current online casinos operate under foreign licences — legal for players but outside Irish jurisdiction.

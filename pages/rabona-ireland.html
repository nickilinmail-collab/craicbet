What Is Rabona?

Rabona is an online platform combining sports betting and casino gaming. It operates under a licence issued by the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), one of the most respected gambling regulators in the EU. The MGA licence is a credible regulatory credential that provides a baseline of player protection and dispute resolution across the jurisdictions the MGA covers.

Rabona offers a wide range of sports markets, covering football, tennis, basketball, and other international sports, alongside a substantial casino games library. It is primarily known in central and eastern European markets and does not have a particular Irish marketing presence.

That said, holding an MGA licence does not automatically authorise an operator to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence, and as of November 2026, Rabona does not appear to hold one.

Rabona’s GRAI Licence Status

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, Rabona does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means Rabona has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

Rabona in Ireland

Rabona may be technically accessible to users with Irish IP addresses via its MGA licence. However, accessible does not mean licensed, and there is a meaningful practical difference for players. When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account.

Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

None of this is intended to suggest Rabona is an unsafe operator in general terms — its MGA licence provides substantial protections in its licensed markets. But Irish players considering Rabona should be clear that they would be operating outside Irish regulatory oversight. Any dispute would be governed by MGA frameworks and Maltese jurisdiction, not Irish consumer law or GRAI oversight.

Ireland Gambling Statistics 2026: Every Verified Figure, Sourced

Ireland's adults spend an estimated €5.5 billion a year on gambling, about 1 in 30 adults (130,000 people) meets the problem-gambling threshold, and 77% of Irish gamblers now bet online. Those three numbers come from the ESRI's research series for the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI). This page collects every Irish gambling statistic we have been able to verify — prevalence, spend, online behaviour, market size, licensing — each with its source and year, and is updated whenever a new study or report is published. ESRI, October 2023: How Common Is Problem Gambling?

The ESRI's Measures of problem gambling, gambling behaviours and perceptions in Ireland (Research Series 169) surveyed a representative sample of 2,850 adults anonymously online in August 2023. It found that about three-quarters of adults had gambled in the previous month and a third had done so online. Its headline estimate — 3.3% of adults, about 130,000 people, with problem gambling — was roughly ten times the previous official figure, a gap the ESRI attributes mainly to the switch from face-to-face interviews to anonymous online surveying. A further 7.1% showed moderate evidence of problem gambling and 15% reported at least one negative experience.

Share of Irish adults by ESRI problem-gambling category, 2023. Source [1][2].

The study's most consequential finding is concentration: the 3.3% with problem gambling account for 28.3% of all gambling spend (about €1.6bn of €5.5bn), averaging more than €1,000 a month each; widen the group to everyone with problem gambling or moderate evidence (10.4% of adults) and it funds 47% of spend. Online gambling accounted for three-fifths of the spend of people with problem gambling. This single study shaped the player-protection provisions of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, from the credit card ban to the National Gambling Exclusion Register.

ESRI, July 2025: Free Bets and Money-Back Offers

A GRAI-commissioned experiment with 622 men under 40, run before Euro 2024, found that inducements such as free bets and money-back guarantees caused participants to spend more than 10% more and roughly halved the number who chose not to bet. Most participants misunderstood the terms: more than half who took a free bet believed their stake would be returned if they won. The ESRI said the results support stricter regulation of offers — which the 2024 Act now provides.

ESRI, November 2026: Online and In-Person Gambling Patterns

Patterns of online and in-person gambling in Ireland (Robertson, Ó Ceallaigh, Timmons and Lunn), published 4 November 2026, surveyed 1,000 adults who had gambled in the previous year. It is the most detailed picture yet of day-to-day gambling behaviour in Ireland. Our full write-up: ESRI study on online accounts.

Share of past-year gamblers, ESRI November 2026. Source [4].

*Of online account holders. "At least one account" is the ~80% figure reported by RTÉ from the launch. Source [4].

Almost four in ten people who opened an online account for horse or sports betting went on to casino games, slots or scratch cards, often via cross-selling; 25% said they were trying, or intended, to reduce their gambling; around one in ten online gamblers reported betting during working hours. The GRAI said the findings confirm the need for its new obligations on account opening and closing, spending limits and advertising.

Market Size, Licensing and the GRAI

Ireland has no single official measure of gambling turnover, so three partial sources are used. The GRAI's first annual report (July 2026) estimates gross gambling revenue at €1.17–€2.57 billion for 2025, with 6,000+ people employed and about €171m a year in tax; it counts 684 retail betting shops at end-2025, down from 1,385 in 2008. Revenue's betting-duty returns, as reported by Gambling.com, put online betting stakes at a record €911.2 million in Q1 2026 against €570.3m in shops. The Irish Times, citing the regulator, has reported industry turnover of €12–€14 billion a year.

On licensing: since 1 July 2026 every bookmaker serving Irish customers needs a GRAI licence. as of 12 November 2026 the register lists 41 online betting licences (37 remote betting, 4 betting intermediary) held by 39 companies, plus one in-person licence — see our register of GRAI-licensed bookmakers and GRAI licence explainer. No casino (gaming) licence has been issued; the regulator told the Oireachtas in September 2026 that its €13.35m allocation (against €26m requested) pushes remote gaming licensing into 2027 — why there is no GRAI casino licence yet.

Problem Gambling: Help in Ireland

If gambling has stopped being entertainment for you or someone close to you: Gambling Awareness Trust — 1800 753 753 (free, confidential) and gamblingcare.ie for counselling referrals and self-help tools. Every GRAI-licensed bookmaker must offer deposit limits, time-outs and self-exclusion; our guide to signs of problem gambling and support services lists every service available in Ireland. 18+ only.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do Irish people spend on gambling?

About €5.5bn a year in adult spend (ESRI, 2023). Industry gross revenue was estimated at €1.17–€2.57bn for 2025 (GRAI annual report); online betting stakes alone reached €911.2m in Q1 2026 (Revenue data).

How many problem gamblers are there in Ireland?

3.3% of adults — about 130,000 people, 1 in 30 — with a further 7.1% (~279,000) showing moderate evidence (ESRI, 2023). Roughly ten times earlier official estimates.

What share of gambling spend comes from problem gamblers?

28.3% from the 3.3% with problem gambling; 47% from the 10.4% with problem gambling or moderate evidence of it (ESRI).

How many Irish gamblers bet online?

77% of past-year gamblers bet online, 59% in person and 44% both; 54% of online account holders have a second account (ESRI, November 2026).

How many GRAI licences have been issued?

41 online betting licences held by 39 companies, plus one in-person licence, as of 12 November 2026. Zero casino licences — expected 2027–2028.

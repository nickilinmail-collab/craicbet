Booking Points Betting Ireland: Yellow & Red Card Markets Explained

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Booking points is a disciplinary betting market that assigns a points value to yellow and red cards shown during a match and asks you to predict whether the total will be over or under a set number. It is primarily a football market and is offered by most major bookmakers operating in Ireland.

How points are assigned

The standard bookmaker scoring system assigns 10 points for a yellow card and 25 points for a red card. A second yellow leading to a red counts for the initial yellow (10) plus the red (25), totalling 35 points for that player. Some bookmakers use a simplified system of 10 for yellow and 25 for red only (treating a second yellow as 25 points, not 35), so always confirm the specific scoring system used by the bookmaker you are betting with before placing. Total booking points markets are usually set at over/under levels such as 25, 30, 35 or 40 points.

What drives booking points

Disciplinary records vary significantly by referee, competition, and fixture type. Derbies, relegation six-pointers, and matches between historic rivals tend to generate more bookings than neutral fixtures. Referees have documented average booking rates: some officials average over 4 bookings per match, others average under 2. This data is publicly available and worth consulting before backing high totals. The tactical style of both teams also matters: pressing teams that commit more defensive fouls and counter-attacking sides that absorb pressure tend to feature in higher-points matches.

Specific player booking markets

Beyond total match booking points, bookmakers also offer markets on specific players to be booked anytime during the match. Aggressive central midfielders, full-backs in tight wide battles, and players with existing disciplinary records are commonly offered at odds of 3.0–6.0. These bets are higher-variance than total points markets but can offer value when a known aggressive player faces a pacey winger or forward who draws fouls. Checking a player’s cards per 90 minutes across the season is straightforward and useful context.

Booking points in accumulators

Booking points markets are sometimes included in multi-bet combinations alongside match results or over/under totals. The appeal is that disciplinary outcomes are partially independent of match result outcomes, theoretically offering a hedge. Frequently Asked Questions

How many points is a red card in booking points betting?

Most bookmakers assign 25 points for a red card and 10 points for a yellow card. A player receiving a second yellow (which leads to a red) is typically scored as 10 + 25 = 35 points, though some bookmakers use different systems. Always check the specific bookmaker rules before betting.

Does a booking in extra time count?

Most booking points markets settle on 90 minutes only, excluding extra time and penalty shootouts. If a card is shown before the final whistle but after the 90 minute mark (such as in injury time), it typically counts. Cards shown during extra time in a cup tie do not count for booking markets settled at 90 minutes.

Which leagues produce the most booking points?

Generally, South American competitions and some Southern European leagues show higher average booking rates than Northern European leagues. Within English football, the Championship and League One tend to have higher average bookings per match than the Premier League due to more physical styles and closer competitive margins.

Can I bet on a specific player to receive a red card?

Yes, many bookmakers offer a market for a specific player to be sent off anytime during the match. These bets are priced long, typically 15.0-30.0 or more, and are high-variance. They occasionally offer value when a player with a poor disciplinary record faces a provocation-heavy fixture.

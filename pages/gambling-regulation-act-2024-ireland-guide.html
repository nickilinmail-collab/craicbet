The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the most fundamental overhaul of Irish gambling law since the foundation of the State. It created a new statutory regulator, mandatory consumer protections, and a licensing regime that replaced a framework dating to the 1920s and 1930s. This page is a comprehensive reference for Irish players, journalists, and researchers.

1. What is the Gambling Regulation Act 2024?

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is an Act of the Oireachtas that fundamentally reformed the regulation of gambling in Ireland. It was described by McCann FitzGerald partner Alan Heuston in SBC News as "the most fundamental reform of gambling regulation since the formation of the Irish State".

It replaced two Acts that had formed the backbone of Irish gambling law for nearly a century: the Totalisator Act 1929 and the Betting Act 1931. Those Acts pre-dated online gambling entirely and left the Irish market without a fit-for-purpose regulatory framework for decades.

"The establishment of the GRAI, while framed as a regulatory reform, is at its core a public health measure." — Alan Heuston, Partner, McCann FitzGerald, quoted in SBC News

The Act covers remote gambling (online), in-person gambling (bookmakers, arcades), and gaming (casinos). It established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the statutory licensing and enforcement body. All major consumer protections — from deposit limits to self-exclusion — are grounded in the Act's requirements for GRAI licence conditions.

3. GRAI: The New Regulator

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) is Ireland's new statutory gambling regulator, established on 5 March 2025 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. The GRAI is responsible for licensing, compliance, enforcement, and consumer protection across all forms of gambling in Ireland.

5. Consumer Protections Under the Act

Credit Card Ban

From 1 July 2026, all GRAI-licensed operators are prohibited from accepting deposits made by credit card. This applies to all remote betting licence holders. The ban is intended partly as a harm-reduction measure, as research links credit card gambling to higher rates of problem gambling by separating the gambler from the immediate financial reality of their bets.

Age Verification

Under the Gambling (Verification of Identity and Age) Regulations 2026, all GRAI-licensed operators must verify that players are 18 or over before allowing any gambling activity. Marketing must not be directed at under-18s.

Mandatory Responsible Gambling Tools

All GRAI-licensed operators are required as a licence condition to offer — and immediately apply — the following tools:

  • Deposit limits (daily, weekly, monthly) — reductions apply immediately; increases require a waiting period
  • Session time limits and reality check notifications
  • Cooling-off periods — cannot be cancelled once activated
  • Self-exclusion at operator level, and via the National Gambling Exclusion Register (applies across all GRAI licensees simultaneously)

Restricted Inducements

Free bets, money-back guarantees, and bonus offers are subject to restrictions under the Act. ESRI research commissioned by the GRAI found these promotions increased gambling spending by over 10% in experimental conditions. GRAI licence conditions specify how such offers may be presented and marketed.

Withdrawal Rights and Complaints

Under Part 7 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, players can file formal complaints directly with the GRAI against operators who fail to enforce responsible gambling obligations, fail to pay winnings, or otherwise breach their licence conditions. The Gambling (Complaints Procedures) Regulations 2026 (S.I. No. 170/2026, 24 April 2026) set out the procedural requirements.

6. Enforcement Powers

The GRAI's enforcement powers are set out in Part 8 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. These are among the most significant aspects of the Act from a consumer protection perspective:

  • Fines: Up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover, whichever is greater, for serious breaches
  • Prison sentences: Up to 8 years for the most serious criminal offences under the Act
  • Licence suspension and revocation: The GRAI can suspend or revoke an operator's licence
  • Compliance agreements: Agreed remediation steps as an alternative to enforcement action
  • Operating without a licence: An offence in itself, with its own penalty provisions
📌 How to make a complaint: If a GRAI-licensed operator has breached your rights — failed to apply a self-exclusion, refused a withdrawal, or used prohibited inducements — you can file a formal complaint at grai.ie under Part 7 of the Act. See our GRAI complaints guide for step-by-step instructions.

7. What's Not Yet in Force

Not all provisions of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 are in force as of November 2026. Key areas still pending:

  • Gaming (casino) licences: Not yet issued. The regulatory framework for gaming licences is still being developed by the GRAI. The timeline is 2027–2028.
  • Combined licence categories (s.85(1)(c) of the Act): Licences that combine betting and gaming have not yet been introduced. This affects operators that wish to offer both sports betting and casino products under a single Irish licence.
  • In-person betting licences: Due from 1 December 2026 — high-street bookmakers are not yet formally transitioned to GRAI licensing as of the date of this guide.
  • Lotteries and gaming machine regulation: Specific provisions for certain licence categories are subject to separate commencement orders.
⚠️ Implication for online casino players: No online casino is legally operating under a GRAI gaming licence in November 2026. Online casinos accessible to Irish players are operating under licences issued by other jurisdictions (Malta, Gibraltar, Isle of Man, etc.). Irish players using these sites have fewer protections than those betting with a GRAI-licensed bookmaker.

9. Frequently Asked Questions

When did the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 come into force?
The Act was signed into law in October 2024. Key provisions came into force on 5 February 2026 under S.I. No. 31/2026, including the licensing and enforcement framework. Remote betting licences were first issued on 1 July 2026.
What does the Gambling Regulation Act mean for players in Ireland?
The Act introduced mandatory consumer protections: credit card deposits are banned, operators must offer deposit limits (effective immediately on request), age verification is compulsory, and players can complain directly to the GRAI under Part 7 of the Act. It also established the National Gambling Exclusion Register for a single self-exclusion across all licensed operators.
Does the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 cover online casinos?
Not yet. The GRAI is currently issuing remote betting licences only. Gaming (casino) licences are scheduled for 2027–2028. Zero casino licences have been issued as of November 2026. Any site claiming to hold a GRAI casino licence is making a false claim.

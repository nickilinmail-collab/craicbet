GRAI Licensed Bookmakers Ireland 2026

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

On 1 July 2026, Ireland became one of a small number of countries in Europe where online bookmakers must hold a domestic licence to legally serve customers. The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) began issuing remote betting licences, and every bookmaker taking bets from Irish residents is now required to hold one. This page explains exactly what GRAI licensing means, how to verify any bookmaker in under two minutes, what your rights are as a bettor, and why a licence matters far more than a welcome bonus headline figure.

What Is the GRAI?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland is Ireland's national gambling regulator, established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — the most significant piece of Irish gambling legislation since the Betting Act 1931. The GRAI replaced a patchwork of Revenue Commissioner permissions that were never designed for online betting: the old system was built around on-course bookmakers in the 1930s and had not kept pace with global digital gambling platforms.

The GRAI opened its licensing regime on 1 July 2026. All previous Revenue permissions expired on 30 June 2026. Any bookmaker that continues to serve Irish customers online without a current GRAI licence is committing a criminal offence under Section 47 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, with fines of up to €20 million or five years' imprisonment.

The GRAI sits in Dublin under the Department of Justice. Its mandate covers all gambling in Ireland: in-person betting shops, remote (online) betting, casinos, lotteries, and bingo. For now, only remote betting licences are live. Casino (remote gaming) licences are a separate category and no applications have been accepted yet — the GRAI has stated it does not expect casino licensing to open until 2027–2028.

The GRAI Licence Number Format Explained

Every GRAI licence follows a consistent format: GRAI-XXXX-TT-YY-NNNN. Here is what each segment means:

YYTwo-digit year of issue26 (2026)

So GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 tells you: operator 191, remote betting licence, issued in 2026, first licence in that series. When you see RI instead of RB, that is a betting exchange (intermediary) licence — a different category. If you see any other two-letter code, that is a licence type the GRAI has yet to open formally (such as remote gaming for casinos).

How to Verify Any Bookmaker in 2 Minutes

The GRAI maintains a free, publicly searchable register at grai.ie. It is updated in real time when licences are issued, suspended, or revoked. Here is the exact process:

  1. Open any betting site and scroll to the very bottom of the page.
  2. Find the legal company name in the footer — not the trading brand. It will be something like "Example Limited" or "Example Interactive (Ireland) Ltd".
  3. Go to grai.ie and open the public licence register.
  4. Search for the legal company name.
  5. Confirm: the licence type shows RB, the status is Active, and the expiry date is in the future.

What GRAI Licensing Means for You as a Bettor

A GRAI licence is not simply a stamp of approval — it is a legal contract between the bookmaker and the Irish state, with you as the protected party. Here is what it actually commits the operator to:

Age Verification

GRAI-licensed bookmakers must verify your age before you can place your first bet. This is not a formality — operators face sanctions for onboarding anyone under 18. You will need to provide valid photographic ID: a passport, driving licence, or national ID card. This verification normally happens within minutes using automated document checks, and your data is handled under GDPR.

Deposit Limits

Under GRAI rules, every licensed operator must offer you daily, weekly, and monthly deposit limit tools. Crucially, a request to reduce a limit must take effect immediately; a request to increase a limit must include a mandatory cooling-off period (typically 24–48 hours). This asymmetry is deliberate — it makes it easy to protect yourself but introduces friction before spending more. See our full GRAI deposit limit rules guide for the detail.

Self-Exclusion

All GRAI-licensed bookmakers must offer self-exclusion. The GRAI is building a national self-exclusion register that all operators will be legally required to check; once live, excluding yourself from one site will trigger a flag visible to all participating operators. In the meantime, each bookmaker manages their own list. If you self-exclude, the operator must immediately stop sending you marketing, and must not allow you to open a new account.

Payouts as a Licence Condition

Under the old Revenue system, paying out winnings was a matter of contract. Under the GRAI, paying out legitimate winnings is a licence condition. If a bookmaker refuses to pay you what you are owed, you can escalate to the GRAI; a pattern of unjustified refusals can cost the operator its licence. This is a fundamental shift from the pre-2026 framework where your only route was civil litigation.

Credit Card Ban

GRAI rules ban credit card deposits for all gambling. The accepted methods are debit cards (Visa, Mastercard), Revolut and other e-money accounts, bank transfer, and certain other payment methods. This is consistent with the approach taken in the UK since 2020 and is designed to prevent gambling on borrowed money. Bookmakers that accept credit card deposits from Irish customers are in breach of their GRAI licence conditions.

Formal Complaints Route

GRAI-licensed bookmakers must maintain an internal complaints procedure. If that fails, you can escalate directly to the GRAI as the national regulator — a route that simply did not exist in the same form before July 2026. The GRAI can investigate, sanction, fine, and ultimately revoke a licence.

The key practical risk of using an unlicensed site: if it refuses to pay your winnings, your only practical remedy is through a foreign court system or regulator. The GRAI cannot act against an operator that does not hold a GRAI licence — it has no jurisdiction over that company. Your Irish legal rights simply do not apply.

Important: GRAI Does NOT Issue Casino Licences

This is one of the most important facts on this page. As of November 2026, the GRAI has issued zero remote gaming (casino) licences. If a website claims to hold a "GRAI casino licence", that claim is false — no such licence exists. Online casinos operating in Ireland currently hold licences from foreign regulators such as the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA), Estonian RGL, or Curaçao eGaming. These are legitimate licences, but they do not give you the same Irish-law protections as a GRAI betting licence.

The GRAI has indicated it expects to begin accepting casino licence applications in 2027, with the first casino licences potentially issued in 2027–2028. Until then, sports betting is the only domestically regulated online gambling vertical in Ireland.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GRAI?

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) is Ireland's national gambling regulator, established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. It began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026 and replaced the old Revenue Commissioner permissions framework. Any bookmaker serving Irish customers online without a GRAI licence is now committing a criminal offence.

What does the GRAI licence number format mean?

GRAI licences follow the format GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-NNNN. XXXX is a four-digit operator identifier. RB stands for Remote Betting. YY is the two-digit year of issue. NNNN is a sequential number. For example, GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 is operator 191, remote betting, issued 2026, sequence 1. You can verify any licence at grai.ie.

How do I verify a GRAI betting licence?

Go to the GRAI's public register at grai.ie. Find the legal company name in the footer of the betting site. Search for that name on the register. Confirm the licence type is RB (Remote Betting), status is Active, and the expiry date is in the future. CraicBet also tracks the register daily — see our GRAI tracker.

Does the GRAI issue casino licences?

No. As of November 2026, the GRAI has issued zero remote gaming (casino) licences. Any site claiming to hold a "GRAI casino licence" is making a false claim. Casino licence applications are not expected to open until 2027–2028. GRAI currently licences remote betting (bookmakers) only.

What rights does a GRAI licence give me as a bettor?

A GRAI-licensed bookmaker must: verify your age before you bet; offer deposit limits effective immediately on request; offer self-exclusion; pay out legitimate winnings (a licence condition under Irish law); ban credit card deposits; and maintain a formal complaints process with the GRAI as escalation point. These are legal requirements, not optional extras.

What are the risks of using an unlicensed betting site in Ireland?

Using an unlicensed offshore site means no Irish legal protection if the site refuses to pay, no GRAI complaints route, no mandatory responsible gambling tools, and potentially no recourse if the site closes. The GRAI cannot act against an operator without an Irish licence. Your entire risk management depends on a foreign regulator's willingness to act.

How long is a GRAI remote betting licence valid?

GRAI remote betting licences run for three years. The first licence issued (GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001) was issued on 16 September 2026 and is valid until 15 September 2029. Operators must renew before expiry; the GRAI can impose new conditions on renewal.

How to Report an Illegal Betting Site in Ireland

Last updated: November 2026 — 18+ | Gambling Awareness Trust | 1800 753 753

If a betting site operating without an Irish licence has taken your money, refused a withdrawal, or simply vanished, here is exactly what to do — step by step.

Key point: As of 1 July 2026, all bookmakers targeting Irish customers must hold a GRAI remote betting licence. Operating without one is an offence under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — but as a player, you commit no offence by having used the site.

Step 1 — Confirm the site is actually unlicensed

Before reporting, check the GRAI public register. Search by the company name (not the brand name). Some sites trade under a different legal entity. If the operator holds a valid GRAI remote betting licence — number format GRAI-XXXX-RB-YY-ZZZZ — it is licensed regardless of what you read elsewhere.

Also check whether the site holds an unexpired Revenue Commissioners remote bookmaker permission from before July 2026 — these remain valid during the transition period.

Step 2 — Report to GRAI

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland accepts complaints and reports from the public at grai.ie. When submitting:

  • Include the full website URL and any brand or company names you can find
  • Attach screenshots of your account, deposits, and any refused withdrawals
  • Note the date you first deposited and the amount currently held
  • Include any emails or chat logs from their support team

GRAI cannot directly recover your funds, but a formal report creates an official record and may trigger enforcement action against the operator.

Step 3 — Chargeback through your bank

If you deposited by debit card or credit card, contact your bank immediately and raise a chargeback. Under Visa and Mastercard rules, you can dispute a transaction where the service was not provided as agreed — a refused withdrawal or account closure often qualifies.

Act quickly: chargeback windows are typically 120 days from the transaction date for Visa and Mastercard. Your bank will ask for evidence — the screenshots you gathered in Step 2 are exactly what you need.

Revolut users: Open the transaction in the Revolut app → tap “Get help with this payment” → select “Something went wrong” to start a dispute. Revolut processes Visa chargeback claims through their in-app dispute flow.

Step 4 — Report to Revenue (for Betting Duty)

Unlicensed operators also typically evade the 2% Betting Duty that licensed bookmakers pay on each bet placed by Irish customers. You can report tax evasion to Revenue at revenue.ie under their “Report Shadow Economy Activity” function. This is separate from GRAI and can run in parallel.

Step 5 — Garda if fraud is suspected

If the site appears to be a deliberate scam — fake company details, impersonating a known brand, or a Ponzi-style operation — file a report at your local Garda station or via the Garda Síochána website. For online fraud you can also contact the Garda National Economic Crime Bureau (GNECB).

What about ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution)?

Licensed operators under GRAI are required to offer access to an approved Alternative Dispute Resolution provider. Unlicensed sites have no such obligation, which is exactly why licensing matters. ADR routes are only available to you if the operator holds a current licence.

Who do I report an illegal betting site to in Ireland?
Report to the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) via grai.ie. You can also report to Revenue if the site lacks a Betting Duty registration. For suspected fraud, contact An Garda Síochána or the Garda National Economic Crime Bureau.
Can I get my money back from an unlicensed betting site?
It is difficult but not impossible. If you paid by debit or credit card, raise a chargeback with your bank immediately — act within 120 days of the transaction. GRAI complaints do not directly recover funds but create a formal record.
Is it illegal to use an unlicensed betting site in Ireland?
No. Irish law does not penalise players for using unlicensed sites — the offence sits with the operator, not the customer. However, you have no regulatory protection if something goes wrong.
How do I check if a betting site is licensed in Ireland?
Check the GRAI public register at grai.ie. All operators holding a remote betting licence are listed there with their licence number, legal company name, and status. Our GRAI checker guide walks you through the process step by step.

GRAI Casino Licence: When Will Online Casinos Be Licensed in Ireland?

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 10 min read

💡 Current Status (October 2026)

The GRAI does not yet issue casino or gaming licences. Only remote betting licences are available (since July 2026). Casino licences are on the GRAI's roadmap and are expected to become available in 2027 or 2028. All online casinos currently serving Irish players operate under international licences.

The Current State of Casino Licensing in Ireland

As of October 2026, the GRAI has successfully launched its first tranche of licences — remote betting licences — covering online sports betting and similar wagering activities. This was a landmark achievement for Irish gambling regulation.

However, the licensing framework for online casino games (slots, table games, live dealer games, and poker) has not yet been finalised. This means there is currently no Irish domestic licence that covers online casino gaming. Irish players who want to play casino games online do so at operators licensed by international regulators such as the MGA (Malta), UKGC (UK), or Gibraltar Gambling Commissioner.

What Will GRAI Casino Licences Cover?

While the final details are still being developed, the GRAI casino licence framework is expected to cover:

  • Online Slots: All real-money slot games offered to Irish players
  • Table Games: Blackjack, roulette, baccarat, and other casino table games
  • Live Dealer Games: Games streamed from live studios with real dealers
  • Poker: Online poker cash games and tournaments
  • Virtual Sports: Simulated sports events with real-money betting

What This Means for Irish Players

When GRAI casino licences become available, it will bring several important changes:

  • Full Domestic Protection: Irish players will have the same level of regulatory protection for casino games as they now have for sports betting.
  • Dispute Resolution: If you have a problem with an online casino, you will be able to complain to the GRAI — an Irish regulator that understands Irish consumer law.
  • National Self-Exclusion: The planned national self-exclusion register will cover casino operators as well as bookmakers.
  • Quality Assurance: GRAI-licensed casinos will need to meet Irish standards for fairness, security, and responsible gambling.

What Happens to Offshore Casinos?

Once the GRAI begins licensing casinos, operators serving Irish players without a GRAI licence may face enforcement actions. The GRAI has the power to:

  • DNS Blocking: Request ISPs to block access to unlicensed sites from Ireland
  • Payment Processing Restrictions: Prevent Irish financial institutions from processing transactions with unlicensed operators
  • Fines: Impose financial penalties on operators that continue to serve Ireland without a licence

This does not mean all offshore casinos will disappear. Major operators licensed by the MGA, UKGC, and Gibraltar are likely to apply for GRAI casino licences, ensuring continued service to Irish players under the new framework. Smaller or less reputable operators may choose not to apply, and Irish players may lose access to them.

Which Operators Are Likely to Apply?

While no operator has publicly confirmed plans to apply for a GRAI casino licence (since applications are not yet open), several types of operators are well positioned:

  • Operators already holding GRAI betting licences — they have an established relationship with the regulator
  • Large international operators with MGA or UKGC licences and an existing Irish customer base
  • Operators with a physical presence in Ireland (offices, employees, or Irish directors)

What Should Irish Players Do Now?

Until GRAI casino licences are available, Irish players should:

  1. Choose well-licensed operators: Prioritise casinos with MGA, UKGC, or Gibraltar licences for the strongest player protection
  2. Verify licences: Check the operator's licence on the regulator's website, not just the casino's own claims
  3. Use responsible gambling tools: Set deposit limits, use reality checks, and take advantage of self-exclusion if needed
  4. Stay informed: Follow CraicBet for updates as the GRAI casino licensing framework develops

FAQ: GRAI Casino Licences

When will the GRAI start licensing online casinos?▾
Casino licence applications are expected to open in 2027, with the first licences likely issued in late 2027 or 2028. The GRAI is currently developing the licensing framework through consultation with the industry and other stakeholders.
Can I still play at online casinos in Ireland right now?▾
Yes. Irish players can legally play at internationally licensed online casinos. The fact that the GRAI does not yet issue casino licences does not make it illegal to play — it simply means casino games are regulated by offshore authorities rather than the Irish domestic regulator.
Will my favourite casino be blocked when GRAI licensing starts?▾
That depends on whether your casino applies for and receives a GRAI licence. Major operators with MGA or UKGC licences are likely to apply. Smaller or less reputable operators may not, and could eventually be blocked. The transition period is expected to give operators time to apply.
How much will a GRAI casino licence cost?▾
The fee structure for GRAI casino licences has not yet been announced. For reference, GRAI remote betting licence fees and the costs charged by comparable regulators (MGA, UKGC) suggest that application fees and annual levies will be substantial but manageable for established operators.

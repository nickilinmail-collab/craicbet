Irish Champion Hurdle 2027 Betting Guide — The Leopardstown Trial

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

The Irish Champion Hurdle is a Grade 1 two-mile hurdle race held at Leopardstown in early February at the Dublin Racing Festival. It is Ireland's most important pre-Cheltenham trial for the Champion Hurdle and produces the season's clearest form reading ahead of the spring festivals. This guide covers how the race works, its relationship to the Cheltenham market, and how to approach it as a betting event.

The Irish Champion Hurdle: Race Profile

The Irish Champion Hurdle is a Grade 1 hurdle race run over 2 miles at Leopardstown's flat, left-handed circuit. Run in early February at the Dublin Racing Festival, it typically draws the leading two-mile hurdlers from both Ireland and Britain. The field is usually 6–12 runners — a championship quality race, not a large-field handicap.

Connection to the Cheltenham Champion Hurdle

The Irish Champion Hurdle has a direct line to the Cheltenham Champion Hurdle. Many of the same horses contest both races, and the Irish result frequently produces the Cheltenham market leader. However, the connection is not automatic:

  • Some trainers prefer not to run their Cheltenham hopefuls at Leopardstown in February, opting instead for the Christmas Hurdle (Kempton, December) or the Fighting Fifth (Newcastle, November).
  • Ground at Leopardstown in February is often softer than at Cheltenham in March. A horse that wins on soft ground may face a different test at the festival.
  • British trainers may send their horses on a different trial path, so the Irish race does not represent the entire Cheltenham market.

Ante-Post from the Irish Champion Hurdle

If you are holding ante-post Cheltenham Champion Hurdle selections, the Irish Champion Hurdle is the most significant single marker to watch. A dominant performance narrows the market significantly; a shock result can open it up. The post-Irish Champion Hurdle window (late February to early March) is the final chance to place ante-post bets at meaningful prices before the Cheltenham market firms up completely.

Each-Way Betting at the Irish Champion Hurdle

With a typical field of 6–12 runners, each-way terms can be as few as 3 places at 1/4 odds, or drop to 1/5 odds in a very small field. Always check the declared runners and confirmed each-way terms before placing. Smaller fields reduce the value of each-way positions compared to handicap races.

In-Running & Live Betting Ireland 2026: What You Can Bet On

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

In-running betting — placing a bet after an event has started — has transformed how Irish punters engage with sport. Where once you placed your bet before kick-off and watched passively, live betting lets you react to what you are seeing: back a team that is dominating but trail at half-time, lay a horse that falls out of the stalls, or take a short-price cash-out during injury time. This guide explains how in-play markets work, which sports offer the most options, how prices move, and what Irish punters should know under the 2026 GRAI framework.

How In-Play Betting Works

In-play (also called in-running or live betting) markets open once the event begins and prices shift constantly based on what is happening. Bookmakers use automated pricing engines fed by real-time data: for football, that means expected goals models, time elapsed, score, red cards and corner counts; for tennis, point-by-point win probability; for horse racing, in-race positioning updates.

When you place an in-play bet, most bookmakers impose a price delay of 3–5 seconds before confirming the bet. This prevents you from exploiting a 10-second broadcast delay to bet on an event (like a goal) you have already seen but the bookmaker's pricing engine has not yet reacted to. It is standard practice across all major operators.

What Sports Can You Bet on In-Play in Ireland?

Virtually every major sport has in-play markets available from at least one GRAI-licensed or internationally licensed bookmaker operating in Ireland:

  • Football: The deepest in-play offering. Next goal, next goalscorer, over/under total goals, corner markets, both teams to score by specific time intervals, red cards, shot specials.
  • Horse racing: In-running markets available on major races. Prices move second-by-second during the race. Some operators offer phone-only in-running betting for large stakes.
  • Tennis: Set winner, game winner, number of games in a set — all available in-play. Tennis is considered one of the best in-play sports due to clear momentum swings.
  • GAA: Match result and total scores available in-running on most major championship matches, though liquidity is thinner than football.
  • Rugby: Match result, next scorer, handicap — available in-play on Premiership, Pro14 and international matches.
  • Cricket, basketball, darts, snooker: All have in-play markets on major events.

How In-Play Odds Move

Understanding odds movement in-play is crucial to in-running strategy. When a goal is scored in football, the team that scored typically shortens significantly in the match winner market; the opposition drifts. The magnitude of the move depends on the time of the goal: a goal at 85 minutes affects odds far more than one at 20 minutes, because there is less time to recover.

Markets are often suspended briefly during key moments — a goal, a penalty, VAR check, or red card — while the pricing engine recalculates. During suspensions you cannot place bets. Cash out is also typically suspended at these moments. Good in-play bettors anticipate these suspensions and try to get bets on before an expected critical moment rather than reacting after it.

GRAI Rules for In-Play Betting in 2026

Under Ireland's Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and the GRAI framework that became fully operative from July 2026, in-running betting is a standard permitted product for licensed remote betting operators. There are no specific restrictions on in-play betting beyond the general licence conditions — operators holding a GRAI remote betting licence (like GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001) can offer full in-running markets.

Irish punters using GRAI-licensed operators benefit from the formal complaints and ADR (Alternative Dispute Resolution) processes that apply to all bets, including in-play. If a bet is declined or settled differently than expected, you have a formal route to dispute it. This consumer protection did not exist in the pre-GRAI era.

Strategies and Common Mistakes

In-play betting creates psychological pressure that pre-match betting does not. Watching a match and betting on it simultaneously can lead to chasing — placing reactive bets to cover losses from earlier in-play bets, escalating stakes to recover. This is one of the most dangerous patterns in sports betting. Set a fixed in-play budget before the match, and when it is gone, stop. Frequently Asked Questions

Can you bet in-running in Ireland?

Yes. In-running (live) betting is legal and widely available from GRAI-licensed and offshore bookmakers operating in Ireland. Most major bookmakers offer in-play markets on football, horse racing, tennis, rugby and more.

Why do odds change so fast in-play?

In-play odds are adjusted in real time based on the current score, time elapsed, possession, expected goals models and other data feeds. Bookmakers use automated pricing engines that react within seconds to events on the pitch.

What is a price delay in in-play betting?

A price delay (usually 3–5 seconds) is the gap between pressing 'place bet' and the bet being accepted. It allows the bookmaker to revise the price if the market moves significantly during that window.

Can bookmakers refuse in-play bets?

Yes. Bookmakers can decline in-play bets at their discretion, particularly at critical moments or if their system cannot price the market in real time. This is legal under their terms.

Are in-play horse racing bets available in Ireland?

Yes, some bookmakers offer in-running betting on horse racing, though the market moves very quickly during a race. Phone betting in-running is also available from some operators for major races.

Trebles Betting Ireland: How a Treble Bet Works

A treble is a single bet combining three selections. All three must win for the bet to pay out. If any one leg loses, the entire treble loses. The odds of all three selections are multiplied together to give the treble price, which is why trebles offer significantly higher returns than betting on each selection individually.

Treble vs Trixie vs 3 Singles: Which Is Better Ireland

The right choice depends on your confidence level and risk tolerance. A treble is all-or-nothing; a Trixie (4 bets: 3 doubles + 1 treble) returns something if only two legs win; three singles let you profit even if only one wins.

Treble Worked Example Ireland

Three selections at 2/1, 3/1, and Evens (2.0 decimal): multiplied together = 3.0 × 4.0 × 2.0 = 24.0 decimal odds. A €10 treble returns €240 (€230 profit) if all three win. The same three selections as singles at €3.33 each returns a maximum of €40.56 if all win, but returns something if only one or two win. The treble offers higher upside but nothing if even one leg fails.

What does DNB mean in betting?

Draw No Bet — if you back a team and the match draws, your stake is returned. Only a loss results in losing your bet. The draw is removed as a result that costs you money.

Is DNB the same as Asian Handicap 0?

Yes — mathematically identical. AH 0 and Draw No Bet have the same settlement rules: draw = stake returned, win = win, loss = lose. The notation differs by market style.

Are DNB odds lower than outright win odds?

Yes — because DNB includes draw insurance. The odds are lower to reflect the fact that you can't lose your stake in the event of a draw.

When should I use Draw No Bet?

When you're confident in a team but worried about a draw. DNB reduces profit on a win but provides a safety net. Worth using when draw probability is high and the team's win margin is uncertain.

What happens to a DNB bet in an accumulator if one game draws?

The DNB leg is treated as a void — stake for that leg is returned and it's removed from the acca. The remaining legs continue at adjusted odds.

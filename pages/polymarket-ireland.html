Polymarket Ireland 2026: Why It’s Blocked & What Irish Users Can Do

Polymarket is a US-based crypto prediction market that gained wide attention for letting users trade on political events, elections, and economic outcomes. In 2026, following reported pressure from the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI), Polymarket geo-blocked Irish users. Here is what happened, what it means, and what GRAI-licensed alternatives exist for Irish event-betting fans.

What Is Polymarket?

Polymarket is a US-based prediction market platform operated by Polymarket.com. Rather than betting with euros or dollars via a traditional bookmaker, Polymarket users trade positions denominated in USDC, a US dollar stablecoin built on blockchain infrastructure. Users can bet on the outcome of real-world events — including elections, political decisions, sports results, economic indicators, and technology milestones — with prices determined by the collective activity of other traders rather than odds set by a bookmaker.

Polymarket attracted substantial mainstream attention during the 2024 US presidential election, when its markets proved highly liquid and closely watched by political commentators and media outlets. The platform operates as a peer-to-peer prediction market, meaning users trade against each other rather than against a house. This model differs fundamentally from traditional fixed-odds sports betting.

Because Polymarket operates on-chain and uses cryptocurrency, it sits in an unusual regulatory space that most traditional gambling licensing frameworks were not designed to address.

Why Was Polymarket Restricted in Ireland?

According to reporting by GamblingInsider, Polymarket geo-blocked Irish users in 2026 following pressure from the GRAI, with threats of court orders reported as part of the enforcement action. This followed the GRAI’s activation as Ireland’s dedicated gambling regulator under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, which came into force for remote operators from 1 July 2026.

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, any platform that offers gambling products — broadly defined — to Irish customers is required to hold a GRAI remote betting licence. Prediction markets, including crypto-based platforms such as Polymarket, appear to fall within the scope of the Act, since participants stake value on uncertain outcomes. The GRAI does not appear to have issued any licence to Polymarket, and without a licence, the GRAI has the authority to pursue enforcement action against operators targeting Irish users.

Rather than pursuing a GRAI licence — a process that may be practically complex given Polymarket’s decentralised architecture — Polymarket is reported to have implemented geographic blocking for Irish IP addresses.

Does Polymarket Have a GRAI Licence?

No. As of November 2026, Polymarket does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. It has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence. You can verify the current position at grai.ie.

Beyond the absence of a licence, Polymarket’s structure creates complications for the traditional licensing process. GRAI remote betting licences are designed for identifiable legal entities operating centralised platforms. Polymarket’s peer-to-peer, blockchain-based model, with positions settled in USDC on-chain, does not map neatly to the framework. It is unclear whether Polymarket intends to seek a GRAI licence; no public announcement to that effect had been reported as of November 2026.

It is worth noting that GRAI currently issues remote betting licences only. Casino gaming licences are expected to follow in 2027–2028. This means no prediction market or gambling platform operating in Ireland has a GRAI casino licence — that category of licence simply does not yet exist.

Responsible Gambling

Prediction markets and sports betting carry a risk of financial loss. The house-edge equivalent in peer-to-peer prediction markets is reflected in the market’s trading fees; in traditional fixed-odds betting, it is reflected in the bookmaker’s margin. Neither model offers guaranteed returns. Set a budget you can afford to lose, use deposit limit tools offered by GRAI-licensed operators, and take breaks if gambling stops being entertainment.

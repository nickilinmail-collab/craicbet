GRAI Player Protection: How Ireland Protects Online Gamblers

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 10 min read
✅ Key Facts: GRAI Player Protection (November 2026)
  • Mandatory deposit limits (daily, weekly, monthly) from day one of every GRAI betting licence
  • NGEI — Ireland's national self-exclusion register — covers all GRAI-licensed betting sites with one registration; learn how to register →
  • Credit card gambling ban in force: debit cards only at all licensed operators
  • GRAI complaints are free and the outcome is binding on the licensed operator
  • Social Impact Fund: €40 million over 5 years for treatment, research and education
  • GRAI issues remote betting licences only — zero casino licences issued (expected 2027–28)

Player protection is at the heart of the GRAI's mission. Ireland's new gambling regulator — established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — was created with one overriding goal: ensuring that gambling in Ireland is safe, fair, and transparent for all players. This guide explains every protection available to Irish gamblers under the new regulatory framework as of November 2026.

Core Player Protection Measures

1. Deposit Limits

GRAI-licensed operators must provide tools that allow players to set daily, weekly, and monthly deposit limits. Once set, these limits cannot be increased without a cooling-off period (typically 24–72 hours), preventing impulsive decisions during a gambling session.

This is one of the most effective responsible gambling tools available. We recommend every player set deposit limits that match their budget before they start playing. Learn how to set limits →

2. Self-Exclusion & the NGEI

The GRAI has established the National Gambling Exclusion Ireland (NGEI) register — Ireland's equivalent of the UK's GAMSTOP system. Once registered, players are excluded from all GRAI-licensed betting operators with a single sign-up. Full NGEI guide: how to register and what it covers →

Self-exclusion periods run from a minimum of 6 months to permanent exclusion. GRAI-licensed operators are legally required to enforce exclusions and cannot reinstate an account until the exclusion period has ended and the player has gone through a formal reactivation process. Players can also self-exclude from individual operators directly through their account settings at any time.

3. Reality Checks

Licensed operators must implement session time notifications that alert players at regular intervals (typically every 30 or 60 minutes) about how long they have been playing and how much they have won or lost. These reality checks help players maintain awareness of their gambling activity and make informed decisions about whether to continue.

4. Activity Statements

Players at GRAI-licensed operators have the right to access detailed activity statements showing their deposits, withdrawals, bets placed, wins, losses, and net position over any time period. This transparency allows players to track their gambling behaviour and spot patterns that might indicate a problem.

5. Cool-Down Periods

If a player wants to take a break without full self-exclusion, GRAI-licensed operators must offer cool-down periods (also called time-outs) ranging from 24 hours to 6 months. During a cool-down period, the player cannot access their account or place any bets.

Advertising Protections

The GRAI has introduced statutory advertising codes that are legally binding on all licensed operators. Key restrictions include:

  • No targeting minors: Gambling advertising must not be directed at or appeal to anyone under 18. This includes restrictions on social media targeting, influencer partnerships, and the use of cartoon characters or themes in advertising.
  • Watershed rules: Television and radio gambling advertising is restricted to post-watershed hours (after 9pm) except during live sporting events.
  • Responsible gambling messaging: All gambling advertisements must include responsible gambling messages, helpline numbers, and age-verification reminders.
  • No misleading claims: Bonus offers and promotions must be presented with clear terms and conditions. Operators cannot advertise bonuses without disclosing wagering requirements.
  • Social media restrictions: Operators face restrictions on targeting gambling advertisements to users under 25 on social media platforms.

How to Make a GRAI Complaint

If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed betting operator, you have a clear, legally-backed path to resolution. The process is free and the outcome is binding on the operator. You do not need a solicitor. See also our detailed guide to the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 for the statutory basis of these rights.

  1. Gather evidence: Screenshot your account history, bonus terms, chat transcripts and any emails. Clear records are essential at every stage.
  2. Contact the operator: Submit a formal written complaint via the operator's official complaints email or form. Note the date. Most issues are resolved here within 2–4 weeks.
  3. Request a deadlock letter: If 8 weeks have passed without a satisfactory resolution (or the operator has explicitly rejected your complaint), ask for a written “deadlock letter” confirming the impasse. This is your ticket to escalate.
  4. Submit to GRAI ADR: File your complaint with the GRAI's Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) service. Attach all evidence and the deadlock letter. The GRAI may investigate directly or appoint an approved ADR body. Submission is free.
  5. Outcome is binding: Once the GRAI or the appointed ADR body issues a decision, the licensed operator must comply. Non-compliance risks licence suspension or revocation.

Important: GRAI jurisdiction covers operators holding a GRAI remote betting licence (such as licence GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001). For operators licensed only offshore (MGA, UKGC, Gibraltar), direct your complaint to that regulator. No GRAI casino licences have been issued as of November 2026 — casino licensing is expected 2027–28.

The Social Impact Fund

The Gambling Regulation Bill established a Social Impact Fund financed by levies on licensed gambling operators. This fund supports:

  • Treatment: Funding for problem gambling treatment services, including counselling and rehabilitation programmes
  • Research: Academic research into gambling behaviour, prevalence, and harm in Ireland
  • Education: Public awareness campaigns about responsible gambling and the risks of problem gambling
  • Support Services: Grants to organisations providing support to problem gamblers and their families

This ensures that the gambling industry directly contributes to mitigating the harm it can cause — a principle known as the "polluter pays" approach.

Protection for Vulnerable Players

The GRAI places particular emphasis on protecting vulnerable players, including:

  • Young adults (18–25): Additional advertising restrictions and enhanced monitoring for early signs of problem gambling
  • Problem gamblers: Operators must train staff to identify signs of problem gambling and intervene appropriately
  • Self-excluded players: Operators must take all reasonable steps to prevent self-excluded players from gambling, including identity verification and account monitoring

GRAI Player Protection Tools Ireland: What You're Entitled To

Under GRAI rules, all licensed operators in Ireland must provide a range of mandatory player protection tools. These include deposit limits, session time alerts, self-exclusion via the National Gambling Exclusion Register, and automatic reality check pop-ups during play. Players can access and adjust most of these tools directly within their account settings.

FAQ: GRAI Player Protection

How do I set deposit limits at an online casino?▾
Most online casinos have deposit limit settings in your account or responsible gambling section. You can set daily, weekly, and monthly limits. At GRAI-licensed operators, this is mandatory — the option must be available. We recommend setting limits before you start playing.
What is the national self-exclusion register?▾
The GRAI is developing a national self-exclusion register that will allow Irish players to exclude themselves from all licensed gambling operators with a single registration. This is similar to the UK's GAMSTOP system. Until it launches, you can self-exclude from individual operators through their account settings.
Can I complain to the GRAI about a casino?▾
As of November 2026, GRAI has only issued remote betting licences — zero casino (gaming) licences have been issued. Casino licences are expected from 2027–28. If you have a complaint about a site currently marketed as a “casino” that holds an offshore licence (MGA, UKGC, Gibraltar), direct your complaint to that regulator. Once GRAI casino licences are issued, the same complaints process described above will apply.
Are gambling adverts regulated in Ireland?▾
Yes. The GRAI has introduced statutory advertising codes that restrict when, where, and how gambling can be advertised. Key rules include no targeting minors, post-watershed broadcasting restrictions, mandatory responsible gambling messaging, and restrictions on social media targeting of young adults.
Can I appeal a GRAI complaint decision?▾
If you are unhappy with the outcome of the GRAI's ADR process, you can appeal to the courts, as the GRAI's decision can be reviewed judicially. However, in practice most players accept the ADR outcome as it is binding on the operator. If you believe the decision was procedurally unfair, contact a solicitor or the Free Legal Advice Centres (FLAC) for guidance.
Does GRAI player protection apply to casino players?▾
As of November 2026, GRAI has issued remote betting licences only — no casino (gaming) licences exist yet (zero issued; expected 2027–28). The protections on this page (deposit limits, NGEI self-exclusion, complaints, advertising rules) apply fully to players using GRAI-licensed betting sites. When GRAI casino licences are issued, the same framework will extend to those operators. Until then, online casinos operating in Ireland hold offshore licences (MGA, UKGC, etc.) and player protections are governed by those regulators.

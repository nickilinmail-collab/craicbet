Corners Betting: All the Markets and How They Work

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Why corners betting exists

Corners are a natural subsidiary betting market in football because they are largely independent of the match result — a team conceding a goal does not necessarily take fewer corners, and a dominant team can take many corners without winning. This independence from the main result makes corners a popular market for punters who have a view on a game’s balance of play without being certain of the result.

Over/under corner totals

The most popular corners market is over/under a total line, typically 9.5, 10.5 or 11.5 corners for a match. Teams with high possession and wide attacking play (Liverpool, Manchester City, Barcelona in historical data) tend to generate more corners. Away sides in defensive fixtures tend to take fewer. The line is set to reflect the expected total; over and under are typically priced at roughly 1.85-2.00 each.

Asian corner handicap and team totals

Asian corner handicap applies a notional corner start to one team (Team A −2.5 corners). Team corner total markets let you back one side to take over or under a specified number of corners (e.g. Home over 5.5). These markets are more niche and may not be available on all fixtures, but are common on Premier League and Champions League matches.

First corner and other specials

First corner (which team takes the first corner of the match) is priced at roughly 1.6 for home and 2.1 for away in most Premier League fixtures, reflecting home advantage in early match pressure. Factors That Drive Corner Totals Up or Down

  • High corners: attacking teams with wide play, teams with large possession advantage, high-tempo matches
  • Low corners: compact defensive teams, direct / long-ball style, rain/heavy pitch (fewer wide runs)
  • Derby matches: more defensive caution often reduces corners; both teams less willing to push forward
  • Asian corner handicap: gives one team a head start (e.g. -1.5) — good when one team dominates possession

What is the most popular corners betting market?

Over/under total corners for the match is the most popular corners market, typically set at a line of 9.5, 10.5 or 11.5. Each side of the market is priced at roughly 1.85-2.00, reflecting an approximately even probability on the line chosen.

What factors influence how many corners a match produces?

Attacking style, possession share, home advantage, defensive organisation of the opposition and the importance of the match for the leading team all influence corner rates. Wide attacking teams with high possession tend to generate more corners; compact defensive sides playing direct football tend to generate fewer.

Can I bet on corners in-play?

Yes. In-play corners betting is available on most televised fixtures. Next corner and updated total corners markets are available from kick-off. In-play prices move rapidly based on the phase of play, so having a pre-match view on corner rates helps when betting live.

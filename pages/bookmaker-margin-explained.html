Bookmaker Margin (Overround) Explained

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Every bet has a price beyond the stake: the margin, or overround, baked into the odds. It is how bookmakers earn a living regardless of results, and it is measurable by anyone with a phone calculator. Punters who never compute it are shopping without looking at price tags.

The one-line formula

Convert every outcome’s decimal odds to implied probability (1 ÷ odds), add them up, subtract 100%. Whatever remains is the overround. A fair book would sum to exactly 100%; a real one always sums to more, because the bookmaker shades each price below its fair value.

A worked example

Match odds: home 1.90, draw 3.50, away 4.20. Implied probabilities: 52.6% + 28.6% + 23.8% = 105.0%. The overround is 5%. Interpreted honestly: if punters spread money across this market in proportion to those prices, the book keeps about €4.76 of every €100 staked (5 ÷ 105), whatever the result. That is the real cost of playing in this market.

Margin varies wildly by market

Margins are not one number per bookmaker — they are set per market. Headline match-result odds on big televised football are kept lean because punters compare them; the overround there is commonly only a few percent. Step into player cards, exact scores, corners brackets or obscure leagues and the same book may be running several times that, because nobody is checking. The pattern to remember: the further from the headline market, the wider the margin — and bet builders compound several wide margins together.

Two-outcome markets: the quickest check

Over/under and most tennis or handicap markets have two sides, so the check is instant. Both sides at 1.90: 52.6% + 52.6% = 105.3%, about a 5% overround. Both at 1.83: over 109%. If you ever see both sides of a two-way market at 2.00 or better between different bookmakers, the margin is zero or negative across them — which is why serious punters hold accounts at more than one book.

Margin is the real price of every bet

Results decide single bets; margin decides careers. A punter who is genuinely as good as the market still loses the overround on turnover, bet after bet. Cutting your average margin — by betting main markets, comparing books, and using a calculator before an impulse — does more for long-run results than any tipster. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate a bookmaker’s margin?

Take 1 ÷ decimal odds for every outcome in the market, add them up and subtract 100%. Example: 1.90, 3.50 and 4.20 give 52.6% + 28.6% + 23.8% = 105%, i.e. a 5% overround.

What is a typical bookmaker margin?

There is no single number — margin is set per market. Heavily compared markets (match result on big football, major racing) are kept lean at a few percent, while props, exact scores and minor leagues can carry several times that. The further from the headline market, the wider the margin.

Why does margin matter if I only bet occasionally?

Margin is the service fee you pay on every single bet, win or lose. Over any run of bets you concede roughly the average overround on your turnover, so choosing lean markets and comparing bookmakers is the most reliable “edge” an ordinary punter has.

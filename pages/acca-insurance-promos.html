Promo Terms Decoder: Enhanced Odds, Acca Insurance and the Standard Catches

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Enhanced odds, acca insurance, money-back if your horse falls at the last: betting promotions are written by marketing and settled by terms and conditions. The gap between the two is where the value quietly leaks out. This page decodes the standard catches — the same handful of mechanisms, reused across almost every offer — so you can price a promotion the way the bookmaker already has.

Catch one: paid as a free bet

The single most important clause in any refund-style offer is how the money comes back. “Money back as cash” is what it sounds like. “Money back as a free bet” is worth roughly half to two-thirds of face value, because free bets are stake-not-returned: a €10 free bet at odds of 3.00 returns €20, not €30. The realisable value depends on the odds you use it at and on whether you can use it at all before it expires — typical windows are measured in days. Any offer comparison should start by converting free-bet refunds to their cash equivalent.

Catch two: caps and minimum odds

Enhanced odds come with a maximum stake — often small — so the headline price applies to pocket money while your normal stakes get the normal price. Refund offers carry a cap (“money back up to €10”) regardless of your stake. And nearly every promotion specifies minimum odds per selection, which pushes you toward longer prices and higher variance than you might otherwise choose. None of these terms is hidden; all of them are doing quiet work in the bookmaker’s favour.

Acca insurance, priced honestly

“One leg lets you down, stake back” sounds like the bookmaker absorbing your worst luck. Run the numbers: the refund is capped, usually paid as a free bet, usually requires five-plus legs at minimum odds — and the compounding margin on a five-leg acca dwarfs the insurance’s expected value. The offer exists because it nudges punters from three legs to five, and the extra two legs of margin cost more than the insurance pays. Insurance that increases your expected spend is not insurance; it is marketing with a deductible.

What the law now says about offers

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 tightened the rules around inducements: GRAI-licensed operators face restrictions on targeted offers and VIP-style treatment, and research commissioned by the regulator has linked promotional offers to increased betting, particularly among people at risk of harm. Our inducements guide covers the detail; the short version is that the era of the unregulated come-on is ending, and offers you do see must live within licence conditions.

A one-page decoder

Before taking any promotion, answer five questions from the terms: Cash or free bet? What is the cap? What are the minimum odds? What is the expiry? What must I stake to qualify? If the answers survive that reading and the bet is one you would have placed anyway, the promotion is a genuine, modest discount. If the offer is the reason for the bet, the bookmaker has achieved exactly what the promotion was priced to achieve. Frequently Asked Questions

Why is money back as a free bet worth less than cash?

Free bets are stake-not-returned: a €10 free bet at odds of 3.00 returns €20, not €30. The realisable value is roughly half to two-thirds of face value, depending on the odds you use it at — and it is zero if it expires unused.

Is acca insurance good value?

Rarely. The refund is capped and usually paid as a free bet, qualifying typically needs five-plus legs at minimum odds, and the compounding margin on those extra legs costs more than the insurance’s expected value. It is marketing that nudges you from three legs to five.

Are betting promotions regulated in Ireland?

Yes. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, GRAI-licensed operators face restrictions on inducements, including targeted offers, and promotions must operate within licence conditions — see our inducements guide for the detail.

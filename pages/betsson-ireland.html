Betsson Ireland 2026: GRAI Licence Status & What Irish Players Need to Know

What Is Betsson?

Betsson is an online betting and casino brand operated by Betsson AB, a Swedish gambling group founded in the 1960s and listed on Nasdaq Stockholm. The company has grown substantially through acquisitions and now operates a portfolio of brands across Europe, including NordicBet, Betsafe, and CasinoEuro, among others.

The Betsson brand itself holds licences from the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) and operates in regulated markets across Sweden, Estonia, and other European jurisdictions. It is a legitimately operating gambling company in its licensed markets, known for a wide range of sports betting markets, live casino products, and an emphasis on Nordic and Central European customers.

That said, holding an MGA licence or licences in other European markets does not automatically authorise an operator to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence issued by the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland.

Does Betsson Have a GRAI Licence?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, Betsson does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators. This means Betsson has not been granted a GRAI remote betting licence and is not currently operating under Irish regulatory authorisation.

It is worth noting that Betsson being absent from the GRAI register does not make it an illegitimate operator overall — it holds valid licences in other jurisdictions. However, it does mean that Betsson is not currently authorised to operate under Ireland’s specific regulatory framework.

Betsson in Ireland

Betsson may remain technically accessible to users with Irish IP addresses via its MGA licence, depending on whether Betsson has implemented geographic restrictions for the Irish market. However, accessible does not mean licensed, and there is a meaningful practical difference for players.

When you use a bookmaker that holds a GRAI licence, you are covered by Irish-specific regulatory requirements. When you use an operator without a GRAI licence — regardless of what other licences it holds — those Irish protections do not apply to your account. Specifically, without a GRAI licence:

  • Deposit limit enforcement: GRAI-licensed operators must enforce deposit limits as required under Irish regulations. An operator outside the GRAI framework is not bound by these requirements.
  • NGEI integration: GRAI licensees must integrate with the National Gambling Exclusion Identification (NGEI) register, Ireland’s national self-exclusion scheme. A non-GRAI operator is not required to check or honour NGEI exclusions.
  • Irish complaints escalation: If you have a dispute with a GRAI-licensed operator that cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the GRAI. No such formal Irish route exists for a non-GRAI operator.
  • Credit card ban compliance: Irish law bans credit card use for gambling with GRAI-licensed operators. This requirement does not bind a non-GRAI operator, so you might technically be able to use a credit card — a form of gambling with borrowed money that Irish regulators have specifically prohibited.
  • GRAI-mandated withdrawal timelines: GRAI licensees must meet Irish-regulated payout standards. Non-GRAI operators are governed only by their own terms and their home regulator.

None of this is intended to suggest Betsson is an unsafe operator in general terms — its MGA licence provides substantial protections in its licensed markets. But Irish players considering Betsson should be clear that they would be operating outside Irish regulatory oversight.

What Is an Accumulator Bet? Acca Betting Explained

Accumulators — universally known as "accas" in Ireland — are the most popular form of multiple bet. The idea is simple: you combine several selections into one bet, and if every selection wins, the returns can be substantial even from a small stake. This guide explains how accas work, how to calculate the payout, and what the honest trade-offs are.

What Is an Accumulator?

An accumulator combines four or more selections into a single bet. (Two selections combined is called a double; three is a treble; four or more is an accumulator.) All selections must win for the bet to pay out — if even one leg loses, the entire acca is lost.

The appeal is that the odds of each selection multiply together, turning modest individual prices into a large combined price.

Simple accumulator example:
You pick three football matches, each priced at 2/1 (decimal 3.0).
Combined decimal odds = 3.0 × 3.0 × 3.0 = 27.0
A €1 stake returns €27 (€26 profit) if all three win.

How Accumulator Odds Multiply

Always convert to decimal odds when calculating acca returns. The formula is straightforward: multiply all the decimal odds together, then multiply by your stake.

Four-team football accumulator — worked example:
Selection 1: 2/1 → decimal 3.0
Selection 2: 3/1 → decimal 4.0
Selection 3: 1/1 (evens) → decimal 2.0
Selection 4: 4/1 → decimal 5.0

Combined = 3.0 × 4.0 × 2.0 × 5.0 = 120.0
€5 stake × 120.0 = €600 total return (€595 profit)

Why the House Edge Multiplies Too

This is the part bookmakers don't advertise prominently. Each leg of your acca includes the bookmaker's margin built into the odds — typically 5–10% per market. When you multiply legs together, the margins multiply too.

Honest maths: If a bookmaker charges a 7% margin on each leg, a 4-leg acca compounds that to roughly 25% of your stake being eaten by margin before the events even kick off. The more legs you add, the worse this gets statistically.

That doesn't mean accas are irrational — entertainment value and the chance of a large return from a small stake are genuine reasons people enjoy them. But going in with eyes open to the maths helps you manage expectations and stakes sensibly.

Acca Insurance

Many GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer acca insurance — usually a refund (as a free bet) if exactly one leg of your accumulator loses, provided the remaining legs all win. This is a genuine promotion that reduces the effective cost of one losing leg. Terms vary: minimum legs required, minimum odds per selection, and free-bet restrictions all differ by operator. Under GRAI consumer protection rules, accumulator insurance terms must be clearly displayed before you place the bet.

When Accas Make Sense vs Singles

Singles (one bet per selection) are almost always better expected-value bets than combining the same selections into an acca, because each single avoids the compounding of margin. However:

  • If your budget is €5 and you want a chance at a meaningful return, an acca gives you that chance a single never could
  • Acca insurance and enhanced acca promotions can tilt value toward accas in specific situations
  • Entertainment and engagement are real (if unquantifiable) benefits for many people

As a general approach: use accas with low stakes for fun; use singles when you've done analysis and want to back your view more seriously.

Other Multiple Bet Types: Yankee, Trixie, Patent

Beyond the straight acca, bookmakers offer structured multiples that cover more combinations:

  • Trixie — 3 selections: 3 doubles + 1 treble = 4 bets
  • Patent — 3 selections: 3 singles + 3 doubles + 1 treble = 7 bets
  • Yankee — 4 selections: 6 doubles + 4 trebles + 1 four-fold = 11 bets

These provide partial returns if not all selections win — but your total stake is multiplied by the number of bets. A €1 Yankee costs €11. These are covered in more detail in our full guides section.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an accumulator in betting?

An accumulator (or acca) combines 4 or more selections into one bet. All selections must win for the bet to pay out. The odds of each selection multiply together, giving the potential for large returns from a small stake.

How is an accumulator payout calculated?

Multiply the decimal odds of each selection together, then multiply by your stake. For example: 2.0 × 3.0 × 4.0 = 24.0. A €5 stake returns €120 (€115 profit).

Is an accumulator a good bet?

Accas are exciting and can produce big wins from small stakes, but statistically you are compounding the bookmaker's margin with each selection. Each additional leg makes it less likely to win. They are best treated as entertainment with a small, affordable stake.

Ireland’s Gambling Advertising Watershed: What You Will Notice

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Irish punters are about to notice something odd: daytime sport with no betting ads. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 provides for a broadcast watershed — no gambling advertising on TV or radio between 5:30am and 9:00pm — and it reshapes not just the ad breaks but, reportedly, which racing channels you can watch. This is the consumer view; the regulatory detail lives in our watershed rule guide.

The Rule in One Line

Between 5:30am and 9pm, licensed operators cannot advertise gambling on television or radio — the hours chosen to keep gambling ads away from children, mirroring the approach long applied to other age-restricted products.

When You Will Actually Notice

Like most of the Act, the watershed applies through phased commencement, attaching to operators as each licensing phase opens — bookmakers first, since remote betting has been licensed since July 2026. Expect daytime betting ads to thin out progressively rather than vanish overnight, with evening sport (after 9pm) carrying the load instead.

Not Just TV: The Online Rules

The watershed itself is a broadcast rule, but the Act pairs it with online restrictions: gambling ads on social media only to users who opted in, and a ban on advertising designed to appeal to children. The days of a betting ad finding you unasked on a feed are numbered — by law, if not yet in practice everywhere.

The Racing Channel Question

Racing broadcasters are the hard case: daytime racing coverage is wrapped in betting advertising by its nature. During the Bill's passage, Racing TV and Sky Sports Racing reportedly warned that an unamended watershed could make broadcasting into Ireland economically unviable, and the Racing Post reported Sky Sports Racing was preparing a separate Irish feed with tailored advertising to comply. Calls for a subscription-channel exemption were made in the Seanad; if you follow Irish racing, the practical effect is worth watching as commencement proceeds.

What It Does Not Change

Sponsorship, stadium hoardings and jersey deals sit under separate provisions and debates — see our sponsorship page. And no advertising rule changes the product: the odds and margins behind the ads are the same before and after 9pm, which is the maths that actually matters.

Why Punters Should Welcome It

Less ambient prompting is good for anyone managing their betting — and an operator complying with Irish advertising law is an operator inside the system, with a licence and €20m sanctions to lose. The ads you do see after 9pm will increasingly be from bookmakers the GRAI can actually reach.

The Inducement Ban: What Bookmakers Can No Longer Offer New Customers

Alongside the watershed, the Gambling Regulation Act limits inducements to new customers. Under the Act, once fully commenced, operators holding GRAI licences will face restrictions on the type of promotional offers they can advertise publicly — particularly those targeting under-18s or vulnerable persons.

The detail on inducement rules is still being implemented through GRAI regulations. What is already clear: aggressive "Sign up and get €X free" advertising directed at non-customers on public media will be curtailed, and all promotions must carry responsible gambling messaging.

For punters who already use licensed sites: bonuses and reload promotions to existing customers are treated differently and are not banned, though GRAI may impose rules on the format of their advertising.

What This Means for Irish Punters in Practice

The watershed does not change what you can bet on, where, or for how much. It changes what bookmakers can advertise on TV and radio during family viewing hours. For punters:

  • Odds and markets remain unchanged — the watershed is about advertising, not operations
  • Welcome bonuses still exist and are still legal — they just cannot be advertised before 9pm on broadcast media
  • Online advertising on sites you visit voluntarily (google search results, review sites like this one) is not affected by the broadcast watershed
  • GRAI-licensed operators (betting licence GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 and others) must comply; unlicensed sites operating in Ireland are not bound by GRAI advertising rules but are operating without an Irish licence
What hours are gambling ads banned on Irish TV and radio?

Between 5:30am and 9:00pm, under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 — commencing in phases as operators come inside the licensing system.

Will the watershed take racing channels off Irish screens?

Racing TV and Sky Sports Racing reportedly warned it could make Irish broadcasting unviable; the Racing Post reported a separate Irish feed was being prepared. Watch how commencement proceeds.

Does the watershed cover online and social media ads?

The watershed covers broadcast only — but the Act separately limits social media gambling ads to opted-in users and bans ads appealing to children.

How Craps Works

Craps is a dice game where one player (the "shooter") rolls two dice and the other players bet on the outcomes. In online craps, a virtual shooter rolls automatically; in live dealer craps, a real dealer rolls physical dice at a physical table streamed by video.

A round of craps has two phases:

  1. The Come Out Roll: The shooter's first roll.
    • 7 or 11 (a "natural") — Pass Line wins, Don't Pass loses.
    • 2, 3 or 12 ("craps") — Pass Line loses. Don't Pass wins on 2 or 3; ties on 12.
    • Any other number (4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10) — that number becomes the Point.
  2. The Point Phase: The shooter keeps rolling until either the Point number is rolled again (Pass Line wins) or a 7 is rolled (Pass Line loses — a "seven out").

The Odds Bet: Zero House Edge

After a Point is established, you can place an Odds bet directly behind your Pass Line bet. The Odds bet is unique in casino gambling: it carries zero house edge. It pays at true mathematical odds:

  • Point of 4 or 10: pays 2:1
  • Point of 5 or 9: pays 3:2
  • Point of 6 or 8: pays 6:5

Most online casinos allow 2x, 3-4-5x or even 10x Odds. The more Odds you take relative to your Pass Line bet, the lower the combined house edge. At 10x Odds, the blended edge of your Pass Line + Odds falls below 0.2%. This is the single best expected-value play available across any casino table game.

Bets to Avoid

The craps table is littered with Proposition bets in the centre — Any 7, Any Craps, Hardways, Horn, World, etc. These bets carry house edges from 9% to over 16% and are designed to look exciting while extracting money rapidly. Treat the centre of the craps layout as off-limits for any serious strategy.

Similarly, Field bets (covering 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11 or 12 on the next roll) carry a 5.6% house edge at standard pay tables — far higher than Pass Line + Odds.

Cash Out and Acca Insurance: What They Really Cost

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Cash Out Is a Bet Sale — at the Shop's Price

Cash out is the bookmaker offering to buy your open bet back. The offer is calculated from the current probabilities — minus the margin, again. You paid margin placing the bet; you pay it a second time selling it back. That is why a "fair" cash out never quite matches what the live odds imply. It is a convenience product, priced like one.

When Cashing Out Actually Makes Sense

Rarely on maths, sometimes on life: when the stake-at-risk genuinely matters to you (a big acca one leg from landing), when real new information has emerged that markets have not priced (a red card you are watching live), or when settling removes a temptation to chase. What it should never be: a reflex. Serial cash-outers pay double margin on every bet and wonder where the bankroll went.

Partial Cash Out and Edit-My-Bet

Partial cash out sells a slice and lets the rest ride — same pricing, smaller dose, occasionally a sensible compromise. Edit/add-a-leg features reprice the whole bet at current margins each time you touch them; every edit is a new toll booth. The pattern is consistent: flexibility features are margin dressed as control.

Acca Insurance: Read the Payout Currency

"Money back if one leg lets you down" almost always means back as a free bet, not cash — and free bet maths (stakes not returned) makes that worth roughly 70–75% of face value. Caps apply (€10–€25 typically), minimum legs and odds apply, and the offer exists because accas are already the house's best product. Take it when you were placing the acca anyway; never add legs to qualify.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is cash out ever good value?

It costs a second helping of margin — worth it only for genuine risk management or live information the market has not priced.

Why is my cash out offer lower than the odds suggest?

It is built from live probabilities minus margin — you pay margin both placing and selling.

Does acca insurance pay cash?

Usually a free bet (capped ~€10–€25), worth roughly 70–75% of face value.

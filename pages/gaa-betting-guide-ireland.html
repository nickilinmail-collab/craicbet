GAA Betting Guide Ireland 2026: Football & Hurling Markets Explained

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 Updated November 2026⏱ 7 min read

The GAA championships — both Gaelic Football and Hurling — are among the most bet-on sports in Ireland, with the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship and the All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship attracting peak betting interest from January through to the September final at Croke Park. This guide explains the key betting markets for both sports, how GAA odds work, and where to bet with a GRAI-licensed bookmaker.

All-Ireland Football Championship: Betting Basics

The All-Ireland Senior Football Championship runs from early in the year through to the All-Ireland final, typically in late July or August. The competition is dominated by a small number of counties — Kerry, Dublin, Galway, Mayo, and Tyrone are historically the most prominent outright contenders, though the introduction of a league-style phase in the round-robin format has expanded the field of competitive teams.

The outright winner market opens in January and is typically led by Kerry and Dublin, with other Connacht and Ulster sides pricing in depending on their National League form. Early-season prices can be generous, as the draw for the provincial championships is not yet set and injury data is limited.

For individual match betting, the match winner market is straightforward. GAA matches cannot end in a draw at All-Ireland Championship level (replays or additional time are used), so the match winner is a two-way market. For lopsided provincial championship games between strong and weak counties, the handicap market is better value — a strong county might be priced at 1/8 on the match winner but -15 on the handicap, offering a genuinely competitive two-way bet.

All-Ireland Hurling Championship: Betting Overview

The All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship has become increasingly competitive at the top level. The Leinster and Munster championships provide the main route to the knockout stages, with the Joe McDonagh Cup serving the lower-ranked counties. Kilkenny, Limerick, Cork, Tipperary, and Galway are the perennial outright contenders, with Limerick having been particularly dominant in recent seasons.

The outright hurling winner market tends to be tighter than football because the sport is concentrated among fewer genuinely competitive counties. When a team is dominating the sport (as Limerick have done in recent years), their outright price compresses significantly — meaning the market offers limited value on the leading contender and potentially more value on the challengers.

Hurling matches at the top level can produce high-scoring encounters, making total points over/under markets interesting — particularly for Munster championship matches between traditional rivals. Lines for top-level hurling matches are typically set in the range of 44–56 combined points, though scores can vary significantly.

Key GAA Betting Markets Explained

Match winner: Straightforward two-way market (no draw in championship). Most popular market; least value for heavily one-sided fixtures.

Handicap (points spread): Most useful for mismatched games. In GAA, goals count as 3 points and are included in the handicap calculation (a score of 2-15 is 21 points; 1-12 is 15 points). Most bookmakers express the handicap in total points.

Total points over/under: Competitive at top-level hurling and football championship matches. Less useful for provincial early-round games where scoring variance is high.

Outright winner: Available for All-Ireland, provincial championships, and National Leagues. Provincial outright markets are less liquid but can carry value early in the season.

First scorer / anytime scorer: Available for major fixtures. Wide bookmaker margins make these entertainment bets rather than systematic value plays.

Frequently Asked Questions

What GAA betting markets are available in Ireland?

GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer match winner, handicap (points spread), total points over/under, half-time/full-time, and outright All-Ireland Football or Hurling Championship winner markets. First and anytime scorer markets are also widely available for high-profile fixtures.

How does handicap betting work in GAA?

GAA handicap betting works the same as other sports. If Kerry are -8 against Leitrim, you need Kerry to win by more than 8 points (goals converted to points: a goal = 3 points) for a handicap bet on Kerry to win.

Are GAA outright markets available year-round?

Yes. All-Ireland Football and Hurling Championship outright winner markets open in January each year and run through to the final in August. Provincial championship outright markets are also available from bookmakers.

Is GAA betting legal in Ireland?

Yes. Betting on GAA matches is entirely legal in Ireland for people aged 18 and over, provided you use a bookmaker holding a GRAI remote betting licence. The GRAI framework has been in place since 1 July 2026.

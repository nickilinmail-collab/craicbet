How to Watch Irish Horse Racing on TV

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 3 November 2026 ⏱ 7 min read

Irish racing is easy to bet on and — compared with most sports — surprisingly awkward to watch. Nearly all of it lives behind one paywall, a little is free-to-air, and the whole arrangement is under pressure from gambling-advertising rules. Here is the practical map as of November 2026.

Racing TV: home of all 26 Irish courses

Racing TV holds the broadcast rights to every one of Ireland's 26 racecourses, under a deal that runs to at least 2029. If you want day-in, day-out coverage of Irish racing — Leopardstown, the Curragh, Galway, Punchestown, Fairyhouse and the rest — this is the channel. In Ireland it launched at a reported €31 a month, and it is carried on Sky (channel 426), Virgin Media (411) and as an app on smart TVs and Apple TV, plus streaming on its own site. Racing TV also carries the bulk of premier British racing, including Cheltenham.

Sky Sports Racing: the other half of the picture

Sky Sports Racing carries a different portfolio: a share of British tracks plus a wide slate of international racing — France, the US, Hong Kong, Australia. It is included in Sky Sports subscriptions rather than sold separately. For a purely Irish-racing household it is optional; for an all-racing household the two channels are complementary rather than substitutes, which is exactly why the combined cost stings.

Free-to-air: the select days

RTÉ and Virgin Media show a limited number of terrestrial days each year — the biggest festivals and feature days, such as the Dublin Racing Festival, the Irish Grand National and days at Galway and the Curragh. These are the exceptions, not the rule: the everyday fare stays behind the paywall. Bookmaker streaming is the other free-ish route — many sites stream races to logged-in customers, typically requiring a small bet on the race, which is a marketing device and should be treated as one.

The watershed cloud over all of it

The Gambling Regulation Act brings in an advertising watershed banning gambling ads on television between 5.30am and 9pm. Racing channels are saturated with bookmaker advertising — it is a large slice of their revenue — and Racing TV's owners have publicly warned that the rules as drafted could make their Irish service economically unviable, with the cost of regionalising an ad-free Irish feed reported at around €2 million. Nothing has gone dark, and talks between the industry and government have been running for some time, but Irish viewers should know the current arrangement is not guaranteed to survive the watershed unchanged.

What a practical punter should actually do

If you bet mainly on Irish racing, Racing TV is effectively the only full-coverage option — price it into your racing budget honestly, the same way you would track your stakes. If you only care about the big days, free-to-air coverage plus bookmaker streams covers a surprising amount of the calendar for nothing. And if a subscription only earns its keep by prompting you to bet more than you otherwise would, that is a reason to cancel it, not keep it. Watching should serve the punting, not drive it.

Each-Way Double Betting: How It Works and When to Use It

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated 4 November 2026⏱ 6 min read

What Is an Each-Way Double?

An each-way double is a bet on two selections, each backed each-way, combined into a double. It costs double a standard double stake because each-way betting is two bets (win and place). An each-way double on two selections at €5 each-way costs €20 total: €5 on the win double, €5 on the place double, repeated for both selections.

For the win portion to pay out, both selections must win. For the place portion, both must be placed (finish in a specified top number). Partial returns are possible if one selection wins and the other places, or both place but neither wins.

How Returns Are Calculated

Calculate an each-way double in two parts. Win double: Stake × Win Odds 1 × Win Odds 2. Place double: Stake × Place Odds 1 × Place Odds 2. Place odds are typically the win odds at 1/4 or 1/5 (depending on the race type and terms). For example: two selections at 5/1 (6.0 decimal) with 1/4 place terms: Win double: €5 × 6.0 × 6.0 = €180. Place double (place odds = 2.25 each): €5 × 2.25 × 2.25 = €25.31. If both win, total return = €205.31. If both place but neither wins: €25.31.

When Each-Way Doubles Make Sense

Each-way doubles offer the most value when both selections are at high enough odds for the place portion to generate meaningful returns. At very short prices, the place portion adds little value. For horses at 5/1 or higher in fields with 8+ runners (where 1/4 odds places terms apply), the each-way double’s place component provides useful insurance against one or both selections narrowly missing the win.

Each-Way Double vs Two Separate Each-Way Bets

Betting each-way separately on two horses gives guaranteed place return if either places, regardless of the other. An each-way double requires both to place for the place component to pay. The double provides higher potential return if both win, but no return at all if either loses without placing. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does an each-way double cost?

An each-way double on two selections costs four times the unit stake: win double (1 unit) + place double (1 unit) = 2 units, multiplied by €X unit stake. A €5 each-way double costs €20.

What place terms apply to an each-way double?

Place terms are set by the bookmaker for each race. Common terms are 1/4 odds for the first 3 places in fields of 8+, or 1/5 odds for the first 4 places in certain race types. Each-way double place terms follow the individual race rules.

Can I do an each-way double on football?

Each-way is not standard on football match markets. Each-way doubles are mainly used in horse racing and golf where each-way terms are standard.

An each-way double is two separate each-way bets combined: win part + place part from selection A combined with win + place from selection B. At €5 each-way (€10 total stake), the win double and place double run independently. Example: Horse A wins at 5/1 (place 1/4 odds for top 3), Horse B places at 8/1 (place 1/4 odds):

When Each-Way Doubles Make Sense

  • Festival racing: When two horses on the same day have strong place claims, an EW double can deliver a significant return even if neither wins outright.
  • Large fields: More places paid means higher probability the place double lands.
  • Price matters: EW doubles work best when at least one selection is 5/1 or bigger — small-priced EW selections rarely justify the combined stake.
  • Avoid at short prices: Two 2/1 selections in an EW double means the win double returns roughly 9/1 on a small base, rarely worth the complexity.

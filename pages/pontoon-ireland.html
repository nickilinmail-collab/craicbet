Pontoon Ireland 2026: Rules, Strategy & Where to Play

Updated: November 2026 • Reading time: 8 min
Quick Summary: Pontoon is a British blackjack variant with two unique winning hands — the Pontoon (Ace + 10-value card, pays 2:1) and the Five-Card Trick (any five cards without busting, also pays 2:1). The house edge with optimal play is around 0.38–0.5%, competitive with standard blackjack.

Pontoon Rules

Pontoon shares the same objective as blackjack — beat the dealer's hand without exceeding 21 — but with several important differences that significantly affect strategy:

  • Both dealer cards are dealt face down. You never see any of the dealer's cards until the end of the round. This is the most significant rule change — it removes the information you normally use to make strategy decisions.
  • Pontoon pays 2:1 (an Ace plus any ten-value card). Regular blackjack usually pays 3:2.
  • Five-Card Trick pays 2:1 — any five cards without busting ranks as the second-best hand, above a 21 made with fewer cards.
  • Dealer wins all ties. There is no push in Pontoon; a dealer with the same total as you wins.
  • You must Twist (hit) on soft 17 or less.
  • Instead of Hit/Stand/Double, Pontoon uses: Twist (hit), Stick (stand) and Buy (double). You may Buy more than once (up to your original stake per card).
  • You cannot Stick on fewer than 15 unless you have five cards.

Pontoon Strategy

The lack of dealer information means Pontoon strategy is simpler (and more defensive) than blackjack strategy:

  • Always Buy (double) with hard 9, 10 or 11.
  • Always Twist (hit) with hard 8 or less.
  • Stick on hard 15, 16, 17 with two or three cards unless building toward a Five-Card Trick.
  • Keep Twisting with four low cards (total below 11) to pursue a Five-Card Trick.
  • Stick on 15 with four cards, as the risk of busting outweighs the Five-Card Trick benefit.
  • Never Stick on 14 or less (the rules usually won't allow it anyway, but just in case a variant does).

The Five-Card Trick opportunity means you should not rush to Stick with low totals early in the hand. If you have 6 + 2 + 3 = 11 on three cards, Buying (doubling) or Twisting to reach a fourth card is correct — you're chasing a valuable bonus hand.

Pontoon vs. Other Blackjack Variants

Pontoon should not be confused with Spanish 21, which removes all ten-value cards (not Jacks, Queens, Kings) from the deck while adding bonus payouts. Spanish 21 and Pontoon have different optimal strategies. Pontoon is also distinct from Australian Pontoon, which uses similar terminology but slightly different rules around doubling and splitting.

For Irish players new to blackjack variants, it is worth mastering basic blackjack strategy first before moving to Pontoon. The rule differences — especially dealer wins ties — can be costly if you bring blackjack habits to the Pontoon table.

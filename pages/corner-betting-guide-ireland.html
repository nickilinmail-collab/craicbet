Corner Betting Ireland: Total Corners, First Corner & More

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Corner betting covers a range of markets based on the number and timing of corner kicks during a football match. It is a growing market in Ireland, partly because corners correlate differently from goals — a team that dominates possession and attacks frequently earns corners regardless of whether the ball goes in. This creates analytical opportunities distinct from pure match result or goals markets.

Total corners over/under

The most common corner market is over/under 9.5, 10.5 or 11.5 total corners across both teams for 90 minutes. Corner rates vary by team style: pressing, possession-dominant teams tend to generate more corners, as do attacking sides that cross frequently. Average corners per match in the Premier League sit around 10–11. As a result, over 9.5 is roughly a 50/50 market in an average Premier League fixture, while over 10.5 and over 11.5 progressively favour under. Using team-by-team corner statistics (corners for and against over the season) gives a more precise baseline than the league average alone.

Asian corner handicap markets

Some bookmakers offer Asian corner handicap markets, where one team is given a corner advantage or deficit for betting purposes. For example, home −3.5 corners means the home team must win the corners count by four or more for the bet to win. These markets allow punters to express a view on which team will win the territorial battle without predicting the exact total. As with other Asian handicap formats, quarter-ball increments are available on some platforms, allowing a half-win or half-refund on key lines.

First corner markets

The first corner market asks which team will win the first corner of the match. Teams with strong early possession and attacking intent tend to earn corners faster. Historically, home teams earn the first corner slightly more often than away teams, but the variance is very high — first corner is essentially a coin flip with a small home bias in most fixtures. It is a low-margin entertainment market rather than a systematic value opportunity.

Corners in combination markets

Corners markets are sometimes combined with match results or goals markets in multi-bet tickets. The key factor is correlation: matches that produce many corners tend to be high-intensity, attack-heavy games that also produce more goals. Frequently Asked Questions

How many corners are typical in a Premier League match?

The Premier League averages approximately 10-11 corners per match across a full season. This makes over 9.5 corners a close to even-money market in an average fixture. High-tempo attacking matches can reach 14-18 corners; defensive or low-possession matches may produce as few as 5-7.

Does a corner awarded but not taken count?

No. Corner betting markets settle on corners actually taken, as recorded by the official match data provider. A corner awarded but waved off or never taken (for example, due to a foul or the match ending) does not count. Always check your bookmaker's specific settlement rules.

What is Asian corner handicap betting?

Asian corner handicap applies a handicap to the number of corners won by each team. For example, home team -3.5 means the home team must win the corners count by four or more for the bet to win. Quarter-ball increments (e.g., -3.75) allow partial refunds when the handicap is exactly matched.

Can you bet on corners in-play?

Yes. Corner markets are widely available in-play, often at faster settlement than goals markets. Watching the first 15-20 minutes of a match to gauge territorial domination before backing an over/under corner total is a common approach used by punters who prefer in-play betting.

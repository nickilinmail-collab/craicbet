Ante-Post vs Day-of-Race Betting Ireland 2026: Price and Risk Compared

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Timing your bet in horse racing — ante-post weeks before the race or on the day itself — has a material impact on the price you get, the risk you face and the value available. This guide explains the trade-offs for Irish racing punters.

Price Movement: When Early Prices Are Worth Taking

Horses are priced longer (bigger odds) ante-post when market uncertainty is high. As race day approaches, the market efficiently prices known information: fitness reports, entries, declarations and overnight betting moves the price shorter. If you have a view on a horse that is not yet reflected in the market, ante-post prices offer value unavailable on race day. Example: a confirmed Cheltenham Festival runner at 20/1 ante-post may shorten to 12/1 by declarations week.

Non-Runner Risk: The Ante-Post Catch

Ante-post bets lose if the selection is not declared to run. Without non-runner money back (NRNB) terms, your stake is forfeited if the horse misses the race. At the Cheltenham Festival, 20–30% of ante-post favourites do not make the final declaration. NRNB terms (offered by most major GRAI-licensed bookmakers on feature races) eliminate this risk for declared non-runners, but may not cover horses that are simply “not entered.”

Best Odds Guaranteed: Protecting Ante-Post Punters

BOG means that if the SP on race day exceeds your ante-post price, you are paid at the SP. This eliminates one of the two risks of ante-post betting (the other being the non-runner risk). With BOG and NRNB together, ante-post betting on feature races at GRAI-licensed operators becomes a one-way proposition: you benefit from an early price if the SP is shorter, and from the SP if it is longer. The only remaining risk is genuine non-runner status under terms not covered by NRNB.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is ante-post betting?

Ante-post betting means placing a bet on a race or event well before the day of the event — sometimes months in advance. It typically offers longer (more generous) odds but carries non-runner risk.

What is non-runner money back?

If your horse is declared a non-runner, non-runner money back (NRNB) means your stake is refunded rather than lost. Most GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer NRNB on feature races. Check terms carefully as coverage varies.

Is BOG available on ante-post bets?

Yes. Most GRAI-licensed operators extend Best Odds Guaranteed to ante-post bets on Irish and UK racing. If the SP on race day exceeds your ante-post price, you are paid at the SP.

When is it better to bet day-of-race?

Day-of-race betting is better when: you need the full field declared, you want to avoid non-runner risk, or the market has not yet fully priced publicly available information. It is safer but typically offers shorter prices.

How far in advance can I bet ante-post?

Major GRAI-licensed operators open ante-post markets for the Cheltenham Festival and Punchestown up to a year in advance. Standard ante-post markets open 2–8 weeks before major races.

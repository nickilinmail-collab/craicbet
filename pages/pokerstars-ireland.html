PokerStars Ireland 2026: Betting Licence Status & What Irish Players Need to Know

PokerStars is the world’s largest online poker room, owned by Flutter Entertainment — the Irish-headquartered gambling group. But Irish players searching for PokerStars betting or PokerStars Sports often hit a confusing picture: Flutter holds GRAI licences in Ireland, but not under the PokerStars brand. As of November 2026, PokerStars does not appear separately on the GRAI’s public register. Here is what that means for Irish players.

What Is PokerStars?

PokerStars is the world’s largest online poker room by player traffic, operating globally and known for its depth of cash game and tournament offerings. It is owned by Flutter Entertainment, the Irish-headquartered betting and gaming group listed on the New York Stock Exchange and the London Stock Exchange. Flutter is one of the largest gambling companies in the world by revenue.

PokerStars holds licences from the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) and the UK Gambling Commission (UKGC) for its poker products — both reputable regulators. In addition, PokerStars Sports, the brand’s sportsbook arm, is available in certain markets alongside the core poker offering. That said, holding an MGA or UKGC licence does not automatically authorise an operator to target Irish customers under Ireland’s new regulatory framework. Since 1 July 2026, that requires a separate GRAI remote betting licence.

The connection to Flutter creates a natural point of confusion for Irish players: Flutter is Ireland-headquartered and holds GRAI licences here, so it is reasonable to wonder whether that coverage extends to the PokerStars brand. The short answer is that GRAI licences are issued per brand, not per parent company — and PokerStars does not appear on the GRAI register as a separately licensed brand.

Does PokerStars Have a GRAI Licence in Ireland?

Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024 established the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) as the country’s dedicated gambling regulator. The GRAI began issuing remote betting licences from 1 July 2026. Any operator wishing to legally target Irish customers with betting products must hold one of these licences.

As of this writing in November 2026, PokerStars does not appear on the GRAI’s public register of licensed remote betting operators as a separately named brand. Flutter Entertainment holds GRAI remote betting licences in Ireland under other brand names within its portfolio, but those licences do not cover the PokerStars brand. PokerStars is not listed separately on the GRAI register.

Being absent from the GRAI register does not make PokerStars an illegitimate operator in general terms — it holds valid MGA and UKGC licences and is a legitimately operating platform in those licensed markets. However, it does mean that PokerStars is not currently authorised to operate under Ireland’s specific regulatory framework as a separately named brand.

PokerStars Poker vs PokerStars Sports in Ireland

It is worth distinguishing between the two main products under the PokerStars brand, as the picture for Irish players differs between them.

PokerStars Poker may be accessible to Irish users via its MGA licence, depending on whether PokerStars has implemented geographic restrictions for the Irish market. Many MGA-licensed poker operators have continued to accept players from jurisdictions that have new but not yet fully enforced licensing regimes. However, as with all non-GRAI operators, Irish players using PokerStars Poker would not receive GRAI-mandated protections.

PokerStars Sports, the sportsbook arm, exists in a number of markets. However, without a GRAI remote betting licence specifically covering the PokerStars brand in Ireland, the status of PokerStars Sports for Irish players is unclear. Under Ireland’s Gambling Regulation Act 2024, sports betting is explicitly covered by the GRAI licensing requirement. An operator offering sports betting to Irish customers without a GRAI licence is not operating under Irish regulatory authorisation for that product.

Irish players considering either product should be clear on the distinction: accessible via another jurisdiction’s licence is not the same as being GRAI-licensed for Ireland.

Flutter Entertainment’s Irish Presence

Flutter Entertainment is headquartered in Dublin and is one of Ireland’s most prominent listed companies. Its Irish roots run deep — it was formed from the merger of Paddy Power and Betfair in 2016, and has since grown into a global group through further acquisitions including PokerStars’ parent company The Stars Group in 2020.

Flutter holds multiple GRAI remote betting licences in Ireland under various brands within its portfolio. These GRAI licences are brand-specific: each licensed brand has its own licence number and regulatory obligations. The PokerStars brand does not appear to hold its own separate GRAI licence at this time, even though the parent company’s other brands do.

This is an important structural point: a parent company holding GRAI licences for some brands does not automatically extend that regulatory coverage to all brands under its umbrella. GRAI licensing is brand-level, not group-level. Irish players should not assume that Flutter’s overall Irish presence means every Flutter brand is GRAI-licensed in Ireland.

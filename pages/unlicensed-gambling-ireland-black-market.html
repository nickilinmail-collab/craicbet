Unlicensed Gambling in Ireland — The Black Market Problem (2026)

Updated 5 November 2026 · CraicBet Editorial

⚠️ Important: Irish players using unlicensed gambling sites have no consumer protection — no complaints process, no mandatory responsible gambling tools, and no recourse if a site refuses to pay out.
By the numbers: ~70% of European online gambling activity is estimated to be on unlicensed platforms — roughly €80 billion per year across approximately 6,000 unregulated operators.

What Is Black Market Gambling?

Black market gambling refers to online betting and casino activity on platforms that do not hold a valid local operating licence. In Ireland's context, this means sites operating without a GRAI remote betting licence — which became mandatory on 1 July 2026 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

The term "black market" is slightly misleading: it's not necessarily illegal for Irish players to use these sites. What is illegal is for operators to provide gambling services to Irish residents without a licence. The distinction matters because players often don't realise they're using an unlicensed platform.

Why It's Dangerous for Players

Beyond missing consumer protections, specific risks include:

  • Account closure without notice — offshore sites frequently close accounts of winning players with no regulatory recourse
  • Withdrawal delays of months — no maximum processing time mandated
  • No self-exclusion linkage — Ireland's national self-exclusion register (launching 2027) won't cover unlicensed sites
  • Data breaches with no GDPR recourse — operators outside the EU may not comply with data requests
  • Payment fraud — some lower-tier sites have reportedly processed payments twice or refused chargebacks

What GRAI Is Doing

GRAI has described tackling the black market as a strategic priority. Tools available include:

  • High Court blocking orders — requiring ISPs to block unlicensed domains
  • Payment blocking — working with Irish banks and card processors to restrict transactions
  • App store removals — requesting Google/Apple remove unlicensed gambling apps
  • Fines up to €20 million — per violation for operating without a licence
  • Cooperation with EU regulators — information sharing under the European Gaming and Betting Association frameworks

Over 100 operators were reportedly under GRAI investigation as of October 2026, though the authority has not publicly named targets.

The Budget Problem

GRAI requested €26 million for its 2026 budget but received only €13.35 million — just over half. This funding gap limits enforcement capacity precisely when the regulator is trying to establish authority over both licensed and unlicensed markets. Critics have argued this will slow black market enforcement significantly.

Each-Way Betting Explained for Irish Punters 2026

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 5 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

Each-way betting is one of the most distinctively Irish aspects of sports betting, rooted in the horseracing tradition but now available across golf, football and other sports. An each-way bet is two separate bets in one: a win bet on your selection and a place bet at a fraction of the win odds. The total stake is doubled — a €5 each-way bet costs €10 (€5 on the win, €5 on the place).

How each-way terms work

Place terms define how many positions qualify for the place component and at what fraction of the win odds. Standard horseracing place terms in Ireland are: 1/4 odds for the first three places in fields of 8–15 runners, 1/5 odds for the first four places in fields of 16 or more runners, and 1/4 odds for the first two places in smaller fields. For a €5 each-way bet on a 10/1 shot (11.0 decimal), a win returns €55 (€5 at 11.0) plus the original €5 stake. The place return at 1/4 odds is €5 at 3.5 (2.5 + 1 = decimal 3.5), which returns €17.50. Total return for a winning each-way bet: €72.50.

Each-way in golf betting

Golf each-way betting in Ireland is widely available and places bets on tournaments are typically offered at 1/5 odds for the top 5 or top 8 finishers, depending on the event. Major tournaments such as The Open Championship and The Masters are popular each-way markets. With large fields (156 players in many events), backing a 50/1 shot each-way at 1/5 for the top 8 means a small return even if the selection finishes 7th or 8th — enough to show a profit on the place component relative to the total stake.

Each-way in football outright betting

Bookmakers offer each-way terms on football outright markets such as league winners, top-four finishes and relegation. Place terms vary significantly: a Champions League winner market in a 32-team group stage might offer 1/4 odds for the top four finishers. Checking the specific each-way terms before placing is essential, as terms vary by bookmaker and event size. Each-way betting on football outright markets can offer exposure to longer-priced sides with legitimate top-four or top-half prospects without the full risk of a straight win bet.

Key considerations and mistakes to avoid

The most common each-way mistake is placing an each-way bet on a short-priced favourite. At odds of 2.0, the place component at 1/4 returns only 1.25 (1.0 × 0.25 + 1 = 1.25), giving a near-certain small loss on the place portion even if the selection places. Frequently Asked Questions

What does each-way mean in horse racing?

An each-way bet is two equal bets: one on your horse to win and one on it to place (finish in a set number of positions, typically top 2-4 depending on field size). The place bet is settled at a fraction of the win odds, usually 1/4 or 1/5. If your horse wins, both bets pay. If it places but does not win, only the place bet pays.

How many places does each-way cover in a horse race?

Place terms depend on field size. Standard terms in Ireland: 2 places for fields under 8 runners, 3 places for 8-15 runners (1/4 odds), 4 places for 16 or more runners (1/5 odds). Bookmakers may offer enhanced terms (extra places or better fractions) in promotions. Always check the specific terms before betting.

Can you bet each-way on football?

Yes. Each-way betting is available on football outright markets such as league winner, top four, relegated team and player awards. Place terms vary by market and bookmaker. Check the terms carefully before placing, as they differ significantly between events.

Is each-way betting worth it on short-priced favourites?

Generally not. At short odds (below 3.0), the place component at 1/4 or 1/5 offers very little return relative to the extra stake. Each-way betting offers most value on medium to long-priced selections where the place return represents a meaningful profit even without a win.

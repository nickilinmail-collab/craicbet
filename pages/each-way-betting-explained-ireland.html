Each-Way Betting Explained — How It Works and When to Use It

Each-way betting is one of the most used bet types in Irish horse racing — from a midweek card at Leopardstown to the big Cheltenham festivals. The concept is simple, but the place terms and payout calculations catch many punters out. This guide makes it clear.

What Is an Each-Way Bet?

An each-way (E/W) bet is actually two bets of equal size placed simultaneously:

  1. The win bet — your selection must finish first
  2. The place bet — your selection must finish in the placed positions (usually top 3 or 4, depending on the race and bookmaker)

Because it is two bets, an each-way bet costs double the stake. A "€10 each-way" costs €20 — €10 on the win, €10 on the place.

Place Terms Explained

The place part of the bet pays a fraction of the win odds. This fraction — the place terms — varies by race type and bookmaker:

How to Calculate an Each-Way Payout

Once you know the win odds and the place terms, the calculation is:

  • Win part: stake × (win odds + 1) [in decimal] — only if selection wins
  • Place part: stake × (place fraction of win odds + 1) [in decimal] — if selection places or wins
Worked example — €10 each-way at 10/1, 1/4 place odds, top 3:

Total cost: €20 (€10 win + €10 place)

If the horse WINS:
Win part: €10 × (10 + 1) = €110
Place part: €10 × (10 ÷ 4 + 1) = €10 × 3.5 = €35
Total return: €145 (profit: €125)

If the horse PLACES (2nd or 3rd) but doesn't win:
Win part: €0 (lost)
Place part: €10 × 3.5 = €35
Total return: €35 (profit: €15 after the €20 total cost)

If the horse is UNPLACED:
Total return: €0 (both bets lost, full €20 gone)

When Each-Way Betting Makes Sense

Each-way is most valuable when:

  • The selection is priced at 8/1 or longer — the place part contributes meaningful return
  • The race has a large field (16+ runners) where finishing in the top 4 is a realistic outcome even if winning is unlikely
  • You're betting on Cheltenham, Irish National, or other big handicaps where field sizes and place terms are favourable
  • You want some return if your selection runs well but doesn't win

When Each-Way Betting Doesn't Make Sense

At short prices (say, 5/4 or 2/1), the place part pays very little. A €10 E/W at 2/1 (place 1/4): place return = €10 × (2÷4 + 1) = €15 — that's just €5 profit on the €10 place bet, while the win bet is at the more attractive odds. The €10 place stake would have been better used as a larger win bet. At evens or shorter, each-way betting is almost never worthwhile.

Cheltenham context: The Cheltenham Festival in March is the biggest event of the Irish racing calendar. Many Irish punters use each-way bets on longer-priced Irish-trained horses, especially in the big handicaps (County Hurdle, Coral Cup) where fields of 25+ runners mean finishing in the top 4 is achievable. For short-priced festival favourites, the win bet alone is usually better value.

Each-Way: Worked Example and When It Makes Sense

A €10 each-way bet costs €20 total (€10 win + €10 place). If your 10/1 horse finishes second in a 12-runner handicap: you lose the €10 win part, but the place pays 1/4 of 10/1 = 2.5/1. Your €10 place stake returns €10 + €25 = €35, for a €15 profit on a €20 outlay.

Each-way is best when: (1) backing outsiders of 7/1 or longer where place returns are meaningful; (2) in big-field handicaps where the favourite is vulnerable; (3) in golf majors where finishing top-5 from a 156-player field is realistic for mid-price selections. At odds of 4/1 or less, an each-way bet's place component returns less than your stake if you don't win — a straight win bet usually offers better value at short prices.

What does each-way mean in betting?

An each-way (E/W) bet is two equal bets — one on the selection to win, one on it to finish in the placed positions (usually top 3 or 4). You need twice the stake. The place part pays a fraction of the win odds (commonly 1/4 or 1/5) plus your stake back.

How much does an each-way bet pay if my horse is placed but doesn't win?

You receive the place portion of the bet: your stake × (place fraction of win odds + 1). At 10/1 with 1/4 odds for a place, a €10 E/W pays €35 for a place (€25 profit on the place bet; the win bet of €10 is lost, leaving a net profit of €15).

Is each-way betting worth it?

It depends on the price. As a general guide, each-way bets offer better value at prices of 8/1 or longer in competitive fields. At short prices, the win bet alone usually offers better expected value because the place part contributes little return relative to cost.

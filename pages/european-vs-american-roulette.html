European vs American Roulette — Which Should Irish Players Choose?

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 October 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

Walk into any online casino and you'll find both European and American roulette side by side. They look almost identical, but one small difference — a single extra pocket — nearly doubles the house edge. Here's what every Irish player needs to know before placing a bet.

🎯 Quick Verdict

European roulette is always the better choice. Its house edge of 2.7% is nearly half that of American roulette (5.26%). With the La Partage rule, it drops to just 1.35% on even-money bets. There is no scenario where American roulette gives you better odds.

The Key Difference: Single Zero vs Double Zero

European roulette has 37 pockets — numbers 1 to 36 plus a single green zero (0). American roulette has 38 pockets — the same 36 numbers plus a single zero (0) and a double zero (00).

That extra pocket matters enormously. Every bet on the table pays out as though those green zeros don't exist. On a straight-up number bet, you're paid 35:1 — but there are 37 (or 38) possible outcomes. The zeros are where the casino makes its money.

With one zero, the casino keeps 1 out of every 37 spins on average. With two zeros, it keeps 2 out of every 38. That's the entire mathematical foundation of why European roulette is superior.

House Edge Breakdown

The house edge tells you the percentage of every bet the casino expects to keep over time. Here's how the two versions compare:

  • European Roulette: 2.70% house edge (1/37 = 0.027)
  • American Roulette: 5.26% house edge (2/38 = 0.0526)

In practical terms, for every €100 you wager on European roulette, you'll lose €2.70 on average. On American roulette, you'll lose €5.26. Over a session of hundreds of bets, that difference adds up fast.

The La Partage Rule — European Roulette's Secret Weapon

Some European roulette tables offer the La Partage rule. When the ball lands on zero and you've placed an even-money bet (red/black, odd/even, high/low), you get half your stake back instead of losing it all.

This reduces the house edge on even-money bets from 2.70% down to just 1.35% — making it one of the best bets in any casino, right alongside blackjack with basic strategy.

A similar rule called En Prison locks your bet in place for the next spin instead of returning half. The mathematical effect is the same: 1.35% house edge.

American roulette never offers La Partage or En Prison. It's another reason European roulette wins every comparison.

Don't Be Fooled — Payouts Are Identical

Both versions pay exactly the same for every bet type. A straight-up number pays 35:1, a split pays 17:1, a column pays 2:1, and even-money bets pay 1:1. The payouts don't change — but your odds of winning do.

On European roulette, your chance of hitting a single number is 1 in 37 (2.70%). On American, it's 1 in 38 (2.63%). You're paid the same, but you win less often. That's the entire scam of the double zero.

The Worst Bet in Roulette

American roulette has one unique bet: the five-number bet (also called the "basket" or "top line"), covering 0, 00, 1, 2, and 3. It pays 6:1 and carries a staggering 7.89% house edge — the worst odds of any standard roulette bet. Never place it.

What About French Roulette?

French roulette is essentially European roulette with the La Partage or En Prison rule built in. The wheel is identical (single zero, 37 pockets), but the table layout uses French terminology: Manque (low), Passe (high), Pair (even), Impair (odd).

The key benefit is the 1.35% house edge on even-money bets — half the standard European edge and a quarter of American roulette's. If you can find a French roulette table, it's the absolute best version to play.

Many online casinos label these as "French Roulette" or "European Roulette with La Partage". In live casino lobbies, look for tables from Evolution or Pragmatic Play that specifically mention La Partage in the table rules.

Popular Roulette Variants Available Online

Beyond the standard versions, you'll find several roulette variants at online casinos. Here's how they compare:

  • European Roulette — Single zero. 2.70% house edge. The standard choice.
  • French Roulette — Single zero with La Partage. 1.35% on even bets. The best option.
  • American Roulette — Double zero. 5.26% house edge. Avoid this.
  • Lightning Roulette — European base with random multipliers (50x–500x). Fun, but higher volatility. House edge around 2.70%.
  • Immersive Roulette — European roulette with cinematic slow-motion camera replays. Same 2.70% edge.
  • Speed Roulette — European roulette with shorter betting windows. Same odds, faster pace.
  • Auto Roulette — No human dealer; automated wheel. European rules, 2.70% edge. Runs 24/7.

As long as the variant uses a single-zero wheel, the base house edge remains 2.70%. The extras (multipliers, cameras, speed) change the experience but not the fundamental mathematics.

Which Should You Play in Ireland?

The answer is simple: always choose European roulette. Every reputable online casino available to Irish players offers it, and many have French roulette (European with La Partage) for the lowest possible house edge.

There is genuinely no advantage to playing American roulette. The game exists primarily because it became the standard in Las Vegas. In Europe and Ireland, the single-zero version has always been the norm — and for good reason.

If you're playing live roulette, look for tables explicitly labelled "European" or "French" in the lobby. Most live casino providers from Evolution, Pragmatic Play Live, and Playtech default to European rules, but always confirm before joining a table.

📈 Did You Know?

The numbers on a roulette wheel add up to 666 — often called "the Devil's number." Whether European or American, the sum is the same. But only the American version takes a devilishly large bite out of your bankroll. Stick to the single-zero wheel and keep the odds in your favour.

✅ Smart Roulette Tips

  • Always select European or French roulette over American
  • Look for tables with the La Partage rule for a 1.35% house edge
  • Stick to even-money bets (red/black, odd/even) for the most consistent experience
  • Set a budget before playing and stick to it
  • Avoid the five-number bet on American roulette at all costs

Live Roulette vs RNG Roulette

Regardless of whether you play European or American, you'll also need to choose between live dealer and RNG (software-based) roulette. Here's what matters:

  • Live roulette uses a real wheel and real dealer, streamed in HD. You can watch the ball spin in real time. European live tables are widely available, and French roulette with La Partage is offered by Evolution and other major providers.
  • RNG roulette uses a random number generator. Results are instant, and you can play at your own pace. Great for practising with free demo mode before wagering real money.

The house edge is identical in both formats — 2.70% for European, 5.26% for American. The choice comes down to whether you prefer the atmosphere of a live table or the speed and convenience of software. For more details, see our live casino vs RNG comparison guide.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Even players who know European is better sometimes make these errors:

  • Assuming past results matter — Every spin is independent. If red has come up 10 times in a row, the next spin has the same odds as always. There's no "correction" coming.
  • Using betting systems as a strategy — The Martingale, Fibonacci, and other systems don't change the house edge. They change the pattern of wins and losses, but the expected loss over time remains the same.
  • Ignoring the table rules — Not all European roulette tables are equal. Check whether La Partage is offered. Check the minimum and maximum bets. Check whether the table is genuinely European (single zero) and not a relabelled variant.
  • Chasing losses — Roulette should be entertainment with a budget. Set a loss limit before you start and walk away when you reach it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is European roulette better than American roulette?▾
Yes, always. European roulette has a house edge of 2.70% compared to American roulette's 5.26%. The only difference is the extra double-zero pocket in American roulette, which nearly doubles the casino's advantage. Payouts are identical in both versions, so there's no benefit to choosing American roulette.
What is the La Partage rule in roulette?▾
La Partage is a rule found on some European and French roulette tables. When the ball lands on zero and you've placed an even-money bet (red/black, odd/even, high/low), you receive half your stake back. This reduces the house edge on those bets from 2.70% to just 1.35%, making it one of the best bets in any casino game.
Why does American roulette have two zeros?▾
When roulette crossed the Atlantic to America in the 19th century, casino operators added the double zero to increase their profit margin. The extra pocket raises the house edge from 2.70% to 5.26%. It became the standard in American casinos and has remained ever since, despite being objectively worse for players.
Can I find European roulette at online casinos in Ireland?▾
Yes, virtually every online casino available to Irish players offers European roulette. Many also offer French roulette with the La Partage rule. Both live dealer and RNG versions are widely available. European roulette is the standard in the Irish and European market.
What are the odds of winning on red in European vs American roulette?▾
In European roulette, the odds of winning a red bet are 18/37, or 48.65%. In American roulette, the odds are 18/38, or 47.37%. The payout is the same (1:1) in both cases, but you win slightly more often with European roulette. Over hundreds of bets, this difference becomes significant.

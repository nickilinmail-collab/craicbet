Overview of Irish Greyhound Racing

Ireland has a rich greyhound racing tradition, with major tracks including Shelbourne Park in Dublin, Curraheen Park in Cork, and Limerick Greyhound Stadium. Racing is governed by Réicsí Con Eireann (IGB), the Irish Greyhound Board. Races are graded from A1 (top open) down to S grades for unraced dogs. BAGS (Bookmaker Afternoon Greyhound Service) races in the UK are also widely available at Irish bookmakers.

Betting on greyhound racing falls under GRAI’s licensing framework. All GRAI-licensed bookmakers offering greyhound markets are subject to the same responsible gambling and player protection obligations as other sports.

Betting Markets for Greyhound Racing

Win: back one of the six or eight runners to win outright. Each-way: win plus a place (top 2 in a standard 6-runner race, top 3 in some larger fields). Forecast: predict the first two in correct order. Combination forecast: predict the first two in either order. Tricast: first three in correct order. Reversed tricast: first three in any order. Each of these markets has different margins built in; win-only betting typically has the most competitive odds.

Some bookmakers also offer match betting (which of two named dogs finishes first), score markets and specials for major events like the Irish Greyhound Derby.

How to Read Greyhound Form

Greyhound form is published as a series of results showing finishing position, trap number, distance, grade, track, time, and margin. Sectional times — split times through the race — help identify early pace dogs versus strong finishers. A dog whose sectional times are consistently fast in the early portions is likely a wide runner who does well from low traps on right-handed circuits.

Recent wins matter but so does consistency. A dog with five consecutive second-place finishes may be better value in terms of place betting than its win record suggests. Track form is also crucial: some dogs perform significantly better at their home track.

Trap Draw and Its Importance

In a standard six-runner race, trap one (inside) is favoured on many right-handed Irish tracks because dogs can avoid the first bend traffic. Trap six (outside) often suits wide-running dogs who can avoid early scrimmaging. However, trap advantage varies by track, distance and bend configuration. For the Irish Greyhound Derby and other major finals, trap draw is a significant betting factor and odds frequently move substantially when the draw is announced. Frequently Asked Questions

How many dogs run in an Irish greyhound race?

Standard Irish greyhound races have six runners. Some open races at major tracks have eight runners. The number of runners affects place terms on each-way bets.

What is a greyhound forecast bet?

A forecast bet requires you to predict the first two finishers in correct order. A combination forecast covers both possible orders of your chosen two dogs and costs twice the single forecast stake.

When is the Irish Greyhound Derby?

The Irish Greyhound Derby is held at Shelbourne Park in Dublin, typically across September. It is Ireland's most prestigious greyhound race and attracts significant betting interest.

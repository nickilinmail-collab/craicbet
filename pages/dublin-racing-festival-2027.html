What Is the Dublin Racing Festival?

The Dublin Racing Festival is a two-day National Hunt festival held at Leopardstown Racecourse each February. It brings together the highest-class staying and two-mile chasers in Ireland and Britain for a series of Grade 1 contests that set the tone for the rest of the jumps season.

Leopardstown is a right-handed, galloping track on Dublin's southside — a surface that tends to favour strong-staying, accurate jumpers. It runs relatively flat compared to the undulating gradients of Cheltenham, but the quality of opposition and the pace of Grade 1 races still provide an excellent fitness and form test.

The festival attracts Ireland's most powerful National Hunt yards. Willie Mullins, Gordon Elliott, and Henry de Bromhead are perennial leading trainers here, and their runners at this meeting are almost always pointed towards major Cheltenham targets. For punters, that trainer-driven narrative is one of the most useful angles to follow.

Key Grade 1 Races

The Dublin Racing Festival card includes several Grade 1 contests. Here is what each race tests and why it matters for Cheltenham:

RaceGradeTarget / Notes
Irish Gold Cup Grade 1 The staying chase highlight of the festival. The Cheltenham Gold Cup is the natural next target for the winner. Run over approximately 3m 1f, it tests stamina, jumping accuracy and class. Irish Gold Cup form has a strong correlation with Cheltenham Gold Cup results.
Dublin Chase Grade 1 The two-mile championship chase at the festival. Winners typically head for the Queen Mother Champion Chase at Cheltenham. The Dublin Chase is frequently used to settle market pecking orders between Ireland's leading two-mile chasers.
Flogas Novice Chase Grade 1 The novice chasing division's most prestigious early-season test. Often points directly to the RSA Insurance Novices' Chase (now the Brown Advisory) at Cheltenham. First-season chasers who win here with authority are worth noting at ante-post prices.
Leopardstown Chase Grade 2 / Listed A strong supporting staying chase that often serves as a trial for lesser-fancied Cheltenham Gold Cup contenders or horses pointing at the National Hunt Chase.

Beyond these headline events, the two-day card also includes Grade 1 novice hurdles and bumpers that frequently produce future Cheltenham Festival winners. Keep an eye on the full card once it is published — supporting races at this meeting carry weight.

Why This Festival Matters for Cheltenham Betting

The Dublin Racing Festival sits roughly four to six weeks before the Cheltenham Festival (typically held in mid-March). That timing is deliberate. Willie Mullins, in particular, has used Leopardstown in February as a final-piece-of-the-puzzle prep run for horses he is targeting at Cheltenham. When a horse wins here with authority — especially with something in reserve — it typically sees its Cheltenham price shorten significantly in the hours after the race.

There are several betting reasons why the Dublin Racing Festival deserves careful attention:

  • Ante-post value window: The period between the Dublin Racing Festival and the Cheltenham Festival is one of the best ante-post windows of the year. Prices have moved based on fresh form but runners are still declared more than a week out. If you have a view on a Cheltenham race, backing it early in this window often gives better value than waiting until declarations.
  • Market confidence signals: Watch how bookmakers react to each race result at Leopardstown. A horse that wins at a canter and whose price immediately contracts from, say, 6/1 to 3/1 for Cheltenham is a market telling you something specific.
  • Trainer intentions: Pre-race interviews in the week before Leopardstown are worth reading. When Willie Mullins says a horse "needs this run before Cheltenham," that is a trainer managing expectations; when he says a horse "has to win here to prove he belongs at the top level," the race takes on additional significance.
  • Rejections carry meaning too: When a leading trainer skips the Dublin Racing Festival and goes straight to Cheltenham, that in itself signals confidence. The absence of a horse from this meeting is sometimes as informative as a performance in it.

Betting Strategy for Leopardstown in February

The following principles apply to betting on the Dublin Racing Festival specifically. None of this constitutes guaranteed advice — no tip ever is.

Ground Conditions

February at Leopardstown almost always produces soft or heavy ground. The track drains reasonably well but the Irish winter means ground rarely comes up good or better at this time of year. Horses with proven form in testing conditions have a significant advantage. When assessing the form book, filter for performances on soft or heavy before drawing conclusions from an autumn run on good ground.

Class and Consistency Over Single Performances

At Grade 1 level, the margins between top horses are small. Rather than betting on a single previous performance, look for horses whose form is consistently good across multiple runs — horses that finish in the first two or three repeatedly at Grade 1 or Grade 2 level are more reliable Leopardstown pointers than a horse with one exceptional run followed by an ordinary one.

Each-Way Considerations

In small-field Grade 1 chases (often five to eight runners), each-way terms are typically one place only at standard each-way fractions. In those fields, place-only bets may offer better value. Larger fields — common in novice hurdles and bumpers — give the standard two or three places. Use our each-way calculator to work out the return before you place any bet.

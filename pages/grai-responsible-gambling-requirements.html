GRAI Responsible Gambling Requirements: What Licensed Operators Must Provide

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

When a betting operator holds a GRAI licence, it is not simply being allowed to take bets — it is accepting a comprehensive set of responsible gambling obligations. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 and GRAI licensing conditions set out exactly what tools and protections every licensed operator must offer Irish customers. This page explains those requirements in plain language.

Why These Rules Exist

Ireland has a long history of problem gambling that was poorly addressed under the old 1956 Gaming and Lotteries Act. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024, which established the GRAI, was designed to change that. For the first time, every online betting operator serving Irish customers must hold a GRAI remote betting licence — and to hold that licence, they must meet enforceable responsible gambling standards.

As of July 2026, approximately 40 operators hold GRAI remote betting licences. Each licence-holder is bound by the same minimum standards, regardless of size or nationality. The GRAI can audit compliance and issue substantial financial penalties for breaches.

Mandatory Deposit and Spending Limits

Every GRAI-licensed betting site must allow customers to set deposit limits — and must make this process straightforward, not buried in account settings. Under the GRAI licensing conditions:

  • Operators must offer daily, weekly and monthly deposit limit options.
  • Reductions in limits must take effect immediately.
  • Increases in limits must include a mandatory cooling-off period before they activate, so a customer who sets a limit in a moment of clarity cannot easily override it in a moment of impulsivity.
  • Operators cannot market to customers who have set a deposit limit in a way that encourages them to increase it.

These rules place Ireland in line with the UK (UKGC) approach and significantly ahead of unregulated jurisdictions where limits are entirely voluntary or absent.

Session Time Limits and Reality Checks

Operators must offer session time limits — a maximum duration the customer can set for a single betting session. When a time limit is approaching, the platform must display a clear alert. Beyond session limits, GRAI-licensed sites are required to provide "reality checks": pop-up notifications during a session that show the customer how long they have been betting and their net win/loss position for that session.

Reality checks are significant because they interrupt automatic, rapid betting behaviour with factual information. Research on problem gambling intervention consistently shows that interrupting the flow of play with neutral factual prompts reduces session length and spending. GRAI has incorporated this evidence into its licensing standards.

Self-Exclusion and the National Gambling Exclusion Register

Possibly the most significant responsible gambling innovation in the Gambling Regulation Act 2024 is the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER). The NGER means a customer can submit a single self-exclusion request that applies simultaneously to every GRAI-licensed operator — not just one site, but all of them.

Under previous arrangements (or on unlicensed offshore sites), self-exclusion was site-by-site and easily circumvented by signing up elsewhere. The NGER closes that gap for all licensed Irish operators.

Operators must:

  • Check the NGER before activating any new customer account.
  • Close existing accounts immediately when a customer is added to the NGER.
  • Refund any deposits made by an excluded customer that have not been wagered.
  • Not re-activate excluded accounts until the exclusion period has expired and the customer has completed a reactivation process.

See our dedicated page on how long GRAI self-exclusion lasts for details on exclusion periods.

Affordability and Interaction Checks

GRAI licensing conditions include requirements for operators to monitor for signs of problem gambling behaviour and to interact with at-risk customers. While the specific triggers are set by GRAI guidance rather than the primary legislation, licensed operators are expected to:

  • Identify patterns such as rapid increases in deposit amounts, chasing losses, or large numbers of consecutive bets.
  • Conduct affordability checks when spending patterns suggest financial stress.
  • Reach out proactively to customers showing early signs of problem gambling.
  • Restrict or close accounts where there is evidence of serious risk and the customer is unresponsive to interactions.

This is a more interventionist model than many offshore operators apply and reflects the GRAI's mandate to treat gambling harm as a public health issue, not just an individual choice.

Signposting to Support Services

Every GRAI-licensed operator must prominently display information about support services. This means the Gambling Awareness Trust helpline (freephone 1800 753 753) and gamblingcare.ie must appear on every page of a licensed site, not just buried in a footer. The GRAI has set standards for font size and prominence.

This requirement exists because research shows that many problem gamblers are not aware of free help services, and that visibility of these services meaningfully increases uptake.

What Operators Cannot Do

Responsible gambling obligations are not just about what operators must offer — they also include prohibited practices:

  • Operators cannot offer bonuses or promotions to self-excluded customers or customers who have set reduced deposit limits.
  • Operators cannot use manipulative design techniques (sometimes called "dark patterns") to discourage customers from setting limits.
  • Operators cannot make it harder to withdraw money than to deposit it without regulatory justification.
  • Operators cannot ignore signs of problem gambling and continue marketing to vulnerable customers.

Enforcement

The GRAI has real teeth. For breaches of responsible gambling conditions, it can issue fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover (whichever is greater), suspend or revoke licences, and publish enforcement actions. See our page on GRAI enforcement powers and penalties for the full picture.

These are not theoretical maximums — similar regulators (UKGC) have issued multi-million-pound fines against major operators for responsible gambling failures, and the GRAI has modelled its enforcement regime on that precedent.

Frequently Asked Questions

What responsible gambling tools must GRAI-licensed operators offer?

GRAI-licensed operators must provide deposit limits, session time limits, reality checks, self-exclusion linked to the National Gambling Exclusion Register, and clear signposting to the Gambling Awareness Trust helpline (1800 753 753).

Can I self-exclude from all GRAI-licensed sites at once?

Yes. The National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER), established under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, allows one self-exclusion request to apply across all GRAI-licensed operators simultaneously.

Are deposit limits mandatory for Irish betting sites?

Yes. Under GRAI rules, licensed operators must offer deposit limits and must not allow customers to increase those limits without a mandatory cooling-off period.

What happens if an operator ignores responsible gambling rules?

GRAI can issue fines of up to €20 million or 10% of annual turnover (whichever is greater), suspend licences, or revoke them entirely for serious or repeated breaches of responsible gambling obligations.

Where can I get help with a gambling problem in Ireland?

Contact the Gambling Awareness Trust on freephone 1800 753 753 or visit gamblingcare.ie. This is a free, confidential service.

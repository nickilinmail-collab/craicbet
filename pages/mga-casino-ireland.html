What Is an MGA Licence and Why Does It Matter for Irish Players?

The Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) is the regulatory body for remote gaming in Malta, an EU member state. It was established in 2001 and has become the world's most recognised gambling licence for international online casinos. When a casino holds an MGA licence, it has met rigorous requirements around financial stability, game fairness, player protection, and anti-money-laundering compliance.

For Irish players specifically, the MGA matters enormously right now because the GRAI has not issued a single casino licence. The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland opened for business in mid-2026, but only for remote betting licences. Casino and gaming licences are not expected until 2027 or 2028 at the earliest. That means every online casino legally serving Irish players is operating on an international licence — and the MGA is the strongest of those licences available.

An MGA licence is issued under EU law, which gives it a level of enforceability and credibility that licences from smaller jurisdictions (such as Curaçao or Isle of Man) cannot match. MGA-licensed casinos are subject to ongoing compliance monitoring, annual audits, and strict rules on how they handle player funds and complaints.

How to Verify an MGA Licence

The MGA provides a public licence checker at mga.org.mt. Any casino with a genuine MGA licence will have its details publicly listed there. Here is exactly what to do:

🔍 How to Check an MGA Licence in Under 60 Seconds

  1. Scroll to the bottom of the casino website — find the MGA logo or licence number in the footer
  2. MGA licence numbers follow the format MGA/B1/XXXX/YYYY or MGA/B2/XXXX/YYYY
  3. Go to mga.org.mt → Licensees → Licence Search
  4. Enter the operator name or licence number
  5. Confirm: status = Active, licence type covers your activity (B1 = casino/gaming; B2 = sports betting)
  6. Check the licence expiry date has not passed

⚠️ If a casino cannot show you an MGA licence number and it does not appear in the mga.org.mt search, treat it as unlicensed.

The MGA licence number in a casino's footer should be clickable — reputable operators link it directly to the MGA verification page. If the licence logo in the footer leads nowhere or links to a third-party site rather than mga.org.mt, that is a warning sign worth investigating before you deposit.

What MGA Protection Gives You vs Unlicensed Casinos

Choosing an MGA-licensed casino is not just a box-ticking exercise. The protections are real and materially different from what an unlicensed or weakly licensed casino offers.

Player Funds Are Kept Separate

MGA-licensed casinos must keep player funds in segregated bank accounts, separate from operational funds. This means that if the casino goes bust, your balance is not swept into the company's creditors. Some MGA operators go further and hold funds in trust or insurance schemes. The MGA publicly grades each operator's player fund protection level (Basic, Medium, or High) on its website.

Certified Random Number Generators

All casino games at MGA-licensed operators must use certified Random Number Generators (RNGs), independently audited by accredited testing labs such as eCOGRA, GLI, or BMM. This means the slot results, roulette spins, and card shuffles you see are genuinely random — not manipulated. Published Return to Player (RTP) percentages must reflect actual game behaviour.

A Formal Complaints Path

At an MGA-licensed casino, if a dispute with the operator cannot be resolved directly, you can escalate it to the MGA. The MGA has an online complaints portal and can compel operators to respond. At an unlicensed casino, you have no equivalent recourse — your only option is to pursue the operator through the courts of whatever jurisdiction they claim to be based in, which is often impractical.

Responsible Gambling Tools

MGA licensees must offer deposit limits, session time limits, self-exclusion, and reality checks. These must be accessible from your account settings, not hidden in obscure menus. Self-exclusion at an MGA casino is also fed into GAMSTOP (if the operator is UKGC-licensed) or the MGA's own player protection systems.

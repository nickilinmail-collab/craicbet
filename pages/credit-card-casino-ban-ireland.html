The Credit Card Gambling Ban in Ireland, Explained

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 31 October 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

The Short Version

Since February 2026, GRAI-licensed operators are prohibited from accepting credit cards or extending any form of credit for gambling. The rule is part of the player-protection provisions of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, mirroring the UK’s 2020 credit card ban. Debit cards, bank transfers and e-wallets remain available.

Who the Ban Applies To

The prohibition binds licensed operators. Since the GRAI currently licenses remote betting only, the hard legal ban applies to bookmakers. Offshore-licensed casinos serving Irish players are not yet inside the GRAI system — some still list credit cards. When Irish casino licensing opens, the ban will extend to every licensed casino automatically.

Why the Ban Exists

Gambling with borrowed money is the single strongest predictor of gambling harm. Research behind the UK ban found a large share of credit-card gamblers were classified as problem gamblers. Removing credit from the equation forces gambling back inside money you actually have — which is the first rule of safe play anyway.

A Practical Red Flag

Use the ban as a quick trust test: an operator that actively promotes credit card deposits to Irish customers in 2026 is either unlicensed or indifferent to Irish rules. Combine that check with our 2-minute licence check before you deposit anywhere.

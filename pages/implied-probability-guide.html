Implied Probability & Value: The Maths Behind the Buzzword

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

“Value” is the most used and least understood word in betting. It has a precise meaning, it is checkable with arithmetic, and — the uncomfortable part — it does not exist without your own probability estimate. This guide gives you the conversions both ways and an honest definition you can actually apply.

Odds to probability, probability to odds

From decimal odds to implied probability: p = 1 ÷ odds. Odds of 4.00 imply 25%. The reverse: fair odds = 1 ÷ p. If you believe something is a 40% chance, its fair price is 2.50. From fractions, convert to decimal first (numerator ÷ denominator + 1). These two lines are the whole toolkit.

Strip the margin before you compare

Quoted prices overstate every outcome’s probability because the market sums above 100%. To estimate the market’s true opinion, normalise: divide each implied probability by the market total. If a two-way market shows 1.90/1.90 (52.6% each, total 105.3%), the margin-free consensus is 52.6 ÷ 105.3 = 50% each — exactly as you’d expect. Comparing your estimate against raw implied probabilities, rather than normalised ones, stacks the test against you from the start.

The definition of a value bet

A bet has value when your probability × decimal odds > 1. Believe a team wins 45% of the time and the price is 2.50: 0.45 × 2.50 = 1.125, so each euro staked is worth €1.125 on average — positive expected value. At 2.10 the same opinion gives 0.945: a losing bet on average even though the team may well win on the night. Value is a property of the price relative to your opinion, never of the team.

Why “value” without a probability is a buzzword

Notice what the definition requires: a number of your own. “This is a value bet” is an empty claim unless the speaker can say what probability they assign and why it beats the normalised market figure. Tipsters who call everything value but never state a percentage are expressing enthusiasm, not analysis. The market’s number comes from enormous amounts of money and information; to claim value is to claim you know better. Sometimes you might — a local injury whisper, a competition you genuinely follow — but it should feel like a strong claim, because it is.

Variance: being right doesn’t look like winning

A genuine 10% edge still loses constantly: the 45% shot above fails more often than it lands. Expected value only shows itself over hundreds of bets, which is why written records — stake, price, your probability, result — are the only honest scoreboard. Without records, memory promotes the wins and the word “value” becomes a story you tell yourself.

The honest summary

Learn the two conversions, normalise the margin out, write your probability down before you look for a price, and bet only when the arithmetic clears 1. Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert odds into probability?

Implied probability = 1 ÷ decimal odds, so 4.00 implies 25% and 2.50 implies 40%. The reverse is fair odds = 1 ÷ probability. Convert fractional odds to decimal first (numerator ÷ denominator + 1).

What exactly is a value bet?

One where your probability × decimal odds > 1. If you make a team 45% and the price is 2.50, that’s 1.125 — positive expected value. The same opinion at 2.10 gives 0.945 — no value, even if the team wins that night.

Can I find value bets without estimating probabilities myself?

No. Value only exists relative to a probability estimate, and the market price already encodes the consensus. A tip calling something “value” without stating a percentage and a reason it beats the normalised market figure is enthusiasm, not analysis.

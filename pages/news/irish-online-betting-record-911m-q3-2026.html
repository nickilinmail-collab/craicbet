Irish Online Betting Hits Record €911m in Q3 2026 as the GRAI Era Takes Hold

Published: 5 November 2026
📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

Irish online betting turnover reached a record €911 million in the third quarter of 2026 — the first complete quarter in which the new Gambling Regulation Act 2024 framework was live. The figure underlines both the scale of Ireland's betting market and the scale of the regulatory task facing GRAI as it works through licensing, enforcement and the rollout of consumer protections. 18+ only. No bet is guaranteed.

A Record Quarter, in Context

The €911m Q3 figure is a snapshot of turnover — the total amount staked across online operators serving Irish customers — not profit or revenue. Gross gaming revenue (the amount operators retain after paying out winnings) is a fraction of that turnover figure. Nevertheless, the record underscores a long-running trend: the Irish online betting market has grown consistently for several years, driven by the shift from retail to mobile and app-based wagering, the expansion of in-play betting, and a broadening of markets beyond traditional horse racing and football.

The timing matters. GRAI began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026, meaning Q3 2026 was the first quarter in which a formal licensing regime was in operation. The record turnover arrived not in a moment of market uncertainty but in what appears to have been a consolidation phase: punters continued placing bets at record levels while the regulatory framework was being established around them.

What This Means for GRAI Licensing Pace

As of November 2026, around 41 remote betting licences had been issued by GRAI. Set against a market generating €911m in a single quarter, the gap between licensed operators and total market activity is significant. GRAI is aware of this. Its enforcement programme — targeting more than 100 unlicensed operators — is running alongside the licensing process, with the authority using access-blocking and compliance notices to push operators toward the application process or out of the Irish market.

The €911m figure will also inform how GRAI resources its operations going forward. The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 funds GRAI partly through licence fees and levies on licensed operators, calculated in relation to market activity. A higher-turnover market generates more regulatory funding — which in theory accelerates the authority's capacity to process applications and pursue enforcement. Whether that virtuous cycle develops in practice remains to be seen, but the Q3 record makes the case for sustained GRAI investment.

In-Person Licences: What Changes in December 2026

The next significant milestone in the GRAI rollout is the commencement of in-person (retail) betting licences, expected from December 2026. This extends the licensing framework to physical betting shops — the network of High Street bookmakers that remains a significant part of the Irish market, particularly for horse racing and older demographic groups.

Until now, GRAI licensing has covered only remote operations — online and telephone betting. The addition of in-person licences will bring retail bookmakers under the same conduct requirements, including mandatory deposit and time-limit tools (adapted for over-the-counter use), training requirements for staff and adherence to GRAI's advertising watershed rules. Retail operators will also be required to participate in the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER), meaning a self-exclusion submitted via grai.ie will apply to both online and in-person channels at licensed operators.

For punters, the practical implication is that the protections they receive online should, once in-person licences are issued, also be available at the counter. That alignment has been a gap in the framework since July — in-person betting has remained outside GRAI's formal licensing net while remote operators have been required to meet the new standards.

Casino Licences: Still Not on the Near-Term Horizon

The record turnover figures cover betting only. GRAI has issued zero casino or gaming licences as of November 2026 — these are an entirely separate regulatory category not expected to open until 2027–2028 at the earliest. The Q3 betting figure should not be read as evidence that a casino licensing round is approaching. Any site claiming to hold a GRAI casino licence at this time is making an inaccurate claim; the public register at grai.ie is the authoritative check.

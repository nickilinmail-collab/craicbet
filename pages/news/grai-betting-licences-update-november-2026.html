GRAI Betting Licences Ireland: What Bettors Need to Know in Late 2026

Published: 5 November 2026

📝 CraicBet Editorial Team 📅 5 November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

It has been four months since the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026 — the most significant overhaul of Irish gambling law since the Betting Act 1931. With approximately 41 remote betting licences now active on the GRAI's public register, we look at the state of play in November 2026: who is licensed, what it means in practice for Irish bettors, and what happens if you use a site that still has not obtained a licence.

Where the GRAI Licensing Roll-Out Stands

The GRAI opened its remote betting licence application window in February 2026, giving operators several months to prepare before the 1 July deadline. All previous Revenue Commissioner betting permissions expired on 30 June 2026; any bookmaker that failed to hold a GRAI remote betting licence from that date onwards is committing a criminal offence under Section 47 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024.

As of early November 2026, the GRAI public register at grai.ie lists approximately 41 active remote betting (RB) licences. These cover the major online bookmakers that Irish bettors use day-to-day. Each licence runs for three years from the date of issue. The first licence on the register — GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 — was issued on 16 September 2026 and is valid until 15 September 2029. Licence numbers follow a consistent format: operator identifier, licence type (RB for Remote Betting), year of issue, and a sequential number.

In-person betting shop licences are due to follow, with applications expected to open in December 2026. This matters for the roughly 900 licensed betting shops across Ireland, which currently operate in a transitional period.

What a GRAI Licence Actually Means for You

A GRAI remote betting licence is not simply a badge — it is a legally enforceable contract between the operator and the Irish state, with protections that run directly in your favour. Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, all GRAI-licensed bookmakers must:

  • Verify your age before accepting your first bet — mandatory, with document checks.
  • Offer deposit limits that take effect immediately when you request a reduction (increases carry a cooling-off period of 24–48 hours).
  • Pay out legitimate winnings as a licence condition under Irish law — not merely a contractual obligation you would have to pursue in a foreign court.
  • Ban credit card deposits — debit cards, e-wallets and bank transfers only.
  • Offer self-exclusion, which will connect to the national register once it goes live.
  • Maintain a formal complaints process with the GRAI as the final escalation point.

These are legal minimums, not extras a bookmaker can opt out of. The GRAI can investigate complaints, impose fines, and ultimately revoke a licence for non-compliance. That backstop simply did not exist in the Revenue Commissioner era.

What Happens to Unlicensed Operators?

Operators serving Irish customers without a GRAI licence face criminal prosecution under Section 47 of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, with fines of up to €20 million or five years' imprisonment. The GRAI has the power to seek injunctions preventing access to unlicensed sites from within Ireland.

For bettors, the risk is different but real. Using an unlicensed offshore site is not currently a criminal offence for the player, but it means you have no Irish legal protection if the site refuses to pay your winnings, no GRAI complaints route, no mandatory responsible gambling tools, and potentially no recourse whatsoever if the site closes. The GRAI has no jurisdiction over companies that do not hold an Irish licence — your complaint goes to whichever foreign regulator the site falls under, if any.

Important: Casino Licences Do Not Exist Yet

This is worth stating clearly. As of November 2026, the GRAI has issued zero remote gaming (casino) licences. If a website claims to hold a "GRAI casino licence", that claim is false — no such licence has been issued and applications are not expected to open until 2027–2028. Online casinos operating legally in Ireland currently hold licences from foreign regulators such as the Malta Gaming Authority or Estonian RGL. These are legitimate regulatory frameworks, but they do not carry the same Irish-law protections as a GRAI betting licence.

Sports betting is currently the only domestically regulated online gambling vertical in Ireland. That will change when casino licensing opens, but we are not there yet.

How to Check Any Bookmaker

Verifying a GRAI licence takes under two minutes. Go to the official public register at grai.ie, find the legal company name in the footer of the betting site (not the brand name), search for it on the register, and confirm that the licence type shows RB, status is Active, and the expiry date is in the future.

GRAI Licence Update: How Many Operators Are Licensed in Ireland — November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 4 November 2026 ⏱ 4 min read

Four months after the Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) began issuing licences on 1 July 2026, we are tracking how the public register is developing — and what Irish players can expect next.

Key Facts: GRAI Licences as of November 2026

  • The GRAI began issuing licences 1 July 2026 under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024
  • Only remote betting licences have been issued so far
  • No casino (gaming) licences have been issued — expected from 2027–2028
  • The public register is searchable at grai.ie
  • CraicBet's partner holds licence GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001 (valid to 15 Sep 2029)

What Is the GRAI Licence Register?

The GRAI maintains a public register of all licence holders, searchable at grai.ie. The register shows the operator name, licence number, type of licence, issue date and expiry date. This is the authoritative source — any operator claiming to be GRAI-licensed can be verified here in seconds.

Betting Licences: Remote Bookmaking

Under the Gambling Regulation Act 2024, any operator accepting online sports bets from Irish customers must hold a GRAI remote betting licence. These licences began being issued on 1 July 2026 and cover sports betting — both pre-match and in-play. They do not cover casino games, poker or other gaming products.

Operators that applied and received approval went through GRAI's vetting process, which checks ownership structures, financial stability, anti-money-laundering procedures, responsible gambling policies, age verification systems, and advertising practices. The process takes several months from application to approval.

Casino Licences: Still Pending

Under the Gambling Regulation Act, casino gaming licences are a separate phase. The GRAI has stated these are expected to follow the betting licence phase — industry commentary as of late 2026 points to 2027–2028 as the realistic window for the first casino licences.

⚠️ Zero GRAI casino licences exist as of November 2026. Any online casino claiming to hold a current GRAI casino licence is making a false claim. If you see this, check grai.ie to verify — and consider it a red flag.

What This Means for Irish Players

Irish players who want to place sports bets with a fully GRAI-licensed operator can do so now — betting licences are live and the register is searchable. The benefit of choosing a GRAI-licensed bookmaker over an offshore operator is that you get the full protection of Irish law: regulated deposit limits, access to the National Gambling Exclusion Register (NGER), segregated player funds, and the right to file a formal complaint with the GRAI if anything goes wrong.

For casino games, Irish players currently rely on licences from other reputable regulators — primarily the Malta Gaming Authority (MGA) and the Gibraltar Regulatory Authority. These regulators have a long track record and their licences provide meaningful player protection, though not the specific Irish statutory rights that a GRAI licence will eventually provide.

GRAI Licensing Update — Autumn 2026

The Gambling Regulatory Authority of Ireland (GRAI) began issuing remote betting licences on 1 July 2026, following the commencement of the Gambling Regulation Act 2024. This marked a fundamental shift in how online bookmakers operating in Ireland are authorised and regulated. The new framework replaces the previous patchwork of self-regulation with a single statutory authority that sets conduct rules, investigates complaints and can revoke licences.

Our partner operator holds betting licence GRAI-0191-RB-26-0001, issued 16 September 2026 and valid through 15 September 2029. This is a remote betting licence — it covers sports betting and similar products offered online to Irish customers.

One critical point for Irish punters: GRAI has not issued any casino (gaming) licences. Casino and gaming licences are a separate regulatory category that GRAI is not expected to open until 2027–2028 at the earliest. Any site claiming a GRAI casino licence as of November 2026 should be treated with serious scepticism. The public licence register at grai.ie is the authoritative source — it lists every valid licence by number, operator and type. See our GRAI-licensed sites list for a summary of verified operators.

What GRAI Licensing Means for Irish Punters

Choosing a GRAI-licensed bookmaker gives Irish punters several concrete protections under the Act. Licensed operators must offer deposit limits (daily, weekly and monthly), spending alerts triggered at thresholds you set, and access to the national GRAI self-exclusion register — which lets you exclude yourself from all licensed operators at once, rather than individually.

To verify a licence: visit grai.ie, navigate to the public register, and search by operator name or licence number. The register shows the licence type (remote betting vs. in-person), validity dates and any conditions. If a site is not on the register, it is not licensed — full stop.

Using an unlicensed site carries meaningful risks: no GRAI complaints pathway, no guaranteed segregation of player funds, and no requirement to offer responsible gambling tools. If a dispute arises with an unlicensed operator, GRAI has no jurisdiction. See our guide to sites that have ceased Irish operations and how self-exclusion works in practice.

Upcoming Racing — November & December 2026

November is a cornerstone month in the Irish jumps calendar. The National Hunt season is now in full swing, with Grade 1 and Graded races at Leopardstown, Navan and Naas providing early form pointers for the spring festivals. The significance of November form for ante-post Cheltenham betting should not be overstated — horses are early in their campaigns and the form book is thin — but high-class performances on testing ground do carry weight in the ante-post market.

Leopardstown's Christmas Festival (traditionally late December) is one of the highlights of the Irish racing year, hosting Grade 1 races across four days that frequently define the Cheltenham Festival ante-post market. Punters planning ante-post Cheltenham bets in December should factor in the Christmas Festival card before committing.

Looking further ahead, the Cheltenham Festival (March 2027) ante-post markets are already open for the Gold Cup, Champion Hurdle, Queen Mother Champion Chase and Ryanair Chase. Early ante-post bets carry the risk of non-runner without refund — always check the each-way terms and non-runner policies before committing. No race result or outcome is guaranteed. 18+ only.

Betting Industry Trends

The Irish betting market has seen significant structural shifts over the past two years. Mobile betting now accounts for the majority of online wagers placed by Irish punters, with app-based betting growing faster than desktop across every major operator. Bookmakers with dedicated iOS and Android apps — including live-streaming of racing and in-play markets — have seen the strongest growth in active accounts.

In-play (live) betting has grown sharply, particularly on football. The ability to bet on events as they unfold — next goal, next card, half-time result — appeals to a generation of punters who follow matches on their phones. GRAI's codes of practice require licensed operators to provide real-time spend summaries and to pause in-play betting when a customer requests a cooling-off period.

Football accumulators remain one of the most popular bet types among Irish punters. The appeal is the potentially large return from a small stake, but the mathematics are unambiguous: each additional selection multiplies both the potential return and the probability of losing. Responsible gambling tools mandated by GRAI — including accumulator stake limits and reality-check prompts — are designed to help punters keep their betting in perspective. No accumulator strategy guarantees profit.

Responsible Gambling Resources

If gambling is causing you or someone you know financial or emotional stress, help is available:

  • Gambling Awareness Trust: Freephone 1800 753 753 (confidential, free, available daily)
  • gamblingcare.ie — structured support, counselling referrals and a self-help portal
  • GRAI self-exclusion register — exclude yourself from all GRAI-licensed operators at once; apply via grai.ie
  • Deposit and time limits — all GRAI-licensed bookmakers must offer these in your account settings; use them proactively, not reactively

Quick Summary: Texas Hold'em is the world's most popular poker variant, played in everything from kitchen tables to the World Series of Poker. This guide covers the rules, hand rankings, strategy fundamentals and what Irish players should know before sitting down at a table.

Texas Hold'em Rules

Texas Hold'em is a community-card poker game. Each player receives two private cards (hole cards) dealt face down. Five community cards are then revealed in three stages — the Flop (three cards), the Turn (one card) and the River (one card). Players make the best possible five-card hand using any combination of their two hole cards and the five community cards.

A standard No-Limit Hold'em hand proceeds as follows:

  1. Blinds posted — The player to the dealer's left posts the small blind; the next player posts the big blind.
  2. Hole cards dealt — Each player receives two face-down cards.
  3. Pre-flop betting — Players act clockwise: fold, call the big blind, or raise.
  4. The Flop — Three community cards are dealt face up. Another betting round follows.
  5. The Turn — A fourth community card is dealt. Another betting round.
  6. The River — The fifth and final community card. Final betting round.
  7. Showdown — Remaining players reveal hands; best five-card combination wins the pot.

Beginner Strategy

Starting Hand Selection

The biggest mistake beginners make is playing too many hands. In a 9-player game, most of the hands dealt to you are unprofitable to play. A solid tight-aggressive (TAG) approach means raising with strong hands (AA, KK, QQ, JJ, AKs) from any position, and expanding to hands like suited connectors and medium pairs only from later position (the dealer button and the seats just before it).

Position Is Everything

Acting last in a betting round (being "in position") is a massive advantage. You see what every other player does before you must decide. The button (dealer position) is the strongest spot at the table. The blinds are the worst — you act first after the flop. Adjust your hand ranges accordingly.

Aggression Wins

Calling is the weakest action in poker. Betting and raising put pressure on opponents and win pots two ways — either by having the best hand at showdown, or by making everyone else fold. Passive play (calling everything, never betting) is a recipe for slow losses.

Pot Odds

When facing a bet, pot odds help you decide if a call is mathematically justified. Divide the amount you must call by the total pot after calling. If that percentage is lower than your probability of making the best hand, the call is profitable. For example, if you need to call €20 into a €100 pot (€120 total), you need at least 16.7% equity to break even.

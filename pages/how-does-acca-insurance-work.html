How Does Acca Insurance Work? Money Back on Accas Explained

Last updated: 5 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor📅 November 2026⏱ 7 min read

Acca insurance — also called "acca edge", "acca protect" or "money back on accas" depending on the bookmaker — is one of the most heavily promoted features at Irish betting sites. The idea is appealing: build a five-fold accumulator, and if exactly one leg lets you down, you get your stake back. For punters who have experienced the agony of a last-leg loser, it sounds like genuine protection. This guide explains what acca insurance actually covers, the conditions attached, and the honest mathematical value it offers.

What Acca Insurance Actually Covers

Acca insurance covers exactly one scenario: exactly one leg of your accumulator loses, and every other leg wins. If your five-fold has four winners and one loser, you receive a refund. If two legs lose, the standard acca result applies — no refund, no insurance.

This is an important limitation that many punters overlook. A five-fold acca has many possible loss scenarios. Acca insurance covers only the most frustrating one (the last-leg loser) while leaving all others fully at risk. In probability terms, the one-leg-fails scenario represents only a portion of all losing outcomes, so the insurance covers only a subset of the ways your acca can fail.

Free Bet, Not Cash: The Key Detail

The refund from acca insurance is almost universally paid as a free bet, not cash. This is a critical distinction. A free bet cannot be withdrawn — it must be used on another bet. Under SNR (Stake Not Returned) terms, you receive only the profit from a winning free bet, not the token value itself.

Practically: a €10 acca insurance refund paid as a free bet is worth roughly €6.50–€8.50 in real terms, depending on what odds you use the free bet at. The headline "get your stake back" implies €10 cash; the reality is €6.50–€8.50 in expected value from a free bet.

Standard Conditions to Expect

Acca insurance terms vary by bookmaker but typically include:

  • Minimum legs: Usually 5 or more selections
  • Minimum odds per leg: Typically 1/2 (1.5 decimal) or similar. Shorter-priced legs often excluded.
  • Minimum stake: Usually €1–€5
  • Refund cap: Maximum refund amount, often €10–€25
  • Specific markets: May exclude certain sports, tournaments or bet types
  • Refund expiry: Free bet usually expires within 7 days
  • Opt-in requirement: Some offers require manual opt-in before placing

Always read the full terms before placing an acca with the expectation of insurance — many punters are surprised to discover their qualifying bet did not meet one condition.

The Honest Maths: Does Acca Insurance Change the Value of Accas?

Marginally, yes — but far less than the marketing implies. The compounded bookmaker margin that makes accas their most profitable product is entirely unchanged by insurance. A 5% margin on each of five legs still compounds to roughly 23% combined edge before the first ball is kicked. Acca insurance covers one losing pattern at the cost of a free bet refund, but the fundamental structural disadvantage of the bet remains the same.

What acca insurance genuinely does: it provides partial recovery in the specific heartbreak scenario (four legs up, one to go, last leg fails). For the entertainment value of accumulators, that is a real quality-of-life improvement. What does acca insurance cover?

Acca insurance typically refunds your stake (as a free bet) if exactly one leg fails and all other legs win. It only covers the one-leg-fails scenario.

Is the acca insurance refund paid in cash or as a free bet?

Almost always as a free bet (SNR), not cash. The refunded value cannot be withdrawn directly — it must be used as a free bet subject to the bookmaker's free bet terms.

What are the typical conditions for acca insurance?

Minimum number of legs (usually 5+), minimum odds per leg (typically 1/2 or 1.3), minimum stake, specific sports or markets only, refund cap, and refund paid as a free bet with an expiry date.

Does acca insurance change the value of placing accumulators?

Marginally. It converts one specific losing scenario into a partial recovery. But the compounded bookmaker margin across all legs is unchanged. The acca is still the bookmaker's most profitable product per pound staked.

Can acca insurance apply to Bet Builders?

Usually not. Acca insurance is typically for multi-event accumulators. Bet Builders (same game multis) are usually explicitly excluded.

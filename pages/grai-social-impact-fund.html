The GRAI Social Impact Fund: Who Pays and Where It Goes

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Every GRAI-licensed operator pays a mandatory levy into a Social Impact Fund that finances problem-gambling research, education and treatment in Ireland — the industry funding the repair of the harm it can cause. Here is who pays, where the money goes, and the little-known detail about what happens to the balance when a betting account is closed.

Who Pays, and How Much

The Gambling Regulation Act 2024 establishes the fund and makes contributing a condition of holding a licence, with the usual sanctions behind it. Contributions are set as a percentage of each licensee's turnover — proportionate to scale — with charitable lottery licensees exempt. The precise rates were still being settled through consultation as of late 2026, much as licence fees were before S.I. 37/2026 fixed them.

The Closed-Account Detail

The levy is not the fund's only source: non-refundable player funds from closed accounts also flow into it. Money left behind when accounts shut does not quietly become operator revenue — it goes to harm reduction. A small rule, but a telling one about the design.

Where the Money Goes

Three destinations written into the Act: research into gambling harm in Ireland (long underfunded — Irish policy has leaned on UK data for years), awareness and education programmes, and treatment services for people harmed by gambling. Today that ecosystem runs largely on services like the Gambling Awareness Trust (1800 753 753, gamblingcare.ie).

Who Runs It

The GRAI forms the fund and operates the levy, with Pobal — the State agency that manages social-inclusion funding — assisting in administering it, and the Department of Justice overseeing allocation. The GRAI ran a national stakeholder consultation on the fund's priorities, reporting in September 2025 with input from over 200 organisations and people with lived experience of gambling harm.

Why This Matters to Punters

Two reasons. First, it is another real cost of being licensed: an operator paying the levy is invested in the Irish system and visible on the register. Second, it funds independent, Irish-specific research into what actually causes harm — which over time shapes smarter rules than imported assumptions. Offshore operators contribute nothing to any of it — one more entry on the unlicensed-site ledger.

What to Watch

The levy rates being prescribed, the first funding allocations, and whether the fund's research arm publishes openly — our GRAI hub tracks the regime as it builds out.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GRAI Social Impact Fund?

A fund under the 2024 Act, financed by a mandatory turnover-based levy on licensed operators — plus non-refundable player funds from closed accounts — paying for research, education and treatment.

How much do operators pay into the fund?

A percentage of turnover, proportionate to scale, with charitable lotteries exempt. The precise rates were still being settled through consultation as of late 2026.

Who administers the Social Impact Fund?

The GRAI operates the levy, assisted by Pobal in administration, with the Department of Justice overseeing allocation.

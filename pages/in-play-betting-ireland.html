In-Play Betting in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 6 min read

In-play betting (also called live betting) means placing bets on a sporting event while it is happening. Odds update in real time — often multiple times per minute — to reflect the current score, time remaining, and game events like goals, red cards or injuries. It is now one of the largest segments of online betting in Ireland, with Premier League football and horse racing driving most volume.

How In-Play Odds Work

Before kick-off, a bookmaker sets prices based on team form, injuries, expected lineups and statistical models. Once a match starts, those prices shift continuously based on what happens. A goal changes the match result market immediately: if the underdog scores first, the favourite's odds shorten on the “they will win” side and lengthen on the “result stays” side. A red card for the stronger team causes a sharp re-pricing across nearly every market.

The bookmaker's margin (overround) on in-play markets is typically higher than pre-match, compensating for the speed at which prices must be generated. Suspension of betting — a brief pause in markets while events are processed — is normal and means your bet may not be accepted at the displayed price. Most bookmakers use a “price request” model for in-play, where you submit a bet at the displayed price and it is confirmed or rejected within a few seconds.

Markets Available for In-Play Football

The range of in-play football markets has expanded significantly since 2020. Standard offerings from GRAI-licensed bookmakers include:

  • Next goal: which team scores the next goal (or no more goals)
  • Next goal scorer: individual player to score the next goal
  • Match result: updated odds reflecting the current score and time
  • Half-time result: who leads at half time
  • Next card (yellow/red): which team receives the next booking
  • Asian totals in-play: over/under goals for the remainder of the match, using fractional lines
  • Correct score: final score, repriced in real time
  • Both teams to score: will the other team score before full time?

Not all markets are available from kick-off or continuously — some open only at half time or after specific game events.

Strategy: Finding Value in Live Markets

In-play betting creates genuine opportunities for punters who watch matches closely. Three scenarios where the odds can lag behind the real probability shift:

Red cards: When a team has a player sent off, in-play markets adjust, but sometimes the initial price change underestimates the impact (particularly in the first half when the attacking team has time to capitalise). The “next goal” market is often the fastest to reprice; “match result” can lag by a few seconds.

Momentum shifts: After a goal, the scoring team often presses less aggressively while the conceding team pushes forward. The “next goal” market can momentarily shorten on the team that just scored, before adjusting for the increased counter-attack risk. This is a narrow window and requires fast decision-making.

Late-match pressure: A team chasing a 1-0 deficit in the final 15 minutes is statistically more likely to concede on the counter than the pre-match probability implied. Over/under and correct score markets can offer value here if you think the game will stay tight rather than open up.

No strategy is a guarantee. In-play betting by nature involves more uncertainty than pre-match, and no pronostic is guaranteed. These are observations about market inefficiencies, not systems.

Cash Out: What It Is and When to Use It

Cash out lets you settle an open bet before the event ends, at a price the bookmaker offers based on the current state of play. If your pre-match acca has 4 of 5 legs winning with one match in the 70th minute at 1-0 to your selection, cash out locks in a profit without waiting for the final whistle.

The honest maths of cash out: the bookmaker offers a price that reflects their margin. If the true fair value of your open position is €80 based on current probabilities, the cash-out offer will typically be €70–€75. You are paying for certainty. Partial cash out — available at some bookmakers — lets you lock in a portion of the return while leaving the rest to run.

When cash out is worth using: when the event result no longer feels certain and you want to remove risk. When it is not worth using: purely to avoid watching an uncomfortable match. The maths of refusing every cash-out offer is neutral in the long run; the maths of accepting every one slightly favours the bookmaker due to the margin built in.

Risks of In-Play Betting and Responsible Gambling

In-play betting carries a higher impulsive betting risk than pre-match betting, because decisions happen quickly and under time pressure. The speed, the flashing odds, and the adrenaline of a live match create conditions where it is easy to bet more than intended or to chase a losing position. The Gambling Awareness Trust (1800 753 753, gamblingcare.ie) supports anyone in Ireland who feels their gambling is becoming a problem.

Practical measures that help: set a session budget before the match starts and do not exceed it; avoid in-play betting while also following the same match on a time-delayed broadcast (your view of events may be 10–30 seconds behind the bookmaker's pricing); take breaks between in-play sessions.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is in-play betting?

In-play betting (also called live betting) means placing bets on a sporting event while it is in progress. Odds update in real time to reflect the current score, time remaining and other factors such as red cards or injuries.

What markets are available for in-play football betting?

Common in-play football markets include: next goal scorer, next team to score, next card, half-time result, Asian totals in-play, correct score, over/under goals (remaining in the match) and match result updated for the current state.

What is cash out in in-play betting?

Cash out lets you settle an open bet before the event ends, at a price the bookmaker offers based on the current state of play. If your selection is winning, cash out locks in a profit; if losing, it limits the loss. The cash-out value reflects the bookmaker's current assessment and includes their margin.

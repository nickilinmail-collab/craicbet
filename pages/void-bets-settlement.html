Void Bets & Settlement Rules: What Stands, What’s Refunded

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 1 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

A void bet is a cancelled bet: stake back, no winnings, as if it never happened. Simple enough on a single — and the source of endless confusion inside accas, props and postponed fixtures, because when a bet voids is governed by each bookmaker’s house rules, and those rules differ. This guide maps the standard conventions and the places disputes actually come from.

What “void” means on the slip

A voided single returns the stake in full. Inside an accumulator, the voided leg is settled at odds of 1.00 and the rest of the bet stands: a five-fold with one void leg becomes a four-fold at the four remaining prices. You don’t lose the acca — you lose that leg’s contribution to the odds. This recalculation is standard across the industry and surprises punters every weekend regardless.

Postponed and abandoned fixtures

The near-universal football convention: if a match is postponed or abandoned and not restaged within a stated window, bets void. The window is where books differ — commonly 24 or 48 hours, sometimes “by the end of the following day” or, for some competitions, whenever the official body restages it. The practical consequence: the same postponed match can leave your bet alive at one bookmaker and refunded at another. Abandonments part-way through usually void match markets but settle anything already decided — a first-goalscorer bet stands if a goal was scored before the floodlights failed.

Non-runners in racing

A horse withdrawn before coming under starter’s orders is a non-runner: fixed-odds singles are refunded, and winning bets on the rest of the field face a Rule 4 deduction that returns the market to honest proportions. Ante-post is the exception that catches people — standard ante-post terms are “all-in, run or not”, meaning no refund, unless you took a non-runner-no-bet concession at shorter odds.

Player props when the player never plays

The modern dispute factory. You back a striker to score anytime; he’s left on the bench for ninety minutes. At most bookmakers an anytime-scorer bet on an unused substitute voids — but a player who comes on for one added-time minute has played, and the bet typically stands as a loser. Other props draw the line differently: some settle “to be shown a card” or shots markets only if the player starts, voiding otherwise. There is no industry-wide rule here, only each book’s terms — which is precisely why the same benched striker can mean a refund at one site and a loss at another.

Where disputes really come from

Three patterns dominate: punters assuming one bookmaker’s void rules apply everywhere; markets settled on official results that are later amended (settlement normally follows the result on the day, not the appeal); and palpable errors, where a book voids a bet struck at an obviously wrong price — legal under their terms, infuriating in practice. The defence is the same in each case: the house rules page, read before the bet, and screenshots of anything odd. Honest conclusions

Voids are not the bookmaker dodging a payout — mostly they are the fair answer to an event that didn’t happen as priced. The unfairness, where it exists, lives in the gaps between different books’ rules and punters’ assumptions. Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to my acca if one leg is void?

The void leg is settled at odds of 1.00 and the rest stands: a five-fold with one void leg becomes a four-fold at the remaining prices. You don’t lose the bet — only that leg’s contribution to the odds.

Are bets refunded if a match is postponed?

Usually — if the match isn’t restaged within the bookmaker’s stated window, commonly 24 or 48 hours. The window differs by bookmaker and competition, so the same postponement can leave a bet standing at one book and refunded at another.

Is my anytime goalscorer bet void if the player doesn't play?

At most bookmakers, yes for an unused substitute — but a player who appears even for a minute has played, and the bet stands as a loser. Prop settlement rules differ between bookmakers, so the terms on the day decide.

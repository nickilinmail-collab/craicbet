Darts Betting in Ireland

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor 📅 Updated November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

Darts has grown into one of the most-watched and bet-on televised sports in Ireland and the UK. The PDC calendar runs year-round, culminating in the World Championship at Alexandra Palace each December and January. The sport's appeal for betting is straightforward: the action is fast, the drama is constant, and in-play markets shift rapidly leg by leg. This guide covers the PDC calendar, the main betting markets, and what Irish punters need to know about darts market liquidity.

The PDC Darts Calendar

PDC World Darts Championship (Alexandra Palace, mid-December to early January): The year's showpiece event. 96 players, single elimination from round one to the final on New Year's Day. This is the most-bet darts event of the year by a significant margin, with outright winner markets open from the summer draw and in-play markets available on every match.

PDC Premier League (February to May): Sixteen weeks of league-stage matches between the world's top players, played at arenas around the UK and occasionally Ireland. The round-robin format provides regular content across the spring and delivers a play-off final in May. Weekly outright odds on individual night winners are available alongside the overall tournament outright.

World Matchplay (Blackpool, July): A major summer TV tournament played in a straight-legs format without sets. The Blackpool crowd atmosphere is famous and the TV audience is high. Outright and in-play markets are well-covered.

Grand Prix (Citywest, Dublin, October): The only major PDC event played in Ireland, with a unique double-in/double-out format. Home crowd atmosphere and Irish tournament interest make this event particularly notable for Irish punters, with strong local following.

World Grand Prix, UK Open, European Championship and the Players Championship events fill the remainder of the calendar.

Main Darts Betting Markets

Match winner: The fundamental market in darts. Available pre-match and in-play. Market liquidity is highest on Premier League and World Championship matches; significantly lower on Players Championship and development tour events.

Correct score (sets or legs): Predict the final score in sets (e.g., 7-5 in a World Championship match) or legs (in shorter formats). Higher odds compensate for the precision required. For high-profile matches, correct score markets are available pre-match; in-play correct-score-from-here markets are available during some events.

Most 180s: Which player hits more maximum scores (180) in the match? Strongly correlated with the player's average but with high variance leg to leg. Priced close to even in matched-ability games, with adjustments for the statistically higher-averaging player.

Highest checkout: Which player hits the highest single checkout (finishing score on a double or bull) in the match? A prop market that is relatively unpredictable and priced accordingly. Available on major events.

Outright winner: Who wins the tournament? Available on all major events. The World Championship outright attracts the most money, with top players typically priced between 4/1 and 10/1 for the top-five ranked players and longer for the field.

In-Play Darts Betting

Darts is one of the best in-play sports for a simple reason: momentum shifts are fast, frequent and visible. A player who loses three straight legs from a winning position in a set is experiencing a momentum collapse that bookmakers reprice in real time. For punters who watch matches live, being able to read these shifts before the market fully adjusts is a genuine skill.

In-play darts is available on all major PDC events at GRAI-licensed bookmakers. The key practical consideration is that darts in-play odds move quickly — a price available at the start of a leg may be significantly different by the time you submit the bet. Most bookmakers have a "request settled" mechanism where in-play prices are confirmed before acceptance. See our in-play betting guide for the mechanics.

Market Liquidity: An Honest Note

Darts market liquidity is substantially lower than football and horse racing, and this matters for punters. On World Championship quarter-finals and later, markets are well-traded and maximum stakes are reasonable. On first-round World Championship matches between lower-ranked players, and especially on Players Championship tour events, maximum stakes on specialist markets can be very low. If you plan to bet larger amounts on darts, focus on the premier events (World Championship, Premier League, World Matchplay) and the main match-winner market rather than specialist side bets.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the PDC World Darts Championship?

The PDC World Darts Championship runs from mid-December through to the final in early January at Alexandra Palace, London. It is the highest-profile darts event of the year and attracts the largest betting volumes in darts.

What are the most popular darts betting markets?

Match winner, correct score in sets or legs, most 180s, highest checkout and tournament outright. The World Championship outright attracts the most money of any single darts market.

Is in-play darts betting popular in Ireland?

Yes. Darts lends itself well to in-play because legs change quickly and momentum shifts are dramatic. Most GRAI-licensed bookmakers offer in-play markets on PDC events, with odds updating leg by leg.

Are darts markets as liquid as football markets?

No. Darts market liquidity is significantly lower than top football, particularly for early-round World Championship matches. Expect lower maximum stakes on specialist markets and higher bookmaker margins than on Premier League football.

What is the PDC Premier League Darts?

A sixteen-week round-robin league from February to May, featuring the world's top-ranked players at arenas around the UK. The top four compete in a play-off final in May. It provides regular darts betting content throughout the spring.

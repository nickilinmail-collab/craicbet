First Goalscorer Betting: Understanding the Market

Last updated: 4 November 2026

📝 Mark Craic, editor (8 years in gambling analytics) 📅 Updated 4 November 2026 ⏱ 5 min read

How first goalscorer markets work

First goalscorer betting asks you to pick which player will score the opening goal of a match. Each outfield player in the starting lineup is listed at odds reflecting their probability of scoring first, with substitutes sometimes listed at longer odds. If no goal is scored, bets are typically void (stake returned). Most operators also offer last goalscorer and anytime goalscorer markets alongside first scorer.

The market margin problem

First goalscorer markets carry some of the widest bookmaker margins in football. With 22 outfield starters plus substitutes to price, the margin on each individual can be 25-40% above fair probability. A striker priced at 5/1 to score first may have a true probability that fairly warrants 7/1 or 8/1. This gap is structural and impossible to overcome with form study alone.

Factors that influence first goalscorer odds

Goal-scoring rate, penalty-taking responsibility, corner and free-kick service, and opposition defensive strength are the main pricing inputs. A penalty taker in a team that wins many penalties is correctly given a tighter price. Set-piece specialists who score from dead-ball situations are also priced accordingly. Injuries and lineup changes can create short-term mispricing — a confirmed first-choice striker missing reduces the value of his backup’s price more than the market sometimes reflects.

Anytime goalscorer as an alternative

Anytime goalscorer (scoring at any point in the match) carries a lower margin than first goalscorer and a higher strike rate. A striker at 5/2 to score anytime is a more defensible bet than the same player at 5/1 to score first, because the event (scoring at all) is more likely. Frequently Asked Questions

What happens to a first goalscorer bet if there is no goal?

In most markets, first goalscorer bets are void if the match ends 0-0, and stakes are returned. Check your operator's specific terms, as some apply this rule only to starters and treat a substitute who does not score differently.

What is the difference between first goalscorer and anytime goalscorer?

First goalscorer bets on who scores the opening goal of the match. Anytime goalscorer bets on a player scoring at any point. Anytime scorer odds are shorter but have a higher strike rate; first scorer odds are longer but carry a wider bookmaker margin per selection.

Can I combine first goalscorer with a match result?

Yes. Some operators offer a combined ‘scorecast’ or ‘wincast’ market combining a first goalscorer with a correct score or match result. These products carry compound margins — both the scorer margin and the result/score margin are built in.

First Goalscorer vs Anytime Goalscorer: Odds Comparison

First goalscorer typically pays roughly 3–4× the anytime goalscorer price for the same player. Example for a striker priced at 3/1 (4.00) to score anytime:

  • Anytime scorer: 4.00 (3/1)
  • First goalscorer: 11.00–14.00 (10/1–13/1)
  • Last goalscorer: 11.00–14.00 (similar to first)
  • To score 2+: 8.00–12.00 (7/1–11/1)

First goalscorer markets typically carry a margin of 20–30%, significantly above the standard 1X2 margin of 5–8%. Restrict to your strongest conviction selections.
